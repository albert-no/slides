A Smaller Matrix, Controlled Error

Given $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, every entry observed  ·  choose $B$ with $\operatorname{rank}(B) \le k$

$\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$

data $A$ (rank 2)

=
$\begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix}$

candidate $B$ (rank 1)

+
$\begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & -1.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$

residual $A - B$

Quality = size of the residual $A - B$, measured by a matrix norm

Two questions: which $B$ is closest, and how small can the error be?

Rank-$k$ factors store $k(m+n)$ numbers: smaller than $mn$ only when $k(m+n) \lt mn$

Recall: Span and Range

$A = [\,a_1 \; \cdots \; a_n\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ has columns $a_j \in \mathbb{R}^m$; input $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$Ax = x_1 a_1 + x_2 a_2 + \cdots + x_n a_n \;\in \mathbb{R}^m$$

Range (column space): everything $A$ can output

$$\operatorname{range}(A) = \{ Ax : x \in \mathbb{R}^n \} = \operatorname{span}\{a_1, \dots, a_n\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$$
$a_1$ $a_2$ $a_1 + a_2$

Example: $x = (1,1)$ gives $Ax = a_1 + a_2$

Span = all linear combinations; the range is a subspace of the output space $\mathbb{R}^m$

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §2.7.3, eq. (2.124). Figure: our illustration with $m = n = 2$.

Rank Counts Independent Directions

Definition. $\operatorname{rank}(A) = \dim \operatorname{range}(A)$: the size of any basis of the range

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U$ $m \times r$ $C$ $r \times n$

Columns of $U$: a basis $u_1, \dots, u_r$ of the range  ·  $C$: each column's coordinates

Not nonzero entries, not columns: $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1 & 1\\ 2 & 2\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ has 4 and 2, yet rank 1

Definition: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §2.7.3 (rk$(A)=\dim \operatorname{Im}$). Factorization $A = UC$: our derivation (see note). Block sizes schematic.

Two Inputs Collapse to One Direction

$$C = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}: \qquad Cx = x_1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + x_2 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = (x_1 + x_2) \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$$
input plane output plane $e_1$ $e_2$ $(1,1)$ $C$ $Ce_1 = Ce_2 = (1,2)$ $C(1,1) = (2,4)$ $\operatorname{range}(C)$

Every output lies on the line $\operatorname{span}\{(1,2)\}$, so $\operatorname{rank}(C) = 1$

Our worked example; the plotted points are exact images under $C$.

Null Space Means Lost Directions

Definition. $\operatorname{null}(A) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^n : Ax = 0 \}$: the inputs sent to zero

For $C$: $Cx = (x_1 + x_2)(1,2)$ vanishes exactly when $x_1 + x_2 = 0$

input plane output plane $\operatorname{null}(C)$ $e_1$ $e_2$ $e_1 - e_2 = (1,-1)$ $C$ $C(1,-1) = 0$ $Ce_1 = Ce_2$

$\operatorname{null}(C) = \operatorname{span}\{(1,-1)\}$: an input direction in $\mathbb{R}^n$, not an output

Definition: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §2.7.3, Def. 2.23. Example: our calculation.

Rank and Nullity Add Up

Theorem (rank–nullity). For every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, square or not:

$$\operatorname{rank}(A) + \dim \operatorname{null}(A) = n \quad (\text{number of columns})$$
$\operatorname{rank}(A)$: kept $\dim \operatorname{null}(A)$: lost all $n$ input dimensions

Check on $C$: rank $1$ + nullity $1$ = $2$ columns

Wide $A \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times 3}$: rank $\le 2$, so a null direction always exists

Used later: $\operatorname{rank}(B) \le k$ forces $\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \ge n - k$

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §2.7.3, Theorem 2.24 (for the map $x \mapsto Ax$). Proof in the note. Bar widths schematic.

Measuring Error

Two sizes for a residual

What Makes a Norm?

Definition. A norm is $\|\cdot\| : \mathbb{R}^n \to [0, \infty)$ with, for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\|x\| = 0 \iff x = 0, \qquad \|\alpha x\| = |\alpha|\, \|x\|, \qquad \|x + y\| \le \|x\| + \|y\|$$
$x$ $y$ $x + y$

Zero only at zero · scaling by $|\alpha|$, sign ignored

Triangle: the direct route is never longer than the detour

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §3.1, Definition 3.1. Triangle sketch schematic.

Three Familiar Vector Norms

For a coordinate vector $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$\ell_1$: absolute sum

$$\|x\|_1 = \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|$$

walk along the axes

$\ell_2$: Euclidean

$$\|x\|_2 = \Big(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2\Big)^{1/2}$$

straight-line length

$\ell_\infty$: max

$$\|x\|_\infty = \max_{i} |x_i|$$

largest single coordinate

Only $\ell_2$ is length: $x = (1,1)$ has $\|x\|_1 = 2$ but $\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{2}$

$\ell_1$, $\ell_2$: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §3.1, Examples 3.1–3.2. $\ell_\infty$: standard definition (not in the cited text).

One Vector, Three Answers: 7, 5, 4

Take $x = (3, 4)$:

$$\begin{aligned} \|x\|_1 &= |3| + |4| = 7 \\ \|x\|_2 &= \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5 \\ \|x\|_\infty &= \max(|3|, |4|) = 4 \end{aligned}$$

Same vector; the norm decides the size

Always $\|x\|_\infty \le \|x\|_2 \le \|x\|_1$

$x = (3,4)$ $\ell_2$: $5$ $\ell_1$: $3 + 4$ $\ell_\infty$: $4$
Our worked example; figure drawn to scale.

Unit ball $\{x : \|x\| \le 1\}$: every vector the norm calls “at most 1”

$(1,0)$ $\big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}\big)$ $(1,1)$ $\ell_1$: diamond $\ell_2$: circle $\ell_\infty$: square

Marked points: norm exactly 1  ·  diamond $\subset$ circle $\subset$ square

Our illustration, drawn to scale. Unit-ball shapes: standard; nesting from $\|x\|_\infty \le \|x\|_2 \le \|x\|_1$ (proof in the note).

Orthogonal: square $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ with

$$Q^\top Q = I = Q Q^\top$$

Columns orthonormal; $Q^{-1} = Q^\top$

Rotations turn vectors; the length stays 1

Tall $W = [e_1 \; e_2] \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 2}$: $W^\top W = I_2$ but $W W^\top \ne I_3$, so not orthogonal

$e_1$ $Q e_1$ $45^\circ$ unit circle
Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §3.4, Definition 3.8. Figure: our illustration, to scale.

Why Length Is Preserved

Claim. Square $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ with $Q^\top Q = I$. For every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$: $\;\|Qx\|_2 = \|x\|_2$ (Euclidean norm)

$$\|Qx\|_2^2 = (Qx)^\top (Qx) = x^\top \underbrace{Q^\top Q}_{=\,I}\, x = x^\top x = \|x\|_2^2$$

Both sides are $\ge 0$; take square roots. $\blacksquare$

Same step with two vectors: $(Qx)^\top (Qy) = x^\top y$, so angles are kept

Only $\ell_2$ is protected: $\|Qe_1\|_1 = \sqrt{2} \ne 1 = \|e_1\|_1$ for the $45^\circ$ rotation

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §3.4, Definition 3.8 and eqs. (3.31)–(3.32).

Spectral Norm: Worst Stretch

Definition. For a matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$:

$$\|A\|_2 = \max_{x \in \mathbb{R}^n,\ \|x\|_2 = 1} \|Ax\|_2$$
$x^\star$ $A$ unit circle (×3) $A x^\star$: longest image $\{Ax\}$

$\|A\|_2$: one number for the whole matrix

Not the vector $\|x\|_2$; not entrywise $\ell_2$ (that is Frobenius)

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §4.6, Definition 4.23; Wilkinson, MATH3030, §3.5.1, Definition 3.2 ($p = 2$). Figure: our running $A$, to scale.

Frobenius Norm: Total Energy

$$\|A\|_F = \Big(\sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}^2\Big)^{1/2}, \qquad \|A - B\|_F^2 = \sum_{i,j} (a_{ij} - b_{ij})^2$$
$1.5^2 = 2.25$
$(-1.5)^2 = 2.25$
$(-0.5)^2 = 0.25$
$0.5^2 = 0.25$

Residual $A - B$ (opening example), squared

Sum $= 5$, so $\|A - B\|_F = \sqrt{5}$

Every entry counts, unweighted

Differs from spectral: $\|I_2\|_2 = 1$, $\|I_2\|_F = \sqrt{2}$

Wilkinson, MATH3030, §3.5.1, Definition 3.1. Numbers: our calculation on the running example.

Reading an SVD

Rotate, stretch, rotate

Recall: Symmetric Eigendecomposition

Recall (spectral theorem). Symmetric $S \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$: $S = Q \Lambda Q^{\top}$, $Q$ orthogonal, $\Lambda$ real diagonal

Apply to $A^{\top}A$: symmetric, $n \times n$, acts on inputs $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$

$$A^{\top}A = Q\Lambda Q^{\top}, \qquad \lambda_i = q_i^{\top}A^{\top}A\,q_i = \|Aq_i\|_2^2 \geq 0$$
$A^{\top}A$ supplies orthonormal input directions $q_1, \dots, q_n \in \mathbb{R}^n$
Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §4.2, Theorems 4.14–4.15

Every Matrix Has an SVD

Theorem (SVD).

Every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ of rank $r$: any shape, symmetric or not, invertible or not

$$A = U \Sigma V^{\top}, \qquad U \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m},\; V \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n} \text{ orthogonal}$$

$\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$: $\Sigma_{ii} = \sigma_i$, zero off the diagonal; $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$, rest $0$

Construction: eigenvectors of $A^{\top}A$ give $V$; then $u_i = Av_i/\sigma_i$

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §4.5, Thm 4.22, §4.5.2

Read the Factor Shapes

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U$ $m \times m$, orthogonal $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r \gt 0$ $\Sigma$ zero rows $m \times n$ $V^{\top}$ $n \times n$, orthogonal

$(m \times m)(m \times n)(n \times n) = m \times n$: drawn for $m \gt n$

Solid diagonal: $r$ nonzero $\sigma_i$ · dashed: zero slots · gray band: zero rows

Shapes as in Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §4.5, eq. (4.64)

A Sum of Rank-One Layers

$$A = U\Sigma V^{\top} = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i\, \underbrace{u_i}_{m\times 1}\,\underbrace{v_i^{\top}}_{1\times n}$$

Each $u_i v_i^{\top}$: $m \times n$, every column a multiple of $u_i$

$\sigma_i \gt 0$: term $i$ has rank one

All $r$ terms together give $A$ exactly

$\sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top}$ largest weight $A = $ layer $1 + \cdots +$ layer $r$ back layers: smaller $\sigma_i$
Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §4.6

Compact SVD Keeps Active Directions

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U_r$ $m \times r$ $\Sigma_r$ $r \times r$ $V_r^{\top}$ $r \times n$ keep only the $r$ nonzero $\sigma_i$
$$A = U_r \Sigma_r V_r^{\top}, \quad \Sigma_r = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r), \quad U_r^{\top}U_r = V_r^{\top}V_r = I_r$$

$r \lt m$: $U_rU_r^{\top} \neq I_m$; thin factors are not orthogonal matrices

Largest Singular Value, Largest Stretch

Theorem (spectral norm).

For every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with largest singular value $\sigma_1$:

$$\|A\|_2 \;=\; \max_{\|x\|_2 = 1} \|Ax\|_2 \;=\; \sigma_1$$

1. Upper bound

$\|Ax\|_2 \leq \sigma_1$ for every unit $x$

2. Witness

one unit $x$ with $\|Ax\|_2 = \sigma_1$

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML §4.6, Def. 4.23, Thm 4.24 · Wilkinson, MATH3030 §3.5.1, Prop. 3.11

No Direction Beats the Largest Stretch

Any unit $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$; set $y = V^{\top}x$, so $\|y\|_2 = 1$ (orthogonal invariance)

$$\begin{aligned} \|Ax\|_2^2 &\;=\; \|U\Sigma y\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(U \text{ orthogonal})}{=}\; \|\Sigma y\|_2^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2 y_i^2 \\ &\;\stackrel{(\sigma_i \leq \sigma_1)}{\leq}\; \sigma_1^2 \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i^2 \;=\; \sigma_1^2 \|y\|_2^2 \;=\; \sigma_1^2 \end{aligned}$$

As $x$ runs over all unit vectors, so does $y$: no direction skipped

Proof idea: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML §4.6, Thm 4.24 (exercise there)

The First Singular Vector Attains It

Witness: the unit vector $x = v_1$

$$Av_1 = U\Sigma V^{\top}v_1 = U\Sigma e_1 = \sigma_1 u_1, \qquad \|Av_1\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\|u_1\|_2 = 1)}{=}\; \sigma_1$$
$$\sigma_1 = \|Av_1\|_2 \;\leq\; \|A\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\text{upper bound})}{\leq}\; \sigma_1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \|A\|_2 = \sigma_1 \;\;\blacksquare$$

Geometry: $\sigma_1 u_1$ is the ellipse's longest semi-axis

Proof idea: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML §4.6, Thm 4.24 (exercise there)

Frobenius Energy Sums Squared Stretches

Theorem (Frobenius norm).

For every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ of rank $r$:

$$\|A\|_F^2 \;=\; \sum_{i,j} a_{ij}^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2$$

Contrast: $\|A\|_2 = \sigma_1$ is the worst single direction; $\|A\|_F$ is total energy

Worked $A$: $\|A\|_F^2 = 9 + 0 + 16 + 25 = 50 = 45 + 5$

Wilkinson, MATH3030 §3.5.1, Prop. 3.10

Orthogonal Coordinates Preserve Total Energy

Orthogonal $Q$, $W$; $B$ with columns $b_j$; orthogonal invariance per column:

$$\begin{aligned} \|QB\|_F^2 &= \textstyle\sum_j \|Qb_j\|_2^2 = \sum_j \|b_j\|_2^2 = \|B\|_F^2 && \text{(columns)} \\ \|BW\|_F &= \|W^{\top}B^{\top}\|_F = \|B^{\top}\|_F = \|B\|_F && \text{(rows, via transpose)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\|A\|_F^2 = \|U^{\top}AV\|_F^2 = \|\Sigma\|_F^2 = \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2 \;\;\blacksquare$$
Wilkinson, MATH3030 §3.5.1, Prop. 3.10 (same invariance chain)

Best Low-Rank Approximation

Eckart–Young–Mirsky

Candidate: Keep the $k$ Largest Layers

Sorted: $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_k \geq \sigma_{k+1} \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$,  budget $0 \leq k \lt r$

$$A \;=\; \underbrace{\sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top} + \cdots + \sigma_k u_k v_k^{\top}}_{\textstyle A_k \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top}\ \text{(kept)}} \;+\; \underbrace{\color{#9aa4af}{\sigma_{k+1} u_{k+1} v_{k+1}^{\top} + \cdots + \sigma_r u_r v_r^{\top}}}_{\textstyle \text{discarded}}$$

$\operatorname{rank}(A_k) = k$: inside the budget $\operatorname{rank} \leq k$

A natural candidate. Not yet proven best.

Worked: $A_1$ Is the Top Layer of $A$

Recall (SVD of the worked matrix).

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}:\quad \sigma_1 = 3\sqrt{5},\;\; \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5},\quad u_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix},\;\; v_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$A_1 = \sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{20}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix}$$

Rank one: both columns equal $1.5\,(1, 3)^{\top}$, along $u_1$

Worked: $A_1$'s Error Is the Discarded Layer

$$A - A_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & -1.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \sigma_2 u_2 v_2^{\top} \qquad \text{(rank one)}$$

Spectral: $\|A - A_1\|_2 = \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5}$, the residual's only singular value

Frobenius: $\|A - A_1\|_F = \sqrt{1.5^2 + 1.5^2 + 0.5^2 + 0.5^2} = \sqrt{5}$

Equal norms here only because the residual has rank one

An error computation for one candidate, not an optimality proof

Eckart–Young–Mirsky: Spectral Error

Theorem (Eckart–Young–Mirsky, spectral norm).

$A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ of rank $r$,  $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$,  any $0 \leq k \lt r$:

$$\min_{\substack{B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n} \\ \operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k}} \|A - B\|_2 \;=\; \sigma_{k+1}, \qquad \text{attained by } B = A_k$$

Minimum over every rank-$\leq k$ matrix, not only truncations

Recall: Low Rank Forces a Big Null Space

Recall (rank–nullity). For $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$:

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \;=\; n - \operatorname{rank}(B)$$
$$\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \dim \operatorname{null}(B) \;\geq\; n - k$$

$n$ = input dimension: $\operatorname{null}(B) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$

Every low-rank competitor has a large blind spot

Large Subspaces Must Intersect

Fact. Subspaces $S_1, S_2 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$\dim(S_1 \cap S_2) \;\geq\; \dim S_1 + \dim S_2 - n$$

Why: $S_1 + S_2 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ has dimension at most $n$

$$\dim S_1 = n - k,\;\; \dim S_2 = k + 1 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \dim(S_1 \cap S_2) \;\geq\; 1$$

Dimension $\geq 1$: a nonzero vector in both

Two Planes in $\mathbb{R}^3$ Share a Line

$S_1$: plane through $0$ $S_2$: plane through $0$ $0$ $w$

$\dim(S_1 \cap S_2) \geq 2 + 2 - 3 = 1$: red line lies in both

Drawn in $\mathbb{R}^3$. The count holds in any $\mathbb{R}^n$.

One Unit Witness Bounds the Maximum

$$\|M\|_2 \;=\; \max_{\|x\|_2 = 1} \|Mx\|_2 \;\geq\; \|Mw\|_2 \qquad \text{for any } w \text{ with } \|w\|_2 = 1$$
  • Lower-bounding a max: exhibit one input
  • Unit length required: scaling $w$ scales $\|Mw\|_2$

Plan: one unit $w$ where $\|(A - B)w\|_2$ must be large

Top $k{+}1$ Directions Stretch by $\geq \sigma_{k+1}$

Unit $w = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i v_i$, so $\sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i^2 = \|w\|_2^2 = 1$ ($v_i$ orthonormal)

$$\begin{aligned} \|Aw\|_2^2 &\;\stackrel{(Av_i = \sigma_i u_i)}{=}\; \Big\| \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i \sigma_i u_i \Big\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(u_i \text{ orthonormal})}{=}\; \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{k+1} \sigma_i^2 c_i^2\\[4pt] &\;\stackrel{(\sigma_i \geq \sigma_{k+1})}{\geq}\; \sigma_{k+1}^2 \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i^2 \;=\; \sigma_{k+1}^2 \end{aligned}$$

Sorted values: each $\sigma_i$ with $i \leq k{+}1$ is at least $\sigma_{k+1}$

Proof Plan: Find One Witness

$$\text{Target: for every } B \text{ with } \operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k, \quad \|A - B\|_2 \;\geq\; \sigma_{k+1} \;=\; \|A - A_k\|_2$$
  1. Blind spot: $\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \geq n - k$
  2. Strong subspace: $W = \operatorname{span}\{v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}\}$
  3. Intersect: unit $w \in \operatorname{null}(B) \cap W$
  4. Witness: $\|A - B\|_2 \geq \|Aw\|_2 \geq \sigma_{k+1}$
  5. Attain: $\|A - A_k\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$

Step 1: Every Competitor Has a Blind Spot

Fix an arbitrary $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with $\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \;\stackrel{(\text{rank–nullity})}{=}\; n - \operatorname{rank}(B) \;\geq\; n - k$$

On $\operatorname{null}(B)$: $(A - B)x = Ax$, so the competitor drops out

Arbitrary $B$, not $A_k$: the bound must hold for all

Step 2: $A$ Stays Strong on $W$

$$W \;=\; \operatorname{span}\{v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}\} \;\subseteq\; \mathbb{R}^n, \qquad \dim W = k + 1$$
  • $k \lt r$: so $v_{k+1}$ exists, with $\sigma_{k+1} \gt 0$
  • Orthonormal $v_i$ $\Rightarrow$ linearly independent $\Rightarrow$ dimension $k+1$
  • Input space $\mathbb{R}^n$, same as $\operatorname{null}(B)$
  • Unit $w \in W$: $\|Aw\|_2 \geq \sigma_{k+1}$ (top-directions bound)

Step 3: The Two Subspaces Share a Unit $w$

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) + \dim W \;\geq\; (n - k) + (k + 1) \;=\; n + 1 \;\gt\; n$$

Intersection bound: $\dim\big(\operatorname{null}(B) \cap W\big) \geq 1$

$$z \neq 0 \text{ in both} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; w = \frac{z}{\|z\|_2} \in \operatorname{null}(B) \cap W, \qquad \|w\|_2 = 1$$

One $w$: killed by $B$, inside $W$

Step 4: Along $w$, the Residual Is $A$

$$\|A - B\|_2 \;\stackrel{(w \text{ unit})}{\geq}\; \|(A - B)w\|_2 \;=\; \|Aw - Bw\|_2 \;\stackrel{(Bw = 0)}{=}\; \|Aw\|_2$$
  • $Bw = 0$: $w \in \operatorname{null}(B)$ from Step 3
  • A lower bound from one witness, no maximum computed

Remaining: $\|Aw\|_2$ cannot be small

Step 4 (continued): The Witness Is Not Small

Recap: $w \in W$ and unit, so $w = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i v_i$ with $\sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i^2 = 1$

$$\|Aw\|_2^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} \sigma_i^2 c_i^2 \;\geq\; \sigma_{k+1}^2 \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i^2 \;=\; \sigma_{k+1}^2$$

Both sides nonnegative: square roots give $\|Aw\|_2 \geq \sigma_{k+1}$

$$\|A - B\|_2 \;\geq\; \|Aw\|_2 \;\geq\; \sigma_{k+1} \qquad \text{for every } B \text{ with } \operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$$

Step 5: Truncation Meets the Bound

$$A - A_k \;=\; \sum_{i=k+1}^{r} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top}, \qquad \text{singular values } \sigma_{k+1} \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r$$

Already an SVD: orthonormal $u_i$, orthonormal $v_i$, sorted $\sigma_i \gt 0$

$$\|A - A_k\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\|M\|_2 = \sigma_1(M))}{=}\; \sigma_{k+1}$$

Bound for every $B$ $+$ attained by $A_k$ $\Rightarrow$ minimum $= \sigma_{k+1}$  $\blacksquare$

One Chain Proves the Spectral Theorem

Any $B$ with $\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$:

$$\begin{aligned} &\dim\operatorname{null}(B) + \dim W \;\stackrel{(1,\,2)}{\geq}\; (n - k) + (k + 1) \gt n \;\stackrel{(3)}{\Longrightarrow}\; \text{unit } w \in \operatorname{null}(B) \cap W\\[6pt] &\|A - B\|_2 \;\stackrel{(w \text{ unit})}{\geq}\; \|(A - B)w\|_2 \;\stackrel{(Bw = 0)}{=}\; \|Aw\|_2 \;\stackrel{(w \in W)}{\geq}\; \sigma_{k+1} \;\stackrel{(5)}{=}\; \|A - A_k\|_2 \end{aligned}$$

Left end: arbitrary $B$. Right end: $A_k$.

So $\min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k} \|A - B\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$; labels name the step or fact used

Eckart–Young–Mirsky: Frobenius Error

Theorem (Eckart–Young–Mirsky, Frobenius norm).

Same setup: $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ of rank $r$,  $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$,  any $0 \leq k \lt r$:

$$\min_{\substack{B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n} \\ \operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k}} \|A - B\|_F^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=k+1}^{r} \sigma_i^2, \qquad \text{attained by } B = A_k$$

The spectral proof does not prove this norm's optimality

Stated, not proved here: the proof needs min–max (Weyl) inequalities

Wilkinson, MATH3030 §3.5.2, Theorem 3.1 (stated for the Frobenius norm; proof on-page covers the 2-norm only)

The Best Rank-One Error Is $\sqrt{5}$

Theorems with $k = 1$, $\sigma_2 = \sqrt{5}$: no rank-$\leq 1$ $B$ does better

$$\min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq 1} \|A - B\|_2 = \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5}, \qquad \min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq 1} \|A - B\|_F^2 = \sigma_2^2 = 5$$
$$\text{attained by } A_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \|A - A_1\|_2 = \|A - A_1\|_F = \sqrt{5}$$

Both errors $\sqrt{5}$: the residual has rank one

One Layer Keeps 90% of the Energy

$$\text{energy kept} = \frac{\|A_1\|_F^2}{\|A\|_F^2} = \frac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2} = \frac{45}{45 + 5} = 90\%$$
$\sigma_1^2 = 45$: kept by $A_1$ $\sigma_2^2 = 5$ bar length $= \|A\|_F^2 = 9 + 16 + 25 = 50$
