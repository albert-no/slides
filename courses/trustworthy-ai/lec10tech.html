01

Counting Success

Estimates, budgets, and judges

ASR Is an Estimate With an Error Bar

Requests $x_1,\dots,x_n$; reply $y_i$; judge $J \in \{0,1\}$:

$$\widehat{\mathrm{ASR}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} J(x_i, y_i), \qquad \mathrm{SE} \approx \sqrt{\frac{\widehat{\mathrm{ASR}}\,(1-\widehat{\mathrm{ASR}})}{n}}.$$

At $\widehat{\mathrm{ASR}} = 0.5$: 95% interval ≈ ±17 points for $n = 32$, ±10 for $n = 100$.

Small denominators cannot separate close numbers.

At Least One Success in $N$ Tries

If each try succeeds independently with probability $p$:

$$\Pr[\text{at least one success}] = 1 - (1-p)^N, \qquad N_q = \left\lceil \frac{\ln(1-q)}{\ln(1-p)} \right\rceil.$$

$p = 1\%$: $N = 100$ gives 63%; reaching $q = 50\%$ needs $N_q = 69$.

$N_q$ = tries needed for success probability $q$.

Real Requests Have Unequal Rates

Request $i$ has its own per-try rate $p_i$. Over a benchmark:

$$\text{per try} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i p_i, \qquad \text{any of } N = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \bigl[1-(1-p_i)^N\bigr].$$
  • Requests with $p_i = 0$ never fall: any-of-$N$ saturates below 100%
  • A high any-of-$N$ rate can hide a low per-try rate

Judge Errors Shift the Measured Rate

Judge with true-positive rate $t$ and false-positive rate $f$:

$$\mathrm{ASR}_{\text{obs}} = t\,\mathrm{ASR} + f\,(1-\mathrm{ASR}), \qquad \mathrm{ASR} = \frac{\mathrm{ASR}_{\text{obs}} - f}{t - f}.$$

Example: $t = 0.9$, $f = 0.1$, true 20% → observed 26%.

Correction needs $t$ and $f$ measured on human labels for the same setting.

Retrying Until a Judge Says Yes Amplifies Its Errors

A reply that is never harmful still "succeeds" if the judge errs once:

$$\Pr[\text{false success within } N] = 1 - (1-f)^N.$$

$f = 1\%$, $N = 100$: 63% of fully refused requests would be counted as jailbroken.

Best-of-$N$ attacks should re-check found successes with a stronger judge.

02

Where Refusal Lives

The training objective, per-token KL, one direction

Preference Training Keeps a Leash to the Base Model

Tune the policy $\pi$ to maximize reward, minus a penalty for drifting:

$$\max_{\pi}\; \mathbb{E}_{x,\,y\sim\pi}\big[\,r(x,y)\,\big] \;-\; \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\!\big(\pi \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}}\big).$$

$r$ = reward model; $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ = the model before RL; $\beta$ = leash strength. The leash keeps most base behavior.

Ouyang et al., "Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback", NeurIPS 2022.

Measuring Depth: KL at Each Position

For prompt $x$ and response prefix $y_{<k}$, compare next-token distributions:

$$D_k = \mathrm{KL}\!\big(\pi_{\text{aligned}}(\cdot \mid x, y_{<k}) \,\big\|\, \pi_{\text{base}}(\cdot \mid x, y_{<k})\big).$$

Shallow alignment: $D_k$ large for the first few $k$, small afterwards.

Qi et al., "Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep", ICLR 2025 — Fig. 1.

The Refusal Direction and Its Ablation

Mean activations at layer $l$ on harmful ($\mu$) and harmless ($\nu$) prompts:

$$r = \mu - \nu, \qquad \hat r = \frac{r}{\|r\|}, \qquad x' \leftarrow x - \hat r\,\hat r^{\top} x.$$

Ablation removes the component of every activation $x$ along $\hat r$.

Arditi et al., "Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction", NeurIPS 2024 — Eqs. 2 and 4.
03

Search and Its Budget

GCG and PAIR as optimization

GCG Minimizes the Loss of an Agreeable Opening

Suffix $s$ appended to request $x$; target opening $y^\ast$ ("Sure, here is …"):

$$\mathcal{L}(s) = -\log p(y^\ast \mid x \oplus s) = -\sum_{t} \log p\big(y^\ast_t \mid x \oplus s,\, y^\ast_{<t}\big).$$

$\oplus$ = concatenation. Tokens are discrete, so no plain gradient step exists.

Zou et al., "Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models", arXiv 2307.15043, 2023 — Section 2.

One GCG Step, and the Total Budget

  1. Gradient of $\mathcal{L}$ w.r.t. each position's one-hot token: top $k = 256$ swaps.
  2. Sample $B = 512$ candidates, one random swap each.
  3. Evaluate $\mathcal{L}$ exactly on all $B$; keep the best.

$T = 500$ steps: $T \times B = 256{,}000$ forward passes per suffix.

Zou et al., arXiv 2307.15043 — Algorithm 1 · count as reported in Chao et al., arXiv 2310.08419 — Table 2.

PAIR's Budget: Streams × Depth

$N_s$ parallel conversation streams, each refined for at most $K$ rounds:

$$\text{queries per request} \;\le\; N_s \times K = 30 \times 3 = 90.$$
  • Stop at the first reply the judge scores as jailbroken
  • Same retry logic as slide 8: judge errors compound
Chao et al., "Jailbreaking Black Box Large Language Models in Twenty Queries", arXiv 2310.08419 — Section 3.3.
04

Scaling Laws

Many-shot and Best-of-N as power laws

Many-Shot: NLL Falls as a Power Law in Shots

With $n$ fake dialogues in the prompt:

$$-\mathbb{E}\big[\log P(\text{harmful reply} \mid n\text{-shot prompt})\big] = C\,n^{-\alpha} + K.$$

Take logs: $\log(\text{NLL} - K) = \log C - \alpha \log n$, a straight line.

Anil et al., "Many-shot Jailbreaking", NeurIPS 2024 — Eq. 1.

Intercept vs. Slope

Intercept $\log C$

Harm likelihood with few shots. SL/RL safety training raised it.

Slope $\alpha$

How fast shots help. Safety training left it roughly unchanged.

Same slope: a higher intercept is offset by more shots, until the context runs out.

Anil et al., NeurIPS 2024 — Section 5 and Fig. 5.

Best-of-N: −log ASR Falls as a Power Law in N

$$-\log \mathrm{ASR}(N) = a\,N^{-b} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{ASR}(N) = \exp\!\big(-a\,N^{-b}\big).$$
  • Fit on small $N$, extrapolate to large $N$
  • $\mathrm{ASR}(N)$ is an any-of-$N$ rate, not per try

The fit describes the tested range; extrapolation is an assumption.

Hughes et al., "Best-of-N Jailbreaking", arXiv 2412.03556, 2024 — Section 3.1.
