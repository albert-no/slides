01

The Attack Objectives

Budget, FGSM, PGD, and C&W

The Perturbation Set

An adversarial example lives in a small ball around $x$.

$$\mathcal{B}_p(x, \varepsilon) \;=\; \bigl\{\, x + \delta \;:\; \|\delta\|_p \le \varepsilon,\; x + \delta \in [0,1]^d \,\bigr\}.$$

$p = \infty$ caps the largest single-pixel change; $p = 2$ caps the total energy.

The Attacker's Goal

Untargeted: find the $\delta$ in the budget that most raises the loss.

$$\max_{\|\delta\|_p \le \varepsilon}\; \ell\bigl(f_\theta(x + \delta),\, y\bigr).$$

Targeted, label $t$: $\;\min_{\|\delta\|_p \le \varepsilon} \ell\bigl(f_\theta(x + \delta),\, t\bigr)$.

$\ell$ = training loss; $y$ = true label; $f_\theta$ = the fixed model.

FGSM: One Step

Take the worst-case step of size $\varepsilon$ in the $\ell_\infty$ box.

$$x_{\text{adv}} \;=\; \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\Bigl(x \;+\; \varepsilon \cdot \operatorname{sign}\!\bigl(\nabla_x\, \ell(f_\theta(x), y)\bigr)\Bigr).$$

Keep only the sign: every pixel moves the maximum allowed amount.

Goodfellow, Shlens, and Szegedy, "Explaining and Harnessing Adversarial Examples", ICLR 2015.

Why the Sign: Linearize, Then Maximize

The sign step exactly solves the linearized attack.

  • Linearize: $\ell(x + \delta) \approx \ell(x) + g^\top \delta$, with $g = \nabla_x\ell$
  • $g^\top\delta = \sum_i g_i\delta_i \le \sum_i |g_i|\,\varepsilon = \varepsilon\|g\|_1$ when $|\delta_i| \le \varepsilon$
  • Equality iff $\delta_i = \varepsilon\,\operatorname{sign}(g_i)$ wherever $g_i \ne 0$

Under $\ell_2$ the same argument gives $\delta = \varepsilon\, g/\|g\|_2$ (Cauchy–Schwarz).

Linear Models: The Gain Grows With Dimension

For a linear score $w^\top x$, FGSM with $\eta = \varepsilon\,\operatorname{sign}(w)$ gives

$$w^\top(x + \eta) \;=\; w^\top x \;+\; \varepsilon\,\|w\|_1 .$$
  • If average $|w_i|$ is $m$ over $d$ inputs, the shift is $\varepsilon m d$
  • $\|\eta\|_\infty = \varepsilon$ stays fixed as $d$ grows

Exact for linear models; for deep nets only a local approximation.

Goodfellow, Shlens, and Szegedy, ICLR 2015, §3.

PGD: Projected Iteration

Random start, then repeat the sign step, projecting back each time.

$$x_0 = x + u,\; u \sim \mathcal{U}[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^d; \qquad x_{t+1} \;=\; \Pi_{\mathcal{B}(x,\varepsilon)}\!\Bigl(x_t + \alpha \cdot \operatorname{sign}\!\bigl(\nabla_x\, \ell(f_\theta(x_t), y)\bigr)\Bigr).$$

$\alpha$ = step size; $K$ steps; keep the best of several restarts. FGSM: $u = 0$, $\alpha = \varepsilon$, $K = 1$.

Madry et al., "Towards Deep Learning Models Resistant to Adversarial Attacks", ICLR 2018.

The Projection Step

$\Pi$ maps a stray point to the nearest point of the ball.

$\ell_\infty$

$\delta_i \leftarrow \min(\max(\delta_i, -\varepsilon), \varepsilon)$, coordinate by coordinate.

$\ell_2$

$\delta \leftarrow \delta \cdot \min\bigl(1,\, \varepsilon / \|\delta\|_2\bigr)$.

Then clip $x + \delta$ to $[0,1]$. Every iterate stays inside the threat model.

C&W: Minimize the Change Instead

Carlini–Wagner ($\ell_2$, target $t$) trade size against success.

$$\min_{\delta}\; \|\delta\|_2^2 \;+\; c \cdot f(x + \delta), \qquad f(x') = \max\Bigl(\max_{i \ne t} Z(x')_i - Z(x')_t,\; -\kappa\Bigr).$$
  • $Z$ = logits; $f \le 0$ iff $t$ wins by margin $\kappa$
  • Box via $\delta = \tfrac12(\tanh w + 1) - x$; optimize $w$ freely
  • $c$: smallest value that succeeds, found by binary search
Carlini and Wagner, "Towards Evaluating the Robustness of Neural Networks", IEEE S&P 2017.

Why a Margin Loss Helps

Cross-entropy can saturate; a logit margin keeps a gradient.

  • Softmax near 1 makes $\nabla_x$ of cross-entropy tiny
  • $\max_{i \ne y} Z_i - Z_y$ stays linear in the logits
  • Madry's "CW-30": PGD on this margin, 30 steps

C&W broke defensive distillation: earlier attacks 0.5% success, C&W 100%.

02

Robust Training

The saddle-point objective

Adversarial Training

Train against the worst case inside every point's budget.

$$\min_{\theta}\; \rho(\theta), \qquad \rho(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}\Bigl[\, \max_{\|\delta\|_p \le \varepsilon}\; \ell\bigl(f_\theta(x + \delta),\, y\bigr)\Bigr].$$

Inner max = the attack; outer min = the defender fitting $\theta$. $\rho(\theta)$ = adversarial risk.

Madry et al., "Towards Deep Learning Models Resistant to Adversarial Attacks", ICLR 2018.

How the Saddle Point Is Solved

Alternate the two players, one batch at a time.

  1. Inner max: run PGD to craft $\delta^*$ for each example.
  2. Outer min: SGD step on $\nabla_\theta\, \ell(f_\theta(x + \delta^*), y)$.
  3. Repeat until the adversarial loss stops falling.

Danskin's theorem: at an exact maximizer, this is a descent direction for $\rho$. PGD only approximates it.

Why It Is Expensive

Every training step now hides an optimization inside it.

  • $K$ PGD steps + 1 update: about $(K{+}1)\times$ the cost
  • Clean accuracy often falls as training $\varepsilon$ grows
  • Tsipras et al.: provably inevitable in their model; debated in practice

You pay in compute, and often in clean accuracy, for a wider safe region.

03

Certified Robustness

Randomized smoothing's provable radius

The Smoothed Classifier

Replace $f$ by the class it returns most often under noise.

$$g(x) \;=\; \arg\max_{c}\; \Pr_{\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)}\bigl[\, f(x + \eta) = c \,\bigr].$$

$f$ = base classifier (any function); $g$ = smoothed classifier; the certificate is about $g$.

Cohen, Rosenfeld, and Kolter, "Certified Adversarial Robustness via Randomized Smoothing", ICML 2019.

Theorem 1: The Certified Radius

Suppose $\Pr[f(x{+}\eta) = c_A] \ge \underline{p_A} \ge \overline{p_B} \ge \max_{c \ne c_A}\Pr[f(x{+}\eta) = c]$. Then

$$g(x + \delta) = c_A \quad \text{for all } \|\delta\|_2 \lt R, \qquad R \;=\; \frac{\sigma}{2}\,\Bigl(\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A}) - \Phi^{-1}(\overline{p_B})\Bigr).$$

$\Phi^{-1}$ = inverse standard normal CDF. No assumption on $f$.

Proof Sketch: The Worst $f$ Is a Half-Space

Let $X = x + \eta$ and $Y = x + \delta + \eta$: two Gaussians, means $\|\delta\|_2$ apart.

  • Neyman–Pearson: the worst $f$ is a half-space normal to $\delta$
  • So $\Pr[f(Y) = c_A] \ge \Phi\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A}) - \|\delta\|_2/\sigma\bigr)$
  • Likewise $\Pr[f(Y) = c] \le \Phi\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(\overline{p_B}) + \|\delta\|_2/\sigma\bigr)$ for $c \ne c_A$

$c_A$ still wins iff $\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A}) - \|\delta\|_2/\sigma \gt \Phi^{-1}(\overline{p_B}) + \|\delta\|_2/\sigma$, i.e. $\|\delta\|_2 \lt R$. $\;\square$

CERTIFY: The Probabilities Are Estimated

$g$ cannot be evaluated exactly, so the certificate is statistical.

  1. $n_0$ noisy samples (100 in the paper): guess the top class $\hat c_A$.
  2. $n$ fresh samples (100,000): count $k$ votes for $\hat c_A$.
  3. $\underline{p_A}$ = one-sided $(1-\alpha)$ Clopper–Pearson lower bound from $k, n$.
  4. If $\underline{p_A} \gt \tfrac12$: return $\hat c_A$, radius $\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A})$. Else abstain.

Step 4 uses $\overline{p_B} = 1 - \underline{p_A}$. Holds with probability $\ge 1-\alpha$; paper uses $\alpha = 0.001$.

Reading the Radius

The formula shows exactly what buys a bigger guarantee.

  • More noise $\sigma$: larger radius, lower clean accuracy of $g$
  • Clearer vote ($\underline{p_A} \to 1$): $\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A})$ grows
  • Sample-limited: with $n$ samples, $\underline{p_A} \lt 1$, so $R$ is capped

With probability $\ge 1-\alpha$, no $\ell_2$ change smaller than $R$ flips $g$.

The Certified Catch

The guarantee is real but modest.

Cost

$n = 100{,}000$ forward passes: about 110 s per ImageNet image in the paper.

Scope

An $\ell_2$ radius for $g$, not $f$; 49% certified at $R = 0.5$ on ImageNet.

A narrow, costly proof, but a proof nonetheless.
