01

Criteria

Each one names its denominator

Four Rates, Four Denominators

Group $A \in \{a, b\}$, label $Y \in \{0,1\}$, score $S \in [0,1]$, decision $\hat Y \in \{0,1\}$, features $X$.

$$\mathrm{SR}_a = \Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid A = a], \qquad \mathrm{TPR}_a = \Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = 1, A = a],$$ $$\mathrm{FPR}_a = \Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = 0, A = a], \qquad \mathrm{PPV}_a = \Pr[Y = 1 \mid \hat Y = 1, A = a].$$

Denominators, in order: group · positives · negatives · selected.

  • Each denominator needs positive probability: $0 \lt p_a \lt 1$, and $\Pr[\hat Y = 1, A = a] \gt 0$ for PPV
  • Base rate $p_a = \Pr[Y = 1 \mid A = a]$; $\mathrm{FNR}_a = 1 - \mathrm{TPR}_a$

Four Criteria on the Decision

Point: each equates one rate of the binary decision $\hat Y$ across groups.

$$\text{Demographic parity:}\quad \mathrm{SR}_a = \mathrm{SR}_b \iff \hat Y \perp A.$$ $$\text{Equal opportunity:}\quad \mathrm{TPR}_a = \mathrm{TPR}_b.$$ $$\text{Equalized odds:}\quad \mathrm{TPR}_a = \mathrm{TPR}_b,\ \mathrm{FPR}_a = \mathrm{FPR}_b \iff \hat Y \perp A \mid Y.$$ $$\text{Predictive parity:}\quad \mathrm{PPV}_a = \mathrm{PPV}_b.$$

Every conditioning event has positive probability.

Hardt, Price, and Srebro, NeurIPS 2016, Defs. 2.1–2.2; Chouldechova, Big Data 2017, §2.1.

Calibration: A Condition on the Score

Point: the fifth criterion constrains the score $S \in [0,1]$, before any threshold.

$$\begin{aligned}\text{Calibration within groups:}\quad &\Pr[Y = 1 \mid S = s, A = a] = s\\ &\text{for all } (s, a) \text{ with } \Pr[S = s, A = a] \gt 0.\end{aligned}$$
  • Conditions on each score value $S = s$
  • Predictive parity conditions on the decision $\hat Y = 1$ instead

Discrete score values here.

Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan, ITCS 2017, condition (A).

Individual Fairness Needs a Task Metric

  • $V$: individuals; $d$: a task metric on $V$
  • $\Delta(\mathcal{O})$: distributions over outcomes $\mathcal{O}$; $D$: a distance on them (e.g. total variation)
  • $M : V \to \Delta(\mathcal{O})$: randomized map; $Mx$: the outcome distribution of $x$

$M$ is $(D, d)$-Lipschitz if

$$D(Mx, My) \;\le\; d(x, y) \qquad \forall\, x, y \in V.$$

Similar people, similar outcome distributions: a constraint, not a conditional probability.

Dwork, Hardt, Pitassi, Reingold, and Zemel, "Fairness Through Awareness", ITCS 2012, Def. 2.1.

Counterfactual Fairness Needs a Causal Model

  • Causal model $(U, V, F)$: latent background variables $U$; observed $V = A \cup X$; structural equations $F$
  • $\hat Y_{A \leftarrow a'}(U)$: the prediction had $A$ been set to $a'$

Counterfactually fair if, for every $x$, $a$, $y$ and every attainable $a'$:

$$\Pr[\hat Y_{A \leftarrow a}(U) = y \mid X = x, A = a] = \Pr[\hat Y_{A \leftarrow a'}(U) = y \mid X = x, A = a].$$

Needs a causal model; the answer depends on it.

Kusner, Loftus, Russell, and Silva, "Counterfactual Fairness", NIPS 2017, Def. 5.

Calibration Averages Over Selected Scores

Let $\hat Y = \mathbf{1}[S \gt t]$ with $\Pr[S \gt t, A = a] \gt 0$. If $S$ is calibrated within groups, the tower rule gives

$$\mathrm{PPV}_a = \Pr[Y = 1 \mid S \gt t, A = a] = \mathbb{E}\big[\Pr[Y=1 \mid S, A=a] \,\big|\, S \gt t, A = a\big]$$ $$= \mathbb{E}[S \mid S \gt t, A = a].$$
  • Equal PPV needs equal mean score above $t$
  • Both groups' selected scores all equal one value $s^\ast$: $\mathrm{PPV}_a = \mathrm{PPV}_b = s^\ast$
  • Different score mixes above $t$: PPV can differ
02

Impossibility

Exact statements and their conditions

Chouldechova's Identity

One group: size $N$, base rate $0 \lt p \lt 1$, and $\mathrm{PPV} \gt 0$.

$$\mathrm{TP} = (1-\mathrm{FNR})\,pN, \qquad \mathrm{FP} = \mathrm{FPR}\,(1-p)N.$$ $$\mathrm{PPV} = \frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}} \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathrm{FP} = \mathrm{TP}\,\frac{1-\mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}}.$$ $$\mathrm{FPR} = \frac{\mathrm{FP}}{(1-p)N} = \frac{p}{1-p}\cdot\frac{1-\mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}}\cdot(1-\mathrm{FNR}).$$

Point: given the base rate, PPV and FNR fix FPR.

Chouldechova, "Fair Prediction with Disparate Impact", Big Data 2017, Eq. (2.6).

Equal PPV, FNR and FPR Force Equal Base Rates

Conditions in each group: $0 \lt p \lt 1$ and $0 \lt \mathrm{PPV} \lt 1$ and $\mathrm{FNR} \lt 1$.

$$\text{Equal PPV, FNR, FPR in (2.6)} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{p_a}{1-p_a} = \frac{p_b}{1-p_b} \;\Longrightarrow\; p_a = p_b.$$

$\mathrm{PPV} \lt 1$ and $\mathrm{FNR} \lt 1$ keep the other two factors nonzero.

PPV and FPR parity alone allow unequal base rates.

Chouldechova, "Fair Prediction with Disparate Impact", Big Data 2017, Eq. (2.6).

Calibration Plus Balance Forces Exceptional Cases

Three conditions on a score, groups $a$ and $b$:

  • (A) calibration within groups
  • (B) mean score of negatives equal across groups
  • (C) mean score of positives equal across groups

Theorem 1.1: (A)–(C) together only with equal base rates or perfect prediction.

Theorem 1.2: $\varepsilon$-approximate (A)–(C) force an $f(\varepsilon)$-approximate case, $f(\varepsilon) \to 0$.

Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan, "Inherent Trade-Offs in the Fair Determination of Risk Scores", ITCS 2017, §1.1, Theorems 1.1–1.2.

Proof Sketch: Common Class Means

Let $x$ and $y$ be the mean scores of negatives and positives.

(B) and (C) make $x$ and $y$ common to both groups.

(A) makes group $g$'s total score equal its number of positives $\mu_g$:

$$(N_g - \mu_g)\,x + \mu_g\, y = \mu_g \iff x = p_g\,(1 + x - y), \qquad p_g = \mu_g / N_g.$$
  • $p_a = p_b$: the two equations coincide
  • $p_a \ne p_b$: forces $x = 0$ and $y = 1$, perfect prediction
Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan, ITCS 2017, Theorem 1.1 and §2.

The Calibrated Example: Equal PPV

Scores $0.2$ or $0.8$; flag if $S \gt 0.5$. Share $q_a$ of group $a$ scores $0.8$.

$$p_a = 0.2 + 0.6\,q_a: \qquad p_a = \tfrac12 \Rightarrow q_a = \tfrac12, \qquad p_b = \tfrac14 \Rightarrow q_b = \tfrac1{12}.$$

The Calibrated Example: Unequal Error Rates

Same groups; flagged means score $0.8$.

$$\mathrm{FPR}_g = \frac{0.2\,q_g}{1 - p_g}: \qquad \mathrm{FPR}_a = \frac{6}{30} = \frac15, \qquad \mathrm{FPR}_b = \frac{1}{45} \approx 0.022.$$ $$\mathrm{FNR}_g = \frac{0.2\,(1 - q_g)}{p_g}: \qquad \mathrm{FNR}_a = \frac{6}{30} = \frac15, \qquad \mathrm{FNR}_b = \frac{11}{15} \approx 0.733.$$

Check with (2.6) for group $b$: $\tfrac13 \cdot \tfrac14 \cdot \tfrac4{15} = \tfrac1{45}$.

Specific to this construction, not every calibrated model.

03

Mitigation

Pre-, in-, and post-processing

Reweighing Needs Cell Support

Assume full support: every cell with $\Pr[A = a]\Pr[Y = y] \gt 0$ is observed, $\Pr[A = a, Y = y] \gt 0$. Weight each cell by expected over observed probability:

$$w(a, y) = \frac{P_{\mathrm{exp}}(a, y)}{P_{\mathrm{obs}}(a, y)} = \frac{\Pr[A = a]\,\Pr[Y = y]}{\Pr[A = a, Y = y]}.$$
  • Under full support and these weights, $A \perp Y$ in the training data
  • An empty required cell cannot be repaired by weighting: independence then fails
Kamiran and Calders, "Data Preprocessing Techniques for Classification without Discrimination", Knowledge and Information Systems 2012, §5.2.

Reweighing Example: 60% Positive in Both Groups

$$w(s, -) = \frac{\Pr[A = s]\,\Pr[Y = -]}{\Pr[A = s,\, Y = -]} = \frac{0.5 \cdot 0.4}{0.3} = \frac23.$$

Reductions: Constraints as a Saddle Point

  • $Q \in \Delta(\mathcal{H})$: randomized classifier
  • $\mu(Q)$: group-conditional moments (e.g. selection rates)
  • $M\mu(Q) \le c$: the $|K|$ linear constraints; hats: sample estimates
$$L(Q, \lambda) = \widehat{\mathrm{err}}(Q) + \lambda^\top\big(M\hat\mu(Q) - \hat c\big), \qquad \min_{Q \in \Delta}\ \max_{\lambda \ge 0,\ \|\lambda\|_1 \le B} L(Q, \lambda).$$

Point: $\lambda$ prices each constraint violation; $B$ caps the multipliers.

Agarwal, Beygelzimer, Dudík, Langford, and Wallach, "A Reductions Approach to Fair Classification", ICML 2018, §3.

Reductions Uses a Cost-Sensitive Oracle

Point: the game is solved by repeated cost-sensitive fits.

  1. $\lambda$-player: exponentiated gradient
  2. $Q$-player: one cost-sensitive fit per round
  3. Returns the average play: a randomized classifier

Tolerance $\nu \gt 0$: neither player gains over $\nu$ by deviating.

Agarwal, Beygelzimer, Dudík, Langford, and Wallach, "A Reductions Approach to Fair Classification", ICML 2018, §3, Algorithm 1, Theorem 1.

Postprocessing Uses an Attainable Region

$\tilde Y$ is derived if it is a possibly randomized function of $(\hat Y, A)$ alone. Write $\gamma_a(\hat Y) = (\mathrm{FPR}_a, \mathrm{TPR}_a)$.

$$\min_{\tilde Y}\ \mathbb{E}\,\ell(\tilde Y, Y) \quad \text{s.t.}\quad \gamma_a(\tilde Y) \in P_a(\hat Y)\ \ \forall a, \qquad \gamma_a(\tilde Y) = \gamma_b(\tilde Y),$$ $$P_a(\hat Y) = \mathrm{conv}\{(0,0),\ \gamma_a(\hat Y),\ \gamma_a(1 - \hat Y),\ (1,1)\}.$$
  • Four variables $\Pr[\tilde Y = 1 \mid \hat Y, A]$: a linear program
  • Score $R$: mix two thresholds per group
Hardt, Price, and Srebro, "Equality of Opportunity in Supervised Learning", NeurIPS 2016, Def. 4.1, Eq. (4.2)–(4.4), Prop. 4.4.
