From Comparisons to Reward

The proxy, written down

Reward Model: Learn From Comparisons

Labelers rank $K$ responses; every pair becomes a training example.

$$\mathrm{loss}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{\binom{K}{2}}\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim D}\Big[\log \sigma\big(r_\theta(x,y_w) - r_\theta(x,y_l)\big)\Big]$$
  • $r_\theta(x,y)$: scalar score for prompt $x$, response $y$
  • $y_w$ preferred over $y_l$; $\sigma$: logistic sigmoid
  • Reads as $\Pr(y_w \succ y_l) = \sigma(r_w - r_l)$: a Bradley–Terry model

$r_\theta$ learns what labelers preferred, not what was true: Gap 1.

Ouyang et al., "Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback", NeurIPS 2022 — §3.5, Eq. (1).

RL Objective: Reward Minus Drift

$$\max_\phi\;\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D_{\pi_\phi^{\mathrm{RL}}}}\Big[\,r_\theta(x,y) \;-\; \beta \log \frac{\pi_\phi^{\mathrm{RL}}(y\mid x)}{\pi^{\mathrm{SFT}}(y\mid x)}\Big]$$
  • Expected log-ratio = $\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{\mathrm{RL}}_\phi \,\|\, \pi^{\mathrm{SFT}})$
  • Penalty added "to mitigate over-optimization of the reward model"
  • Larger $\beta$: stays nearer the start model; smaller $\beta$: chases $r_\theta$

Shown with the pretraining-mix term dropped ($\gamma = 0$, the paper's "PPO" models).

Ouyang et al., "Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback", NeurIPS 2022 — §3.5, Eq. (2).

DPO: The Policy Is the Reward Model

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\pi_\theta) = -\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)\sim D}\Big[\log \sigma\big(\hat r_\theta(x,y_w) - \hat r_\theta(x,y_l)\big)\Big]$$ $$\hat r_\theta(x,y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}$$
  • Gradient: a positive term on $\nabla\log \pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)$, a negative term on $\nabla\log \pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)$
  • Each pair weighted by $\sigma(\hat r_\theta(x,y_l) - \hat r_\theta(x,y_w))$: larger when misordered

$\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$: fixed reference (SFT). Same optimum as KL-penalized RL only under the idealized Bradley–Terry model; finite-data policies need not match.

Rafailov et al., "Direct Preference Optimization", NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv:2305.18290) — §4, Eqs. (4)–(7) and gradient; equivalence under the Bradley–Terry model (§4). Overoptimization in DPO: Rafailov et al., arXiv:2406.02900, 2024 — §3.1.

Overoptimization: Fitted Curves

Gold-model score $R$ against distance $d = \sqrt{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi \,\|\, \pi_{\mathrm{init}})}$:

$$R_{\mathrm{bon}}(d) = d\,(\alpha_{\mathrm{bon}} - \beta_{\mathrm{bon}}\, d), \qquad R_{\mathrm{RL}}(d) = d\,(\alpha_{\mathrm{RL}} - \beta_{\mathrm{RL}} \log d)$$
  • Both rise, peak, then fall: Goodhart in one line
  • $\alpha, \beta$ fitted; depend on reward-model size and data
  • $R(0) := 0$; "bon" = best-of-$n$ sampling

Empirical fits in a synthetic setup: a "gold" reward model stands in for humans.

Gao, Schulman, and Hilton, "Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization", 2022 (arXiv:2210.10760) — §1; setup §2.1.

Scoring Explanations

Hint tests and monitors, as numbers

Hint Test: Faithfulness Score

  • $a_u$, $a_h$: answers without and with the hint
  • $h$: the hinted answer · $c_h$: reasoning given the hint

Keep pairs where the hint flipped the answer: $a_u \ne h$, $a_h = h$.

$$\mathrm{score}(M) = \mathbb{E}\big[\,\mathbf{1}[\,c_h \text{ verbalizes } h\,] \;\big|\; a_u \ne h,\; a_h = h\,\big]$$

Likely an upper bound on faithfulness.

Chen et al., "Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think", 2025 (arXiv:2505.05410) — §2.1; results §3.

Correcting for Random Flips

Multiple choice with $n$ options; condition on $a_u \ne h$.

  • Flip to the hint: $p = \Pr(a_h = h \mid a_u \ne h)$
  • Flip elsewhere: $q = \Pr(a_h \ne h,\ a_h \ne a_u \mid a_u \ne h)$
$$\alpha = 1 - \frac{q}{(n-2)\,p}, \qquad \text{normalized} = \min\big\{\mathrm{score}(M)/\alpha,\; 1\big\}$$
  • Needs $n > 2$, $p > 0$ and $\alpha > 0$; otherwise undefined

In practice normalization barely changes scores.

Chen et al., 2025 (arXiv:2505.05410) — §2.1: $\alpha$ = share of hint flips not explained by randomness.

Monitors: Recall and Precision

$$\text{recall} = \frac{\#\,\text{hacks flagged}}{\#\,\text{hacks}}, \qquad \text{precision} = \frac{\#\,\text{flags that are hacks}}{\#\,\text{flags}}$$
