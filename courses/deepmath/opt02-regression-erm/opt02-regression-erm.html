02

Linear Regression

Model, loss, interpretation

The Data

$$(\mathbf{x}^{(1)}, y^{(1)}),\; (\mathbf{x}^{(2)}, y^{(2)}),\; \dots,\; (\mathbf{x}^{(N)}, y^{(N)})$$
  • $\mathbf{x}^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^n$: features of example $i$
  • $y^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}$: its label
  • Goal: predict $y$ for a new pair $(\mathbf{x}, y)$

The Linear Model

$$f_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \theta^{\top} \mathbf{x} \;=\; \theta_1 x_1 + \dots + \theta_n x_n \;\approx\; y$$
  • One parameter vector $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^n$ — nothing else
  • Trainable: choose $\theta$ from the data
  • Prediction: one inner product

Example — Height to Weight

$n = 1$:  $x$ = height, $y$ = weight

$$y \;\approx\; \theta_1 x$$

Predict: multiply height by $\theta_1$

one number summarizes the trend

weight $y$ height $x$ slope $\theta_1$

illustrative sketch

No Bias Term? The Dummy Feature

$\theta^{\top}\mathbf{x}$ forces $f_\theta(\mathbf{0}) = 0$. Fix: prepend a constant.

$$\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = (1, x_1, \dots, x_n), \qquad y \;\approx\; \tilde{\theta}^{\top} \tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \theta_0 + \theta_1 x_1 + \dots + \theta_n x_n$$

Bias $\theta_0$: just a weight on the constant feature $1$ — still linear.

Linear Regression Is Interpretable

  • Small $\theta_k$: feature $x_k$ has little influence
  • $\theta_k = 0$: prediction ignores $x_k$
  • $\theta_k$ = change in $f_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ when $x_k$ grows by $1$

deep networks trade this readability away for capacity

How Good Is a Fit? Squared Loss

“Approximates $y$ well” needs a number:

$$\ell_2\big(f_\theta(\mathbf{x}), y\big) \;=\; \big(f_\theta(\mathbf{x}) - y\big)^2$$
  • Zero iff exact hit; grows with the miss
  • Averaged over data: Mean Squared Error (MSE)

The Training Problem

$$\begin{aligned} \theta^\star &= \operatorname*{argmin}_\theta\; \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \ell_2\big(f_\theta(\mathbf{x}^{(i)}), y^{(i)}\big)\\[2pt] &= \operatorname*{argmin}_\theta\; \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \big(\theta^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{(i)} - y^{(i)}\big)^2 \end{aligned}$$

the $\tfrac{1}{N}$ scales the value, not the argmin — drop it freely

Running Example — Three Points

$x$$y$
$-1$$0$
$0$$1$
$1$$3$

Model with dummy feature:

$$y \;\approx\; \theta_0 + \theta_1 x$$
$-1$ $1$ $1$ $2$ $3$ $x$ $y$

03

Matrix Form

$N$ equations, $n$ unknowns

Stack the Data

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} (\mathbf{x}^{(1)})^{\top} \\ (\mathbf{x}^{(2)})^{\top} \\ \vdots \\ (\mathbf{x}^{(N)})^{\top} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times n}, \qquad B = \begin{bmatrix} y^{(1)} \\ y^{(2)} \\ \vdots \\ y^{(N)} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$$
  • One row per example, one column per feature
  • All $N$ predictions at once: $A\theta$

One Objective, One Norm

$$\sum_{i=1}^N \big(\theta^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{(i)} - y^{(i)}\big)^2 \;=\; \|A\theta - B\|_2^2$$
$$\theta^\star \;=\; \operatorname*{argmin}_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}^n}\; \frac{1}{2}\|A\theta - B\|_2^2$$

the $\tfrac12$ is cosmetic too — it cleans up derivatives later

Tall, Square, Fat

tall: $N \gt n$ square: $N = n$ fat: $N \lt n$
  • Square, invertible: $\theta = A^{-1}B$ — done
  • Tall: over-determined — usually no exact solution
  • Fat, full rank: under-determined — usually many solutions

Regression Is the Tall Case

  • Standard setting: many examples, few features — $N \gt n$
  • $A\theta = B$:  $N$ equations, only $n$ unknowns
  • Noise in $y$  $\Rightarrow$  no line passes through everything

Give up on solving exactly; minimize the miss $\|A\theta - B\|_2$ instead.

Running Example — No Exact Solution

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad A\theta = B:\; \begin{cases} \theta_0 - \theta_1 = 0 \\ \theta_0 \phantom{{}-\theta_1} = 1 \\ \theta_0 + \theta_1 = 3 \end{cases}$$
  • Rows 1–2 force $\theta_0 = 1$, $\theta_1 = 1$
  • Row 3 then reads $2 = 3$ — contradiction

Best We Can Do

$$\min_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}^2}\; \frac{1}{2}\left\| \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \theta - \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} \right\|_2^2$$
  • Claim: the minimizer is $\theta^\star = A^{\dagger} B$
  • Next section: the proof — every step
04

The Least-Squares Theorem

$\theta^\star = A^\dagger B$, proved in full

Technique Review 1 — Compact SVD

Recall. Any $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times n}$ of rank $r$:

$$A = U_c \Sigma_c V_c^{\top}, \qquad U_c \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times r},\;\; \Sigma_c \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r},\;\; V_c \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$$
  • $\Sigma_c = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r)$, all $\sigma_i \gt 0$ — invertible
  • Orthonormal columns: $U_c^{\top}U_c = I_r$,  $V_c^{\top}V_c = I_r$

Technique Review 2 — Pseudo-Inverse

Recall. The Moore–Penrose pseudo-inverse:

$$A^{\dagger} \;=\; V_c \Sigma_c^{-1} U_c^{\top} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{n \times N}$$
  • Invert the stretches; undo the rotations
  • Exists for every matrix — no invertibility needed
  • Square invertible $A$:  $A^{\dagger} = A^{-1}$

Technique Review 3 — Rotations Are Free

Recall. $U$ orthogonal ($U^{\top}U = I$):

$$\|U^{\top} z\|_2 \;=\; \|z\|_2 \quad \text{for all } z$$
  • Multiplying inside a norm by $U^{\top}$ costs nothing
  • Today's use: rotate the residual into friendly coordinates

Technique Review 4 — Split a Vector

Recall. Subspace $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$: every $\theta$ splits uniquely,

$$\theta = \theta_1 + \theta_2, \quad \theta_1 \in S,\; \theta_2 \in S^{\perp}, \qquad \|\theta\|_2^2 = \|\theta_1\|_2^2 + \|\theta_2\|_2^2$$
  • Pythagoras: the cross term dies by orthogonality
  • Also: $\operatorname{null}(V_c^{\top}) = \operatorname{range}(V_c)^{\perp}$

Theorem 1 — Least Squares Solution

Theorem 1.

For any $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times n}$ of rank $r$ and any $B \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$, the problem

$$\min_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}^n}\; \frac{1}{2}\|A\theta - B\|_2^2 \qquad \text{is solved by} \qquad \theta^\star = A^{\dagger} B$$

Unique if $r = n \leq N$; otherwise $A^{\dagger}B$ is the minimizer of least norm $\|\theta\|_2$.

Reading the Statement

  • No assumptions on $A$ — any shape, any rank
  • A minimizer always exists — and it is a formula
  • Ties (rank-deficient case) broken by smallest $\|\theta\|_2$

why least norm is the right tie-break: answered by ridge, §09

Proof Overview

  1. Insert the SVD; extend $U_c$ to a full orthogonal $U$  (TR1)
  2. Rotate the residual by $U^{\top}$ — objective splits in two  (TR3)
  3. One piece is constant in $\theta$ — drop it
  4. Split $\theta$ along $\operatorname{range}(V_c)$ vs $\operatorname{null}(V_c^{\top})$  (TR4)
  5. Solve the surviving piece exactly; least norm by Pythagoras

Step 1 — Insert the SVD, Complete the Basis

$$A = U_c \Sigma_c V_c^{\top}, \qquad U = \big[\, U_c \;\; \tilde{U} \,\big] \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N} \text{ orthogonal}$$
  • $\tilde{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times (N-r)}$: any orthonormal completion of $U_c$'s columns
  • Bookkeeping: $U_c^{\top}U_c = I_r$,  $\tilde{U}^{\top}\tilde{U} = I_{N-r}$,  $\tilde{U}^{\top}U_c = 0$

$\tilde{U}$ spans the directions of $\mathbb{R}^N$ that $A$ can never reach

Step 2 — Rotate the Residual

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\|A\theta - B\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(\text{TR3})}{=}\; \tfrac{1}{2}\big\|U^{\top}(A\theta - B)\big\|_2^2$$

Compute the rotated residual block by block:

$$U^{\top}(U_c \Sigma_c V_c^{\top}\theta - B) = \begin{bmatrix} U_c^{\top} \\ \tilde{U}^{\top} \end{bmatrix}\!(U_c \Sigma_c V_c^{\top}\theta - B) \;\stackrel{(\tilde{U}^{\top}U_c = 0)}{=}\; \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_c V_c^{\top}\theta - U_c^{\top}B \\ -\tilde{U}^{\top}B \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 2, Finished — the Objective Splits

Squared length of a stacked vector = sum of block lengths:

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\|A\theta - B\|_2^2 \;=\; \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}\big\|\Sigma_c V_c^{\top}\theta - U_c^{\top}B\big\|_2^2}_{\text{depends on } \theta} \;+\; \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}\big\|\tilde{U}^{\top}B\big\|_2^2}_{\text{no } \theta \text{ anywhere}}$$

Step 3 — Drop the Constant

$$\operatorname*{argmin}_{\theta}\; \tfrac{1}{2}\|A\theta - B\|_2^2 \;=\; \operatorname*{argmin}_{\theta}\; \tfrac{1}{2}\big\|\Sigma_c V_c^{\top}\theta - U_c^{\top}B\big\|_2^2$$
  • $\tfrac{1}{2}\|\tilde{U}^{\top}B\|_2^2$ shifts the value, never the argmin
  • It is the part of $B$ outside $\operatorname{range}(A)$ — unreachable error

this constant becomes the projection picture in §05

Step 4 — Split $\theta$

$$\theta = \theta_1 + \theta_2, \qquad \theta_1 \in \operatorname{range}(V_c), \quad \theta_2 \in \operatorname{null}(V_c^{\top}) \quad \text{(TR4)}$$
  • $V_c^{\top}\theta_2 = 0$  $\Rightarrow$  objective sees only $\theta_1$
  • Also $A\theta_2 = U_c \Sigma_c (V_c^{\top}\theta_2) = 0$:  $\theta_2$ is invisible to $A$
$$\tfrac{1}{2}\big\|\Sigma_c V_c^{\top}\theta - U_c^{\top}B\big\|_2^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\big\|\Sigma_c V_c^{\top}\theta_1 - U_c^{\top}B\big\|_2^2$$

Step 5 — Solve the Surviving Piece

Write $\theta_1 = V_c\, c$ with $c \in \mathbb{R}^r$; then $V_c^{\top}\theta_1 \stackrel{(V_c^{\top}V_c = I)}{=} c$:

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Sigma_c\, c - U_c^{\top}B\|_2^2 \;\geq\; 0, \qquad = 0 \;\text{ at }\; c^\star = \Sigma_c^{-1} U_c^{\top} B$$
$$\theta_1^\star \;=\; V_c\, c^\star \;=\; V_c \Sigma_c^{-1} U_c^{\top} B \;=\; A^{\dagger} B$$

the reachable part of the error is driven all the way to zero

Every Minimizer, and the Smallest

All minimizers: $\theta^\star = A^{\dagger}B + \theta_2$, any $\theta_2 \in \operatorname{null}(A)$.

$$\|A^{\dagger}B + \theta_2\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(\text{TR4: } \theta_1^\star \perp \theta_2)}{=}\; \|A^{\dagger}B\|_2^2 + \|\theta_2\|_2^2 \;\geq\; \|A^{\dagger}B\|_2^2$$
  • Equality iff $\theta_2 = 0$ — least-norm minimizer is exactly $A^{\dagger}B$  $\blacksquare$

When Is the Minimizer Unique?

$$r = n \;\iff\; \operatorname{null}(A) = \{0\} \;\iff\; \text{no free } \theta_2$$
  • Tall $A$ with independent columns: the usual regression case
  • $r \lt n$: a whole affine set ties — least norm picks one

Proof Summary — the Skeleton

$$\begin{aligned} \tfrac{1}{2}\|A\theta - B\|_2^2 \;&\stackrel{(\text{rotate by } U^{\top})}{=}\; \tfrac{1}{2}\|\Sigma_c V_c^{\top}\theta - U_c^{\top}B\|_2^2 + \text{const}\\[2pt] \;&\stackrel{(\theta = V_c c + \theta_2)}{=}\; \tfrac{1}{2}\|\Sigma_c\, c - U_c^{\top}B\|_2^2 + \text{const}\\[2pt] \;&\stackrel{(c^\star = \Sigma_c^{-1}U_c^{\top}B)}{\longrightarrow}\; \theta^\star = A^{\dagger}B \;\; (+ \operatorname{null}(A),\ \text{least norm wins}) \end{aligned}$$

The Key Trick, Named

Rotate until the problem is diagonal. Rotations are free; the SVD supplies them.

  • After rotating: $r$ solvable scalar equations + unreachable rest
  • The recurring move — every $\ell_2$ question becomes a $\sigma$ question

Worked — SVD of the Running Example

$$A^{\top}A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \;\Rightarrow\; V_c = I_2, \quad \sigma_1 = \sqrt{3},\; \sigma_2 = \sqrt{2}$$
$$u_1 = \frac{A v_1}{\sigma_1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad u_2 = \frac{A v_2}{\sigma_2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

$A^{\top}A$ came out diagonal because the $x$'s are centered — free SVD

Worked — $A^{\dagger}$ and $\theta^\star$

$$A^{\dagger} = V_c \Sigma_c^{-1} U_c^{\top} = \begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{1}{3} & \tfrac{1}{3} & \tfrac{1}{3} \\[2pt] -\tfrac{1}{2} & 0 & \tfrac{1}{2} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \theta^\star = A^{\dagger} B = \begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{4}{3} \\[2pt] \tfrac{3}{2} \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Row 1 of $A^{\dagger}$: average the labels  $\rightarrow$  intercept $\tfrac{4}{3}$
  • Row 2: end-to-end difference quotient  $\rightarrow$  slope $\tfrac{3}{2}$

Worked — the Fitted Line

$$\hat{y} = \tfrac{4}{3} + \tfrac{3}{2}x$$
$x$$y$$\hat{y}$resid.
$-1$$0$$-\tfrac16$$\tfrac16$
$0$$1$$\tfrac43$$-\tfrac13$
$1$$3$$\tfrac{17}{6}$$\tfrac16$

squared error $\tfrac1{36} + \tfrac4{36} + \tfrac1{36} = \tfrac16$ — the smallest possible

$-1$ $1$ $1$ $2$ $3$ $x$ $y$ $\hat{y} = \tfrac43 + \tfrac32 x$ residuals

Implication — Why Care About Least Norm?

  • Many tied minimizers — which one generalizes?
  • Small $\|\theta\|_2$  $\Rightarrow$  predictions move little when $\mathbf{x}$ moves
  • §09 makes this precise — and ridge picks it automatically

$A^{\dagger}B$ = the tied optimum with the calmest predictor.

Implication — What the Formula Buys

  • Fitting any linear layer on fixed features: one formula, no iteration
  • Linear probes on frozen embeddings — solved by $A^{\dagger}B$
  • Sanity baseline: before gradient descent, know the exact answer here

everything else needs iteration — no formula exists

Geometry & Normal Equations

Projection + orthogonality

What Can $A\theta$ Reach?

$$\{A\theta : \theta \in \mathbb{R}^n\} \;=\; \operatorname{range}(A) \;\subseteq\; \mathbb{R}^N, \qquad \dim = r$$
  • Predictions live on an $r$-dimensional subspace of label space
  • Minimizing $\|A\theta - B\|_2$: find the point of $\operatorname{range}(A)$ closest to $B$

range = everything the columns can build

The Projection Picture

$\operatorname{range}(A)$ $0$ $B$ $A\theta^\star$ residual $B - A\theta^\star$

Best prediction $A\theta^\star$ = foot of the perpendicular from $B$ onto $\operatorname{range}(A)$

The Solution Is a Projection

$$A\theta^\star \;=\; A A^{\dagger} B \;\stackrel{(\text{plug SVDs})}{=}\; U_c \Sigma_c V_c^{\top} V_c \Sigma_c^{-1} U_c^{\top} B \;=\; U_c U_c^{\top} B$$
  • $U_c U_c^{\top}$: keep the $\operatorname{range}(A)$ part of $B$, kill the rest
  • Recall: $AA^{\dagger}$ is the projection onto $\operatorname{range}(A)$

bookkeeping: $UU^{\top} = I$ gives $U_cU_c^{\top} + \tilde{U}\tilde{U}^{\top} = I$

The Residual Is Orthogonal

$$A\theta^\star - B \;=\; (U_cU_c^{\top} - I)B \;=\; -\tilde{U}\tilde{U}^{\top} B$$
$$A^{\top}(A\theta^\star - B) \;=\; -V_c \Sigma_c U_c^{\top} \tilde{U}\tilde{U}^{\top} B \;\stackrel{(U_c^{\top}\tilde{U} = 0)}{=}\; 0$$
  • The leftover error is invisible to every column of $A$
  • The dashed perpendicular in the picture — now an identity

Technique Review — Gradients of Quadratics

Two rules (symmetric $Q$; check entrywise):

$$\nabla_\theta\, (b^{\top}\theta) = b, \qquad \nabla_\theta\, (\theta^{\top} Q\, \theta) = 2\, Q\, \theta$$

Expand the objective, apply both rules:

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\|A\theta - B\|_2^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\theta^{\top}\!A^{\top}\!A\,\theta - (A^{\top}B)^{\top}\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}\|B\|_2^2 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \nabla_\theta = A^{\top}(A\theta - B)$$

Theorem 2 — Normal Equations

Theorem 2.

$\hat{\theta}$ minimizes $\tfrac{1}{2}\|A\theta - B\|_2^2$  if and only if

$$A^{\top}A\, \hat{\theta} \;=\; A^{\top} B$$

If $\operatorname{rank}(A) = n$: unique solution $\hat{\theta} = (A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}B$.

“normal” = perpendicular: the equations say residual $\perp$ columns

Proof — One Expansion, Both Directions

For any candidate $\hat\theta$ and any perturbation $\delta$:

$$J(\hat\theta + \delta) - J(\hat\theta) \;\stackrel{(\text{expand the square})}{=}\; \delta^{\top}\big(A^{\top}A\hat\theta - A^{\top}B\big) \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\|A\delta\|_2^2$$
  • Linear term: the gradient at $\hat\theta$ — call it $g$
  • Quadratic term: never negative

Proof (continued) — Read Off Both Directions

Recap: $J(\hat\theta + \delta) - J(\hat\theta) = \delta^{\top} g + \tfrac{1}{2}\|A\delta\|_2^2$.

  • If $g = 0$: change $\geq 0$ for every $\delta$  $\Rightarrow$  global minimum
  • If $g \neq 0$: take $\delta = -t\,g$, small $t \gt 0$:
$$J(\hat\theta - t g) - J(\hat\theta) = -t\|g\|_2^2 + \tfrac{t^2}{2}\|Ag\|_2^2 \;\lt\; 0 \quad \text{for small } t$$

Not a minimum — so minimizers are exactly the solutions of $g = 0$  $\blacksquare$

Two Formulas, One Answer

Full column rank ($r = n$): plug the SVD into the normal-equation formula,

$$(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top} = \big(V\Sigma_c^2 V^{\top}\big)^{-1} V\Sigma_c U_c^{\top} = V\Sigma_c^{-2}V^{\top} V\Sigma_c U_c^{\top} = V\Sigma_c^{-1}U_c^{\top} = A^{\dagger}$$
  • Theorem 2 recovers Theorem 1 whenever it applies
  • $r \lt n$: $A^{\top}A$ not invertible — only $A^{\dagger}$ survives

Worked — Normal Equations by Hand

$$A^{\top}A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A^{\top}B = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad 3\,\theta_0 = 4, \;\; 2\,\theta_1 = 3$$
$$\hat\theta = \big(\tfrac{4}{3},\, \tfrac{3}{2}\big) \quad = \quad A^{\dagger}B \;\;\checkmark$$

diagonal $A^{\top}A$ (centered inputs)  $\Rightarrow$  two scalar equations, no matrix inverse

Worked — Check the Perpendicular

Residual $B - A\hat\theta = \big(\tfrac16, -\tfrac13, \tfrac16\big)$ against each column of $A$:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{intercept column } (1,1,1):&\quad \tfrac16 - \tfrac13 + \tfrac16 = 0 \;\;\checkmark\\[2pt] \text{slope column } (-1,0,1):&\quad -\tfrac16 + 0 + \tfrac16 = 0 \;\;\checkmark \end{aligned}$$

first line = residuals sum to zero — true whenever an intercept is fitted

Which Route, When?

RouteNeedsGives
normal equations$\operatorname{rank}(A) = n$$(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}B$, one linear solve
pseudo-inversenothing$A^{\dagger}B$, least norm among ties

They agree whenever both apply — two roads, one optimum.

06

Feature Maps

Nonlinear in $x$, linear in $\theta$

Bend the Model, Not the Machinery

Curved trend? Feed in transformed copies of $x$  ($n = 1$):

$$f_\theta(x) \;=\; \theta_1 \log x + \theta_2\, x + \theta_3\, x^2$$
  • Nonlinear in $x$ — but still linear in $\theta$
  • Theorem 1 applies verbatim — same closed form

The Feature Map

$$\phi : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^d, \qquad f_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = \theta^{\top} \phi(\mathbf{x}), \qquad A_\phi = \begin{bmatrix} \phi(\mathbf{x}^{(1)})^{\top} \\ \vdots \\ \phi(\mathbf{x}^{(N)})^{\top} \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Rows of $A$ become $\phi(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})^{\top}$ — nothing else changes
  • Optimum: $\theta^\star = A_\phi^{\dagger} B$, as before

Worked — Add $x^2$ to the Running Example

$$\phi(x) = (1,\, x,\, x^2): \qquad A_\phi = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Now $3$ equations, $3$ unknowns — square system
  • Rows independent  $\Rightarrow$  an exact solution exists

Worked — Solve by Elimination

$$\begin{aligned} \text{row 2}:&\quad \theta_0 = 1\\[2pt] \text{row 3} - \text{row 1}:&\quad 2\theta_1 = 3 \;\Rightarrow\; \theta_1 = \tfrac{3}{2}\\[2pt] \text{row 3} + \text{row 1}:&\quad 2\theta_0 + 2\theta_2 = 3 \;\Rightarrow\; \theta_2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \end{aligned}$$
$$\hat{y} = 1 + \tfrac{3}{2}x + \tfrac{1}{2}x^2, \qquad \text{training error } = 0$$

Line vs Parabola

ModelTrain error
line ($d=2$)$\tfrac16$
parabola ($d=3$)$0$

$3$ parameters, $3$ points: interpolation

is zero training error a victory? hold that thought

$-1$ $1$ $1$ $2$ $3$ $x$ $y$ parabola: exact line: miss $1/6$

More Features Never Hurt — on Training Data

$$\phi \subseteq \phi' \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \min_{\theta}\|A_{\phi'}\theta - B\|_2^2 \;\leq\; \min_{\theta}\|A_{\phi}\theta - B\|_2^2$$
  • Bigger feature set = minimizing over a superset — can only go down
  • Enough features: training error hits $0$ exactly
  • New data is the real test — §08 gives this a name

Feature Engineering → Feature Learning

  • Picking $\log x$, $x^2$, … by hand: feature engineering
  • Which features are useful? — feature selection, later courses
  • Deep learning: the network learns $\phi$ from data

A deep net = learned $\phi$ + linear map on top. Today: the top, exactly.

07

Multi-Output Regression

$m$ regressions at once

Labels Can Be Vectors

$$\mathbf{y}^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^m, \qquad f_{\Theta}(\mathbf{x}) = \Theta\, \mathbf{x} \approx \mathbf{y}, \qquad \Theta = \begin{bmatrix} \theta_1^{\top} \\ \vdots \\ \theta_m^{\top} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$$
  • Row $k$ of $\Theta$: its own weight vector $\theta_k \in \mathbb{R}^n$
  • Output $k$ of the model: $\theta_k^{\top}\mathbf{x} \approx y_k$

The Objective Splits by Output

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{i=1}^N \big\|\Theta \mathbf{x}^{(i)} - \mathbf{y}^{(i)}\big\|_2^2 \;&\stackrel{(\text{norm entrywise})}{=}\; \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^m \big(\theta_k^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{(i)} - y^{(i)}_k\big)^2\\[2pt] \;&\stackrel{(\text{swap finite sums})}{=}\; \sum_{k=1}^m \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^N \big(\theta_k^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{(i)} - y^{(i)}_k\big)^2}_{\text{ordinary regression for output } k} \end{aligned}$$

$m$ Independent Regressions

  • Term $k$ involves only $\theta_k$ — minimize each separately
  • $B_k$ = the $k$-th label components stacked:  $\theta_k^\star = A^{\dagger} B_k$
  • Stack the winners back into $\Theta^\star$

One SVD of $A$ serves all $m$ outputs — $A$ never changes.

Implication — a Linear Layer Is This

  • Network's last layer: $W h \approx$ targets, $h$ = learned features
  • Freeze $h$  $\Rightarrow$  fitting $W$ is multi-output regression
  • $m$ classes, $m$ scores: one $A^{\dagger}$, applied $m$ times

Empirical Risk Minimization

Least squares, seen from above

One Frame for All of Training

$$\min_{\theta}\; L(\theta) \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \ell\big(f_\theta(\mathbf{x}^{(i)}),\, y^{(i)}\big)$$
  • Three choices: model class $\{f_\theta\}$, loss $\ell$, data
  • Least squares, logistic regression, neural-net training — all this one problem

We Have Met This Frame

Recall. Risk $R(h)$, empirical risk $\hat{R}_N(h)$, ERM $= \operatorname{argmin} \hat{R}_N$; generalization gap $R - \hat{R}_N$, bounded via concentration.

  • Earlier: the statistics of the gap
  • Today: the optimization — when can we actually solve the argmin?

Definition — Loss Function

Definition.

A loss function $\ell(\hat{y}, y)$ measures the cost of predicting $\hat{y}$ when the truth is $y$.

Typically $\ell \geq 0$, and $\ell(y, y) = 0$: perfect prediction costs nothing.

the loss encodes what “wrong” means for your application

Two Classic Losses

$$\ell_{\text{quad}}(\hat{y}, y) = \|\hat{y} - y\|_2^2$$
$$\ell_{\text{abs}}(\hat{y}, y) = \|\hat{y} - y\|_1$$
  • Quadratic: punishes big misses hard, smooth
  • Absolute: robust to outliers, kink at $0$
quadratic absolute error

Predictions Need Not Live in Label Space

$$f : \mathcal{X} \to \tilde{\mathcal{Y}}, \qquad \ell : \tilde{\mathcal{Y}} \times \mathcal{Y} \to \mathbb{R}$$
  • Classifier outputs a probability vector, label is a class index
  • Loss bridges the two spaces — e.g. cross-entropy
  • Recall: cross-entropy = expected code length under the wrong model

Definition — Expected Risk

Definition.

Data drawn from a distribution: $(X, Y) \sim P$. The expected risk of a predictor $f$:

$$R[f] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{(X,Y)\sim P}\big[\ell(f(X), Y)\big]$$
  • Average cost on fresh data — the quantity we actually care about
  • Defined over all functions $f$, not just linear ones

Definition — Bayes Risk & Bayes Predictor

Definition.

$$R^\star \;=\; \inf_{f}\; R[f]$$

Infimum over all measurable functions; a minimizer $f^\star$: the Bayes predictor.

  • The floor no algorithm can beat — set by noise in $P$ itself

For Squared Loss, We Know $f^\star$

$$\ell = (\hat{y} - y)^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f^\star(x) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\big[Y \mid X = x\big]$$
  • Recall: the conditional mean minimizes mean-squared error — the MMSE theorem
  • Bayes risk $R^\star = \mathbb{E}\big[\operatorname{Var}(Y \mid X)\big]$: the irreducible noise

regression = trying to learn a conditional expectation

We Never See $P$ — Only Samples

$$\hat{R}[f] \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \ell\big(f(\mathbf{x}^{(i)}),\, y^{(i)}\big) \;\;\xrightarrow[N \to \infty]{}\;\; R[f]$$
  • Replace the expectation by the sample average
  • Recall: law of large numbers — averages converge to means
  • ERM: minimize $\hat{R}$ over a class $\mathcal{F}$, hope $R$ follows

Two Ways to Lose

all predictors model class $\mathcal{F}$ $f^\star$ (Bayes) best in $\mathcal{F}$ ERM pick approximation estimation

approximation error (class too small) + estimation error (data too few)

Least Squares Is ERM

ERM ingredientLeast-squares choice
model class $\mathcal{F}$$f_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = \theta^{\top}\phi(\mathbf{x})$
loss $\ell$$(\hat{y} - y)^2$
$\operatorname{argmin} \hat{R}$$\theta^\star = A^{\dagger}B$

The one ERM problem with a closed-form answer. Everything after it needs iteration.

When ERM Backfires

  • Rich class: $\hat{R} = 0$ by interpolation — our parabola did it with $3$ points
  • Zero training error $\neq$ zero risk: the gap can be huge
  • ERM only sees $\hat{R}$ — it cannot tell fitting from memorizing

Cure: change the objective so it prefers tamer predictors — §09.

Ridge Regression

Regularization, with a closed form

What Makes a Predictor Fragile?

  • Fragile: tiny change in $\mathbf{x}$, wild change in $f(\mathbf{x})$
  • Intuition: predictors insensitive to input perturbations generalize better
  • Goal: measure sensitivity, then penalize it

Sensitivity of a Linear Model

$$\big|\theta^{\top}\mathbf{x} - \theta^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\big| \;\stackrel{(\text{Cauchy--Schwarz})}{\leq}\; \|\theta\|_2\, \|\mathbf{x} - \tilde{\mathbf{x}}\|_2$$
  • $\|\theta\|_2$ is exactly the sensitivity knob of a linear predictor
  • Small $\|\theta\|_2$  $\Rightarrow$  nearby inputs, nearby predictions

Definition — Regularizer

Definition.

A regularizer $r : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$ scores each $\theta$ by the predictor's sensitivity.

$$r_{\ell_2}(\theta) = \sum_{i} \theta_i^2 = \|\theta\|_2^2, \qquad r_{\ell_1}(\theta) = \sum_{i} |\theta_i| = \|\theta\|_1$$

$\ell_2$ shrinks all weights smoothly; $\ell_1$ pushes some to exactly zero (sparsity)

Regularized ERM

$$\min_{\theta}\;\; \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^N \big(\theta^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{(i)} - y^{(i)}\big)^2}_{\text{fit the data}} \;+\; \lambda\, \underbrace{\|\theta\|_2^2}_{\text{stay tame}}$$
  • $\lambda \gt 0$: the dial — $0$ = pure ERM, $\infty$ = ignore data
  • With squared loss + $\ell_2$: ridge regression

Theorem 3 — Ridge Has a Closed Form

Theorem 3.

For any $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^N$, and any $\lambda \gt 0$, the ridge objective $\|A\theta - B\|_2^2 + \lambda\|\theta\|_2^2$ has the unique minimizer

$$\theta^\star_\lambda \;=\; \big(A^{\top}A + \lambda I\big)^{-1} A^{\top} B$$

no rank condition on $A$ — the $+\lambda I$ does all the repair work

Reading the Statement

  • Compare Theorem 2: $(A^{\top}A)^{-1}$ needed $\operatorname{rank}(A) = n$
  • Here: any $A$, even $r \lt n$ — and the minimizer is unique
  • Two claims to prove: the inverse exists, and the formula minimizes

Technique Review — Eigenvalues Under a Shift

Recall. Symmetric matrices have real eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors.

$$\theta^{\top}\!A^{\top}\!A\,\theta = \|A\theta\|_2^2 \geq 0 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; A^{\top}\!A \text{ has eigenvalues } \mu_i \geq 0$$
$$A^{\top}\!A\, v = \mu_i v \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; (A^{\top}\!A + \lambda I)\, v = (\mu_i + \lambda)\, v$$

adding $\lambda I$ shifts every eigenvalue up by $\lambda$ — eigenvectors untouched

Lemma — $A^{\top}A + \lambda I$ Is Always Invertible

Lemma.

For every $A$, $\lambda \gt 0$: eigenvalues of $A^{\top}A + \lambda I$ are $\mu_i + \lambda \geq \lambda \gt 0$ — invertible.

  • No zero eigenvalue  $\Rightarrow$  trivial null space  $\Rightarrow$  invertible  $\blacksquare$
  • Rank-deficient $A$? $\mu_i = 0$ allowed — the shift rescues it

Proof of Theorem 3 — Overview

  1. Gradient of the ridge objective  $\rightarrow$  candidate $\theta^\star_\lambda$
  2. Lemma  $\rightarrow$  candidate is well defined
  3. Direct verification: every other $\theta$ is strictly worse

same expansion trick as Theorem 2 — now with a bonus $\lambda\|\delta\|^2$

Step 1 — Set the Gradient to Zero

$$\nabla_\theta \Big( \|A\theta - B\|_2^2 + \lambda \|\theta\|_2^2 \Big) \;\stackrel{(\text{quadratic rules})}{=}\; 2A^{\top}(A\theta - B) + 2\lambda\theta$$
$$= 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (A^{\top}A + \lambda I)\,\theta = A^{\top}B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \theta = \theta^\star_\lambda$$

the last step uses the Lemma: the inverse exists, so the candidate is unique

Step 2 — Verify It Wins Strictly

Expand at $\theta^\star_\lambda + \delta$; the linear terms cancel by Step 1:

$$J_\lambda(\theta^\star_\lambda + \delta) - J_\lambda(\theta^\star_\lambda) \;=\; \|A\delta\|_2^2 + \lambda\|\delta\|_2^2 \;\gt\; 0 \quad \text{for } \delta \neq 0$$
  • Even if $A\delta = 0$: the $\lambda\|\delta\|_2^2$ term is strictly positive
  • Unique global minimizer  $\blacksquare$

Proof Summary — Ridge

$$\text{gradient} = 0 \;\rightsquigarrow\; (A^{\top}A + \lambda I)\theta = A^{\top}B \;\rightsquigarrow\; \text{invertible} \;\rightsquigarrow\; +\lambda\|\delta\|^2 \gt 0$$

Key trick, named: $\lambda I$ lifts every eigenvalue off zero — strict curvature everywhere.

Ridge Through the SVD — Shrinkage

$$\theta^\star_\lambda \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_i^2 + \lambda}\, \big(u_i^{\top}B\big)\, v_i \qquad \text{vs} \qquad \theta^\star = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \frac{1}{\sigma_i}\, \big(u_i^{\top}B\big)\, v_i$$
  • Each SVD direction shrunk by factor $\dfrac{\sigma_i^2}{\sigma_i^2 + \lambda} \lt 1$
  • Small $\sigma_i$ (shaky directions) shrunk hardest — exactly where least squares is unstable

$\lambda \to 0^+$ Answers the Least-Norm Teaser

$$\frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_i^2 + \lambda} \;\xrightarrow[\lambda \to 0^+]{}\; \frac{1}{\sigma_i} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \theta^\star_\lambda \;\to\; A^{\dagger}B$$
  • Ridge always picks a unique point; its limit is the least-norm least-squares solution
  • The §04 crown explained: $A^{\dagger}B$ is the $\lambda \to 0$ ridge answer

Worked — Ridge on the Running Example

$$A^{\top}A + \lambda I = \begin{bmatrix} 3 + \lambda & 0 \\ 0 & 2 + \lambda \end{bmatrix} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \theta^\star_\lambda = \Big( \frac{4}{3+\lambda},\; \frac{3}{2+\lambda} \Big)$$
$$\lambda = 1: \quad \theta^\star_1 = (1,\, 1)$$

still two scalar divisions — the diagonal structure survives the shift

Turning the Dial

$0$$4/3$$3/2$
$1$$1$$1$
$4$$4/7$$1/2$

cross at $\lambda = 1$ $\theta_0$ intercept $\theta_1$ slope $0$ $12$ $\lambda$

Worked — Three Fits, One Dataset

$-1$ $1$ $1$ $2$ $3$ $x$ $y$ $\lambda = 0$ $\lambda = 1$ $\lambda = 8$

Bigger $\lambda$: flatter, safer — the fit leans toward $\hat{y} = 0$

Underfit — Just Right — Overfit

underfit $\lambda$ too big just right $\lambda$ tuned overfit $\lambda = 0$, many features

$\lambda$ slides you left to right — validation picks the middle panel

Ridge Is a Prior in Disguise

  • Recall: Gaussian-prior MAP adds $\lambda\|\theta\|_2^2$ — “regularization is a prior”
  • Ridge = “before seeing data, I believe weights are small”
$$\underbrace{-\log p(B \mid \theta)}_{\text{squared loss}} \;+\; \underbrace{-\log p(\theta)}_{\lambda\|\theta\|_2^2}$$

What $\lambda$ Buys — and Costs

  • Recall: $\text{MSE} = \text{Bias}^2 + \text{Variance}$
  • $\lambda \uparrow$: bias up (shrunk toward $0$), variance down (less sensitive)
  • Best $\lambda$: minimize the sum — in practice, pick by validation data

Ridge never beats least squares on training data. It aims at fresh data.
