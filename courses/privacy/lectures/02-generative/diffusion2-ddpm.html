01

The Variance Preserving Process

Scaled noise, and an exact Gaussian terminal

Why Variance Preserving?

  • Additive noise: $\mathrm{Var}(X^{(n)}) = \sigma_0^2 + n\sigma^2 \to \infty$.
  • Terminal law then depends on the data variance.
$1$ $\mathrm{Var}(X^{(n)})$ $n$

VP fixes the scale: one prior serves every dataset.

VP Forward Process

Schedule $\{\beta_n\}_{n=1}^N$ with $0 \lt \beta_n \lt 1$:

$$X^{(n)} = \sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,X^{(n-1)} + \sqrt{\beta_n}\,Z^{(n)},\qquad Z^{(n)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\ \text{i.i.d.}$$
$X^{(0)}$ $\xrightarrow{\;\text{shrink} + \text{noise}\;}$ $X^{(1)}$ $\cdots$ $\xrightarrow{\;\text{shrink} + \text{noise}\;}$ $X^{(N)}$

Equivalently $X^{(n)}\,|\,X^{(n-1)} \sim \mathcal{N}\bigl(\sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,X^{(n-1)},\; \beta_n\bigr)$.

Signal shrinks by $\sqrt{1-\beta_n}$; noise of variance $\beta_n$ fills the gap.

Variance Is Preserved

Proposition (unit variance is a fixed point).

If $\mathrm{Var}(X^{(0)}) = 1$, then $\mathrm{Var}(X^{(n)}) = 1$ for every $n$.

Proof. Induction; $Z^{(n)}$ is independent of $X^{(n-1)}$:

$$\mathrm{Var}(X^{(n)}) = (1-\beta_n)\,\mathrm{Var}(X^{(n-1)}) + \beta_n = (1-\beta_n) + \beta_n = 1. \qquad\blacksquare$$

The mean contracts instead: $\mathbb{E}[X^{(n)}] = \sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,\mathbb{E}[X^{(n-1)}]$.

$n$ Steps in One Shot

Cumulative product $\bar{\alpha}_n := \prod_{i=1}^n (1-\beta_i)$.

Proposition (one-shot corruption).

$$X^{(n)} \;\stackrel{(d)}{=}\; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}\,X^{(0)} + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}\,Z,\qquad Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$$

with $Z$ independent of $X^{(0)}$, so $q(X^{(n)}|X^{(0)}) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}X^{(0)},\,1-\bar\alpha_n)$.

Any noise level, one Gaussian draw. No chain simulation.

Proof — Induction Step

Hypothesis at $n-1$, with $Z' \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ independent of $X^{(0)}$:

$$X^{(n-1)} \;\stackrel{(d)}{=}\; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}\,X^{(0)} + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}\,Z'.$$
$$X^{(n)} = \underbrace{\sqrt{(1-\beta_n)\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}}_{=\;\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}}\,X^{(0)} \;+\; \underbrace{\sqrt{(1-\beta_n)(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1})}\,Z' + \sqrt{\beta_n}\,Z^{(n)}}_{\text{independent Gaussians}}.$$

Key trick. Variances add: $(1-\beta_n)(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}) + \beta_n = 1-\bar{\alpha}_n$. $\blacksquare$

Schedule and the Signal Budget

Signal share $\bar{\alpha}_n$, noise share $1-\bar{\alpha}_n$; linear $\beta_n$ from $10^{-4}$ to $0.02$, $N=1000$.

$\bar{\alpha}_n$
  • Each $\beta_n$ small: keeps the small-noise reverse rule valid.
  • $\bar{\alpha}_N \approx 0$: makes the terminal law match the prior.

Moment Generating Function

Definition (MGF).

For real $X$, on the interval where the expectation is finite:

$$M_X(t) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\bigl[e^{tX}\bigr].$$
  • Independence: $M_{X+Y}(t) = M_X(t)\,M_Y(t)$.
  • Scaling: $M_{aX}(t) = M_X(at)$.
  • Gaussian: $M_{\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)}(t) = \exp(\mu t + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t^2)$.

Lévy Continuity

Theorem (Lévy continuity, MGF form).

$M_{X_n}$ finite near $0$, $M_{X_n}(t) \to M(t)$ pointwise, $M$ the MGF of $\mu$. Then

$$X_n \;\xrightarrow{d}\; \mu.$$

Convergence of one scalar function gives convergence in distribution.

Gaussian Terminal

Theorem (terminal law of the VP chain).

Let $M_{X^{(0)}}$ be finite near $0$, and let the schedule satisfy $\bar{\alpha}_n \to 0$. Then

$$X^{(n)} \;\xrightarrow{d}\; \mathcal{N}(0,1).$$

Plan. One-step MGF recursion, unroll to $n$, let $\bar{\alpha}_n \to 0$, invoke Lévy.

Same limit for every data law with a finite MGF.

Proof — One-Step Recursion

Independence of $Z^{(n)}$ factors the expectation:

$$\begin{aligned} M_{X^{(n)}}(t) &= \mathbb{E}\bigl[e^{t\sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,X^{(n-1)}}\bigr]\;\mathbb{E}\bigl[e^{t\sqrt{\beta_n}\,Z^{(n)}}\bigr] \\ &= M_{X^{(n-1)}}\bigl(\sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,t\bigr)\;e^{\beta_n t^2/2}. \end{aligned}$$

Key trick. Scaling enters the MGF argument: recursion on $t$, not on $X$.

Proof (continued) — Unroll to $n$

Iterate the recursion down to level $0$:

$$M_{X^{(n)}}(t) \;=\; M_{X^{(0)}}\bigl(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}\,t\bigr)\,\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}{2}\,t^2\Bigr).$$
  • Arguments multiply: $\prod_{i\leq n}\sqrt{1-\beta_i} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}$.
  • Exponents accumulate: $(1-\beta_n)(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}) + \beta_n = 1-\bar{\alpha}_n$.

Same recursion as the one-shot proposition, one level up.

Proof (continued) — Take the Limit

Fix $t$ and send $\bar{\alpha}_n \to 0$:

$$M_{X^{(n)}}(t) \;=\; \underbrace{M_{X^{(0)}}\bigl(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}\,t\bigr)}_{\to\;M_{X^{(0)}}(0)\;=\;1}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{\exp\Bigl(\tfrac{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}{2}t^2\Bigr)}_{\to\;e^{t^2/2}}.$$

Key trick. Continuity of $M_{X^{(0)}}$ at $0$ erases the data law.

The limit $e^{t^2/2}$ is the $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ MGF; Lévy closes the argument. $\blacksquare$

Proof Recap — One Chain

$$\begin{aligned} M_{X^{(n)}}(t) &\stackrel{\text{(indep.)}}{=}\; M_{X^{(n-1)}}\bigl(\sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,t\bigr)\,e^{\beta_n t^2/2} \\ &\stackrel{\text{(unroll)}}{=}\; M_{X^{(0)}}\bigl(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}\,t\bigr)\,e^{(1-\bar{\alpha}_n)t^2/2} \\ &\stackrel{(\bar{\alpha}_n\to 0)}{\longrightarrow}\; e^{t^2/2} \\ &\stackrel{\text{(Lévy)}}{\Longrightarrow}\; X^{(n)} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1). \end{aligned}$$

The Terminal Forgets the Data

three different data laws $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ $\bar{\alpha}_n \to 0$

Prior is fixed and data-free: sampling starts from pure noise.

All information about $X^{(0)}$ must be restored by the reverse chain.

02

DDPM Reverse Model

Rescale the step, read off the reverse mean

Recall — Small-Noise Reverse Rule

Recall (additive small noise).

$Y = X + Z$ with $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ independent and $\sigma^2 \ll 1$:

$$X\,|\,Y=y \;\stackrel{\text{approx.}}{\sim}\; \mathcal{N}\bigl(y + \sigma^2\,\partial_y \log P_X(y),\; \sigma^2\bigr).$$

The VP step is not additive: the signal is scaled first.

$$X^{(n)} = \sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,X^{(n-1)} + \sqrt{\beta_n}\,Z^{(n)}.$$

Rescaling the VP Step

Divide the step by $\sqrt{1-\beta_n}$:

$$\begin{aligned} W \;&:=\; \frac{X^{(n)}}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}} \;=\; X^{(n-1)} + \sqrt{\frac{\beta_n}{1-\beta_n}}\;Z^{(n)}, \\ \sigma_{\text{eff}}^2 \;&=\; \frac{\beta_n}{1-\beta_n} \;=\; \beta_n + O(\beta_n^2). \end{aligned}$$

Pure additive noise, with a slightly inflated variance.

Key trick. One division turns a scaled step into the additive case.

Applying the Rule

Small-noise rule on the pair $(X^{(n-1)}, W)$:

$$X^{(n-1)}\,|\,W \;\approx\; \mathcal{N}\bigl(W + \sigma_{\text{eff}}^2\,\partial_w \log P_W(W),\; \sigma_{\text{eff}}^2\bigr).$$

Key trick. Change of variables converts the score of $W$ into the score of $X^{(n)}$:

$$\begin{aligned} P_W(w) &= \sqrt{1-\beta_n}\;P_{X^{(n)}}\bigl(\sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,w\bigr), \\ \partial_w \log P_W(w) &= \sqrt{1-\beta_n}\;\partial_x \log P_{X^{(n)}}(x)\big|_{x = \sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,w}. \end{aligned}$$

Reverse Conditional, VP Version

Result (DDPM reverse step).

Substituting $W = X^{(n)}/\sqrt{1-\beta_n}$ and $\sigma_{\text{eff}}^2 \approx \beta_n$:

$$\begin{aligned} X^{(n-1)}\,|\,X^{(n)} &\;\approx\; \mathcal{N}\bigl(\mu(X^{(n)}, n),\; \beta_n\bigr), \\ \mu(X^{(n)}, n) &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}\Bigl(X^{(n)} + \beta_n\,\partial_x\log P_{X^{(n)}}(X^{(n)})\Bigr). \end{aligned}$$
Ho, Jain and Abbeel, "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models", NeurIPS 2020.

Why Not Train $\mu$ Directly?

Tempting: parameterize $\mu_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)$ and regress on data.

$$\mu(X^{(n)}, n) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}\bigl(X^{(n)} + \beta_n\,\underbrace{\partial_x\log P_{X^{(n)}}(X^{(n)})}_{\text{the only unknown}}\bigr).$$

Learning $\mu_\theta$ is learning the score.

Data gives samples, not score values: no direct target.

Two Routes to the Score

(a) Denoiser route

Train $f_\theta(X^{(n)}; n) \approx \mathbb{E}[X^{(0)}\,|\,X^{(n)}]$ by squared-loss regression.

Tweedie's formula turns the denoiser into a score.

(b) Likelihood route

Start from likelihood, bound the intractable marginal, optimize the bound.

Yields $\epsilon$-prediction: plain regression.

Route (b) below: it also fixes the loss weighting.

03

Variational Lower Bound

Likelihood, Jensen, and a telescoping product

Maximum Likelihood Objective

Data $X^{(0)}_1,\ldots,X^{(0)}_M \sim q$ i.i.d. Fit $p_\theta$ by minimizing the NLL:

$$\mathbb{E}_{X^{(0)}\sim q}\bigl[-\log p_\theta(X^{(0)})\bigr] \;=\; D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q\,\Vert\,p_\theta\bigr) \;+\; H(q).$$

$H(q)$ is $\theta$-free: minimizing NLL is closing the KL gap.

  • Empirical average converges to the population NLL.
  • Requires an evaluable $p_\theta(X^{(0)})$: the obstruction ahead.

Marginal Is Intractable

Reverse model with $p(X^{(N)}) = \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, writing $X^{(i:j)} = (X^{(i)},\ldots,X^{(j)})$:

$$\begin{aligned} p_\theta\bigl(X^{(0:N)}\bigr) &= p\bigl(X^{(N)}\bigr)\prod_{n=1}^{N} p_\theta\bigl(X^{(n-1)}\,|\,X^{(n)}\bigr), \\ p_\theta\bigl(X^{(0)}\bigr) &= \int p_\theta\bigl(X^{(0:N)}\bigr)\,dX^{(1:N)}. \end{aligned}$$
$X^{(N)}$ $\xrightarrow{\;p_\theta\;}$ $X^{(N-1)}$ $\to \cdots \to$ $X^{(1)}$ $\xrightarrow{\;p_\theta\;}$ $X^{(0)}$

An $N$-fold integral: minimize a tractable upper bound instead.

Lemma — Bayes on the Chain

Lemma (forward Markov identity).

For a forward Markov chain and $n \geq 2$:

$$q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(n-1)}\bigr)\,q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(0)}\bigr) \;=\; q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)}, X^{(0)}\bigr)\,q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(0)}\bigr).$$
$X^{(0)}$ $\to \cdots \to$ $X^{(n-1)}$ $\rightleftarrows$ $X^{(n)}$ $\to \cdots \to$ $X^{(N)}$

Reads a forward edge backwards, at the price of conditioning on $X^{(0)}$.

Proof — Two Chain Rules

Condition on $X^{(0)}$ throughout. Expand the pair joint one way:

$$q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}, X^{(n)}|X^{(0)}\bigr) = q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)}, X^{(0)}\bigr)\,q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(0)}\bigr).$$

The other way, then Markov drops $X^{(0)}$ from the first factor:

$$\begin{aligned} q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}, X^{(n)}|X^{(0)}\bigr) &= q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(n-1)}, X^{(0)}\bigr)\,q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(0)}\bigr) \\ &= q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(n-1)}\bigr)\,q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(0)}\bigr). \end{aligned}$$

Equate the two expansions. $\blacksquare$

Bound — Proof Outline

Target: a computable upper bound on the NLL.

$$-\log p_\theta\bigl(X^{(0)}\bigr) \;=\; -\log\int p_\theta\bigl(X^{(0:N)}\bigr)\,dX^{(1:N)}.$$
  1. Step 1. Insert the forward joint into the integral.
  2. Step 2. Jensen: push $-\log$ inside the expectation.
  3. Step 3. Factor the ratio, telescope with the lemma.
  4. Step 4. Group into three named terms.

Only Step 2 loses anything; Steps 1, 3, 4 are exact rewrites.

Step 1: Insert the Forward Joint

Key trick. Multiply and divide by $q(X^{(1:N)}|X^{(0)}) = \prod_{n=1}^{N} q(X^{(n)}|X^{(n-1)})$:

$$-\log p_\theta\bigl(X^{(0)}\bigr) = -\log \int q\bigl(X^{(1:N)}|X^{(0)}\bigr)\,\frac{p_\theta(X^{(0:N)})}{q(X^{(1:N)}|X^{(0)})}\,dX^{(1:N)}.$$

The integral is now an expectation over the forward process:

$$=\; -\log \mathbb{E}_q\!\left[\frac{p_\theta(X^{(0:N)})}{q(X^{(1:N)}|X^{(0)})}\,\middle|\,X^{(0)}\right].$$

Step 2: Jensen

$-\log$ is convex, so the expectation moves inside:

$$-\log \mathbb{E}_q[R] \;\leq\; \mathbb{E}_q[-\log R],\qquad R \;=\; \frac{p_\theta(X^{(0:N)})}{q(X^{(1:N)}|X^{(0)})}.$$
$$-\log p_\theta\bigl(X^{(0)}\bigr) \;\leq\; \mathbb{E}_q\bigl[-\log R \,\big|\, X^{(0)}\bigr] \;=:\; \mathrm{VLB}\bigl(X^{(0)}\bigr).$$

Upper bound on the NLL: pushing it down pushes the likelihood up.

Step 3: Factor the Ratio

Expand both products, splitting off the $n=1$ factors:

$$R \;=\; \frac{p(X^{(N)})\;p_\theta(X^{(0)}|X^{(1)})\;\prod_{n=2}^{N} p_\theta(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)})}{q(X^{(1)}|X^{(0)})\;\prod_{n=2}^{N} q(X^{(n)}|X^{(n-1)})}.$$
  • Numerator already reverse-facing.
  • Denominator forward-facing: the obstruction.
  • Fix: apply the lemma to every $n \geq 2$ factor.

Step 3 (continued): Telescope

Key trick. Rewrite each forward factor, then telescope:

$$\begin{aligned} q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(n-1)}\bigr) &\;=\; q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)}, X^{(0)}\bigr)\;\frac{q(X^{(n)}|X^{(0)})}{q(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(0)})}, \qquad n \geq 2, \\[4pt] \prod_{n=2}^{N}\frac{q(X^{(n)}|X^{(0)})}{q(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(0)})} &\;=\; \frac{q(X^{(N)}|X^{(0)})}{q(X^{(1)}|X^{(0)})}. \end{aligned}$$

The leftover $q(X^{(1)}|X^{(0)})$ cancels the $n=1$ denominator.

Step 4: Three Named Factors

After cancellation the ratio splits into three groups:

$$R \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{p(X^{(N)})}{q(X^{(N)}|X^{(0)})}}_{\to\,L_N}\;\cdot\;\prod_{n=2}^{N}\underbrace{\frac{p_\theta(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)})}{q(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)}, X^{(0)})}}_{\to\,L_{n-1}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{p_\theta(X^{(0)}|X^{(1)})}_{\to\,L_0}.$$

Every factor now compares a model law against a known law.

Step 4 (continued): Sum of Three Terms

Take $-\log$ inside $\mathbb{E}_q$: the product becomes a sum.

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{VLB} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_q\Bigl[\;&\underbrace{-\log\frac{p(X^{(N)})}{q(X^{(N)}|X^{(0)})}}_{L_N} \;+\; \sum_{n=2}^{N}\underbrace{\left(-\log\frac{p_\theta(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)})}{q(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)}, X^{(0)})}\right)}_{L_{n-1}} \\[6pt] &+\; \underbrace{\bigl(-\log p_\theta(X^{(0)}|X^{(1)})\bigr)}_{L_0}\;\Bigr]. \end{aligned}$$

Prior match $L_N$, reverse-step match $L_{n-1}$, boundary $L_0$.

Proof Recap — One Chain

$$\begin{aligned} -\log p_\theta\bigl(X^{(0)}\bigr) &\stackrel{(1)}{=}\; -\log \mathbb{E}_q\bigl[R \,\big|\, X^{(0)}\bigr] \\ &\stackrel{(2)}{\leq}\; \mathbb{E}_q\bigl[-\log R \,\big|\, X^{(0)}\bigr] \\ &\stackrel{(3)}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_q\Bigl[-\log\tfrac{p(X^{(N)})}{q(X^{(N)}|X^{(0)})} - \textstyle\sum_{n=2}^{N}\log\tfrac{p_\theta(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)})}{q(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)},X^{(0)})} - \log p_\theta(X^{(0)}|X^{(1)})\Bigr] \\ &\stackrel{(4)}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_q\bigl[L_N\bigr] + \textstyle\sum_{n=2}^{N}\mathbb{E}_q\bigl[L_{n-1}\bigr] + \mathbb{E}_q\bigl[L_0\bigr]. \end{aligned}$$

$L_N$: Match the Prior

$$\mathbb{E}_q[L_N] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{X^{(0)}\sim q}\Bigl[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q(X^{(N)}\,|\,X^{(0)})\,\Vert\,p(X^{(N)})\bigr)\Bigr].$$
  • Distance from the forward endpoint to the Gaussian prior.
  • No $\theta$: a constant to the optimizer.

Small by schedule design ($\bar{\alpha}_N \approx 4\times 10^{-5}$). Ignored in training.

$L_{n-1}$: Match Each Reverse Step

$$\mathbb{E}_q[L_{n-1}] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_q\Bigl[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q(X^{(n-1)}\,|\,X^{(n)}, X^{(0)})\,\Vert\,p_\theta(X^{(n-1)}\,|\,X^{(n)})\bigr)\Bigr].$$
  • The training signal, one term per reverse step.
  • Both arguments Gaussian: KL in closed form.
  • $L_0 = -\log p_\theta(X^{(0)}|X^{(1)})$: boundary term, small, dropped.

Exercise. Show $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{N}(\mu_0,\sigma^2 I)\,\Vert\,\mathcal{N}(\mu_1,\sigma^2 I)) = \tfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}\|\mu_1-\mu_0\|^2$.

Where the Three Terms Live

$X^{(0)}$ $X^{(1)}$ $\cdots$ $X^{(n-1)}$ $X^{(n)}$ $\cdots$ $X^{(N)}$ $L_0$ boundary $L_{n-1}$ training $L_N$ prior

All the learning happens on the middle edges.

$L_0$

One step, dropped.

$L_{n-1}$

$N-1$ KLs, the loss.

$L_N$

$\theta$-free, near zero.

04

Exact Posterior to $\epsilon$-Loss

Gaussian posterior, mean matching, noise prediction

The Target Posterior Is Exactly Gaussian

Theorem (data-conditional posterior).

$$q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}\,|\,X^{(n)}, X^{(0)}\bigr) \;=\; \mathcal{N}\bigl(\tilde{\mu}_n(X^{(n)}, X^{(0)}),\; \tilde{\beta}_n\bigr),$$
$$\tilde{\mu}_n = \tfrac{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1})}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(n)} + \tfrac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}\,\beta_n}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(0)},\qquad \tilde{\beta}_n = \tfrac{1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}\,\beta_n.$$

Exact equality; no small-$\beta_n$ approximation anywhere.

Why Conditioning on $X^{(0)}$ Helps

prior $q(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(0)})$ likelihood $q(X^{(n)}|X^{(n-1)})$ product: Gaussian
  • Two Gaussians in $X^{(n-1)}$: the product is Gaussian.
  • Without $X^{(0)}$ the prior is $P_{X^{(n-1)}}$, an arbitrary density.
  • That is exactly where the small-$\beta_n$ rule was needed.

Proof — Bayes and Markov

Conditioning on $X^{(0)}$ throughout, Bayes then Markov:

$$q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(n)}, X^{(0)}\bigr) \;\propto\; q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(n-1)}\bigr)\,q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(0)}\bigr).$$

Both factors are Gaussian in $X^{(n-1)}$:

$$\begin{aligned} q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(n-1)}\bigr) &\propto \exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2\beta_n}\bigl(X^{(n)} - \sqrt{1-\beta_n}X^{(n-1)}\bigr)^2\Bigr), \\ q\bigl(X^{(n-1)}|X^{(0)}\bigr) &\propto \exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1})}\bigl(X^{(n-1)} - \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}X^{(0)}\bigr)^2\Bigr). \end{aligned}$$

Proof (continued) — Collect the Quadratic

Key trick. Read the variance off the coefficient of $(X^{(n-1)})^2$:

$$\frac{1}{\tilde{\beta}_n} \;=\; \frac{1-\beta_n}{\beta_n} + \frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}} \;=\; \frac{(1-\beta_n)(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}) + \beta_n}{\beta_n(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1})}.$$

The numerator is the familiar $1-\bar{\alpha}_n$, so

$$\tilde{\beta}_n \;=\; \frac{1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}\,\beta_n \;\leq\; \beta_n.$$

Proof (continued) — Read Off the Mean

Linear coefficient in $X^{(n-1)}$:

$$b \;=\; \frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,X^{(n)}}{\beta_n} + \frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}\,X^{(0)}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}.$$

A Gaussian exponent $-\tfrac{1}{2\tilde\beta_n}x^2 + b\,x$ has mean $\tilde{\beta}_n\,b$:

$$\tilde{\mu}_n \;=\; \tilde{\beta}_n\,b \;=\; \frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1})}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(n)} + \frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}\,\beta_n}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(0)}. \qquad\blacksquare$$

Posterior Mean in Score Form

Conditional score of the one-shot law, with $X^{(n)} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(0)} + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}\,\epsilon$:

$$\partial_x \log q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(0)}\bigr) \;=\; -\,\frac{X^{(n)} - \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(0)}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n} \;=\; -\,\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}}.$$

Substituting into $\tilde{\mu}_n$ gives an exact rewrite:

$$\tilde{\mu}_n \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}\Bigl(X^{(n)} + \beta_n\,\partial_x \log q\bigl(X^{(n)}|X^{(0)}\bigr)\Bigr).$$

Verifying the Rewrite

Expand the score form and use $\bar{\alpha}_n = (1-\beta_n)\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}$:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}\Bigl(X^{(n)} - \tfrac{\beta_n (X^{(n)} - \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(0)})}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}\Bigr) &= \frac{X^{(n)}}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}\cdot\frac{1-\bar{\alpha}_n-\beta_n}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n} + \frac{\beta_n\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}\,X^{(0)}}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,(1-\bar{\alpha}_n)} \\ &= \frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1})}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(n)} + \frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}}\,\beta_n}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(0)}. \end{aligned}$$

Key trick. $1-\bar{\alpha}_n-\beta_n = (1-\beta_n)(1-\bar{\alpha}_{n-1})$. $\blacksquare$

From KL to Mean Matching

Model side uses the marginal score, with variance fixed at $\beta_n$:

$$\mu_\theta(X^{(n)}, n) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}\bigl(X^{(n)} + \beta_n\,s_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)\bigr).$$

Equal variances (note $\tilde{\beta}_n \to \beta_n$ for large $n$) collapse the KL:

$$L_{n-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{2\beta_n}\,\mathbb{E}_q\bigl[\bigl\|\tilde{\mu}_n(X^{(n)}, X^{(0)}) - \mu_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)\bigr\|^2\bigr].$$

Not Ordinary Mean Matching

$X^{(n)}$ $X^{(0)}$ $\tilde{\mu}_n$ $X^{(n)}$ $X^{(0)}$ $\mu_\theta$

$\mu_\theta$ never sees $X^{(0)}$; its optimum is the $X^{(0)}$-averaged target.

  • Optimum: $\mu_\theta^\star(X^{(n)},n) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[\tilde{\mu}_n \,\big|\, X^{(n)}\bigr]$.
  • Conditional score is matched only on average.

Mean Matching to Score Matching

Both means share the shape $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}(X^{(n)} + \beta_n\,\cdot\,)$, so the difference is

$$\tilde{\mu}_n - \mu_\theta \;=\; \frac{\beta_n}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}\Bigl(\partial_x \log q(X^{(n)}|X^{(0)}) - s_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)\Bigr).$$

Insert into $L_{n-1}$ and simplify the constant:

$$L_{n-1} \;=\; \frac{\beta_n}{2(1-\beta_n)}\,\mathbb{E}_q\bigl\|\partial_x\log q(X^{(n)}|X^{(0)}) - s_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)\bigr\|^2.$$

$\epsilon$ and Score: Same Vector

$$\partial_x\log q\bigl(X^{(n)}\,|\,X^{(0)}\bigr) \;=\; -\,\frac{X^{(n)}-\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}\,X^{(0)}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_n} \;=\; -\,\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}}.$$
$\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}\,X^{(0)}$ $X^{(n)}$ noise: $+\,\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}\,\epsilon$ score: $-\,\epsilon\,/\,\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}$

$\epsilon$-Reparameterization

Score and noise differ by a known scale, so predict the noise:

$$s_\theta(X^{(n)}, n) \;=\; -\,\frac{\epsilon_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}}.$$
  • Target $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$: same scale at every $n$.
  • The score itself blows up as $\bar{\alpha}_n \to 1$.

Predicting $\epsilon$ is predicting the score, better conditioned.

$\epsilon$-Prediction Loss

Substitute both score forms into $L_{n-1}$:

$$L_{n-1} \;=\; \frac{\beta_n}{2(1-\beta_n)(1-\bar{\alpha}_n)}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl\|\epsilon_n - \epsilon_\theta\bigl(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(0)} + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}\,\epsilon_n,\; n\bigr)\bigr\|^2.$$

DDPM drops the $n$-dependent prefactor:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}}(\theta) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{X^{(0)}, n, \epsilon}\bigl\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)\bigr\|^2.$$
Ho, Jain and Abbeel, "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models", NeurIPS 2020.

Training and Sampling

Two short loops, three sources of error

Training Algorithm

DDPM Training (repeat until converged)
  1. Sample $X^{(0)} \sim p_{\text{data}}$.
  2. Sample $n \sim \text{Unif}\{1, \ldots, N\}$.
  3. Sample $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$.
  4. Form $X^{(n)} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}\,X^{(0)} + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}\,\epsilon$.
  5. Gradient step on $\tilde{\lambda}_n\,\bigl\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)\bigr\|^2$.

Original DDPM: $\tilde{\lambda}_n = 1$, unweighted.

From $\mu_\theta$ to the Update

Reverse step is a Gaussian draw around $\mu_\theta$:

$$X^{(n-1)} \;=\; \mu_\theta(X^{(n)}, n) + \sqrt{\beta_n}\,Z^{(n)},\qquad Z^{(n)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1).$$

Insert $s_\theta = -\epsilon_\theta/\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}$ into $\mu_\theta$:

$$X^{(n-1)} \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}\Bigl(X^{(n)} - \frac{\beta_n}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}}\,\epsilon_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)\Bigr) + \sqrt{\beta_n}\,Z^{(n)}.$$

Subtract predicted noise, rescale, add fresh noise.

Sampling Algorithm

DDPM Sampling
  1. Sample $X^{(N)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$.
  2. For $n = N, N-1, \ldots, 1$:
    1. Sample $Z^{(n)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$.
    2. $X^{(n-1)} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_n}}\!\left(X^{(n)} - \dfrac{\beta_n}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}}\,\epsilon_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)\right) + \sqrt{\beta_n}\,Z^{(n)}$.
  3. Return $X^{(0)}$.

Three Approximation Errors

Forward $q$: $X^{(0)}$ $\to$ $X^{(1)}$ $\to$ $\cdots$ $\to$ $X^{(N-1)}$ $\to$ $X^{(N)}$
Reverse $p_\theta$: $\bar{X}^{(0)}$ $\leftarrow$ $\bar{X}^{(1)}$ $\leftarrow$ $\cdots$ $\leftarrow$ $\bar{X}^{(N-1)}$ $\leftarrow$ $\bar{X}^{(N)}$

1. Gaussian reverse

$p_\theta \approx \mathcal{N}$ needs $\beta_n$ small.

2. Imperfect score

$\epsilon_\theta \neq \epsilon$. Errors compound.

3. Prior mismatch

$\bar{\alpha}_N \neq 0$ exactly.

Only 1 is removable: take $\beta_n \to 0$ and the chain becomes an SDE.

Lecture Recap

  • VP forward: unit variance preserved, $\bar{\alpha}_n$ signal share.
  • Terminal: $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ in the limit, by MGF plus Lévy.
  • VLB: $L_N$ constant, $\sum L_{n-1}$ training, $L_0$ boundary.
  • Posterior: exactly Gaussian once $X^{(0)}$ is given.
$$L_{n-1} \;\propto\; \bigl\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(X^{(n)}, n)\bigr\|^2.$$

Open: remove the small-$\beta_n$ approximation with a continuous-time limit.
