Bookkeeping Under $(\varepsilon, \delta)$

Recall (advanced composition).

$k$ adaptive $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP steps are $(\varepsilon', k\delta + \delta')$-DP for every slack $\delta' \gt 0$, with

$$\varepsilon' \;=\; \sqrt{2k \ln(1/\delta')}\;\varepsilon \;+\; k\varepsilon\,\frac{e^{\varepsilon}-1}{e^{\varepsilon}+1}$$

02

KL Divergence Privacy

First attempt: bound the average of the privacy loss, not its maximum.

From Events to Distributions

Fix neighbors $X \simeq X'$; write $P = \mathcal{M}(X)$, $Q = \mathcal{M}(X')$, and the privacy loss

$$L(y) \;=\; \ln \frac{P(y)}{Q(y)}$$

Pure DP is a statement about the worst outcome:

$$\varepsilon\text{-DP} \;\iff\; \max_{y}\, L(y) \,\le\, \varepsilon \quad \text{for every neighboring pair}$$

Idea: compare the whole distributions $P$ and $Q$ by a divergence instead of a max.

Definition — KL Divergence Privacy

Definition (KL-DP).

$\mathcal{M}$ is $\varepsilon$-KL-DP if for all neighbors $X \simeq X'$:

$$D\big(\mathcal{M}(X) \,\|\, \mathcal{M}(X')\big) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{y \sim P}\big[L(y)\big] \;\le\; \varepsilon$$

Same loss $L$, new aggregation: the mean under $P$ replaces the max.

Immediate: $\varepsilon$-DP implies $\varepsilon$-KL-DP, since the mean never exceeds the max.

Average vs Worst Case

Both definitions look at the distribution of $L(y)$ for $y \sim P$; they read off different points.

mean of $L$: KL-DP max of $L$: pure DP privacy loss $L(y)$, $y \sim P$

The gap between the two marks is exactly what a tail can hide.

The Weakness of KL-DP

A tiny mean can hide a catastrophic tail.

Suppose $L(y) = 0$ with probability $1 - 10^{-4}$ and $L(y) = 20$ with probability $10^{-4}$.

$$D(P \| Q) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_P[L] \;=\; 20 \cdot 10^{-4} \;=\; 0.002 \qquad \text{yet} \qquad e^{L} = e^{20} \approx 5 \times 10^{8}$$

KL-DP certifies an excellent average, while the unlucky $10^{-4}$ of outcomes expose users outright.

Needed: control of higher moments of the loss, not just its mean. Enter a knob.

03

Rényi Divergence

One parameter $\alpha$ sweeps from the average ($\alpha \to 1$) to the worst case ($\alpha \to \infty$).

Definition — Rényi Divergence

Definition (Rényi divergence of order $\alpha$).

For distributions $P, Q$ and $\alpha \gt 1$:

$$D_{\alpha}(P \,\|\, Q) \;=\; \frac{1}{\alpha - 1} \ln\, \mathbb{E}_{x \sim Q}\!\left[ \left( \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \right)^{\!\alpha} \right]$$

The $\alpha$-th moment of the likelihood ratio, on a log scale, normalized by $\alpha - 1$.

Larger $\alpha$ weights large-ratio outcomes more heavily: $\alpha$ is a tail-sensitivity dial.

Reading the Formula — a Moment of the Loss

Rewriting under $P$ turns the divergence into a moment of the privacy loss $L = \ln(P/Q)$:

$$e^{(\alpha-1) D_{\alpha}(P \| Q)} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{Q}\!\left[ \Big(\tfrac{P}{Q}\Big)^{\!\alpha} \right] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[ \Big(\tfrac{P}{Q}\Big)^{\!\alpha-1} \right] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{P}\big[ e^{(\alpha-1) L} \big]$$
$(\alpha - 1)\, D_{\alpha}$ is the log-MGF of the privacy loss under $P$. This identity powers every proof today.

Check the middle step: $Q \cdot (P/Q)^{\alpha} = P \cdot (P/Q)^{\alpha-1}$ pointwise.

Worked Example — Two Shifted Gaussians

Fact (proved in Section 05).

$$D_{\alpha}\big( \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2) \big) \;=\; \frac{\alpha\, \mu^2}{2\sigma^2} \qquad \text{for all } \alpha \gt 1$$

Anchor numbers for $\mu = 1$, $\sigma = 1$:

$D_{\alpha}$$0.5$$1$$5$$\infty$

Linear growth in $\alpha$, no ceiling: Gaussian pairs never satisfy pure DP.

Limit $\alpha \to 1$ — Rényi Recovers KL

Lemma (order-1 limit).

$$\lim_{\alpha \to 1^{+}} D_{\alpha}(P \,\|\, Q) \;=\; D(P \,\|\, Q) \qquad \text{(convention: } D_1 = D \text{)}$$

At $\alpha = 1$ the definition reads $\tfrac{0}{0}$: numerator $\ln \mathbb{E}_Q[P/Q] = \ln 1 = 0$, denominator $\alpha - 1 = 0$.

Trick: a genuine $0/0$ limit, so differentiate top and bottom in $\alpha$ (L'Hôpital).

Proof — L'Hôpital on the $0/0$ Limit

Differentiate numerator and denominator of $D_{\alpha} = \frac{\ln \mathbb{E}_Q[(P/Q)^{\alpha}]}{\alpha - 1}$ in $\alpha$:

$$\begin{aligned} \lim_{\alpha \to 1^{+}} D_{\alpha} \;&\stackrel{(\text{diff.})}{=}\; \lim_{\alpha \to 1^{+}} \frac{ \mathbb{E}_Q\big[ (P/Q)^{\alpha} \ln(P/Q) \big] }{ \mathbb{E}_Q\big[ (P/Q)^{\alpha} \big] } \\[4pt] \;&\stackrel{(\alpha = 1)}{=}\; \frac{ \mathbb{E}_Q\big[ (P/Q) \ln(P/Q) \big] }{ 1 } \;=\; \mathbb{E}_P\big[ \ln(P/Q) \big] \;=\; D(P \,\|\, Q) \end{aligned}$$

First line: differentiate under the expectation. Then set $\alpha = 1$; the denominator is $1$.

Last step: $Q \cdot (P/Q) = P$ pointwise, the same rewriting as before. $\blacksquare$

Limit $\alpha \to \infty$ — Rényi Recovers the Max

Lemma (order-$\infty$ limit).

$$\lim_{\alpha \to \infty} D_{\alpha}(P \,\|\, Q) \;=\; D_{\infty}(P \,\|\, Q) \;=\; \max_{x:\, Q(x) \gt 0}\, \ln \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$$

$D_{\infty}$ is the max divergence: the worst-case privacy loss from Section 02.

So pure DP is exactly a max-divergence bound: $\varepsilon$-DP $\iff D_{\infty} \le \varepsilon$ for all neighbors.

Proof — Squeeze the $\alpha$-th Moment

Let $M = \max_x P(x)/Q(x)$, attained at $x^{\star}$. Bound the moment from both sides:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_Q\big[ (P/Q)^{\alpha} \big] \;&\le\; M^{\alpha - 1}\, \mathbb{E}_Q\big[ P/Q \big] \;=\; M^{\alpha - 1} && \Rightarrow\;\; D_{\alpha} \le \ln M \\[4pt] \mathbb{E}_Q\big[ (P/Q)^{\alpha} \big] \;&\ge\; Q(x^{\star})\, M^{\alpha} && \Rightarrow\;\; D_{\alpha} \ge \frac{\alpha \ln M + \ln Q(x^{\star})}{\alpha - 1} \end{aligned}$$

The lower bound tends to $\ln M$; squeezed, $D_{\alpha} \to \ln M = D_{\infty}$. $\blacksquare$

Bonus (first line): $D_{\alpha} \le D_{\infty}$ for every $\alpha$.

Monotonicity in $\alpha$

Lemma (monotone in the order).

$\alpha \mapsto D_{\alpha}(P \| Q)$ is nondecreasing, from $D_1 = $ KL up toward $D_{\infty} = $ max.

$D_1 = $ KL $D_{\infty}$ (bounded pair) Gaussian pair: $\alpha\mu^2/2\sigma^2$, no ceiling $\alpha$

Proof idea: power-mean (Jensen) applied to $\mathbb{E}_P[(P/Q)^{\alpha-1}]$; higher moments dominate.

04

Rényi Differential Privacy

Bound $D_{\alpha}$ between neighboring outputs; compose by adding; convert at the end.

Definition — $(\alpha, \varepsilon)$-RDP

Definition (Rényi differential privacy).

$\mathcal{M}$ is $(\alpha, \varepsilon)$-RDP if for all neighbors $X \simeq X'$:

$$D_{\alpha}\big( \mathcal{M}(X) \,\|\, \mathcal{M}(X') \big) \;\le\; \varepsilon$$

One mechanism, one curve of guarantees: the function $\alpha \mapsto \varepsilon(\alpha)$.

Bookkeeping: track $\varepsilon(\alpha)$ per step, add curves, optimize $\alpha$ at the end.

Mironov, "Rényi Differential Privacy", CSF 2017.

The $\alpha$ Spectrum

The two limits place the old definitions at the ends of one axis.

$\alpha \to 1$: KL-DP average loss $(\alpha, \varepsilon(\alpha))$-RDP family $\alpha \to \infty$: pure $\varepsilon$-DP worst-case loss

Since $D_{\alpha} \le D_{\infty}$: an $\varepsilon$-DP mechanism is $(\alpha, \varepsilon)$-RDP for every $\alpha$, including $\alpha = \infty$.

Property 1 — Post-Processing

Anything computed from a private output should stay private, with no extra cost.

Theorem (post-processing).

If $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\alpha, \varepsilon)$-RDP and $f$ is any (randomized) map, then $f \circ \mathcal{M}$ is $(\alpha, \varepsilon)$-RDP.

It suffices to prove a data-processing inequality for Rényi divergence:

$$D_{\alpha}\big( f(P) \,\|\, f(Q) \big) \;\le\; D_{\alpha}(P \,\|\, Q)$$

Proof — Outputs Are Mixtures of Inputs

Model $f$ as a channel $K(z \mid y)$; output distributions are $P'(z) = \sum_y K(z \mid y) P(y)$.

$$\frac{P'(z)}{Q'(z)} \;=\; \sum_y \underbrace{\frac{K(z \mid y)\, Q(y)}{Q'(z)}}_{=:\, w_z(y),\ \text{sums to } 1} \cdot \frac{P(y)}{Q(y)}$$

Each output likelihood ratio is a weighted average of input likelihood ratios.

Averaging cannot create extreme ratios; the next slide makes that precise via convexity.

Proof (continued) — Jensen Collapses the Mixture

Recap: $P'/Q'$ at $z$ is a $w_z$-average of $P/Q$. Now $t \mapsto t^{\alpha}$ is convex for $\alpha \gt 1$:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_{Q'}\!\Big[ \big(\tfrac{P'}{Q'}\big)^{\alpha} \Big] \;&\le\; \sum_z Q'(z) \sum_y w_z(y) \Big( \tfrac{P(y)}{Q(y)} \Big)^{\alpha} && \text{(Jensen at each } z \text{)} \\[4pt] \;&=\; \sum_y Q(y) \Big( \tfrac{P(y)}{Q(y)} \Big)^{\alpha} \sum_z K(z \mid y) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{Q}\!\Big[ \big(\tfrac{P}{Q}\big)^{\alpha} \Big] && \text{(} \textstyle\sum_z K = 1 \text{)} \end{aligned}$$

Take $\tfrac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln$ of both ends: $D_{\alpha}(P' \| Q') \le D_{\alpha}(P \| Q)$. $\blacksquare$

Property 2 — Composition Is Additive

Intuition: $(\alpha - 1) D_{\alpha}$ is a log-MGF, and log-MGFs of independent losses add.

Theorem (adaptive composition).

If $\mathcal{M}_1$ is $(\alpha, \varepsilon_1)$-RDP and $\mathcal{M}_2$ is $(\alpha, \varepsilon_2)$-RDP given any past, $(\mathcal{M}_1, \mathcal{M}_2)$ is $(\alpha,\ \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2)$-RDP.

Exact addition at every fixed $\alpha$: no slack parameter, no union bound, no $\delta$.

Proof — Factorize the Joint Ratio

Joint outputs $(y_1, y_2)$; densities chain: $P_{12}(y_1, y_2) = P_1(y_1)\, P_2(y_2 \mid y_1)$, same for $Q$.

$$\mathbb{E}_{Q_{12}}\!\Big[ \big(\tfrac{P_{12}}{Q_{12}}\big)^{\alpha} \Big] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{y_1 \sim Q_1}\!\bigg[ \Big(\tfrac{P_1(y_1)}{Q_1(y_1)}\Big)^{\!\alpha}\, \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{y_2 \sim Q_2(\cdot \mid y_1)}\!\Big[ \Big(\tfrac{P_2(y_2 \mid y_1)}{Q_2(y_2 \mid y_1)}\Big)^{\!\alpha} \Big]}_{\text{inner moment, past } y_1 \text{ fixed}} \bigg]$$

Trick: condition on the first output; the joint $\alpha$-th moment splits into outer times inner.

Proof (continued) — Bound Inner, Then Outer

Recap: joint moment $=$ outer moment weighted by an inner conditional moment.

$$\begin{aligned} \text{inner} \;&\le\; e^{(\alpha-1)\varepsilon_2} \quad \text{for every fixed } y_1 && \text{(} \mathcal{M}_2 \text{ is RDP given any past)} \\[4pt] \mathbb{E}_{Q_{12}}\!\Big[ \big(\tfrac{P_{12}}{Q_{12}}\big)^{\alpha} \Big] \;&\le\; e^{(\alpha-1)\varepsilon_2}\, \mathbb{E}_{Q_1}\!\Big[ \big(\tfrac{P_1}{Q_1}\big)^{\alpha} \Big] \;\le\; e^{(\alpha-1)(\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2)} && \text{(} \mathcal{M}_1 \text{ is RDP)} \end{aligned}$$

Take $\tfrac{1}{\alpha-1} \ln$: $D_{\alpha} \le \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2$. $\blacksquare$

Corollary ($k$-fold).

$k$ adaptive mechanisms, each $(\alpha, \varepsilon_i)$-RDP, compose to $\big(\alpha,\ \sum_{i=1}^{k} \varepsilon_i\big)$-RDP.

Property 3 — Conversion to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP

RDP is the ledger; $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP is the currency we report. The exchange rate:

Theorem (RDP to approximate DP).

If $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\alpha, \varepsilon)$-RDP, then for every $\delta \in (0, 1)$, $\mathcal{M}$ is

$$\Big( \varepsilon + \frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{\alpha - 1},\ \delta \Big)\text{-DP}$$

Price of conversion: $\ln(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$, small when $\alpha$ is large. $\alpha$ stays free to optimize.

Mironov, "Rényi Differential Privacy", CSF 2017.

Proof Roadmap — Markov on the Loss

  1. Split: cut any event $S$ into small-loss and large-loss parts at threshold $c$.
  2. Markov: bound the large-loss tail using the $(\alpha-1)$-th moment that RDP controls.
  3. Choose $c$: set the threshold so the tail is exactly $\delta$.

Everything runs through the identity $\mathbb{E}_P\big[e^{(\alpha-1)L}\big] = e^{(\alpha-1) D_{\alpha}} \le e^{(\alpha-1)\varepsilon}$.

Same architecture as approximate DP: bound the loss tail, pay it as $\delta$.

Proof — Split the Event at Loss $c$

Fix neighbors, an event $S$, and a threshold $c$. On $\{L \le c\}$ we have $P(y) \le e^{c} Q(y)$ pointwise:

$$\Pr_P[S] \;=\; \Pr_P\big[S \cap \{L \le c\}\big] + \Pr_P\big[S \cap \{L \gt c\}\big] \;\le\; e^{c}\, \Pr_Q[S] \;+\; \Pr_P\big[L \gt c\big]$$

First term: pure-DP shape at level $c$. Second: a tail to pay as $\delta$.

Remaining goal: show $\Pr_P[L \gt c] \le \delta$ for the right choice of $c$.

Proof (continued) — Markov on the Exponentiated Loss

Recap: need $\Pr_P[L \gt c] \le \delta$. Exponentiate ($e^{(\alpha-1)t}$ is increasing for $\alpha \gt 1$), then Markov:

$$\Pr_P\big[ L \gt c \big] \;=\; \Pr_P\Big[ e^{(\alpha-1)L} \gt e^{(\alpha-1)c} \Big] \;\le\; \frac{\mathbb{E}_P\big[ e^{(\alpha-1)L} \big]}{e^{(\alpha-1)c}} \;=\; e^{(\alpha-1)(D_{\alpha} - c)} \;\le\; e^{(\alpha-1)(\varepsilon - c)}$$
The Chernoff trick: RDP hands us the $(\alpha-1)$-th moment; Markov turns it into a tail bound.

Proof (continued) — Choose the Threshold

Set $c = \varepsilon + \frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{\alpha - 1}$, so that $e^{(\alpha-1)(\varepsilon - c)} = \delta$. Chaining the two steps:

$$\Pr_P[S] \;\stackrel{(\text{split})}{\le}\; e^{c}\, \Pr_Q[S] + \Pr_P[L \gt c] \;\stackrel{(\text{Markov})}{\le}\; e^{c}\, \Pr_Q[S] + e^{(\alpha-1)(\varepsilon - c)} \;\stackrel{(\text{choice})}{=}\; e^{c}\, \Pr_Q[S] + \delta$$

This is exactly $(c, \delta)$-DP with $c = \varepsilon + \ln(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$. $\blacksquare$

Cashing Out — a Numeric Conversion

Example: $\mathcal{M}$ is $(2, \varepsilon)$-RDP; pick $\delta = e^{-m}$. The conversion price is $\frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1} = m$:

$$(2, \varepsilon)\text{-RDP} \;\Longrightarrow\; \big( \varepsilon + m,\ e^{-m} \big)\text{-DP}$$

At $\alpha = 2$, each digit of certainty costs one unit of $\varepsilon$; larger $\alpha$ pays less.

Tension: raising $\alpha$ cheapens conversion but raises $\varepsilon(\alpha)$ itself. Optimize at the end.

Bridging Lemma — Pure DP Enters the Ledger

To account pure-DP steps in RDP, we need their $D_{\alpha}$, and it is quadratically small:

Lemma (pure DP to RDP).

If $D_{\infty}(P \| Q) \le \varepsilon$ and $D_{\infty}(Q \| P) \le \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon \le 1$, then for all $\alpha \gt 1$:

$$D_{\alpha}(P \,\|\, Q) \;\le\; 2 \alpha \varepsilon^2$$

So an $\varepsilon$-DP step costs $\varepsilon^2$, not $\varepsilon$, in the RDP ledger (for moderate $\alpha$).

This $\varepsilon \to \varepsilon^2$ drop is the entire source of the $\sqrt{k}$ composition law.

Proof — the Loss Is Bounded, Use Hoeffding

Both-sided $D_{\infty}$ bounds pin the loss: $L(y) = \ln(P/Q) \in [-\varepsilon, \varepsilon]$ for every $y$.

$$(\alpha - 1) D_{\alpha} \;=\; \ln \mathbb{E}_P\big[ e^{(\alpha-1) L} \big] \;\le\; (\alpha - 1)\, \mathbb{E}_P[L] \;+\; \frac{(\alpha-1)^2 (2\varepsilon)^2}{8}$$
Hoeffding's lemma: a random variable in $[a, b]$ has log-MGF at most $t\,\mathbb{E}[L] + t^2 (b-a)^2 / 8$.

Bounded implies sub-Gaussian: standard, by convexity of $\exp$ plus a Taylor bound.

Proof (continued) — the Mean Is Quadratically Small

Recap: need $\mathbb{E}_P[L] = D(P \| Q)$ small. Symmetrize, then bound pointwise:

$$\begin{aligned} D(P \| Q) \;&\le\; D(P \| Q) + D(Q \| P) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_y \big( P(y) - Q(y) \big) L(y) \\[4pt] \;&\le\; \varepsilon \textstyle\sum_y \big| P(y) - Q(y) \big| \;\le\; \varepsilon \big( e^{\varepsilon} - 1 \big) \textstyle\sum_y Q(y) \;\le\; 2 \varepsilon^2 \end{aligned}$$

Pointwise: $|P - Q| \le (e^{\varepsilon} - 1) \min(P, Q)$, since $P, Q$ are within $e^{\pm\varepsilon}$ of each other.

Last step: $e^{\varepsilon} - 1 \le 2\varepsilon$ for $\varepsilon \le 1$. Worst case $\varepsilon$, average case $\varepsilon^2$.

Proof (continued) — Combine and Simplify

Recap: Hoeffding bounded the MGF; the mean is at most $2\varepsilon^2$. Insert and divide:

$$D_{\alpha} \;\le\; \mathbb{E}_P[L] + \frac{(\alpha - 1) \varepsilon^2}{2} \;\le\; 2\varepsilon^2 + \frac{(\alpha - 1) \varepsilon^2}{2} \;\le\; 2 \alpha \varepsilon^2$$

Final inequality: $2 + \frac{\alpha - 1}{2} \le 2\alpha \iff 3 \le 3\alpha$, true for all $\alpha \ge 1$. $\blacksquare$

Constants are loose by design; sharper versions exist, but $2\alpha\varepsilon^2$ is all we need.

Optimizing $\alpha$ — the $\sqrt{k}$ Law

Substitute $t = \alpha - 1$ and use AM–GM, $a t + b/t \ge 2\sqrt{ab}$, with equality at $t = \sqrt{b/a}$:

$$\min_{\alpha \gt 1} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tot}}(\alpha) \;=\; \underbrace{2 k \varepsilon^2}_{\text{drift: } k \times \varepsilon^2} \;+\; \underbrace{2 \varepsilon \sqrt{2 k \ln(1/\delta)}}_{\text{fluctuation: } \sqrt{k}\, \varepsilon}$$

Same two terms as advanced composition: linear drift in $k\varepsilon^2$, deviation scaling as $\sqrt{k}\,\varepsilon$.

Advanced composition falls out of three mechanical RDP steps: no martingales required.

The Gaussian Mechanism
via RDP

Exactly $\big(\alpha,\ \alpha\Delta^2/2\sigma^2\big)$-RDP, by completing a square.

Recall — the Gaussian Mechanism

Recall (Gaussian mechanism).

$\mathcal{M}(X) = f(X) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ where $f$ has $\ell_2$-sensitivity $\Delta = \max_{X \simeq X'} \|f(X) - f(X')\|_2$.

The direct analysis gave $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP for $\varepsilon \lt 1$ whenever

$$\sigma \;\ge\; \frac{\Delta \sqrt{2 \ln(1.25/\delta)}}{\varepsilon}$$

Today: the same mechanism through the RDP lens, where its cost becomes exact and additive.

Dwork, Roth, "The Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy", 2014.

Theorem — Gaussian Mechanism in RDP

Theorem (Mironov, 2017).

The Gaussian mechanism with $\ell_2$-sensitivity $\Delta$ is $\Big( \alpha,\ \dfrac{\alpha \Delta^2}{2\sigma^2} \Big)$-RDP for every $\alpha \gt 1$. In particular,

$$D_{\alpha}\big( \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(\Delta, \sigma^2) \big) \;=\; \frac{\alpha \Delta^2}{2 \sigma^2} \qquad \text{(equality, not just a bound)}$$

Linear in $\alpha$, quadratic in $\Delta/\sigma$: the cleanest privacy cost yet.

Mironov, "Rényi Differential Privacy", CSF 2017.

Intuition — a Gaussian Loss Has a Gaussian MGF

The direct analysis showed the privacy loss is itself Gaussian: $L \sim \mathcal{N}(\eta, 2\eta)$ with $\eta = \frac{\Delta^2}{2\sigma^2}$.

$$(\alpha - 1) D_{\alpha} \;=\; \ln \mathbb{E}_P\big[ e^{(\alpha-1) L} \big] \;=\; (\alpha - 1)\eta + \frac{(\alpha-1)^2}{2} \cdot 2\eta \;=\; \alpha (\alpha - 1)\, \eta$$

Divide by $\alpha - 1$: $\ D_{\alpha} = \alpha \eta = \alpha \Delta^2 / 2\sigma^2$. The linear-in-$\alpha$ law is the Gaussian MGF.

Gaussian MGF: $\ln \mathbb{E}[e^{tZ}] = t\mu + t^2 v/2$ for $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, v)$. Next: a self-contained proof.

Proof — Set Up the Integral

Neighbors give centers $u = f(X)$, $v = f(X')$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$; densities $p, q$ of $\mathcal{N}(u, \sigma^2 I)$, $\mathcal{N}(v, \sigma^2 I)$:

$$\begin{aligned} e^{(\alpha-1) D_{\alpha}(p \| q)} \;&=\; \int p(y)^{\alpha}\, q(y)^{1-\alpha}\, dy \\[4pt] \;&=\; \frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^2)^{d/2}} \int \exp\!\Big( {-\tfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}} \big[ \alpha \|y - u\|^2 + (1-\alpha) \|y - v\|^2 \big] \Big)\, dy \end{aligned}$$

Prefactors merge: $(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d\alpha/2} (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d(1-\alpha)/2} = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d/2}$, one Gaussian's worth.

Proof (continued) — Expand the Exponent

Recap: the exponent holds $\alpha \|y - u\|^2 + (1-\alpha) \|y - v\|^2$. Expand both squares in $y$:

$$\begin{aligned} \alpha \|y - u\|^2 &+ (1 - \alpha) \|y - v\|^2 \\[4pt] \;&=\; \|y\|^2 - 2 \langle y,\ \alpha u + (1-\alpha) v \rangle + \alpha \|u\|^2 + (1-\alpha) \|v\|^2 \end{aligned}$$

The $\|y\|^2$ terms carry total weight $\alpha + (1 - \alpha) = 1$: still a unit quadratic in $y$.

Shorthand for the mixed center: $w = \alpha u + (1 - \alpha) v$.

Proof (continued) — Complete the Square

Recap: exponent $= \|y\|^2 - 2\langle y, w \rangle + \alpha\|u\|^2 + (1-\alpha)\|v\|^2$. Add and subtract $\|w\|^2$:

$$\alpha \|y - u\|^2 + (1-\alpha) \|y - v\|^2 \;=\; \underbrace{\|y - w\|^2}_{\text{a shifted Gaussian in } y} \;+\; \underbrace{\alpha \|u\|^2 + (1-\alpha) \|v\|^2 - \|w\|^2}_{\text{constant: no } y}$$

The $\|y - w\|^2$ part integrates against $(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d/2}$ to exactly $1$: a normalized Gaussian.

Complete-the-square trick: the integral evaluates itself; only the constant survives.

Proof (continued) — the Constant Is the Answer

Recap: everything reduces to the constant. Expand $\|w\|^2 = \|\alpha u + (1-\alpha) v\|^2$ and collect:

$$\begin{aligned} \alpha \|u\|^2 + (1-\alpha) \|v\|^2 - \|w\|^2 \;&=\; \alpha(1 - \alpha) \big( \|u\|^2 - 2 \langle u, v \rangle + \|v\|^2 \big) \\[4pt] \;&=\; \alpha (1 - \alpha)\, \|u - v\|^2 \end{aligned}$$

With the $-\tfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}$ prefactor and $\alpha \gt 1$, the sign flips positive:

$$e^{(\alpha-1) D_{\alpha}} \;=\; \exp\!\Big( \frac{\alpha (\alpha - 1) \|u - v\|^2}{2 \sigma^2} \Big)$$

Proof (continued) — Finish and Recap

Take $\tfrac{1}{\alpha - 1} \ln$ and apply sensitivity $\|u - v\|_2 \le \Delta$:

$$D_{\alpha} \;\stackrel{(\text{def})}{=}\; \tfrac{1}{\alpha-1} \ln \!\int\! p^{\alpha} q^{1-\alpha} \;\stackrel{(\text{square})}{=}\; \frac{1}{\alpha - 1} \cdot \frac{\alpha (\alpha-1) \|u - v\|^2}{2\sigma^2} \;\stackrel{(\text{cancel})}{=}\; \frac{\alpha \|u - v\|^2}{2\sigma^2} \;\stackrel{(\text{sens.})}{\le}\; \frac{\alpha \Delta^2}{2\sigma^2}$$

Equality holds throughout for the worst pair $\|u - v\| = \Delta$: the guarantee is exact. $\blacksquare$

For $d = 1$, $u = 0$, $v = \mu$ this is the two-Gaussian fact quoted in Section 03.

Back to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ — Optimize $\alpha$

$$\varepsilon(\delta) \;=\; \min_{\alpha \gt 1} \Big[ \frac{\alpha \Delta^2}{2\sigma^2} + \frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{\alpha - 1} \Big] \;=\; \frac{\Delta^2}{2\sigma^2} + \frac{\Delta}{\sigma} \sqrt{2 \ln(1/\delta)}$$
total $\varepsilon(\alpha)$ RDP cost $\alpha\Delta^2/2\sigma^2$ conversion $\ln(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$ $\alpha^{\star}$

AM–GM again with $t = \alpha - 1$; the minimizer is $\alpha^{\star} = 1 + (\sigma/\Delta)\sqrt{2 \ln(1/\delta)}$.

Reading It Off — RDP Matches the Classic Rule

Drop the small drift term and solve $\frac{\Delta}{\sigma}\sqrt{2\ln(1/\delta)} = \varepsilon$ for $\sigma$:

$$\sigma \;\approx\; \frac{\Delta}{\varepsilon} \sqrt{2 \ln(1/\delta)} \qquad \text{vs. direct analysis} \qquad \sigma \;\ge\; \frac{\Delta}{\varepsilon} \sqrt{2 \ln(1.25/\delta)}$$

Single release, $\Delta = 1$, $\sigma = 5$, $\delta = 10^{-5}$: direct gives $\varepsilon \approx 0.97$; RDP gives $\varepsilon \approx 0.98$.

One shot is a tie. The payoff is structural: the RDP cost adds across steps.

Recap

  • KL-DP: bounds the average loss $\mathbb{E}_P[L]$; a tiny mean hides catastrophic tails.
  • Rényi divergence: $(\alpha-1) D_{\alpha} = \ln \mathbb{E}_P[e^{(\alpha-1)L}]$; sweeps KL ($\alpha \to 1$) to max ($\alpha \to \infty$).
  • RDP: composes by exact addition; free post-processing; converts at cost $\ln(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)$.
  • Bridge: $\varepsilon$-DP gives $(\alpha, 2\alpha\varepsilon^2)$-RDP; add, convert, optimize: the $\sqrt{k}$ law rederived.
  • Gaussian: exactly $(\alpha, \alpha\Delta^2/2\sigma^2)$-RDP by completing the square; big wins under composition.
