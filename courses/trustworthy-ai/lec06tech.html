01

Calibration Error

From the diagram to one number

The Reliability Diagram

Bin predictions by confidence; plot accuracy against confidence per bin.

Each bar's height gap from the diagonal is that bin's miscalibration.

Expected Calibration Error

Average the per-bin gaps, weighted by how many points land in each bin.

$$\text{ECE} \;=\; \sum_b \frac{n_b}{n}\,\bigl|\,\text{acc}_b - \text{conf}_b\,\bigr|.$$

$n_b$ = points in bin $b$; $\text{acc}_b, \text{conf}_b$ = its accuracy and mean confidence.

Guo, Pleiss, Sun, and Weinberger, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", ICML 2017.

Reading ECE

Each term compares a bin's two numbers.

  • $\text{acc}_b$: fraction of the bin that is correct
  • $\text{conf}_b$: the bin's average stated confidence
  • $n_b / n$: the bin's share of all predictions

$\text{ECE} = 0$ means every bar sits on the diagonal.

Temperature Scaling

Divide the logits by one number $T$ before the softmax.

$$p_i \;=\; \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T)}.$$

$z_i$ = logits; fit $T$ on held-out data to minimize calibration error.

Guo, Pleiss, Sun, and Weinberger, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", ICML 2017.

What Temperature Does

One knob softens or sharpens, without changing which class wins.

  • $T > 1$: flatter probabilities, humbler confidence
  • $T < 1$: sharper probabilities, bolder confidence
  • $T = 1$: leaves the model untouched

The argmax is fixed, so accuracy never changes.

02

Conformal Coverage

A threshold that comes with a proof

The Coverage Guarantee

The prediction set $C(x)$ should contain the true answer with high probability.

$$\Pr[\,y \in C(x)\,] \;\ge\; 1 - \alpha.$$

Choose the miss rate $\alpha$; e.g. $\alpha = 0.10$ gives 90% coverage.

Angelopoulos and Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification", 2021.

Calibrating the Threshold

Score each held-out point by how "surprised" the model is at its true label.

  1. On $n$ calibration points, compute nonconformity scores $s_1, \dots, s_n$.
  2. Set the threshold $\hat q$ to their $\lceil (n{+}1)(1{-}\alpha) \rceil / n$ quantile.
  3. At test time, keep every label whose score is at most $\hat q$.
Angelopoulos and Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification", 2021.

Why It Holds

The test score is exchangeable with the calibration scores.

$$\Pr[\,s_{\text{test}} \le \hat q\,] \;\ge\; 1 - \alpha.$$

Its rank is uniform among $n{+}1$ scores, so it falls below $\hat q$ with the right frequency.

Angelopoulos and Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification", 2021.

Semantic Entropy

Sample answers, cluster by meaning, take entropy over meaning clusters.

$$\text{SE} \;=\; -\sum_{c} p(c)\,\log p(c), \qquad p(c) = \sum_{a \in c} p(a).$$

$c$ = a cluster of same-meaning answers; high SE flags likely hallucination.

Farquhar et al., "Detecting Hallucinations in LLMs Using Semantic Entropy", Nature 2024.
