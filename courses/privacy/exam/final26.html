Exact Score and Denoiser of a Two-Point Data Distribution.02 · diffusion (30 pts) Let the data be the fair two-point distribution $X_0 \in \{+1, -1\}$, $\Pr[X_0 = +1] = \Pr[X_0 = -1] = \tfrac12$. Fix one diffusion noise level and form the forward sample $Y = a\,X_0 + \sqrt{v}\,\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ independent of $X_0$, where $a = \sqrt{\bar\alpha} \in (0,1)$ and $v = 1-\bar\alpha$. Throughout, write the Gaussian density, the logistic sigmoid, and the hyperbolic tangent as $$f(x;\mu,\sigma^2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Bigr), \qquad \sigma(t) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-t}}, \qquad \tanh(t) = \frac{e^{t}-e^{-t}}{e^{t}+e^{-t}}.$$
    1. (5 pts) Show that the marginal density of $Y$ is the two-component mixture $p(y) = \tfrac12\,f(y; a, v) + \tfrac12\,f(y; -a, v)$.
    2. (5 pts) Let $r_+(y) = \Pr[X_0 = +1 \mid Y = y]$. Show $r_+(y) = \sigma\!\bigl(\tfrac{2a}{v}y\bigr)$, and hence the optimal (posterior-mean) denoiser is $\mathbb{E}[X_0 \mid Y = y] = \tanh\!\bigl(\tfrac{a}{v}\,y\bigr)$. $r_+/r_- = f(y;a,v)/f(y;-a,v) = e^{2ay/v}$; then $\mathbb{E}[X_0 \mid y] = r_+ - r_-$.
    3. (10 pts) Show the exact score is $\partial_y \log p(y) = -\tfrac{y}{v} + \tfrac{a}{v}\tanh\!\bigl(\tfrac{a}{v}\,y\bigr)$, and verify consistency with (b) via the denoiser–score identity $\mathbb{E}[X_0 \mid Y = y] = \tfrac{1}{a}\bigl(y + v\,\partial_y \log p(y)\bigr)$ (which you should also prove).
    4. (10 pts) Show separately that the denoiser $\mathbb{E}[X_0 \mid Y = y]$ converges to $\mathrm{sign}(y)$ as $\bar\alpha \to 1$, and converges to $0$ as $\bar\alpha \to 0$. Interpret each limit.
    Solution.
    1. Conditioning on the bit, $Y \mid (X_0 = +1) \sim \mathcal{N}(a, v)$ and $Y \mid (X_0 = -1) \sim \mathcal{N}(-a, v)$, each with prior weight $\tfrac12$. By the law of total probability, $$p(y) = \tfrac12\,f(y; a, v) + \tfrac12\,f(y; -a, v).$$
    2. By Bayes' rule the two posteriors share the $\tfrac12$ prior and the normalizer $p(y)$, so $$\frac{r_+(y)}{r_-(y)} = \frac{f(y;a,v)}{f(y;-a,v)} = \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{-(y-a)^2 + (y+a)^2}{2v}\Bigr) = \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{2ay}{v}\Bigr).$$ Since $r_+ + r_- = 1$, $r_+(y) = \dfrac{e^{2ay/v}}{1 + e^{2ay/v}} = \sigma\!\bigl(\tfrac{2a}{v}y\bigr)$ and $r_-(y) = \sigma\!\bigl(-\tfrac{2a}{v}y\bigr)$. The posterior mean is $$\mathbb{E}[X_0 \mid Y = y] = (+1)\,r_+ + (-1)\,r_- = \sigma\!\bigl(\tfrac{2a}{v}y\bigr) - \sigma\!\bigl(-\tfrac{2a}{v}y\bigr) = \tanh\!\Bigl(\frac{a}{v}\,y\Bigr),$$ using $\sigma(2z) - \sigma(-2z) = \dfrac{e^{2z}-1}{e^{2z}+1} = \tanh z$ with $z = ay/v$.
    3. Differentiating the mixture, $$p'(y) = \tfrac12\,f(y;a,v)\Bigl(-\tfrac{y-a}{v}\Bigr) + \tfrac12\,f(y;-a,v)\Bigl(-\tfrac{y+a}{v}\Bigr).$$ Dividing by $p(y)$ and recognizing $r_\pm$, $$\partial_y \log p(y) = -\tfrac{1}{v}\bigl[r_+(y-a) + r_-(y+a)\bigr] = -\tfrac{1}{v}\bigl[y - a(r_+ - r_-)\bigr] = -\frac{y}{v} + \frac{a}{v}\tanh\!\Bigl(\frac{a}{v}y\Bigr).$$ Identity. For $Y = aX_0 + \sqrt v\,\varepsilon$ with Gaussian noise, $p(y) = \int p_{X_0}(x)\,f(y; ax, v)\,dx$, so $$\partial_y p(y) = \int p_{X_0}(x)\Bigl(-\tfrac{y-ax}{v}\Bigr)f(y; ax, v)\,dx = -\tfrac{y}{v}p(y) + \tfrac{a}{v}\,p(y)\,\mathbb{E}[X_0 \mid Y=y].$$ Dividing by $p(y)$ gives $\partial_y \log p = -\tfrac{y}{v} + \tfrac{a}{v}\mathbb{E}[X_0\mid y]$, i.e. $\mathbb{E}[X_0\mid y] = \tfrac1a(y + v\,\partial_y\log p)$. Substituting our score back reproduces $\tanh(ay/v)$, matching (b).
    4. Recall $a = \sqrt{\bar\alpha}$, $v = 1-\bar\alpha$, so $a/v = \sqrt{\bar\alpha}/(1-\bar\alpha)$.
      Vanishing noise $\bar\alpha \to 1$: $a \to 1$, $v \to 0^+$, so $a/v \to +\infty$ and for every $y \ne 0$, $\tanh(ay/v) \to \mathrm{sign}(y)$. The denoiser snaps to the nearest clean value $\pm1$ — the bit is recoverable because almost no noise was added.
      Saturating noise $\bar\alpha \to 0$: $a \to 0$, so $\tanh(ay/v) \to 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_0\mid y] \to 0 = \mathbb{E}[X_0]$: the observation carries no information, so the best estimate is the prior mean. Correspondingly the score collapses to $-y/v$, exactly the score of the pure-noise Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(0,v)$.
  2. LiRA Closed Form and Decision Boundary.04 · MIA (30 pts) For a target record $z$, the per-shadow-model loss is Gaussian under the two membership hypotheses: $$\ell \mid (z \in D) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathrm{in}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2), \qquad \ell \mid (z \notin D) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^2).$$
    1. (10 pts) (Warm-up.) Derive the log-likelihood ratio $$\log \Lambda(\ell) = \frac{(\ell - \mu_{\mathrm{out}})^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^2} - \frac{(\ell - \mu_{\mathrm{in}})^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2} + \log\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}}.$$ Subtract the two log-densities $\log\mathcal{N}(\ell;\mu,\sigma^2) = -\tfrac{(\ell-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} - \tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)$.
    2. (10 pts) For $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} \ne \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$, show the region $\{\log\Lambda > \log\tau\}$ is a bounded interval, its complement, a half-line, all of $\mathbb{R}$, or empty, and say which from the sign of $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} - \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$. For equal variances $\sigma = \sigma_{\mathrm{in}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$ (and $\mu_{\mathrm{in}} < \mu_{\mathrm{out}}$), show it becomes a threshold $\mathcal{A}(z) = \mathbf{1}[\ell \le c]$ and find $c$. $\log\Lambda$ is a quadratic $A\ell^2 + B\ell + C'$ with $A = \tfrac12(\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^{-2} - \sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{-2})$; the sign of $A$ (concave / convex / linear) sets the shape.
    3. (10 pts) With equal variances, derive the ROC: at false-positive rate $\alpha$, $\mathrm{TPR}(\alpha) = \Phi\!\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(\alpha) + \tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{out}} - \mu_{\mathrm{in}}}{\sigma}\bigr)$, where $\Phi(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{t}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-s^2/2}\,ds$ is the standard normal CDF. Solve $\mathrm{FPR}(c) = \Phi(\tfrac{c-\mu_{\mathrm{out}}}{\sigma}) = \alpha$ for $c$, then substitute into $\mathrm{TPR}(c) = \Phi(\tfrac{c-\mu_{\mathrm{in}}}{\sigma})$.
    Solution.
    1. Using $\log\mathcal{N}(\ell;\mu,\sigma^2) = -\tfrac{(\ell-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} - \tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)$ and subtracting the out-density log from the in-density log, the $-\tfrac12\log(2\pi)$ terms cancel and $$\log\Lambda(\ell) = -\frac{(\ell-\mu_{\mathrm{in}})^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2} + \frac{(\ell-\mu_{\mathrm{out}})^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^2} + \tfrac12\log\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^2}{\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2},$$ which is the claimed expression.
    2. Collecting powers of $\ell$, $\log\Lambda(\ell) = A\ell^2 + B\ell + C'$ with $$A = \frac12\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^2} - \frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2}\Bigr).$$ Note $A < 0 \iff \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^{-2} < \sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{-2} \iff \sigma_{\mathrm{in}} < \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$, so $\mathrm{sign}(A) = \mathrm{sign}(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} - \sigma_{\mathrm{out}})$. The region $\{A\ell^2 + B\ell + (C' - \log\tau) > 0\}$ is:
      • $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} < \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$ ($A < 0$, downward parabola): a bounded interval $(c_-, c_+)$ (members are concentrated, so the LR is large only over a middle band) — degenerating to empty if the parabola's max is below $\log\tau$;
      • $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} > \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$ ($A > 0$, upward parabola): the complement of a bounded interval, $(-\infty,c_-)\cup(c_+,\infty)$ — degenerating to all of $\mathbb{R}$ if the min exceeds $\log\tau$;
      • $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$ ($A = 0$, affine): a half-line.
      Equal variances. With $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{out}} = \sigma$ the quadratic terms cancel: $$\log\Lambda(\ell) = \frac{(\ell-\mu_{\mathrm{out}})^2 - (\ell-\mu_{\mathrm{in}})^2}{2\sigma^2} = \frac{(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}})(\mu_{\mathrm{in}}+\mu_{\mathrm{out}} - 2\ell)}{2\sigma^2}.$$ Since $\mu_{\mathrm{out}} > \mu_{\mathrm{in}}$, the coefficient of $\ell$ is $-(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}})/\sigma^2 < 0$, so $\log\Lambda$ is strictly decreasing in $\ell$. Hence $\log\Lambda(\ell) \ge \log\tau \iff \ell \le c$ where $$c = \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{in}}+\mu_{\mathrm{out}}}{2} - \frac{\sigma^2\log\tau}{\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}}},$$ i.e. $\mathcal{A}(z) = \mathbf{1}[\ell \le c]$. (Low loss $\Rightarrow$ member, as expected.)
    3. For the threshold test $\mathcal{A} = \mathbf{1}[\ell \le c]$, $$\mathrm{FPR}(c) = \Pr_{\mathrm{out}}[\ell \le c] = \Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{c-\mu_{\mathrm{out}}}{\sigma}\Bigr).$$ Setting $\mathrm{FPR} = \alpha$ gives $c = \mu_{\mathrm{out}} + \sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)$. Then $$\mathrm{TPR}(\alpha) = \Pr_{\mathrm{in}}[\ell \le c] = \Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{c-\mu_{\mathrm{in}}}{\sigma}\Bigr) = \Phi\!\Bigl(\Phi^{-1}(\alpha) + \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}}}{\sigma}\Bigr).$$ The whole ROC is governed by the standardized gap $(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}})/\sigma$; it is $\ge \alpha$ for all $\alpha$ (with equality iff the gap is $0$), confirming the threshold test is informative exactly when the in/out means separate.

Why NPO's Gradient Stays Bounded and GA's Does Not.05 · unlearning (40 pts) For LLM unlearning let $\pi_\theta(y\mid x) \in (0,1]$ be smooth in $\theta$, with $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x) \in (0,1]$ a fixed reference. On the forget set $\mathcal{D}_F$ the two losses are $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GA}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_F}\bigl[\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\bigr], \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}}(\theta) = \frac{2}{\beta}\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_F}\!\left[\log\!\Bigl(1 + \bigl(\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\bigr)^{\beta}\Bigr)\right]\quad(\beta>0),$$ where $\sigma(t) = 1/(1+e^{-t})$ is the logistic sigmoid. Assume the probability gradient is bounded, $\|\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\| \le G$. (You may differentiate finite sums and expectations term by term without further justification.)
    1. (10 pts) Let $u(\theta) = \beta\log\bigl(\pi_\theta(y\mid x)/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)\bigr)$. Show that $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}}(\theta) = \tfrac{2}{\beta}\,\mathbb{E}[\log(1+e^{u})], \qquad \tfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\log(1+e^u) = \sigma(u) \in (0,1),$$ and that $\nabla_\theta u = \beta\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta$. $(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})^\beta = e^u$; $\tfrac{d}{du}\log(1+e^u) = e^u/(1+e^u) = \sigma(u)$, and $0<\sigma<1$ since $0<e^u<1+e^u$; and $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is constant in $\theta$.
    2. (10 pts) Using part (a) and the chain rule, show $\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}} = 2\,\mathbb{E}[\sigma(u)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta]$, and conclude the uniform bound $$\|\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}}\| \le 2\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta\|\bigr].$$ Differentiate $\tfrac{2}{\beta}\log(1+e^u)$ with $\nabla_\theta u = \beta\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta$; then use the triangle inequality $\|\mathbb{E}[V]\| \le \mathbb{E}[\|V\|]$ together with $\sigma(u)<1$.
    3. (10 pts) Define the per-example NPO contribution $g_{\mathrm{NPO}} := \sigma(u)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta$. Show that $$\|g_{\mathrm{NPO}}\| = \sigma(u)\,\frac{\|\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta\|}{\pi_\theta}, \qquad \sigma(u) = \frac{(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})^\beta}{1+(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})^\beta},$$ and hence that as $\pi_\theta(y\mid x)\to 0$, $\;\|g_{\mathrm{NPO}}\| \le \dfrac{G}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}^\beta}\,\pi_\theta^{\beta-1}$. $\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta = \nabla_\theta\pi_\theta/\pi_\theta$; bound the denominator $1 + (\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})^\beta \ge 1$ to get $\sigma(u) \le (\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})^\beta$; combine with $\|\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta\| \le G$.
    4. (10 pts) Using part (c), show that as $\pi_\theta \to 0$ the NPO per-example gradient $\|g_{\mathrm{NPO}}\|$ stays bounded for $\beta \ge 1$, while the GA per-example gradient $\|\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta\|$ diverges. Why does this make NPO stable but GA collapse? Read off the limits of $\pi_\theta^{\beta-1}$ from part (c); the GA norm is $\|\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta\|/\pi_\theta$, whose numerator stays bounded while $\pi_\theta\to0$.
    Solution.
    1. Since $(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})^\beta = e^{\beta\log(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})} = e^{u}$, the loss is $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}} = \tfrac{2}{\beta}\mathbb{E}[\log(1+e^u)]$. Differentiating, $\tfrac{d}{du}\log(1+e^u) = \tfrac{e^u}{1+e^u} = \sigma(u)$, and $0 < \sigma(u) < 1$ because $0 < e^u < 1+e^u$ for all finite $u$. Finally $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ does not depend on $\theta$, so $u = \beta(\log\pi_\theta - \log\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$ gives $\nabla_\theta u = \beta\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta$.
    2. By the chain rule (differentiating term by term), $\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}} = \tfrac{2}{\beta}\,\mathbb{E}[\sigma(u)\,\nabla_\theta u] = \tfrac{2}{\beta}\,\mathbb{E}[\sigma(u)\,\beta\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta] = 2\,\mathbb{E}[\sigma(u)\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta]$. Taking norms and using the triangle inequality $\|\mathbb{E}[V]\| \le \mathbb{E}[\|V\|]$ with $V = 2\sigma(u)\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta$, together with $\sigma(u) < 1$, $$\|\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}}\| \le 2\,\mathbb{E}[\sigma(u)\,\|\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta\|] \le 2\,\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta\|].$$ This bound holds for every $\theta$ — it is uniform.
    3. From $g_{\mathrm{NPO}} = \sigma(u)\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta$ and $\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta = \nabla_\theta\pi_\theta/\pi_\theta$ we get $\|g_{\mathrm{NPO}}\| = \sigma(u)\,\|\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta\|/\pi_\theta$. Writing $\rho := (\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})^\beta = e^u$, we have $\sigma(u) = \rho/(1+\rho)$. As $\pi_\theta\to0$, $\rho\to0$, and $1+\rho\ge1$ gives $\sigma(u) \le \rho = (\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})^\beta$. Combined with $\|\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta\| \le G$, $$\|g_{\mathrm{NPO}}\| \le \frac{(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})^\beta\,G}{\pi_\theta} = \frac{G}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}^\beta}\,\pi_\theta^{\beta-1}.$$
    4. From part (c): for $\beta > 1$, $\pi_\theta^{\beta-1}\to0$ so $\|g_{\mathrm{NPO}}\|\to0$; for $\beta = 1$, $\|g_{\mathrm{NPO}}\| \le G/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, a finite constant. By contrast the GA per-example contribution has norm $\|\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta\|/\pi_\theta \sim 1/\pi_\theta \to \infty$ as $\pi_\theta\to0$ (the numerator stays bounded while $\pi_\theta\to0$). Mechanism: as the forget likelihood $\pi_\theta\to0$ the saturating factor $\sigma(u)\to0$ at rate $\pi_\theta^\beta$, which cancels (for $\beta\ge1$) the $1/\pi_\theta$ growth of the log-likelihood gradient — that is why "sigmoid saturation" keeps the NPO step bounded and prevents the GA-style collapse.
