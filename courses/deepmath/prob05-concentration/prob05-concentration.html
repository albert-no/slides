The Gamble Behind Every Average

What we can compute vs. what we want:

$$\underbrace{\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i}_{\text{computable, random}} \qquad \text{vs.} \qquad \underbrace{\mu = \mathbb{E}[X]}_{\text{wanted, fixed}}$$
  • Report $\bar{X}_n$, hope it is near $\mu$
  • Hope is not an argument: $\bar{X}_n$ is random

Today's question: how likely is $\bar{X}_n$ to be far from $\mu$?

How Many Samples Do I Need?

Concrete engineering question:

$$\Pr\!\left( |\bar{X}_n - \mu| \geq 0.01 \right) \leq 0.05 \quad \text{needs} \quad n \geq \; ?$$
  • Estimate a model's accuracy within $\pm 1\%$
  • Confidence $95\%$: wrong at most once in $20$

Today answers this twice: a safe answer, then a much cheaper one.

02

Warm-Up

Mean, variance, independence

Recall — Expectation

Recall (expectation). For a random variable $X$ with pmf $p_X$:

$$\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_x x\, p_X(x), \qquad \mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \sum_x g(x)\, p_X(x) \;\;\text{(LOTUS)}$$
  • Linearity: $\mathbb{E}[aX + bY] = a\,\mathbb{E}[X] + b\,\mathbb{E}[Y]$, always
  • Monotonicity: $X \leq Y$ pointwise $\Rightarrow$ $\mathbb{E}[X] \leq \mathbb{E}[Y]$

Monotonicity does today's heavy lifting: bound the variable, bound its mean.

Recall — Variance

Recall (variance). Spread around the mean $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X]$:

$$\mathrm{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[(X-\mu)^2\right] = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - \mu^2, \qquad \sigma = \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(X)}$$
  • Scaling: $\mathrm{Var}(aX) = a^2\,\mathrm{Var}(X)$
  • Bernoulli($p$): $\;\mathbb{E}[X^2] = p$, so $\mathrm{Var}(X) = p(1-p)$

Fair coin ($p = \tfrac12$): variance $\tfrac14$, the largest a coin can have.

Recall — Independence

Recall (independence). $X \perp Y$ when the joint pmf factorizes:

$$p_{X,Y}(x,y) = p_X(x)\, p_Y(y) \quad \text{for all } x, y$$

Consequence we need today — products of expectations:

$$\mathbb{E}[g(X)\, h(Y)] = \mathbb{E}[g(X)]\; \mathbb{E}[h(Y)]$$

Any function of $X$ times any function of $Y$. Proof: next slide.

Product Rule — One-Slide Proof

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[g(X)h(Y)] &\stackrel{(\text{LOTUS})}{=} \sum_{x,y} g(x)h(y)\, p_{X,Y}(x,y) \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{indep.})}{=} \sum_{x,y} g(x)h(y)\, p_X(x)\, p_Y(y) \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{factor})}{=} \Big(\sum_x g(x) p_X(x)\Big)\Big(\sum_y h(y) p_Y(y)\Big) = \mathbb{E}[g(X)]\,\mathbb{E}[h(Y)] \;\; \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

The double sum splits because the joint pmf splits.

Variance Adds Under Independence

Center first: $\tilde{X} = X - \mu_X$, $\tilde{Y} = Y - \mu_Y$, both mean $0$

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Var}(X+Y) &\stackrel{(\text{def})}{=} \mathbb{E}\big[(\tilde{X} + \tilde{Y})^2\big] = \mathbb{E}[\tilde{X}^2] + 2\,\mathbb{E}[\tilde{X}\tilde{Y}] + \mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}^2] \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{product rule})}{=} \mathrm{Var}(X) + 2\,\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\tilde{X}]\,\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}]}_{=\,0} + \mathrm{Var}(Y) \end{aligned}$$

Independent noise adds in variance, not in size: the cross term dies.

Running Example — $n$ Coin Flips

Fair coins $X_1, \dots, X_n$ i.i.d. Bernoulli($\tfrac12$); their average:

$$\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i, \qquad \mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_n] \stackrel{(\text{linearity})}{=} \tfrac12$$

One tail question, asked to every bound today:

$$\Pr\!\left(\bar{X}_n \geq \tfrac34\right) \;=\; ?$$

At least $75\%$ heads from a fair coin: how unlikely, as a function of $n$?

Why Not Just Compute It?

For coins the tail is an exact binomial sum:

$$\Pr\!\left(\bar{X}_n \geq \tfrac34\right) = \sum_{k \geq 3n/4} \binom{n}{k} 2^{-n}$$
  • Works only because we know the full distribution
  • Real losses / accuracies: distribution unknown

Goal: bounds from partial knowledge only — a mean, a variance, an MGF.

03

Markov's Inequality

One number in, one bound out

Technique — Indicator Variables

Turn an event into a random variable:

$$\mathbf{1}\{A\} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } A \text{ happens} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

Its mean is the probability:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}\{A\}\big] = 1 \cdot \Pr(A) + 0 \cdot \Pr(A^c) = \Pr(A)$$

Bounding a probability = bounding the mean of an indicator.

Technique — The Indicator Trick

$a$ $a$ $x$ $y = x$ $y = a\,\mathbf{1}\{x \geq a\}$

Blue $y = x$ always sits above gray $y = a \cdot \mathbf{1}\{x \geq a\}$ (for $x \geq 0$)

A cheap step function hides under the variable. Take expectations next.

Theorem — Markov's Inequality

Theorem 1 (Markov's inequality).

If $X$ is a non-negative random variable, then for any $\alpha \gt 0$:

$$\Pr\!\big( X \geq \alpha\, \mathbb{E}[X] \big) \;\leq\; \frac{1}{\alpha}$$

Exceeding $\alpha$ times your mean: probability at most $1/\alpha$.

Markov — Threshold Form

Equivalent statement, for any absolute level $a \gt 0$:

$$\Pr(X \geq a) \;\leq\; \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{a}$$
  • Substitute $a = \alpha\,\mathbb{E}[X]$ to move between the two forms
  • $\alpha$-form: level in units of the mean; $a$-form: an absolute level

We prove the $a$-form; proofs below use whichever is cleaner.

Markov — Proof Overview

Target: $\Pr(X \geq a) \leq \mathbb{E}[X] / a$ for fixed $a \gt 0$

1Dominatethe pointwise bound $a\,\mathbf{1}\{X \geq a\} \leq X$
2Averagetake $\mathbb{E}$ on both sides (monotonicity)
3Read off$\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}] = \Pr$, divide by $a$

Three lines. Non-negativity is used exactly once — watch for it.

Markov Proof — Step 1: Dominate

Claim: for every outcome, $\;a\,\mathbf{1}\{X \geq a\} \leq X$

$$\begin{aligned} \text{if } X \geq a: &\quad a \cdot 1 = a \;\leq\; X &&\text{(the event itself)} \\[4pt] \text{if } X \lt a: &\quad a \cdot 0 = 0 \;\leq\; X &&\text{(needs } X \geq 0\text{!)} \end{aligned}$$

Non-negativity enters here and only here: the zero branch must stay below $X$.

Markov Proof — Step 2: Average

Pointwise inequality + monotonicity of $\mathbb{E}$ (Warm-Up):

$$\begin{aligned} a\,\mathbf{1}\{X \geq a\} \;\leq\; X \quad &\stackrel{(\text{monotone } \mathbb{E})}{\Longrightarrow} \quad \mathbb{E}\big[a\,\mathbf{1}\{X \geq a\}\big] \;\leq\; \mathbb{E}[X] \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{linearity})}{\Longrightarrow} \quad a\; \mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}\{X \geq a\}\big] \;\leq\; \mathbb{E}[X] \end{aligned}$$

An inequality between random variables becomes one between numbers.

Markov Proof — Step 3: Read Off

Indicator mean = probability, then divide by $a \gt 0$:

$$a \Pr(X \geq a) \leq \mathbb{E}[X] \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Pr(X \geq a) \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{a} \qquad \blacksquare$$

The lecture-note form: substitute $a = \alpha\,\mathbb{E}[X]$

$$\Pr\!\big(X \geq \alpha\,\mathbb{E}[X]\big) \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{\alpha\,\mathbb{E}[X]} = \frac{1}{\alpha}$$

Markov — Proof Summary

$$a \Pr(X \geq a) \stackrel{(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}] = \Pr)}{=} \mathbb{E}\big[a\,\mathbf{1}\{X \geq a\}\big] \stackrel{(\text{dominate})}{\leq} \mathbb{E}[X]$$
  • The trick: hide a step function under the variable
  • Weight carrier: monotonicity of expectation

Every bound today reruns this one argument on a transformed variable.

Coin Flips — Markov's Answer

$\bar{X}_n \geq 0$ with mean $\tfrac12$; threshold $\tfrac34 = \tfrac32 \cdot \tfrac12$, so $\alpha = \tfrac32$:

$$\Pr\!\left(\bar{X}_n \geq \tfrac34\right) \;\leq\; \frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_n]}{3/4} = \frac{1/2}{3/4} = \frac{2}{3}$$
  • Valid for every $n$: 1 flip or 1 million
  • And that is the problem: the bound never improves

$n$ does not appear: Markov cannot see averaging at all.

Markov Is Tight — Blame the Input

Some variable with mean $\mu$ achieves the bound at threshold $a$:

$$X = \begin{cases} a & \text{w.p. } \mu / a \\ 0 & \text{w.p. } 1 - \mu/a \end{cases} \qquad \mathbb{E}[X] = \mu, \qquad \Pr(X \geq a) = \frac{\mu}{a}$$
  • Knowing only the mean, no better bound exists
  • Sharper bounds must feed in more than the mean

Illustration beyond the lecture notes: why the fix is more input, not more cleverness.

Markov — What It Buys

  • Cost: one number ($\mathbb{E}[X]$) + non-negativity
  • Non-negativity is essential: for $X = \pm 1$ fair, $\mathbb{E}[X] = 0$, yet $\Pr(X \geq 1) = \tfrac12$
  • Rarely used raw: used as the engine inside better bounds

Next: run Markov on the squared deviation — variance enters.

04

Chebyshev's Inequality

Variance enters, tails shrink

Idea — Bound Distance, Not Size

The real question is two-sided: how far from the mean?

$$\Pr\big( |X - \mu| \geq a \big) \;=\; ?$$
  • $|X - \mu|$ is not the variable Markov likes: no mean handy
  • But $(X - \mu)^2$ is non-negative with known mean: $\sigma^2$

Escalation step 1: apply Markov to the transformed variable $(X-\mu)^2$.

Technique — Squaring the Event

For non-negative quantities, squaring keeps order:

$$|X - \mu| \geq a \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (X-\mu)^2 \geq a^2 \qquad (a \gt 0)$$
  • Same event, two descriptions — so same probability
  • Right side: a tail of the non-negative variable $(X-\mu)^2$

Rewriting the event is free; it just changes which variable Markov sees.

Theorem — Chebyshev's Inequality

Theorem 2 (Chebyshev's inequality).

Let $X$ have mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$. For any $\alpha \gt 0$:

$$\Pr\!\big( |X - \mu| \geq \alpha \sigma \big) \;\leq\; \frac{1}{\alpha^2}$$

Equivalent $\epsilon$-form, for any $\epsilon \gt 0$:   $\Pr\!\big( |X - \mu| \geq \epsilon \big) \leq \sigma^2 / \epsilon^2$

No non-negativity assumption on $X$: the square provides it.

Chebyshev — Proof Overview

Target: $\Pr(|X - \mu| \geq \alpha\sigma) \leq 1/\alpha^2$

1Squarethe event: $|X-\mu| \geq \alpha\sigma \iff (X-\mu)^2 \geq \alpha^2\sigma^2$
2Markovon $Y = (X-\mu)^2$: non-negative, mean $\sigma^2$
3Simplify$\sigma^2$ cancels

All new work is in step 1; step 2 is Theorem 1 verbatim.

Chebyshev Proof — Step 1: Square

$$\Pr\!\big( |X - \mu| \geq \alpha\sigma \big) \stackrel{(\text{same event})}{=} \Pr\!\big( (X-\mu)^2 \geq \alpha^2 \sigma^2 \big)$$

Set $Y = (X - \mu)^2$ and check Markov's entry ticket:

  • $Y \geq 0$  ✓ (a square)
  • $\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^2] = \sigma^2$  ✓ (definition of variance)

Chebyshev Proof — Step 2: Markov

Theorem 1 (threshold form) on $Y$ at level $a = \alpha^2\sigma^2$:

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr\!\big( Y \geq \alpha^2\sigma^2 \big) &\stackrel{(\text{Markov})}{\leq} \frac{\mathbb{E}[Y]}{\alpha^2 \sigma^2} \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\mathbb{E}[Y] = \sigma^2)}{=} \frac{\sigma^2}{\alpha^2\sigma^2} \;=\; \frac{1}{\alpha^2} \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

The $\epsilon$-form: same steps with $a = \epsilon^2$, giving $\sigma^2/\epsilon^2$.

Chebyshev — Proof Summary

$$\Pr\!\big(|X-\mu| \geq \alpha\sigma\big) \stackrel{(\text{square})}{=} \Pr\!\big((X-\mu)^2 \geq \alpha^2\sigma^2\big) \stackrel{(\text{Markov})}{\leq} \frac{1}{\alpha^2}$$
  • The trick: choose the variable Markov acts on
  • Squaring buys two things: two-sidedness and decay $1/\alpha^2$

Chebyshev is not a new proof — it is Markov with a better input.

Reading Chebyshev — The $\alpha\sigma$ Rule

Distribution-free tail budget, any $X$ with finite variance:

DeviationChebyshev boundGaussian actual
$\geq 2\sigma$$1/4 = 25\%$$\approx 4.6\%$
$\geq 3\sigma$$1/9 \approx 11.1\%$$\approx 0.27\%$
$\geq 5\sigma$$1/25 = 4\%$$\approx 0.00006\%$

Worst case over all distributions vs. one nice distribution: a big gap. Remember it.

Key Computation — Variance of $\bar{X}_n$

$X_1, \dots, X_n$ i.i.d., mean $\mu$, variance $\sigma^2$:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Var}(\bar{X}_n) &\stackrel{(\text{scaling})}{=} \frac{1}{n^2}\,\mathrm{Var}\!\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\Big) \stackrel{(\text{indep: adds})}{=} \frac{1}{n^2} \cdot n \sigma^2 = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \end{aligned}$$

Averaging divides variance by $n$: the single engine behind LLN, error bars, minibatches.

Coin Flips — Chebyshev's Answer

$\mathrm{Var}(\bar{X}_n) = \frac{1/4}{n}$; the one-sided tail sits inside the two-sided one:

$$\Pr\!\left(\bar{X}_n \geq \tfrac34\right) \;\leq\; \Pr\!\left(|\bar{X}_n - \tfrac12| \geq \tfrac14\right) \;\stackrel{(\epsilon\text{-form})}{\leq}\; \frac{1/(4n)}{(1/4)^2} = \frac{4}{n}$$
$n$16100400
bound $4/n$$0.25$$0.04$$0.01$

Finally a bound that improves with $n$.

Markov vs. Chebyshev — Picture

$1$ $0$ $n$ flips Markov: $2/3$ forever Chebyshev: $4/n$

Same question, same coin: variance turns a constant into a vanishing bound

Chebyshev — How Many Samples?

Demand $\Pr(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| \geq \epsilon) \leq \delta$; Chebyshev delivers when:

$$\frac{\sigma^2}{n\epsilon^2} \leq \delta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n \;\geq\; \frac{\sigma^2}{\epsilon^2 \delta}$$

Coin-accuracy demand ($\sigma^2 = \tfrac14$, $\epsilon = 0.01$, $\delta = 0.05$):

$$n \;\geq\; \frac{1/4}{(0.01)^2 (0.05)} = 50{,}000$$

Safe, but expensive. Chernoff will cut this by almost $3\times$.

05

Law of Large Numbers

$\bar{X}_n \to \mu$, with a proof

Definition — Convergence in Probability

Definition (convergence in probability).

$Z_n \to c$ in probability if for every tolerance $\epsilon \gt 0$:

$$\Pr\big( |Z_n - c| \gt \epsilon \big) \;\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$
  • Fix the band $[c - \epsilon,\, c + \epsilon]$ first, then grow $n$
  • Mass escaping the band $\to 0$, for every band width

Convergence in Probability — Picture

mean $\mu$ band small $n$ larger $n$ large $n$

Distribution of $\bar{X}_n$ tightens; mass outside the fixed band $\to 0$

Theorem — Weak Law of Large Numbers

Theorem 3 (weak law of large numbers).

$X_1, X_2, \dots$ i.i.d. with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2 \lt \infty$. Then:

$$\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \;\longrightarrow\; \mu \quad \text{in probability}$$

A strong LLN also holds (probability-1 convergence); stated without proof, as in the notes.

Weak LLN — Proof Overview

Target: $\Pr(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| \gt \epsilon) \to 0$ for every fixed $\epsilon \gt 0$

1Center & shrink$\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_n] = \mu$, $\;\mathrm{Var}(\bar{X}_n) = \sigma^2/n$
2Chebyshevon $\bar{X}_n$ with the fixed $\epsilon$
3Let $n$ growthe bound vanishes

The whole theorem rides on one fact: averaging divides variance by $n$.

Weak LLN — Proof

Fix $\epsilon \gt 0$. Chebyshev ($\epsilon$-form) applied to $\bar{X}_n$:

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr\big( |\bar{X}_n - \mu| \gt \epsilon \big) &\stackrel{(\text{Chebyshev})}{\leq} \frac{\mathrm{Var}(\bar{X}_n)}{\epsilon^2} \stackrel{(\text{Var} = \sigma^2/n)}{=} \frac{\sigma^2}{n\,\epsilon^2} \\[4pt] &\longrightarrow\; 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

$\epsilon$ and $\sigma$ are fixed constants here; only $n$ moves.

Weak LLN — Proof Summary

$$\Pr\big(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| \gt \epsilon\big) \;\stackrel{(\text{Chebyshev})}{\leq}\; \frac{\sigma^2}{n \epsilon^2} \;\stackrel{(n \to \infty)}{\longrightarrow}\; 0$$
  • Key trick: none new — Chebyshev + variance $\sigma^2/n$
  • Bonus: not just convergence, a rate: $\sigma^2 / (n\epsilon^2)$

Averages are trustworthy because averaging destroys variance.

Seeing the LLN — One Sample Path

$1/2$ $1$ $0$ $n$ (1 to 200)

Running mean $\bar{X}_n$ of 200 simulated fair flips: wild early, glued to $\tfrac12$ late

Simulated once with a fixed seed; band = $\tfrac12 \pm 0.05$. Checkpoints: $\bar{X}_{10} = 0.30$, $\bar{X}_{200} = 0.49$.

Seeing the LLN — Tails Actually Vanish

$1$ $0$ $n$ flips Chebyshev bound $4/n$ exact tail

$\Pr(|\bar{X}_n - \tfrac12| \geq \tfrac14)$, exact, at $n = 4, 8, \dots, 24$:  $0.63 \to 0.29 \to 0.15 \to 0.08 \to 0.04 \to 0.02$

Bound valid everywhere — but the truth falls much faster. Foreshadowing.

LLN — Where AI Leans on It

  • Test accuracy stabilizes as the test set grows: mean of 0/1 variables
  • Minibatch gradients: batch mean $\to$ true gradient; std shrinks like $1/\sqrt{B}$
  • Monte Carlo: sample mean $\to$ expectation, with rate $\sigma^2/(n\epsilon^2)$

Every "estimate by sampling" recipe is the weak LLN wearing a different hat.

Illustrations, not new theorems: each is Theorem 3 applied to a different $X_i$.

The Gap — Truth Beats the Bound

At $n = 16$, one-sided tail $\Pr(\bar{X}_{16} \geq \tfrac34)$:

$$\text{Chebyshev: } 0.25 \qquad \text{exact: } \frac{2517}{65536} \approx 0.038$$
  • Bound off by $6.5\times$ already — and the gap explodes with $n$
  • Diagnosis: variance is only two numbers of information

To capture exponential decay, feed Markov an exponential. Enter the MGF.

06

Moment Generating Functions

All moments in one function

Definition — Moment Generating Function

Definition (moment generating function).

For a random variable $X$, the MGF is the function of $t \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$M_X(t) = \mathbb{E}\big[ e^{tX} \big]$$
  • One random variable $\to$ one deterministic function of $t$
  • $M_X(0) = \mathbb{E}[1] = 1$ always

Think: a summary of $X$ far richer than mean + variance.

Why "Moment Generating" — First Derivative

Differentiate in $t$, swap derivative and expectation (as in the notes):

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t} M_X(t) &\stackrel{(\text{swap})}{=} \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t} e^{tX} \right] = \mathbb{E}\big[ X e^{tX} \big] \end{aligned}$$

Evaluate at $t = 0$: $\;e^{0 \cdot X} = 1$, so

$$M_X'(0) = \mathbb{E}[X]$$

The slope at zero is the mean.

Second Derivative, and the Pattern

Differentiate once more:

$$\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} M_X(t) = \mathbb{E}\big[ X^2 e^{tX} \big] \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M_X''(0) = \mathbb{E}[X^2]$$

Every derivative at $0$ produces the next moment:

$$M_X^{(k)}(0) = \mathbb{E}[X^k]$$

Mean, second moment (so variance), third, fourth, ... all from one function.

Taylor View — Moments as Coefficients

Expand $e^{tX}$ as a series and take expectations termwise:

$$M_X(t) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(tX)^k}{k!} \right] = 1 + \mathbb{E}[X]\, t + \frac{\mathbb{E}[X^2]}{2}\, t^2 + \cdots$$
  • The MGF is the moments' generating series — hence the name
  • Near $t = 0$, mean and second moment dominate: the CLT will live here

Worked Example — Bernoulli MGF

$X \sim$ Bernoulli($p$): two outcomes, direct computation:

$$M_X(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{tX}] = (1-p)\, e^{t \cdot 0} + p\, e^{t \cdot 1} = 1 - p + p\, e^t$$

Sanity checks against the moment machine:

  • $M_X'(t) = p e^t$, so $M_X'(0) = p = \mathbb{E}[X]$  ✓
  • $M_X''(0) = p = \mathbb{E}[X^2]$ (since $X^2 = X$)  ✓

The running example's building block — keep $1 - p + pe^t$ in view.

Exercise (from the notes) — Gaussian MGF

Recall (Gaussian). $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ has density

$$f_X(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\!\left( -\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)$$

Exercise. Show that its MGF is

$$M_X(t) = \exp\!\left( \frac{t^2\sigma^2}{2} + t\mu \right)$$

Technique — Completing the Square

Any quadratic in $x$ is a shifted square plus a constant:

$$-\frac{x^2 - 2bx}{2\sigma^2} = -\frac{(x - b)^2}{2\sigma^2} + \frac{b^2}{2\sigma^2}$$
  • The shifted square rebuilds a Gaussian density (integrates to 1)
  • The constant exits the integral — it is the answer

Standard move for every Gaussian integral.

Gaussian MGF — Setting Up

Write out the expectation; merge the exponents:

$$\begin{aligned} M_X(t) &= \int \! e^{tx}\, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} dx = \int \! \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\!\Big( \underbrace{tx - \tfrac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}_{\text{quadratic in } x} \Big) dx \end{aligned}$$

Plan: complete the square in that exponent, in $x$.

Gaussian MGF — Completing the Square

$$\begin{aligned} tx - \frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} &\stackrel{(\text{expand})}{=} -\frac{x^2 - 2(\mu + \sigma^2 t)x + \mu^2}{2\sigma^2} \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{complete})}{=} -\frac{\big(x - (\mu + \sigma^2 t)\big)^2}{2\sigma^2} + \frac{(\mu + \sigma^2 t)^2 - \mu^2}{2\sigma^2} \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{simplify})}{=} -\frac{(x - m)^2}{2\sigma^2} + \mu t + \frac{\sigma^2 t^2}{2}, \qquad m = \mu + \sigma^2 t \end{aligned}$$

Gaussian MGF — Reading Off

The constant leaves; a $\mathcal{N}(m, \sigma^2)$ density remains:

$$\begin{aligned} M_X(t) &= e^{\mu t + \sigma^2 t^2 / 2} \underbrace{\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} e^{-\frac{(x-m)^2}{2\sigma^2}} dx}_{=\,1} = \exp\!\left( \frac{t^2 \sigma^2}{2} + t\mu \right) \;\; \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$
  • Sanity: $M_X'(0) = \mu$  ✓,  $M_X''(0) = \mu^2 + \sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}[X^2]$  ✓

Theorem — The MGF Is a Fingerprint

Theorem 4 (uniqueness).

If $M_X(t) = M_Y(t)$ for all $t$, then $X$ and $Y$ have the same distribution:

$$F_X(x) = F_Y(x) \quad \text{for all } x$$
  • Proof out of scope (as in the notes) — we use it, twice
  • Usage pattern: compute an MGF, recognize it, name the distribution

Proposition — MGFs Turn Sums into Products

$X \perp Y$, $Z = X + Y$; apply the Warm-Up product rule to $g = h = \exp$:

$$\begin{aligned} M_Z(t) = \mathbb{E}\big[ e^{t(X+Y)} \big] &\stackrel{(\text{split})}{=} \mathbb{E}\big[ e^{tX} e^{tY} \big] \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{product rule})}{=} \mathbb{E}\big[e^{tX}\big]\, \mathbb{E}\big[e^{tY}\big] = M_X(t)\, M_Y(t) \end{aligned}$$

Sums of independent variables — hard to convolve, trivial to multiply.

Worked Example — Sum of Independent Gaussians

$X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2) \perp Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_2, \sigma_2^2)$; multiply MGFs:

$$M_{X+Y}(t) = e^{\mu_1 t + \sigma_1^2 t^2/2} \, e^{\mu_2 t + \sigma_2^2 t^2/2} = e^{(\mu_1 + \mu_2)t + (\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2) t^2 / 2}$$

Recognize a Gaussian MGF; invoke the fingerprint (Theorem 4):

$$X + Y \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\big(\mu_1 + \mu_2,\; \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2\big)$$

Three tools in one line: Gaussian MGF, product rule, uniqueness.

Running Example — MGF of the Coin Sum

$S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n$, i.i.d. fair coins; product rule $n$ times:

$$M_{S_n}(t) = \prod_{i=1}^n M_{X_i}(t) = \left( \frac{1 + e^t}{2} \right)^{\! n}$$
  • Bernoulli MGF with $p = \tfrac12$: $\;\tfrac12 + \tfrac12 e^t$
  • The exponent $n$ is the seed of exponential tail bounds

Chernoff will feed exactly this into Markov.

Chernoff Bound

Exponentially small tails

Idea — Escalate Once More

The escalation pattern, third rung:

$$X \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{Chebyshev}\;}\; (X - \mu)^2 \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{Chernoff}\;}\; e^{tX}$$
  • $e^{tX}$ is always non-negative — Markov-ready, no centering needed
  • Its mean is exactly the MGF: $\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}] = M_X(t)$
  • Exponentials amplify tails, so Markov punishes them harder

Same engine, richest input we have.

Technique — Exponentiating an Event

For $t \gt 0$, the map $x \mapsto e^{tx}$ is strictly increasing:

$$X \geq \alpha \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e^{tX} \geq e^{t\alpha}$$
$e^{t(x-\alpha)}$ $\mathbf{1}\{x \geq \alpha\}$ $\alpha$

Rescaled: $e^{t(x - \alpha)} \geq \mathbf{1}\{x \geq \alpha\}$ for all $x$ — a smooth lid over the step.

Theorem — Chernoff Bound

Theorem 5 (Chernoff bound).

Let $X$ have MGF $M(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]$ (finite at the $t$ used). For any $\alpha$:

$$\Pr(X \geq \alpha) \;\leq\; M(t)\, e^{-t\alpha} \qquad \text{for all } t \gt 0$$
  • Not one bound: a family, one per $t \gt 0$
  • We may pick the best $t$ afterwards — for free

Chernoff — Proof Overview

Target: $\Pr(X \geq \alpha) \leq M(t)\, e^{-t\alpha}$ for each fixed $t \gt 0$

1Exponentiatethe event: $X \geq \alpha \iff e^{tX} \geq e^{t\alpha}$
2Markovon $e^{tX}$ at level $e^{t\alpha}$
3Rename$\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}] = M(t)$

Two slides ago this was Chebyshev's proof with a square. Same skeleton.

Chernoff Proof — Step 1: Exponentiate

Fix $t \gt 0$. Monotonicity of $x \mapsto e^{tx}$ (technique slide):

$$\Pr(X \geq \alpha) \stackrel{(\text{same event})}{=} \Pr\!\big( e^{tX} \geq e^{t\alpha} \big)$$

Check Markov's entry ticket for $W = e^{tX}$:

  • $W \gt 0$  ✓ (exponentials are positive)
  • $\mathbb{E}[W] = M(t)$, assumed finite  ✓

Chernoff Proof — Step 2: Markov

Theorem 1 (threshold form) on $W = e^{tX}$ at level $e^{t\alpha}$:

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr\!\big( e^{tX} \geq e^{t\alpha} \big) &\stackrel{(\text{Markov})}{\leq} \frac{\mathbb{E}\big[e^{tX}\big]}{e^{t\alpha}} \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{def MGF})}{=} M(t)\, e^{-t\alpha} \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

True for every $t \gt 0$ separately — that is the whole proof.

Chernoff — Proof Summary

$$\Pr(X \geq \alpha) \stackrel{(\text{exponentiate})}{=} \Pr\!\big(e^{tX} \geq e^{t\alpha}\big) \stackrel{(\text{Markov})}{\leq} M(t)\, e^{-t\alpha}$$
  • The trick: a tunable transform — $t$ is ours to choose
  • All distribution knowledge now flows in through $M(t)$

Markov three ways: on $X$, on $(X-\mu)^2$, on $e^{tX}$. One idea, three strengths.

The Free Parameter — Pick the Best $t$

Each $t$ gives a valid bound, so take the tightest:

$$\Pr(X \geq \alpha) \;\leq\; \inf_{t \gt 0}\; M(t)\, e^{-t\alpha}$$
  • Small $t$: weak amplification; huge $t$: $M(t)$ blows up
  • Sweet spot in between — found by calculus, per problem

Now run the full recipe on the coin.

Coin Flips — Chernoff, Step 1: Plug In

Tail of the sum: $\bar{X}_n \geq \tfrac34 \iff S_n \geq \tfrac{3n}{4}$; use $M_{S_n}(t) = \big(\tfrac{1+e^t}{2}\big)^n$:

$$\Pr\!\left( S_n \geq \tfrac{3n}{4} \right) \;\leq\; e^{-3nt/4} \left( \frac{1+e^t}{2} \right)^{\!n} = \left[ e^{-3t/4}\, \frac{1+e^t}{2} \right]^{n}$$

Everything is a power of $n$: beat $1$ per flip, win exponentially overall.

Coin Flips — Chernoff, Step 2: Optimize $t$

Minimize the per-flip log-cost $g(t) = -\tfrac34 t + \ln\!\frac{1+e^t}{2}$:

$$g'(t) = -\frac34 + \frac{e^t}{1+e^t} = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e^t = 3 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t^\star = \ln 3$$
  • Log of a min is min of the log: safe to optimize $g$
  • $e^t/(1+e^t)$ increases from $\tfrac12$ to $1$: unique solution

Coin Flips — Chernoff, Step 3: Evaluate

Plug $t^\star = \ln 3$ back (note $\tfrac{1 + e^{t^\star}}{2} = 2$):

$$g(t^\star) = -\tfrac34 \ln 3 + \ln 2 \approx -0.1308 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Pr\!\left( \bar{X}_n \geq \tfrac34 \right) \leq e^{-0.1308\, n}$$
$n$16100400
bound $e^{-0.1308 n}$$0.12$$2.1 \times 10^{-6}$$1.9 \times 10^{-23}$

Chebyshev at $n = 400$: $0.01$. Chernoff: $10^{-23}$. Different universes.

The Exponent Is a KL Divergence

Same three steps with a general target $a \gt \tfrac12$ give $t^\star = \ln\frac{a}{1-a}$ and:

$$\Pr(\bar{X}_n \geq a) \;\leq\; e^{-n\, D\left(\mathrm{Bern}(a)\, \|\, \mathrm{Bern}(1/2)\right)}$$
  • Check $a = \tfrac34$: $\;D = \tfrac34\ln\tfrac{3}{2} + \tfrac14\ln\tfrac{1}{2} \approx 0.1308$ nats  ✓
  • KL = the cost of pretending $a$-biased data is fair

Illustration beyond the notes: the optimized bound rediscovers KL on its own.

Chernoff — How Many Samples, Revisited

Coin-accuracy demand: $\Pr(|\bar{X}_n - \tfrac12| \geq 0.01) \leq 0.05$ for a fair coin

$$2\, e^{-n D(\mathrm{Bern}(0.51) \| \mathrm{Bern}(0.5))} = 2\, e^{-n \cdot 0.0002} \leq 0.05 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; n \geq \frac{\ln 40}{0.0002} \approx 18{,}444$$
  • Factor $2$: both tails, symmetric for a fair coin
  • Chebyshev asked for $50{,}000$; Chernoff: $\approx 18{,}400$

Observation: $D(\mathrm{Bern}(\tfrac12 + \epsilon) \| \mathrm{Bern}(\tfrac12)) \approx 2\epsilon^2$ — remember this shape.

Chernoff — What It Buys

  • Cost: the whole MGF (heaviest input so far)
  • Reward: tails shrinking exponentially in $n$ for sums
  • Everywhere in ML theory: confidence bounds, bandit algorithms, generalization

Exponential tail bounds are why moderate sample sizes give strong guarantees.

The named workhorse (Hoeffding) is built exactly this way.

Central Limit Theorem

The shape of fluctuations — a sketch via MGFs

Beyond the LLN — The Shape Question

LLN: fluctuations of $\bar{X}_n$ die. New question: dying how?

$$\bar{X}_n - \mu \approx \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \cdot (\text{what?}) \qquad \text{std of } \bar{X}_n = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$$
  • Zoom in by $\sqrt{n}$: magnify the dying fluctuation to size $1$
  • Claim, for i.i.d. $X_i$ of finite variance: one shape — a bell

Setup — Standardize Each Variable

From the notes: define $Y_i = (X_i - \mu)/\sigma$, so that

$$\mathbb{E}[Y] = 0, \qquad \mathbb{E}[Y^2] = \mathrm{Var}(Y) = 1$$

Assume $M_Y$ finite near $t = 0$; the moment machine then gives:

$$M_Y'(0) = 0, \qquad M_Y''(0) = 1, \qquad M_Y(0) = 1$$

Units of $\sigma$, centered at $0$: all coins, dice, losses now look alike near $t = 0$.

Theorem — Central Limit Theorem

Theorem 6 (central limit theorem).

$X_1, X_2, \dots$ i.i.d. with mean $\mu$ and variance $0 \lt \sigma^2 \lt \infty$. Then:

$$Z_n = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{X_i - \mu}{\sqrt{n}\,\sigma} \;\xrightarrow{\;(d)\;}\; \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$$

$\xrightarrow{(d)}$ = in distribution: the CDF of $Z_n$ approaches the Gaussian CDF

Note $Z_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_i Y_i$: the standardized sum, zoomed by $\sqrt{n}$.

What "Sketch" Means Today

Following the notes, a non-rigorous MGF proof; we take on faith:

  • $M_Y(t)$ exists near $t = 0$ (not guaranteed by finite variance)
  • MGF convergence $\Rightarrow$ distribution convergence (fingerprint, in the limit)
  • Error control in the Taylor step

The full proof swaps MGFs for characteristic functions $\mathbb{E}[e^{itX}]$ — beyond this course.

CLT Sketch — Overview

Target: $M_{Z_n}(t) \to e^{t^2/2}$, the $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ MGF

1Productindependence turns $M_{Z_n}$ into a power of $M_Y$
2Taylornear $0$, $M_Y(s) \approx 1 + s^2/2$
3Limit$(1 + \frac{t^2}{2n})^n \to e^{t^2/2}$
4Recognizethat is the Gaussian fingerprint

Every ingredient is a moment-generating function fact.

Technique — Taylor at Zero

Second-order expansion of $M_Y$ around $t = 0$, coefficients known:

$$M_Y(t) \approx M_Y(0) + M_Y'(0)\, t + \frac{M_Y''(0)}{2} t^2 = 1 + 0 \cdot t + \frac{t^2}{2}$$
  • Standardization killed the linear term: that was its job
  • Valid for small $t$ — and $t/\sqrt{n}$ will be very small

Technique — The Compound-Interest Limit

Classic calculus fact, for any fixed $x$:

$$\left(1 + \frac{x}{n}\right)^{\!n} \;\longrightarrow\; e^{x} \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$

Numeric feel at $x = 1$:  $(1.1)^{10} \approx 2.594$,  $(1.01)^{100} \approx 2.705$,  $e \approx 2.718$

Small gains, compounded $n$ times, become an exponential.

CLT Sketch — Step 1: Product

$Z_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_i Y_i$ with $Y_i$ i.i.d.; MGF of an independent sum:

$$\begin{aligned} M_{Z_n}(t) &\stackrel{(\text{def})}{=} \mathbb{E}\!\left[ e^{\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_i Y_i} \right] \stackrel{(\text{indep.})}{=} \prod_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}\!\left[ e^{\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} Y_i} \right] \stackrel{(\text{i.i.d.})}{=} M_Y\!\left( \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} \right)^{\!n} \end{aligned}$$

One unknown function, evaluated ever closer to $0$, raised to the $n$-th power.

CLT Sketch — Step 2: Taylor

Insert the expansion at $s = t/\sqrt{n}$ (small once $n$ is large):

$$M_Y\!\left( \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} \right) \approx 1 + \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{t}{\sqrt{n}} \right)^{\!2} = 1 + \frac{t^2}{2n}$$
$$\Longrightarrow\quad M_{Z_n}(t) \approx \left( 1 + \frac{t^2}{2n} \right)^{\!n}$$

Only the mean-0, variance-1 structure survives: universality begins here.

CLT Sketch — Step 3: Limit & Recognize

Compound-interest limit with $x = t^2/2$, then the fingerprint:

$$M_{Z_n}(t) \approx \left(1 + \frac{t^2}{2n}\right)^{\!n} \;\longrightarrow\; e^{t^2 / 2} \stackrel{(\text{Gaussian MGF: } \mu = 0,\, \sigma = 1)}{=} M_{\mathcal{N}(0,1)}(t)$$

Conclusion (sketch level): $\;Z_n \xrightarrow{(d)} \mathcal{N}(0,1) \qquad \blacksquare$

The limit MGF is Gaussian, whatever distribution the $X_i$ had.

CLT Sketch — Summary

$$M_{Z_n}(t) \stackrel{(\text{indep.})}{=} M_Y\!\big(\tfrac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\big)^n \stackrel{(\text{Taylor})}{\approx} \Big(1 + \tfrac{t^2}{2n}\Big)^{\!n} \stackrel{(\text{limit})}{\longrightarrow} e^{t^2/2}$$
  • Weight carriers: sum $\to$ product, and $M_Y''(0) = 1$
  • Individuality of $X$ lives in higher moments — crushed by $1/\sqrt{n}$ scaling

Seeing the CLT — 16 Coins vs. the Bell

standardized value $z$ ($-4$ to $4$) $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ density Bin$(16, 1/2)$ standardized

CLT — What It Buys

  • Error bars: for large $n$, a metric sits near $\mu \pm z\,\sigma/\sqrt{n}$
  • Universality: sums of many small independent effects look Gaussian
  • Maximum entropy: Gaussians as the default noise model

$z = 1.96$ at 95%; an approximation, and $\sigma$ itself is estimated from the sample.

LLN says averages land; CLT says how they wobble: a bell of width $\sigma/\sqrt{n}$.
