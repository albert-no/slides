Why Low Rank?

Fewer directions, measured loss

The Compression Question

Store $A \in \mathbb{R}^{10^4 \times 10^4}$: $10^8$ numbers. Rank-$20$ version:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{20} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top}: \quad 20 \times (10^4 + 10^4) = 4 \times 10^5 \text{ numbers} \;\; (0.4\%)$$
  • Question 1: among rank-$k$ matrices, which is closest to $A$?
  • Question 2: how large is the unavoidable error?

Definition — Span & Range

Recall. Span = all linear combinations of given vectors

Definition (range / column space).

$$\operatorname{range}(A) = \Big\{ \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i\, a_i \;:\; \alpha_i \in \mathbb{R} \Big\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$$

Everything $A$ can output — a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^m$

Definition — Rank

Recall. Dimension of a subspace = size of a basis = max independent vectors

Definition (rank).

$$\operatorname{rank}(A) = \dim\big( \operatorname{range}(A) \big)$$

Fact (stated): row space has the same dimension — row rank = column rank

Worked — a Collapsing Matrix

$$C = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad a_1 = a_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Both columns on one line
  • $\operatorname{range}(C) = \operatorname{span}\{(1,2)\}$
  • All outputs collapse onto that line
$(1,2)$ $\operatorname{range}(C)$: a line

Definition — Null Space

Definition (null space).

$$\operatorname{null}(A) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^{n} : Ax = 0 \}$$
  • The inputs $A$ destroys
  • Lives in the input space $\mathbb{R}^n$; range lives in $\mathbb{R}^m$

Theorem 1 — Rank–Nullity

Theorem 1 (rank–nullity).

For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ ($n$ = number of columns):

$$\operatorname{rank}(A) + \dim\big( \operatorname{null}(A) \big) = n$$

Every input dimension either survives (rank) or dies (nullity)

Stated, not proved (as in source) — it powers Theorem 8, Step 1

Measuring Error

Two sizes for a residual

Definition — Norm

Definition (norm on $\mathbb{R}^n$).

$\|\cdot\| : \mathbb{R}^n \to [0,\infty)$ with, for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\|x+y\| \leq \|x\| + \|y\|, \qquad \|\alpha x\| = |\alpha| \|x\|, \qquad \|x\| = 0 \iff x = 0$$

Triangle inequality · absolute homogeneity · definiteness

The $\ell_p$ Family

$$\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{x^{\top}x} = \sqrt{x_1^2 + \cdots + x_n^2}, \qquad \|x\|_p = \Big( |x_1|^p + \cdots + |x_n|^p \Big)^{1/p}$$
  • $p \geq 1$ required — below 1 the triangle inequality fails
  • Limit case: $\|x\|_{\infty} = \max_i |x_i|$

Worked — One Vector, Three Norms

Take $x = (3, 4)$:

$$\|x\|_1 = 3 + 4 = 7, \qquad \|x\|_2 = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5, \qquad \|x\|_{\infty} = 4$$
  • Always ordered: $\|x\|_{\infty} \leq \|x\|_2 \leq \|x\|_1$
  • Same vector, three sizes — the norm is a choice

Unit Balls — the Norms as Shapes

$\ell_1$: diamond $\ell_2$: circle $\ell_\infty$: square

$\ell_1$'s corners sit on the axes — corners will mean sparsity (§06, nuclear norm)

Definition — Orthogonal Matrix

Definition (orthogonal matrix).

$U \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ is orthogonal if any (hence all) of:

$$U^{\top}U = I \quad\iff\quad UU^{\top} = I$$
$$\iff\quad \text{columns orthonormal} \quad\iff\quad \text{rows orthonormal}$$

Square only — the thin SVD factors will not qualify (§04)

Theorem 4 — Orthogonal Maps Preserve Length

Theorem 4 (orthogonal invariance).

$U$ orthogonal $\Rightarrow$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$:  $\|Ux\|_2 = \|x\|_2$

Proof.

$$\|Ux\|_2^2 = (Ux)^{\top}(Ux) = x^{\top} U^{\top} U\, x \;\stackrel{(U^{\top}U = I)}{=}\; x^{\top} x = \|x\|_2^2 \qquad \blacksquare$$

One line — the source states it, the proof is free

Definition — Induced (Operator) Norm

Definition.

For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and $p \geq 1$:

$$\|A\|_p \;=\; \max_{x \neq 0} \frac{\|Ax\|_p}{\|x\|_p} \;=\; \max_{\|x\|_p = 1} \|Ax\|_p$$
  • Biggest amplification factor over all inputs
  • Scale-free: doubling $x$ doubles $Ax$, ratio unchanged

Definition — Frobenius Norm

Definition.

$$\|A\|_F \;=\; \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m} \sum_{j=1}^{n} A_{ij}^2} \;=\; \sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(A^{\top}A)}$$
  • Flatten the matrix, take the $\ell_2$ norm of the $mn$ numbers
  • Total "energy" — every entry counts, not just the worst direction

Reading an SVD

Rotate, stretch, rotate

Recall: Symmetric Eigendecomposition

Recall (spectral theorem). Symmetric $S \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$: $S = Q \Lambda Q^{\top}$, $Q$ orthogonal, $\Lambda$ real diagonal

Apply to $A^{\top}A$: symmetric, $n \times n$, acts on inputs $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$

$$A^{\top}A = Q\Lambda Q^{\top}, \qquad \lambda_i = q_i^{\top}A^{\top}A\,q_i = \|Aq_i\|_2^2 \geq 0$$
$A^{\top}A$ supplies orthonormal input directions $q_1, \dots, q_n \in \mathbb{R}^n$
Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §4.2, Theorems 4.14–4.15

Every Matrix Has an SVD

Theorem 5 (SVD).

Every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ of rank $r$: any shape, symmetric or not, invertible or not

$$A = U \Sigma V^{\top}, \qquad U \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m},\; V \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n} \text{ orthogonal}$$

$\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$: $\Sigma_{ii} = \sigma_i$, zero off the diagonal; $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$, rest $0$

Construction: eigenvectors of $A^{\top}A$ give $V$; then $u_i = Av_i/\sigma_i$

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §4.5, Thm 4.22, §4.5.2

Read the Factor Shapes

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U$ $m \times m$, orthogonal $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r \gt 0$ $\Sigma$ zero rows $m \times n$ $V^{\top}$ $n \times n$, orthogonal

$(m \times m)(m \times n)(n \times n) = m \times n$: drawn for $m \gt n$

Solid diagonal: $r$ nonzero $\sigma_i$ · dashed: zero slots · gray band: zero rows

Shapes as in Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §4.5, eq. (4.64)

A Sum of Rank-One Layers

$$A = U\Sigma V^{\top} = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i\, \underbrace{u_i}_{m\times 1}\,\underbrace{v_i^{\top}}_{1\times n}$$

Each $u_i v_i^{\top}$: $m \times n$, every column a multiple of $u_i$

$\sigma_i \gt 0$: term $i$ has rank one

All $r$ terms together give $A$ exactly

$\sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top}$ largest weight $A = $ layer $1 + \cdots +$ layer $r$ back layers: smaller $\sigma_i$
Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §4.6

Compact SVD Keeps Active Directions

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U_r$ $m \times r$ $\Sigma_r$ $r \times r$ $V_r^{\top}$ $r \times n$ keep only the $r$ nonzero $\sigma_i$
$$A = U_r \Sigma_r V_r^{\top}, \quad \Sigma_r = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r), \quad U_r^{\top}U_r = V_r^{\top}V_r = I_r$$

$r \lt m$: $U_rU_r^{\top} \neq I_m$; thin factors are not orthogonal matrices

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §4.5

The Eigenvalues Are 45 and 5

$$\lambda_1 = 45 \;\geq\; \lambda_2 = 5 \qquad (\text{check: } 45 + 5 = 50 = \operatorname{tr},\;\; 45 \cdot 5 = 225 = \det)$$

Eigenvalues of $A^{\top}A$, not of $A$: squared stretches $\sigma_i^2$

Largest Singular Value, Largest Stretch

Theorem 6 (spectral norm).

For every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with largest singular value $\sigma_1$:

$$\|A\|_2 \;=\; \max_{\|x\|_2 = 1} \|Ax\|_2 \;=\; \sigma_1$$

1. Upper bound

$\|Ax\|_2 \leq \sigma_1$ for every unit $x$

2. Witness

one unit $x$ with $\|Ax\|_2 = \sigma_1$

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML §4.6, Def. 4.23, Thm 4.24 · Wilkinson, MATH3030 §3.5.1, Prop. 3.11

No Direction Beats the Largest Stretch

Any unit $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$; set $y = V^{\top}x$, so $\|y\|_2 = 1$ (orthogonal invariance)

$$\begin{aligned} \|Ax\|_2^2 &\;=\; \|U\Sigma y\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(U \text{ orthogonal})}{=}\; \|\Sigma y\|_2^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2 y_i^2 \\ &\;\stackrel{(\sigma_i \leq \sigma_1)}{\leq}\; \sigma_1^2 \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i^2 \;=\; \sigma_1^2 \|y\|_2^2 \;=\; \sigma_1^2 \end{aligned}$$

As $x$ runs over all unit vectors, so does $y$: no direction skipped

Proof idea: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML §4.6, Thm 4.24 (exercise there)

The First Singular Vector Attains It

Witness: the unit vector $x = v_1$

$$Av_1 = U\Sigma V^{\top}v_1 = U\Sigma e_1 = \sigma_1 u_1, \qquad \|Av_1\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\|u_1\|_2 = 1)}{=}\; \sigma_1$$
$$\sigma_1 = \|Av_1\|_2 \;\leq\; \|A\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\text{upper bound})}{\leq}\; \sigma_1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \|A\|_2 = \sigma_1 \;\;\blacksquare$$

Geometry: $\sigma_1 u_1$ is the ellipse's longest semi-axis

Proof idea: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML §4.6, Thm 4.24 (exercise there)

Frobenius Energy Sums Squared Stretches

Theorem 7 (Frobenius norm).

For every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ of rank $r$:

$$\|A\|_F^2 \;=\; \sum_{i,j} a_{ij}^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2$$

Contrast: $\|A\|_2 = \sigma_1$ is the worst single direction; $\|A\|_F$ is total energy

Worked $A$: $\|A\|_F^2 = 9 + 0 + 16 + 25 = 50 = 45 + 5$

Wilkinson, MATH3030 §3.5.1, Prop. 3.10

Orthogonal Coordinates Preserve Total Energy

Orthogonal $Q$, $W$; $B$ with columns $b_j$; orthogonal invariance per column:

$$\begin{aligned} \|QB\|_F^2 &= \textstyle\sum_j \|Qb_j\|_2^2 = \sum_j \|b_j\|_2^2 = \|B\|_F^2 && \text{(columns)} \\ \|BW\|_F &= \|W^{\top}B^{\top}\|_F = \|B^{\top}\|_F = \|B\|_F && \text{(rows, via transpose)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\|A\|_F^2 = \|U^{\top}AV\|_F^2 = \|\Sigma\|_F^2 = \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2 \;\;\blacksquare$$
Wilkinson, MATH3030 §3.5.1, Prop. 3.10 (same invariance chain)
04

Best Low-Rank Approximation

Eckart–Young–Mirsky

The Candidate — Truncated SVD

Definition.

Keep the $k$ loudest layers:

$$A_k \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top} \;=\; U \Sigma_k V^{\top}, \quad \Sigma_k = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_k, 0, \dots)$$

$\operatorname{rank}(A_k) = k$ (its range is $\operatorname{span}\{u_1,\dots,u_k\}$) — but is it the best rank-$k$?

Worked — $A_1$ for the Running Matrix

$$A_1 = \sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \text{(computed in §04)}$$
  • Rank $1$: both columns $= 1.5\,(1,3)$
  • Four entries, but only $2 + 2$ stored numbers

Worked — the Error of $A_1$

$$A - A_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & -1.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \sigma_2 u_2 v_2^{\top}$$
$$\|A - A_1\|_2 = \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5}, \qquad \|A - A_1\|_F = \sqrt{1.5^2 + 1.5^2 + 0.5^2 + 0.5^2} = \sqrt{5}$$

The error is exactly the discarded layer — norm $\sigma_2$. Coincidence, or a law?

Theorem 8 — Eckart–Young–Mirsky

Theorem 8 (EYM).

For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with singular values $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r$, and any $k \lt r$:

$$\min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k} \|A - B\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}, \qquad \min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k} \|A - B\|_F = \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_{i \gt k} \sigma_i^2}$$

Both minima achieved by the truncated SVD $B = A_k$.

Proof follows Wilkinson, MATH3030 notes §3.5 (source's citation). We prove the spectral case in full.

Reading the Statement

  • Truncation is optimal — no clever rank-$k$ matrix does better
  • The floor is the first sigma you threw away: $\sigma_{k+1}$
  • Fast-decaying sigmas $\Rightarrow$ tiny $k$ already excellent

Source claims a unique minimizer — dropped (spectral: non-unique; Frobenius: needs $\sigma_k \gt \sigma_{k+1}$)

Technique Review 1 — Rank–Nullity

Recall (Thm 1). For $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$:

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) = n - \operatorname{rank}(B)$$
  • Low rank $\Rightarrow$ big null space
  • $\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$ $\Rightarrow$ $B$ kills a subspace of dimension $\geq n - k$

The proof's engine: every low-rank competitor has a large blind spot

Technique Review 2 — Big Subspaces Must Meet

Fact. $W_1, W_2 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ subspaces:

$$\dim(W_1 \cap W_2) \;\geq\; \dim W_1 + \dim W_2 - n$$
  • If $\dim W_1 + \dim W_2 \gt n$: intersection has dimension $\geq 1$ — a nonzero common vector
  • Picture: two planes ($2+2 \gt 3$) in $\mathbb{R}^3$ always share a line

Picture — Two Planes in $\mathbb{R}^3$

$W_1$, $\dim = 2$ $W_2$, $\dim = 2$ shared line: $2 + 2 - 3 = 1$ too big for $\mathbb{R}^3$ to keep them apart

Technique Review 3 — Bounding a Max From Below

$$\|M\|_2 = \max_{\|x\|_2 = 1} \|Mx\|_2 \;\geq\; \|Mw\|_2 \quad \text{for any single unit } w$$
  • To lower-bound a max: exhibit one witness
  • Strategy: find a unit $w$ where $\|(A - B)w\|_2$ must be large

Technique Review 4 — $\|Aw\|^2$ in the $v$-Basis

Write a unit $w$ in the right-singular basis: $w = \sum_i w_i v_i$, $\sum_i w_i^2 = 1$. Then

$$\|Aw\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(Av_i = \sigma_i u_i)}{=}\; \Big\| \sum_i w_i \sigma_i u_i \Big\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(u_i \text{ orthonormal})}{=}\; \sum_i \sigma_i^2 w_i^2$$

Theorem 6's algebra — there an upper bound, here a lower one

Proof Overview (Spectral Case)

  1. Competitor $B$, rank $\leq k$ $\Rightarrow$ $\dim\operatorname{null}(B) \geq n - k$  (TR1)
  2. Where $A$ is strong: $V_{k+1} = \operatorname{span}\{v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}\}$, dimension $k+1$
  3. $(n-k) + (k+1) \gt n$ $\Rightarrow$ a unit $w$ in both  (TR2)
  4. Witness bound: $\|A - B\|_2 \geq \|(A-B)w\|_2 = \|Aw\|_2 \geq \sigma_{k+1}$  (TR3, TR4)
  5. Tightness: $B = A_k$ achieves $\sigma_{k+1}$ exactly

Step 1 — Every Competitor Has a Blind Spot

Let $B$ be any matrix with $\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$.

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \;\stackrel{(\text{Thm 1})}{=}\; n - \operatorname{rank}(B) \;\geq\; n - k$$
  • A subspace of dimension $\geq n - k$ that $B$ maps to $0$
  • On it, $(A - B)x = Ax$ — the competitor vanishes from the error

Step 2 — Where $A$ Is Still Strong

$$V_{k+1} \;=\; \operatorname{span}\{v_1, v_2, \dots, v_{k+1}\}, \qquad \dim V_{k+1} = k + 1$$
  • The top $k{+}1$ input directions — every stretch $\geq \sigma_{k+1}$
  • Why $k+1$, not $k$? Step 3 needs the dimensions to overfill $\mathbb{R}^n$

Step 3 — The Two Subspaces Collide

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) + \dim V_{k+1} \;\geq\; (n - k) + (k + 1) \;=\; n + 1 \;\gt\; n$$

By TR2 they share a nonzero vector; normalize it:

$$\exists\, w \in \operatorname{null}(B) \cap V_{k+1}, \quad \|w\|_2 = 1, \qquad w = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} w_i v_i, \;\; \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} w_i^2 = 1$$

Only $k{+}1$ coordinates: $w \in V_{k+1}$ has no $v_i$ component past $i = k{+}1$

Step 4 — The Witness Bound

$$\|A - B\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(\text{TR3: } w \text{ unit})}{\geq}\; \|(A - B)w\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(Bw = 0)}{=}\; \|Aw\|_2^2$$
  • $B$ is blind at $w$ — the error there is all of $Aw$
  • Remains: show $\|Aw\|_2$ can't be small when $w \in V_{k+1}$

Step 4, Finished — $A$ Is Strong at $w$

$$\|Aw\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(\text{TR4})}{=}\; \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} \sigma_i^2 w_i^2 \;\stackrel{(\sigma_i \geq \sigma_{k+1})}{\geq}\; \sigma_{k+1}^2 \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} w_i^2 \;\stackrel{(\|w\|_2 = 1)}{=}\; \sigma_{k+1}^2$$
$$\Longrightarrow\;\; \|A - B\|_2 \;\geq\; \sigma_{k+1} \quad \text{for every rank-}k\text{ competitor } B$$

Step 5 — Truncation Hits the Floor

$$A - A_k = \sum_{i \gt k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top} = U \operatorname{diag}(0, \dots, 0, \sigma_{k+1}, \dots, \sigma_r)\, V^{\top}$$
$$\|A - A_k\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\text{Thm 6: top sigma})}{=}\; \sigma_{k+1}$$
  • The residual is itself an SVD — its largest singular value is $\sigma_{k+1}$
  • Lower bound met by $A_k$ $\Rightarrow$ minimum $= \sigma_{k+1}$  $\blacksquare$
