What We Already Have

Recall (optimal attack).

$$\sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}(\mathcal{A}) \;=\; \mathrm{TV}(P_1, P_0)$$

Every optimal attack is a likelihood-ratio test.

Recall (estimation).

$$\mathrm{Adv}(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}) \;\geq\; \mathrm{Adv}(\mathcal{A}^\star) - 2\,\mathrm{TV}(\widehat{P}, P)$$

Mis-specification costs at most twice the TV error.

Open: which statistic of the model should the ratio be built on?

Section 01

What Yeom et al. Actually Prove

  • Differential privacy caps advantage: $\mathrm{Adv} \leq e^{\varepsilon}-1$
  • A concrete adversary attains advantage exactly $\Delta/B$
  • Gaussian-error model: advantage in closed form
  • Overfitting is sufficient for leakage, not necessary

Headline: overfitting is a lower bound on what an attacker can do.

Yeom, Giacomelli, Fredrikson, Jha, "Privacy Risk in Machine Learning: Analyzing the Connection to Overfitting", CSF 2018

Section 01

The Membership Experiment

Experiment 1 (Yeom et al., 2018).

  1. Sample $S \sim \mathcal{D}^n$; train $A_S = A(S)$.
  2. Flip $b \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$.
  3. If $b=1$ draw $z \sim S$ (uniform); if $b=0$ draw $z \sim \mathcal{D}$.
  4. Adversary sees $(z, A_S, n, \mathcal{D})$ and outputs $\hat{b}$.

Randomness: $S$, the coin $b$, the point $z$, and $A$'s own coins.

Section 01

Membership Advantage

Definition 4 (membership advantage).

$$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} \;=\; 2\Pr[\hat b = b] - 1 \;=\; \underbrace{\Pr[\hat b = 1 \mid b=1]}_{\text{TPR}} - \underbrace{\Pr[\hat b = 1 \mid b=0]}_{\text{FPR}}$$

With $P_1, P_0$ the laws of $(A_S,z)$ given $b=1,0$: $\sup_{\mathcal{A}}\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} = \mathrm{TV}(P_1,P_0)$.

Section 01

Why Non-Members Come from $\mathcal{D}$

Yeom draws the $b=0$ challenge from $\mathcal{D}$, not from $\mathcal{D}\setminus S$.

$$\Pr[b=1 \mid z] \;=\; \frac{\Pr_{S,\,z\sim S}[z]\Pr[b{=}1]}{\Pr[z]} \;=\; \frac{\Pr_{z\sim\mathcal{D}}[z]\Pr[b{=}0]}{\Pr[z]} \;=\; \Pr[b=0 \mid z]$$

The challenge point alone carries no membership information.

All advantage must come from the model. Ceiling: $1 - \mu(n,\mathcal{D})$.

Section 01

Average Generalization Error

Definition 3 (average generalization error).

$$\Delta \;:=\; R_{\mathrm{gen}}(A,n,\mathcal{D},\ell) \;=\; \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\substack{S\sim\mathcal{D}^n \\ z\sim\mathcal{D}}}\big[\ell(A_S,z)\big] \;-\; \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\substack{S\sim\mathcal{D}^n \\ z\sim S}}\big[\ell(A_S,z)\big]$$

Both expectations average over the draw of $S$ and the coins of $A$.

$\Delta = \mathbb{E}_{P_0}[\ell] - \mathbb{E}_{P_1}[\ell]$: a difference of two conditional means.

Section 01

Average Gap vs. Fixed-Set Gap

Yeom (Definition 3)

$$\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{S,z\sim\mathcal{D}}[\ell] - \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{S,z\sim S}[\ell]$$

$S$ is random. A property of the algorithm.

The other common gap

$$\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim\mathcal{D}}[\ell(A_S,z)] - \tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_{z\in S}\ell(A_S,z)$$

$S$ is fixed. A property of one trained model.

Only the averaged version equals the ARO-stability rate. Everything below uses it.

The Picture Behind Everything

members $P_1$ non-members $P_0$ $\tau$ FPR FNR $\Delta$ loss $\ell(A_S,z)$
Section 01

Reading the Picture

Separation

How far apart the two curves sit. Governs $\mathrm{TV}(P_1,P_0)$.

Gap $\Delta$

Distance between the two means only.

Threshold $\tau$

Where you cut. Trades FPR against FNR.

Separation is what the attacker exploits. $\Delta$ sees only first moments.

Two distributions can have equal means and still be perfectly separable.

Section 01

The Bounded-Loss Assumption

Assume $\ell(A_S,z) \in [0,B]$ for all $S$ and all challenge points $z$.

Holds

0-1 loss, $B=1$. Clipped or truncated losses. Bounded regression targets.

Fails

Cross-entropy: $\ell = -\log p_y$ is unbounded as $p_y \to 0$.

Boundedness is used once: to make $\ell/B$ a valid probability.

Section 01

Adversary 1: A Loss-Proportional Coin

Adversary 1 (Yeom et al., 2018).

  1. Query the model to obtain $A_S(x)$, hence $\ell(A_S,z)$.
  2. Output $\hat b = 0$ with probability $\ell(A_S,z)/B$; else output $\hat b = 1$.

Equivalently: declare member with probability $1 - \ell(A_S,z)/B$.

No threshold, no shadow models, no knowledge of $\Delta$.

Section 01

Key Trick

Threshold rule

$$\Pr[\hat b = 1 \mid \ell] = \mathbf{1}[\ell \leq \tau]$$

A step in $\ell$: averaging gives a CDF.

Proportional coin

$$\Pr[\hat b = 1 \mid \ell] = 1 - \ell/B$$

Affine in $\ell$: averaging gives a mean.

Advantage is a difference of means, and $\Delta$ is a difference of means.

Section 01

Theorem 2: Exact Advantage

Theorem 2 (Yeom et al., 2018).

If $\ell$ takes values in $[0,B]$, then Adversary 1 satisfies

$$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathcal{A}_1) \;=\; \frac{R_{\mathrm{gen}}(A)}{B} \;=\; \frac{\Delta}{B}.$$

An equality, not an inequality. For 0-1 loss, $B=1$ and advantage $=\Delta$.

Read it as a construction: any gap is realisable as attack advantage.

Section 01

Proof of Theorem 2 — Outline

Three moves
  1. Condition. Average out the adversary's private coin at fixed $(A_S,z)$.
  2. Linearity. The affine rule turns each rate into a conditional mean.
  3. Identify. Those two means are exactly $R_{\mathrm{train}}$ and $R_{\mathrm{pop}}$.

No inequality is used anywhere. That is why the result is an equality.

Section 01

Proof of Theorem 2 — Step 1, Condition

The output coin is independent of everything given $(A_S,z)$.

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr[\hat b = 1 \mid b] &= \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(A_S,z)\sim P_b}\Big[\Pr\big[\hat b = 1 \,\big|\, A_S, z\big]\Big] \\[2pt] &= \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(A_S,z)\sim P_b}\Big[\,1 - \tfrac{1}{B}\ell(A_S,z)\Big] \end{aligned}$$

Tower property, then the definition of Adversary 1.

Legitimate because $\ell/B \in [0,1]$: the bounded-loss assumption.

Section 01

Proof of Theorem 2 — Step 2, Linearity

Subtract the two rates. The constant $1$ cancels.

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} &= \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_1}\Big[1-\tfrac{\ell}{B}\Big] - \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_0}\Big[1-\tfrac{\ell}{B}\Big] \\[2pt] &= \tfrac{1}{B}\Big(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_0}[\ell] - \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_1}[\ell]\Big) \end{aligned}$$

The sign flips: high loss votes non-member, so $P_0$ ends up on top.

Section 01

Proof of Theorem 2 — Step 3, Identify

Unfold the two conditional laws.

$$\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_1}[\ell] = \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\substack{S\sim\mathcal{D}^n \\ z\sim S}}[\ell(A_S,z)] = R_{\mathrm{train}}, \qquad \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_0}[\ell] = \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\substack{S\sim\mathcal{D}^n \\ z\sim\mathcal{D}}}[\ell(A_S,z)] = R_{\mathrm{pop}}$$

These are literally the two terms of Definition 3. Hence

$$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathcal{A}_1) = \frac{R_{\mathrm{pop}} - R_{\mathrm{train}}}{B} = \frac{\Delta}{B}. \qquad \blacksquare$$
Section 01

Proof of Theorem 2 — Recap

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathcal{A}_1) &\stackrel{(1)}{=} \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_1}\Big[1-\tfrac{\ell}{B}\Big] - \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_0}\Big[1-\tfrac{\ell}{B}\Big] \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(2)}{=} \tfrac{1}{B}\Big(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_0}[\ell] - \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{P_1}[\ell]\Big) \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(3)}{=} \tfrac{1}{B}\big(R_{\mathrm{pop}} - R_{\mathrm{train}}\big) \;=\; \frac{\Delta}{B} \end{aligned}$$

(1) tower property  ·  (2) linearity, constant cancels  ·  (3) Definition 3

Section 01

What Theorem 2 Buys

Existence

Advantage $\Delta/B$ is achieved, by an attacker who knows nothing.

Lower bound

$$\sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} \geq \Delta/B$$

Not an upper bound

Says nothing about attackers who beat the proportional coin.

Regularisation shrinking $\Delta$ removes a guarantee of attack, not the attack.

Section 01

Sandwiching the Gap

Corollary (gap is below the TV ceiling).

$$\frac{\Delta}{B} \;\leq\; \sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathcal{A}) \;=\; \mathrm{TV}(P_1, P_0)$$

Theorem 2 puts a floor under a supremum already known to equal the total variation.

Section 01

Proof of the Corollary

Adversary 1 accepts with probability $g = 1 - \ell/B$, and $g$ maps into $[0,1]$.

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\Delta}{B} &\stackrel{(1)}{=} \big|\mathbb{E}_{P_1}[g] - \mathbb{E}_{P_0}[g]\big| \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(2)}{\leq} \sup_{0 \leq h \leq 1}\big|\mathbb{E}_{P_1}[h] - \mathbb{E}_{P_0}[h]\big| \stackrel{(3)}{=} \mathrm{TV}(P_1,P_0) \quad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

(1) Theorem 2  ·  (2) enlarge the class of test functions  ·  (3) variational form of TV

Section 01

Which Way Does the Bound Point?

$\Delta/B$ $\mathrm{TV}(P_1,P_0)$ every attack lives here slack: unknown, can be huge guaranteed advantage

$\Delta/B$ is a floor on the best attack. It is not a ceiling on any attack.

Section 01

The Folklore Misstatement

Often written

$$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} \;\leq\; \Delta$$

"Zero gap implies zero leakage."

What is proved

$$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathcal{A}_1) \;=\; \Delta/B$$

For one specific adversary.

Next two slides: explicit counterexamples to the inequality on the left.

Section 01

Counterexample A: Zero Gap, Full Advantage

Take $B=1$ and the following loss laws.

PopulationLoss lawMean
Members ($P_1$)$\ell = 0$ or $\ell = 1$, each with prob. $1/2$$1/2$
Non-members ($P_0$)$\ell = 1/2$ with prob. $1$$1/2$
$$\Delta = R_{\mathrm{pop}} - R_{\mathrm{train}} = \tfrac12 - \tfrac12 = 0$$

Yet the two laws have disjoint supports.

Section 01

Counterexample A: The Attack

Use the rule $\hat b = \mathbf{1}\big[\ell \neq \tfrac12\big]$.

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{TPR} &= \Pr\big[\ell \neq \tfrac12 \,\big|\, b=1\big] = 1, \qquad \mathrm{FPR} = \Pr\big[\ell \neq \tfrac12 \,\big|\, b=0\big] = 0 \\[2pt] \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} &= 1 \;\;\gg\;\; 0 = \Delta \end{aligned}$$

Perfect membership inference at zero generalization gap.

Consistent with Theorem 2: Adversary 1 scores exactly $\Delta/B = 0$ here.

A large gap, so the model is badly overfitted by the usual reading.

Section 01

Lesson: Moments vs. Distributions

$\Delta$ is a first moment

Blind to variance, tails, multimodality.

Invariant under mean-preserving spreads.

$\mathrm{TV}$ is the whole law

Sees separation wherever it occurs.

Exactly the attainable advantage.

Defences that shrink $\Delta$ need not shrink $\mathrm{TV}$. Section 02 works with $\mathrm{TV}$.

Section 01

So What Is an Upper Bound?

Theorem 1 (Yeom et al., 2018).

If $A$ is $\varepsilon$-differentially private, then for every adversary $\mathcal{A}$,

$$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathcal{A}, A, n, \mathcal{D}) \;\leq\; e^{\varepsilon} - 1.$$

Uniform over adversaries and over $\mathcal{D}$: a genuine ceiling.

The same cap follows from the DP hypothesis-testing region.

Section 01

Proof of Theorem 1 — Step 1, Couple

Given $S=(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ and fresh $z' \sim \mathcal{D}$, write $S^{(i)}$ for $S$ with $z_i$ replaced by $z'$.

Key trick: $\big(z', A_S\big)$ and $\big(z_i, A_{S^{(i)}}\big)$ are identically distributed.

Both are "a fresh point and a model trained without it". So

$$\mathrm{FPR} = \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{S}\Big[\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n \mathcal{A}(z_i, A_{S^{(i)}})\Big], \qquad \mathrm{TPR} = \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{S}\Big[\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n \mathcal{A}(z_i, A_{S})\Big]$$

Here $\mathcal{A}(\cdot) \in [0,1]$ denotes the probability of answering "member".

Section 01

Proof of Theorem 1 — Step 2, Apply DP

Assume models live in a finite set $\{A^1,\dots,A^k\}$. Replace-one DP gives

$$\Pr[A_{S} = A^j] \;\leq\; e^{\varepsilon}\,\Pr[A_{S^{(i)}} = A^j] \quad \text{for all } i,j.$$

Expand the advantage over the model atoms:

$$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} = \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{S}\bigg[\tfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^k \Big(\Pr[A_S = A^j] - \Pr[A_{S^{(i)}} = A^j]\Big)\,\mathcal{A}(z_i, A^j)\bigg]$$
Section 01

Proof of Theorem 1 — Step 3, Collect

Bound each bracket by $(e^{\varepsilon}-1)\Pr[A_{S^{(i)}} = A^j]$ and use $\mathcal{A} \leq 1$:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} &\stackrel{(1)}{\leq} (e^{\varepsilon}-1)\,\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{S}\bigg[\tfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^k \Pr[A_{S^{(i)}} = A^j]\,\mathcal{A}(z_i,A^j)\bigg] \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(2)}{\leq} (e^{\varepsilon}-1)\,\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{S}\bigg[\tfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^n 1\bigg] \;=\; e^{\varepsilon}-1. \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

(1) DP on each atom  ·  (2) probabilities sum to $1$, decisions are at most $1$

Section 01

Two Bounds, Two Directions

From below (Theorem 2)

$$\sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} \geq \Delta/B$$

A property of the trained model.

From above (Theorem 1)

$$\sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} \leq e^{\varepsilon}-1$$

A property of the algorithm.

Consistency check: an $\varepsilon$-DP learner must satisfy $\Delta/B \leq e^{\varepsilon}-1$.

Section 01

Adversary 2: Known Error Law

Regression setting. Residual $\epsilon = y - A_S(x)$, densities $f(\epsilon \mid b)$ known.

Adversary 2 (threshold on the residual).

  1. Query $A_S(x)$ and form $\epsilon = y - A_S(x)$.
  2. Output $\arg\max_{b \in \{0,1\}} f(\epsilon \mid b)$.

A maximum-likelihood rule: exactly the Neyman–Pearson test at equal priors.

Section 01

Theorem 3: Gaussian Errors

Theorem 3 (Yeom et al., 2018).

Suppose $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_S^2)$ for members and $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}^2)$ for non-members, with $\rho = \sigma_{\mathcal{D}}/\sigma_S$ known. Then Adversary 2 has advantage

$$\mathrm{erf}\!\left(\rho\sqrt{\tfrac{\ln \rho}{\rho^2-1}}\right) - \mathrm{erf}\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\ln \rho}{\rho^2-1}}\right).$$

Equals $0$ at $\rho = 1$; tends to $1$ as $\rho \to \infty$.

Section 01

Proof of Theorem 3 — The Equal-Density Radius

Set the two Gaussian densities equal and solve for $|\epsilon|$.

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{\sigma_S}e^{-\epsilon^2/2\sigma_S^2} = \frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}e^{-\epsilon^2/2\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}^2} \;&\Longleftrightarrow\; \ln\rho = \frac{\epsilon^2}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma_S^2}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}^2}\Big) \\[4pt] &\Longleftrightarrow\; \epsilon_{\mathrm{eq}} = \sigma_{\mathcal{D}}\sqrt{\frac{2\ln\rho}{\rho^2-1}} \end{aligned}$$

For $\sigma_S \lt \sigma_{\mathcal{D}}$: member density wins exactly when $|\epsilon| \lt \epsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

Section 01

Proof of Theorem 3 — Evaluate the Rates

Adversary 2 reduces to the two-sided rule $\hat b = \mathbf{1}[|\epsilon| \lt \epsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}]$.

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} &\stackrel{(1)}{=} \Pr\big[|\epsilon| \lt \epsilon_{\mathrm{eq}} \mid b{=}1\big] - \Pr\big[|\epsilon| \lt \epsilon_{\mathrm{eq}} \mid b{=}0\big] \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(2)}{=} \mathrm{erf}\Big(\tfrac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}}{\sqrt2\,\sigma_S}\Big) - \mathrm{erf}\Big(\tfrac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}}{\sqrt2\,\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}\Big) \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(3)}{=} \mathrm{erf}\!\left(\rho\sqrt{\tfrac{\ln \rho}{\rho^2-1}}\right) - \mathrm{erf}\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\ln \rho}{\rho^2-1}}\right) \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

(1) definition  ·  (2) Gaussian tail identity  ·  (3) substitute $\epsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}$

Section 01

Unknown Standard Error

Often only one standard error is published. Assume $\sigma_S \approx \sigma_{\mathcal{D}}$ and threshold at $|\epsilon| = \sigma_S$.

$$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} \;=\; \mathrm{erf}\big(1/\sqrt2\big) - \mathrm{erf}\big(\sigma_S/(\sqrt2\,\sigma_{\mathcal{D}})\big)$$

This is the limit of $\epsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}$ as $\sigma_{\mathcal{D}} \to \sigma_S$.

Calibration matters even here: the threshold, not the statistic, is the free parameter.

Section 01

Overfitting Is Not Necessary

Theorem 4 (Yeom et al., 2018).

Set $d = \log|X|$, $m = \log|Y|$. Let $\ell \leq B$, let $A$ be ARO-stable with rate $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stable}}(n)$, and assume $x$ determines $(x,y)$.

For every integer $k \gt 0$ there is an ARO-stable $A_k$ with rate at most

$$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{stable}}(n) + k\,n\,B\,2^{-d} + \mu(n,\mathcal{D})$$

and an adversary with $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{M}} = 1 - \mu(n,\mathcal{D}) - 2^{-mk}$.

Section 01

The Colluding Construction

Algorithm $A_C$

Keyed pseudorandom $F_{K}: X \to X$, $G_{K}: X \to Y$.

For each $(x_i,y_i) \in S$ and $j \in [k]$, add $\big(F_{K_j}(x_i),\, G_{K_j}(x_i)\big)$ to the training set.

Adversary $\mathcal{A}_C$

Given $z=(x,y)$, query the model at $F_{K_j}(x)$ for $j \in [k]$.

Declare member iff every answer equals $G_{K_j}(x)$.

The tags sit at pseudorandom inputs, so ordinary behaviour is untouched.

Section 01

Where the Two Error Terms Come From

TermSource
$k\,n\,B\,2^{-d}$chance that a real query lands on a planted input
$\mu(n,\mathcal{D})$a fresh draw happens to be in $S$
$2^{-mk}$all $k$ tags match by chance for a non-member

Larger $k$: sharper attack, slightly worse stability. Both terms vanish for large domains.

This is an algorithm-substitution attack: subverted training, ordinary-looking model.

Section 01 Takeaway

Sufficient

$\Delta/B$ is attained by a trivial adversary (Theorem 2).

Overfitting always buys the attacker something.

Not necessary

The Bayes-Optimal Attack

Sablayrolles et al., ICML 2019

Section 02

Change of Question

Yeom asked

How much advantage does this adversary get?

Sablayrolles asks

What is the best possible adversary, and what does it look at?

Answer: it looks only at the loss. Black-box is asymptotically enough.

Sablayrolles, Douze, Ollivier, Schmid, Jégou, "White-box vs Black-box: Bayes Optimal Strategies for Membership Inference", ICML 2019

Section 02

The Generative Story

How the world produces $(\theta, m)$
  1. Fix data points $z_1,\dots,z_n$.
  2. Draw membership bits $m_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\lambda)$, independently.
  3. Draw the model from the Gibbs posterior over selected points.
$$P(\theta \mid z_{1:n}, m_{1:n}) \;\propto\; \exp\Big(-\tfrac{1}{T}\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n m_i\,\ell(\theta, z_i)\Big)$$
Section 02

Why This Posterior

TemperatureWhat training becomes
$T = 1$Bayesian posterior with loss as negative log-likelihood
$T \to 0$mass concentrates on the empirical risk minimiser
small $T$models reachable by noisy or averaged SGD

A tractable stand-in for "the distribution of models a trainer might return".

Section 02

Temperature Concentrates the Posterior

$\hat\theta$ (empirical minimiser) $T$ large $T$ small $T \to 0$ $\theta$

Lower temperature means the model memorises the selected points harder.

Section 02

The Object We Want

Definition (posterior membership probability).

$$\mathcal{M}(\theta, z_1) \;:=\; P\big(m_1 = 1 \,\big|\, \theta,\, z_1\big)$$

Collect the nuisance into $\mathcal{T} = (z_2,\dots,z_n,\, m_2,\dots,m_n)$.

By Neyman-Pearson, thresholding $\mathcal{M}$ is optimal at every operating point.

Section 02

Two Pieces of Notation

$$\sigma(u) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-u}}, \qquad t_\lambda = \log\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}$$

$\sigma$

Turns a log-odds into a probability.

$t_\lambda$

Prior log-odds of membership. Zero when $\lambda = 1/2$.

The same $\sigma$ is the optimal discriminator of a log-likelihood ratio.

Section 02

Theorem 5: Membership as a Sigmoid

Theorem 5 (Sablayrolles et al., 2019, Thm 1).

$$\mathcal{M}(\theta, z_1) \;=\; \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{T}}\left[\sigma\!\left(\log\frac{P(\theta \mid m_1{=}1, z_1, \mathcal{T})}{P(\theta \mid m_1{=}0, z_1, \mathcal{T})} + t_\lambda\right)\right]$$

No assumption on the form of the posterior. Pure Bayes plus tower property.

Section 02

Proof of Theorem 5 — Outline

Three moves
  1. Condition on the nuisance $\mathcal{T}$ and average back out.
  2. Inside, apply Bayes with two unnormalised weights $\alpha, \beta$.
  3. Recognise $\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)$ as a sigmoid of $\log(\alpha/\beta)$.

Move 3 is the whole trick: a normalised pair is always a sigmoid of its log-ratio.

Section 02

Step 1 — Condition on the Nuisance

By the tower property over $\mathcal{T}$,

$$\mathcal{M}(\theta, z_1) = \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{T} \mid \theta, z_1}\big[\, P(m_1 = 1 \mid \theta, z_1, \mathcal{T})\,\big].$$

Inside the expectation everything but $m_1$ is fixed, so the problem is binary.

The outer average is exactly what shadow models estimate by Monte Carlo.

Section 02

Step 2 — Bayes on Two Weights

Write the two unnormalised posterior weights

$$\alpha := P(\theta \mid m_1{=}1, z_1, \mathcal{T})\,\lambda, \qquad \beta := P(\theta \mid m_1{=}0, z_1, \mathcal{T})\,(1-\lambda).$$

Bayes' rule gives

$$P(m_1 = 1 \mid \theta, z_1, \mathcal{T}) \;=\; \frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta}.$$
Section 02

Step 3 — The Sigmoid Identity

Key trick: for positive $\alpha, \beta$, dividing top and bottom by $\alpha$ gives a sigmoid.

$$\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta} = \frac{1}{1 + \beta/\alpha} = \frac{1}{1+e^{-\log(\alpha/\beta)}} = \sigma\!\big(\log(\alpha/\beta)\big)$$

Substituting $\alpha, \beta$ splits the log-ratio into a likelihood part and $t_\lambda$.

Section 02

Proof of Theorem 5 — Recap

$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal{M}(\theta, z_1) &\stackrel{(1)}{=} \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{T}}\big[P(m_1{=}1 \mid \theta, z_1, \mathcal{T})\big] \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(2)}{=} \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{T}}\Big[\tfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}\Big] \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(3)}{=} \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{T}}\big[\sigma(\log(\alpha/\beta))\big] \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(4)}{=} \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{T}}\Big[\sigma\Big(\log\tfrac{P(\theta \mid m_1{=}1, z_1, \mathcal{T})}{P(\theta \mid m_1{=}0, z_1, \mathcal{T})} + t_\lambda\Big)\Big] \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

(1) tower  ·  (2) Bayes  ·  (3) sigmoid identity  ·  (4) split the log-ratio

Section 02

Not Yet Useful

Theorem 5 still needs both posteriors, one per hypothesis. Now impose the Gibbs form.

The posterior over the other points.

$$p_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta) \;:=\; \frac{\exp\big(-\tfrac1T \sum_{i \geq 2} m_i\,\ell(\theta, z_i)\big)}{\int \exp\big(-\tfrac1T \sum_{i \geq 2} m_i\,\ell(t, z_i)\big)\,dt}$$

The law of models trained on everything except $z_1$.

Section 02

The Calibration Constant

Definition (soft-min loss).

$$\tau_p(z_1) \;:=\; -T \log \int e^{-\ell(t, z_1)/T} \, p(t)\, dt$$

Reading

A soft minimum of $\ell(\cdot, z_1)$ over outsider models.

Limits

$T \to 0$: the true minimum. $T \to \infty$: the plain mean.

Section 02

The Membership Score

Definition (score).

$$s(z_1, \theta, p) \;:=\; \frac{1}{T}\Big(\tau_p(z_1) - \ell(\theta, z_1)\Big)$$

"How much lower is this model's loss on $z_1$ than a typical outsider's?"

A calibrated loss, not a raw loss. Per-class conditioning is a coarse version.

Section 02

Theorem 6: The Optimal Attack

Theorem 6 (Sablayrolles et al., 2019, Thm 2).

Under the Gibbs posterior,

$$\mathcal{M}(\theta, z_1) \;=\; \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{T}}\Big[\sigma\big(s(z_1, \theta, p_{\mathcal{T}}) + t_\lambda\big)\Big].$$

The optimal statistic is a temperature-scaled, calibrated loss.

Section 02

Proof of Theorem 6 — Outline

Three moves
  1. Single out $z_1$ in the Gibbs density: one factor $e^{-\ell(\theta,z_1)/T}$ times $p_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta)$.
  2. Form $\alpha$ and $\beta$; observe that $p_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta)$ appears in both.
  3. Cancel it, take logs, apply Theorem 5.

Key trick: the nuisance posterior $p_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta)$ cancels in the ratio.

Section 02

Step 1 — Split Off $z_1$

Split the Gibbs density on the two hypotheses:

$$\begin{aligned} P(\theta \mid m_1{=}1, z_1, \mathcal{T}) &= \frac{e^{-\ell(\theta, z_1)/T}\; p_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta)}{\int e^{-\ell(t, z_1)/T}\; p_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\, dt} \\[6pt] P(\theta \mid m_1{=}0, z_1, \mathcal{T}) &= p_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta) \end{aligned}$$

Under $m_1=0$ the point contributes nothing at all.

Section 02

Step 2 — Cancel the Nuisance

With $\alpha = \lambda\,P(\theta \mid m_1{=}1,\cdot)$ and $\beta = (1-\lambda)\,p_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta)$,

$$\frac{\alpha}{\beta} \;=\; \frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\cdot\frac{e^{-\ell(\theta,z_1)/T}\,\cancel{p_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta)}}{\Big(\int e^{-\ell(t,z_1)/T} p_{\mathcal{T}}(t)dt\Big)\,\cancel{p_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta)}}$$

Everything about the other $n-1$ points disappears from the ratio.

Section 02

Step 3 — Take Logs

$$\begin{aligned} \log\frac{\alpha}{\beta} &\stackrel{(1)}{=} t_\lambda - \frac{\ell(\theta,z_1)}{T} - \log \int e^{-\ell(t,z_1)/T} p_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\,dt \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(2)}{=} t_\lambda - \frac{\ell(\theta,z_1)}{T} + \frac{\tau_{p_{\mathcal{T}}}(z_1)}{T} \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(3)}{=} s(z_1,\theta,p_{\mathcal{T}}) + t_\lambda \end{aligned}$$

(1) logs of Step 2  ·  (2) definition of $\tau_p$  ·  (3) definition of $s$. Then Theorem 5. $\blacksquare$

Section 02

The Consequence

Corollary (loss is sufficient).

There is a non-increasing $g_{z_1}$ with

$$\mathcal{M}(\theta, z_1) \;=\; g_{z_1}\big(\ell(\theta, z_1)\big).$$

Proof. In Theorem 6, $\theta$ enters only through $\ell(\theta,z_1)$.

$\sigma$ increases, $s$ decreases in the loss, so the expectation is non-increasing. $\blacksquare$

Section 02

Thresholding the Loss Is Optimal

$g_{z_1}$ is non-increasing, so for every $c$

$$\{\theta : \mathcal{M}(\theta,z_1) \geq c\} \;=\; \{\theta : \ell(\theta, z_1) \leq \tau_c\}.$$

Every level set of the Bayes-optimal rule is a loss threshold. Nothing else is needed.

Any optimal test is a likelihood-ratio test. Here the ratio is the loss.

Section 02

From Optimal Rule to Privacy Bound

Property 1 (Sablayrolles et al., 2019).

If the posterior density satisfies $\varepsilon$-differential privacy, then

$$P(m_1 = 1 \mid \theta, z_1) \;\leq\; \lambda + \frac{\varepsilon}{4}.$$

A per-record statement about the posterior, complementing Yeom's Theorem 1.

Section 02

Proof of Property 1

DP bounds the log-ratio inside the sigmoid by $\varepsilon$. Use the Lipschitz bound

$$\sigma(u) \;\leq\; \sigma(v) + \tfrac{1}{4}\max(u - v, 0),$$

valid because $\sigma' = \sigma(1-\sigma) \leq 1/4$. Take $u = \log(\alpha/\beta) + t_\lambda$ and $v = t_\lambda$:

$$\mathcal{M}(\theta,z_1) \leq \mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{T}}\big[\sigma(t_\lambda) + \varepsilon/4\big] = \lambda + \varepsilon/4. \qquad \blacksquare$$
Section 02

A Weaker, Cheaper Guarantee

Definition ($(\varepsilon,\delta)$-membership privacy).

With probability $1-\delta$ over $\mathcal{T}$,

$$\int \ell(t, z_1)\, p_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\,dt \;-\; \ell(\theta, z_1) \;\leq\; \varepsilon.$$

"The model's loss on $z_1$ is not much below what outsiders achieve."

Only constrains the point in question, not every pair of neighbouring datasets.

Section 02

Property 2 and Its Proof

Property 2 (Sablayrolles et al., 2019).

$$P(m_1 = 1 \mid \theta, z_1) \;\leq\; \lambda + \frac{\varepsilon}{4T} + \delta.$$

Proof. Jensen with $f = -\ell/T$ gives $\tau_{p_{\mathcal{T}}}(z_1) \leq \int \ell\, p_{\mathcal{T}}$, hence $s \leq \varepsilon/T$ on the good event.

Apply the $\tfrac14$-Lipschitz bound there, and the trivial bound $1$ elsewhere. $\blacksquare$

Section 02

Temperature Is a Privacy Knob

$$P(m_1 = 1 \mid \theta, z_1) \;\leq\; \lambda + \frac{\varepsilon}{4T} + \delta$$

High $T$

Diffuse posterior, weak memorisation, small excess over $\lambda$.

Low $T$

Sharp posterior, bound degrades like $1/T$.

The same trade-off as noise scale in DP-SGD, in a different coordinate.

Section 02

MALT and MAST

MALT

$$s = -\ell(\theta, z_1) + \tau$$

Classification: threshold the log-probability of the true class, $\log \phi_\theta(x)_y$.

MAST

$$s = -\ell(\theta, z_1) + \tau(z_1)$$

$\tau(z_1)$ estimated by retraining on subsamples that exclude $z_1$.

MAST is shadow-model machinery aimed at one calibration constant.

Section 02

MATT: A Local Quadratic Model

Laplace-approximate both posteriors at their modes, assuming a common Hessian $H$:

$$\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta} \;\approx\; (\theta - \theta_0^*)^{\!\top} H\,(\theta_1^* - \theta_0^*) - \tfrac12 (\theta_1^*-\theta_0^*)^{\!\top} H (\theta_1^*-\theta_0^*)$$

An influence-function step gives $\theta_1^* - \theta_0^* \approx -H^{-1}\nabla_\theta \ell(\theta_0^*, z_1)$.

$\theta - \theta_0^*$ is $O(1/\sqrt n)$ and the quadratic term $O(1/n)$, so the linear term dominates.

Section 02

MATT Collapses to an Inner Product

$$s_{\mathrm{MATT}}(\theta, z_1) \;=\; -\,(\theta - \theta_0^*)^{\!\top}\, \nabla_\theta \ell(\theta_0^*, z_1)$$

Reading

Did the model move in the direction that lowers this point's loss?

Caveat

Needs a unique minimiser. Neural nets have permutation symmetries, so $\theta_0^*$ is not identified.

Section 02

Worked Example: Gaussian Mean

Let $\ell(\theta,z) = \tfrac12\|z - \theta\|^2$ and $\theta = \tfrac{1}{n'}\sum_i m_i z_i$, $n' = \sum_i m_i$.

$$\begin{aligned} \tau(z_i) &= \frac{n'}{2(n'+1)}\,\|z_i - \mu\|^2 \\[4pt] \tau &= \frac{n'}{2(n'+1)}\, d \quad \text{(averaged over } z_i \text{ in dimension } d) \end{aligned}$$
Section 02

Why the Example Matters

Closed form

The optimal threshold is computable, not learned.

Scales with $d$

High dimension inflates the typical outsider loss.

Nearly constant

$\tau(z_i)$ varies only through $\|z_i-\mu\|^2$: outliers differ.

Per-example calibration only pays where examples are genuinely atypical.

Section 03

Recall: The Cost of a Wrong Model

Recall (mis-specification bound).

Running the optimal test for a surrogate pair $(Q_1, Q_0)$ instead of the truth $(P_1,P_0)$ costs at most

$$\mathrm{Adv}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}) \;\geq\; \sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}(\mathcal{A}) \;-\; 2\max_{b \in \{0,1\}} \mathrm{TV}(Q_b, P_b).$$

Proved in the shadow-model lecture. Here it becomes a budget to spend.

Section 03

Total Variation Is a Metric

Lemma (triangle inequality).

$$\mathrm{TV}(P, R) \;\leq\; \mathrm{TV}(P, Q) + \mathrm{TV}(Q, R)$$

Proof. For any event $E$, $|P(E) - R(E)| \leq |P(E)-Q(E)| + |Q(E)-R(E)|$, which is at most $\mathrm{TV}(P,Q) + \mathrm{TV}(Q,R)$. Take $\sup_E$. $\blacksquare$

So relaxations add: the losses cannot compound worse than linearly.

So member and non-member gradient distributions ought to differ. Question: by how much?

Section 04

The Last Layer, Set Up

Let $a \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ be the penultimate activation, $W \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times d}$ the last weight matrix,

$$u = W a + b, \qquad p_k = \frac{e^{u_k}}{\sum_j e^{u_j}}, \qquad \ell = -\log p_y.$$

Write $e_y$ for the one-hot label vector.

Everything below is the standard softmax cross-entropy computation, kept explicit.

Section 04

Proposition: Rank-One Gradient

Proposition.

$$\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial W} \;=\; \big(p - e_y\big)\, a^{\!\top}$$

a rank-one matrix, unless $p = e_y$ exactly.

The error vector sets the direction; the activation sets the profile across inputs.

Section 04

Proof — Step 1, Differentiate the Logits

Since $\ell = -u_y + \log\sum_j e^{u_j}$,

$$\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial u_k} \;=\; -\mathbf{1}[k = y] + \frac{e^{u_k}}{\sum_j e^{u_j}} \;=\; p_k - (e_y)_k.$$

The famous cancellation: softmax and cross-entropy compose to a plain residual.

Section 04

Proof — Step 2, Chain Rule

$u_k = \sum_j W_{kj} a_j + b_k$, so $\partial u_k / \partial W_{kj} = a_j$ and other logits do not depend on $W_{kj}$. Hence

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial \ell}{\partial W_{kj}} &\stackrel{(1)}{=} \frac{\partial \ell}{\partial u_k}\cdot\frac{\partial u_k}{\partial W_{kj}} \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(2)}{=} \big(p_k - (e_y)_k\big)\, a_j \end{aligned}$$

(1) chain rule  ·  (2) Step 1. Collecting indices gives the outer product. $\blacksquare$

Section 04

Picture of the Outer Product

$p - e_y$ $a^{\top}$ $\partial \ell / \partial W$ rank $1$ $\times$

Every row is the same activation vector, rescaled by one error coordinate.

Section 04

Consequence: A Product Norm

Corollary.

$$\Big\|\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial W}\Big\|_F \;=\; \|p - e_y\|_2 \cdot \|a\|_2$$

Proof. $\|vw^{\top}\|_F^2 = \sum_{k,j} v_k^2 w_j^2 = \|v\|^2\|w\|^2$. $\blacksquare$

Gradient magnitude factorises into "how wrong" times "how strongly activated".

Section 04

For a confident correct prediction, $\|p - e_y\|_2$ is a monotone function of the loss.

Error part

Carries essentially the same information as the loss.

Activation part

Depends on $x$, not on membership, once the loss is fixed.

Section 04

Data Processing for Total Variation

Lemma (data-processing inequality).

For any Markov kernel $K$ and any $P, Q$,

$$\mathrm{TV}(KP, KQ) \;\leq\; \mathrm{TV}(P, Q).$$

Proof. For an event $E$, $|KP(E) - KQ(E)| = \big|\int K(E \mid x)\,(dP - dQ)(x)\big|$.

The integrand lies in $[0,1]$, and $\mathrm{TV}(P,Q) = \sup_{0 \leq f \leq 1} |\int f\,d(P-Q)|$. Take $\sup_E$. $\blacksquare$

Section 04

Proposition: Trajectories Dominate Snapshots

Proposition.

Let $\theta_T = g(\theta_0, \Delta\theta_1,\dots,\Delta\theta_T)$ for a fixed map $g$. Then

$$\sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}\big(\mathcal{A}\,;\,\theta_T\big) \;\leq\; \sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}\big(\mathcal{A}\,;\,\Delta\theta_{1:T}\big).$$

Observing the update sequence is never worse than observing the final model.

Section 04

Proof of the Proposition

$$\begin{aligned} \sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}(\mathcal{A}; \theta_T) &\stackrel{(1)}{=} \mathrm{TV}\big(P_1^{\theta_T},\, P_0^{\theta_T}\big) \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(2)}{=} \mathrm{TV}\big(g_{\#}P_1^{\Delta},\, g_{\#}P_0^{\Delta}\big) \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(3)}{\leq} \mathrm{TV}\big(P_1^{\Delta},\, P_0^{\Delta}\big) \;\stackrel{(4)}{=}\; \sup_{\mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Adv}(\mathcal{A}; \Delta\theta_{1:T}) \quad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

(1) optimal advantage is TV  ·  (2) pushforward of the updates  ·  (3) data processing  ·  (4) same identity

Section 04

Trajectory Versus Snapshot

$\theta_0$ $\Delta\theta_1$ $\Delta\theta_2$ $\Delta\theta_3$ $\Delta\theta_T$ $\theta_T$ $g$: the only thing a snapshot keeps

The dashed arrow discards information; the solid path retains all of it.

Section 04

When Is the Gap Strict?

Equality

If $g$ is injective on the support, no information is lost.

Strict

Cancelling updates: $\Delta\theta_1 = v$, $\Delta\theta_2 = -v$ leave $\theta_T = \theta_0$ but expose $v$.

The proposition guarantees a direction, never a size. Sizes are measured, not proved.

Section 04

The Active Attacker, Formalised

A participant or server may choose its contribution. Formally it solves

$$\max_{\;\delta \,\in\, \mathcal{U}} \;\; \mathrm{TV}\Big(P_1^{\,\Delta\theta_{1:T}(\delta)},\; P_0^{\,\Delta\theta_{1:T}(\delta)}\Big)$$

where $\mathcal{U}$ is the set of updates that look legitimate.

Passive observation is the special case $\mathcal{U} = \{\text{honest update}\}$; the max can only grow.

Section 04

Gradient Ascent as a Probe

The attacker's move on target $x$
  1. Apply the update $W \leftarrow W + \gamma\, \partial \ell_x / \partial W$: raise the loss on $x$ on purpose.
  2. Wait one round and observe the next global model.
  3. Member: honest training pulls the loss back down. Non-member: it stays high.

The attacker manufactures the separation instead of waiting for it.

Settled

Overfitting is sufficient, not necessary.

Trajectories dominate snapshots.

Not settled

How loose $\Delta/B \leq \mathrm{TV}$ is for real models.

Whether real SGD is close enough to Gibbs.

Per-record rather than average-case guarantees.

Section 05

Consequences for a Defender

Do not report $\Delta$

A small gap is not evidence of privacy.

Do not gate on access

Black box is asymptotically as strong as white box.

Do restrict observation

Every extra release is another conditioning event.

The only bound that survives all of this is the algorithmic one, $e^{\varepsilon}-1$.
