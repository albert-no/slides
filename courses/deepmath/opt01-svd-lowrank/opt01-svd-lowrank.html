A Smaller Matrix, Controlled Error

Given $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, every entry observed  ·  choose $B$ with $\operatorname{rank}(B) \le k$

$\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$

data $A$ (rank 2)

=
$\begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix}$

candidate $B$ (rank 1)

+
$\begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & -1.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$

residual $A - B$

Quality = size of the residual $A - B$, measured by a matrix norm

Two questions: which $B$ is closest, and how small can the error be?

Rank-$k$ factors store $k(m+n)$ numbers: smaller than $mn$ only when $k(m+n) \lt mn$

Recall: Span and Range

$A = [\,a_1 \; \cdots \; a_n\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ has columns $a_j \in \mathbb{R}^m$; input $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$Ax = x_1 a_1 + x_2 a_2 + \cdots + x_n a_n \;\in \mathbb{R}^m$$

Range (column space): everything $A$ can output

$$\operatorname{range}(A) = \{ Ax : x \in \mathbb{R}^n \} = \operatorname{span}\{a_1, \dots, a_n\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$$
$a_1$ $a_2$ $a_1 + a_2$

Example: $x = (1,1)$ gives $Ax = a_1 + a_2$

Span = all linear combinations; the range is a subspace of the output space $\mathbb{R}^m$

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §2.7.3, eq. (2.124). Figure: our illustration with $m = n = 2$.

Rank Counts Independent Directions

Definition. $\operatorname{rank}(A) = \dim \operatorname{range}(A)$: the size of any basis of the range

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U$ $m \times r$ $C$ $r \times n$

Columns of $U$: a basis $u_1, \dots, u_r$ of the range  ·  $C$: each column's coordinates

Not nonzero entries, not columns: $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1 & 1\\ 2 & 2\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ has 4 and 2, yet rank 1

Definition: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §2.7.3 (rk$(A)=\dim \operatorname{Im}$). Factorization $A = UC$: our derivation (see note). Block sizes schematic.

Two Inputs Collapse to One Direction

$$C = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}: \qquad Cx = x_1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + x_2 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = (x_1 + x_2) \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$$
input plane output plane $e_1$ $e_2$ $(1,1)$ $C$ $Ce_1 = Ce_2 = (1,2)$ $C(1,1) = (2,4)$ $\operatorname{range}(C)$

Every output lies on the line $\operatorname{span}\{(1,2)\}$, so $\operatorname{rank}(C) = 1$

Null Space Means Lost Directions

Definition. $\operatorname{null}(A) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^n : Ax = 0 \}$: the inputs sent to zero

For $C$: $Cx = (x_1 + x_2)(1,2)$ vanishes exactly when $x_1 + x_2 = 0$

input plane output plane $\operatorname{null}(C)$ $e_1$ $e_2$ $e_1 - e_2 = (1,-1)$ $C$ $C(1,-1) = 0$ $Ce_1 = Ce_2$

$\operatorname{null}(C) = \operatorname{span}\{(1,-1)\}$: an input direction in $\mathbb{R}^n$, not an output

Definition: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §2.7.3, Def. 2.23. Example: our calculation.

Rank and Nullity Add Up

Theorem (rank–nullity). For every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, square or not:

$$\operatorname{rank}(A) + \dim \operatorname{null}(A) = n \quad (\text{number of columns})$$
$\operatorname{rank}(A)$: kept $\dim \operatorname{null}(A)$: lost all $n$ input dimensions

Check on $C$: rank $1$ + nullity $1$ = $2$ columns

Wide $A \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times 3}$: rank $\le 2$, so a null direction always exists

Used later: $\operatorname{rank}(B) \le k$ forces $\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \ge n - k$

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §2.7.3, Theorem 2.24 (for the map $x \mapsto Ax$). Proof in the note. Bar widths schematic.

Measuring Error

Two sizes for a residual

What Makes a Norm?

Definition. A norm is $\|\cdot\| : \mathbb{R}^n \to [0, \infty)$ with, for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\|x\| = 0 \iff x = 0, \qquad \|\alpha x\| = |\alpha|\, \|x\|, \qquad \|x + y\| \le \|x\| + \|y\|$$
$x$ $y$ $x + y$

Zero only at zero · scaling by $|\alpha|$, sign ignored

Triangle: the direct route is never longer than the detour

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (CUP 2020), §3.1, Definition 3.1. Triangle sketch schematic.

Three Familiar Vector Norms

For a coordinate vector $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$\ell_1$: absolute sum

$$\|x\|_1 = \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|$$

walk along the axes

$\ell_2$: Euclidean

$$\|x\|_2 = \Big(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2\Big)^{1/2}$$

straight-line length

$\ell_\infty$: max

$$\|x\|_\infty = \max_{i} |x_i|$$

largest single coordinate

Only $\ell_2$ is length: $x = (1,1)$ has $\|x\|_1 = 2$ but $\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{2}$

$\ell_1$, $\ell_2$: Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §3.1, Examples 3.1–3.2. $\ell_\infty$: standard definition (not in the cited text).

One Vector, Three Answers: 7, 5, 4

Take $x = (3, 4)$:

$$\begin{aligned} \|x\|_1 &= |3| + |4| = 7 \\ \|x\|_2 &= \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5 \\ \|x\|_\infty &= \max(|3|, |4|) = 4 \end{aligned}$$

Same vector; the norm decides the size

Always $\|x\|_\infty \le \|x\|_2 \le \|x\|_1$

$x = (3,4)$ $\ell_2$: $5$ $\ell_1$: $3 + 4$ $\ell_\infty$: $4$
Our worked example; figure drawn to scale.

Unit Balls Reveal the Metric

Unit ball $\{x : \|x\| \le 1\}$: every vector the norm calls “at most 1”

$(1,0)$ $\big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}\big)$ $(1,1)$ $\ell_1$: diamond $\ell_2$: circle $\ell_\infty$: square

Marked points: norm exactly 1  ·  diamond $\subset$ circle $\subset$ square

Our illustration, drawn to scale. Unit-ball shapes: standard; nesting from $\|x\|_\infty \le \|x\|_2 \le \|x\|_1$ (proof in the note).

Orthogonal Maps Preserve Geometry

Orthogonal: square $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ with

$$Q^\top Q = I = Q Q^\top$$

Columns orthonormal; $Q^{-1} = Q^\top$

Rotations turn vectors; the length stays 1

Tall $W = [e_1 \; e_2] \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 2}$: $W^\top W = I_2$ but $W W^\top \ne I_3$, so not orthogonal

$e_1$ $Q e_1$ $45^\circ$ unit circle
Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §3.4, Definition 3.8. Figure: our illustration, to scale.

Why Length Is Preserved

Claim. Square $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ with $Q^\top Q = I$. For every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$: $\;\|Qx\|_2 = \|x\|_2$ (Euclidean norm)

$$\|Qx\|_2^2 = (Qx)^\top (Qx) = x^\top \underbrace{Q^\top Q}_{=\,I}\, x = x^\top x = \|x\|_2^2$$

Both sides are $\ge 0$; take square roots. $\blacksquare$

Same step with two vectors: $(Qx)^\top (Qy) = x^\top y$, so angles are kept

Only $\ell_2$ is protected: $\|Qe_1\|_1 = \sqrt{2} \ne 1 = \|e_1\|_1$ for the $45^\circ$ rotation

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §3.4, Definition 3.8 and eqs. (3.31)–(3.32).

Spectral Norm: Worst Stretch

Definition. For a matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$:

$$\|A\|_2 = \max_{x \in \mathbb{R}^n,\ \|x\|_2 = 1} \|Ax\|_2$$
$x^\star$ $A$ unit circle (×3) $A x^\star$: longest image $\{Ax\}$

$\|A\|_2$: one number for the whole matrix

Not the vector $\|x\|_2$; not entrywise $\ell_2$ (that is Frobenius)

Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML (CUP 2020), §4.6, Definition 4.23; Wilkinson, MATH3030, §3.5.1, Definition 3.2 ($p = 2$). Figure: our running $A$, to scale.

Frobenius Norm: Total Energy

$$\|A\|_F = \Big(\sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}^2\Big)^{1/2}, \qquad \|A - B\|_F^2 = \sum_{i,j} (a_{ij} - b_{ij})^2$$
$1.5^2 = 2.25$
$(-1.5)^2 = 2.25$
$(-0.5)^2 = 0.25$
$0.5^2 = 0.25$

Residual $A - B$ (opening example), squared

Sum $= 5$, so $\|A - B\|_F = \sqrt{5}$

Every entry counts, unweighted

Differs from spectral: $\|I_2\|_2 = 1$, $\|I_2\|_F = \sqrt{2}$

Wilkinson, MATH3030, §3.5.1, Definition 3.1. Numbers: our calculation on the running example.
03

Reading an SVD

Rotate, stretch, rotate

Recall — Eigendecomposition

Recall. Symmetric $S \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$:

$$S = Q \Lambda Q^{\top}, \qquad Q \text{ orthogonal}, \quad \Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)$$
  • Eigenvector: $S q_i = \lambda_i q_i$ — direction $S$ keeps
  • PSD case (covariance matrices): all $\lambda_i \geq 0$

Theorem 5 — the SVD Exists

Theorem 5 (SVD).

Every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with $\operatorname{rank}(A) = r$ has

$$A = U \Sigma V^{\top}, \qquad U \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m}\!,\; V \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n} \text{ orthogonal},$$

$\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ diagonal with $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$, rest $0$

Existence stated, not proved (as in source) — the $A^{\top}A$ route, by hand below

Reading the Shapes (Full SVD)

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U$ $m \times m$, orthogonal $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r$ $\Sigma$ $m \times n$, diagonal $V^{\top}$ $n \times n$, orthogonal

$u_1, \dots, u_m$: basis of $\mathbb{R}^m$ · $v_1, \dots, v_n$: basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ · $r$ nonzero stretches

What Each Factor Does

$$Ax = U\big( \Sigma (V^{\top} x) \big)$$
  • $V^{\top}$: rotate input into the $v_i$ coordinates
  • $\Sigma$: stretch coordinate $i$ by $\sigma_i$ (kill it if $\sigma_i = 0$)
  • $U$: rotate into output space

$v_i$: right singular vectors · $u_i$: left singular vectors · $\sigma_i$: singular values

Circle to Ellipse — Rotate First

1. unit circle, inputs $x$ 2. after $V^{\top}$: still a circle $v_1$ $v_2$ $e_1$ $e_2$ $V^{\top}$

An orthogonal rotation: every length is preserved, so the circle survives

Circle to Ellipse — Stretch, Then Rotate Back

3. after $\Sigma$: stretch the axes 4. after $U$: the ellipse $Ax$ $6.71\,e_1$ $2.24\,e_2$ $6.71\,u_1$ $2.24\,u_2$ $U$

$\Sigma$ sets the axis lengths $\sigma_1 = 6.71$, $\sigma_2 = 2.24$; $U$ only turns the result

Technique Review — the Outer-Product View

$$A = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i\, u_i v_i^{\top}$$
  • Each $u_i v_i^{\top}$: a rank-1 layer
  • Layer $i$ acts: $x \mapsto u_i \,(v_i^{\top} x)$
  • $\sigma_i$ = how loudly layer $i$ speaks
$\sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top}$ loudest layer $A = $ layer 1 $+$ layer 2 $+ \cdots$ back layers: quieter $\sigma_i$

Thin (Compact) SVD

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U_c$ $m \times r$ $\Sigma_c$ $r \times r$ $V_c^{\top}$ $r \times n$ keep only the $r$ nonzero stretches
$$A = U_c\, \Sigma_c\, V_c^{\top}, \qquad \Sigma_c = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r)$$

Worked — SVD by Hand: the Plan

Running matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ — why $A^{\top}A$ helps:

$$A^{\top}A = (U\Sigma V^{\top})^{\top} (U\Sigma V^{\top}) \;\stackrel{(U^{\top}U = I)}{=}\; V\, \Sigma^{\top}\Sigma\, V^{\top}$$
  1. Form $A^{\top}A$ — symmetric PSD
  2. Eigendecompose: eigenvectors $= v_i$, eigenvalues $= \sigma_i^2$
  3. $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i}$, then $u_i = A v_i / \sigma_i$

Worked — Step 1: Form $A^{\top}A$

$$A^{\top}A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 25 & 20 \\ 20 & 25 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Symmetric ✓ — and PSD: $x^{\top}A^{\top}Ax = \|Ax\|_2^2 \geq 0$
  • Same species as a covariance matrix

Worked — Step 2: Eigenvalues

$$\det\!\begin{bmatrix} 25 - \lambda & 20 \\ 20 & 25 - \lambda \end{bmatrix} = (25-\lambda)^2 - 400 = 0$$
$$25 - \lambda = \pm 20 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \lambda_1 = 45, \quad \lambda_2 = 5$$

Both positive — as PSD promised

Worked — Step 2: Eigenvectors

$$\lambda_1 = 45:\; \begin{bmatrix} -20 & 20 \\ 20 & -20 \end{bmatrix} v = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; v_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \lambda_2 = 5:\; v_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Check: $v_1^{\top} v_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(-1 + 1) = 0$ — orthonormal ✓
  • $V = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ — the $45°$ rotation from §03

Worked — Step 3: Sigmas and $u_i$

$$\sigma_1 = \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5} \approx 6.71, \qquad \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.24$$
$$u_1 = \frac{A v_1}{\sigma_1} = \frac{(3,\, 9)}{\sqrt{2}\cdot 3\sqrt{5}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad u_2 = \frac{A v_2}{\sigma_2} = \frac{(-3,\, 1)}{\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{5}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Check: $u_1^{\top}u_2 = \tfrac{1}{10}(-3 + 3) = 0$ ✓,  $\|u_1\|_2 = \|u_2\|_2 = 1$ ✓

Worked — Step 4: Assemble

$$A \;=\; U \Sigma V^{\top} \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{U} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 3\sqrt{5} & 0 \\ 0 & \sqrt{5} \end{bmatrix}}_{\Sigma} \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{V^{\top}}$$

Two rotations and a stretch — found with nothing but a $2\times 2$ eigenproblem

Worked — Verify via Rank-1 Layers

$$\sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{20}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \sigma_2 u_2 v_2^{\top} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & -1.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\text{sum} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix} = A \;\;\checkmark$$

The outer-product view, live — layer 1 is most of $A$ (see §07)

Eigenvalues $\neq$ Singular Values

Same $A$, two different spectra:

$$\text{eigenvalues of } A:\; 3,\; 5 \text{ (triangular)} \qquad \text{singular values:}\; 6.71,\; 2.24$$
  • Eigen: directions $A$ maps to themselves
  • Singular: directions of maximal stretch (input $\to$ output)
  • They agree only for symmetric PSD matrices

Theorem 6 — Spectral Norm $=$ Top Sigma

Theorem 6.

The induced $2$-norm ("spectral norm") of $A$ is

$$\|A\|_2 \;=\; \max_{\|x\|_2 = 1} \|Ax\|_2 \;=\; \sigma_1$$

Geometry: the ellipse's longest semi-axis (§04). Proof: nobody beats $\sigma_1$; $v_1$ achieves it.

Proof (Thm 6) — No Vector Beats $\sigma_1$

Take any $\|x\|_2 = 1$; write $y = V^{\top}x$, so $\|y\|_2 = 1$ (Thm 4):

$$\|Ax\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(A = U\Sigma V^{\top})}{=}\; \|U \Sigma y\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(\text{Thm 4: } U \text{ free})}{=}\; \|\Sigma y\|_2^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2 y_i^2$$
$$\sum_i \sigma_i^2 y_i^2 \;\stackrel{(\sigma_i \leq \sigma_1)}{\leq}\; \sigma_1^2 \sum_i y_i^2 \;\leq\; \sigma_1^2 \|y\|_2^2 = \sigma_1^2$$

Proof (Thm 6) — And $v_1$ Achieves It

$$\|A v_1\|_2 = \|\sigma_1 u_1\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\|u_1\|_2 = 1)}{=}\; \sigma_1$$
  • $Av_1 = \sigma_1 u_1$: read it off $A = \sum_i \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top}$, since $v_i^{\top}v_1 = 0$ for $i \neq 1$
  • Upper bound $+$ witness $\Rightarrow$ $\|A\|_2 = \sigma_1$  $\blacksquare$

Remember this two-line algebra — the same move powers Theorem 8, Step 4

Theorem 7 — Frobenius via Sigmas

Theorem 7.

$$\|A\|_F^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2$$

Frobenius energy = sum of squared stretches — proof: one trace chain

Proof (Thm 7) — One Trace Chain

$$\|A\|_F^2 = \operatorname{tr}(A^{\top}A) \;\stackrel{(A^{\top}A = V\Sigma^{\top}\Sigma V^{\top})}{=}\; \operatorname{tr}(V \Sigma^{\top}\Sigma V^{\top})$$
$$\stackrel{(\operatorname{tr}(XY) = \operatorname{tr}(YX))}{=}\; \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{\top}\Sigma V^{\top} V) = \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{\top}\Sigma) = \sum_i \sigma_i^2 \;\;\blacksquare$$

Orthogonal invariance again — this time via the trace cycling $V^{\top}$ around to cancel $V$

04

Best Low-Rank Approximation

Eckart–Young–Mirsky

The Candidate — Truncated SVD

Definition.

Keep the $k$ loudest layers:

$$A_k \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top} \;=\; U \Sigma_k V^{\top}, \quad \Sigma_k = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_k, 0, \dots)$$

$\operatorname{rank}(A_k) = k$ (its range is $\operatorname{span}\{u_1,\dots,u_k\}$) — but is it the best rank-$k$?

Worked — $A_1$ for the Running Matrix

$$A_1 = \sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \text{(computed in §04)}$$
  • Rank $1$: both columns $= 1.5\,(1,3)$
  • Four entries, but only $2 + 2$ stored numbers

Worked — the Error of $A_1$

$$A - A_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & -1.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \sigma_2 u_2 v_2^{\top}$$
$$\|A - A_1\|_2 = \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5}, \qquad \|A - A_1\|_F = \sqrt{1.5^2 + 1.5^2 + 0.5^2 + 0.5^2} = \sqrt{5}$$

The error is exactly the discarded layer — norm $\sigma_2$. Coincidence, or a law?

Theorem 8 — Eckart–Young–Mirsky

Theorem 8 (EYM).

For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with singular values $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r$, and any $k \lt r$:

$$\min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k} \|A - B\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}, \qquad \min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k} \|A - B\|_F = \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_{i \gt k} \sigma_i^2}$$

Both minima achieved by the truncated SVD $B = A_k$.

Proof follows Wilkinson, MATH3030 notes §3.5 (source's citation). We prove the spectral case in full.

Reading the Statement

  • Truncation is optimal — no clever rank-$k$ matrix does better
  • The floor is the first sigma you threw away: $\sigma_{k+1}$
  • Fast-decaying sigmas $\Rightarrow$ tiny $k$ already excellent

Source claims a unique minimizer — dropped (spectral: non-unique; Frobenius: needs $\sigma_k \gt \sigma_{k+1}$)

Technique Review 1 — Rank–Nullity

Recall (Thm 1). For $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$:

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) = n - \operatorname{rank}(B)$$
  • Low rank $\Rightarrow$ big null space
  • $\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$ $\Rightarrow$ $B$ kills a subspace of dimension $\geq n - k$

The proof's engine: every low-rank competitor has a large blind spot

Technique Review 2 — Big Subspaces Must Meet

Fact. $W_1, W_2 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ subspaces:

$$\dim(W_1 \cap W_2) \;\geq\; \dim W_1 + \dim W_2 - n$$
  • If $\dim W_1 + \dim W_2 \gt n$: intersection has dimension $\geq 1$ — a nonzero common vector
  • Picture: two planes ($2+2 \gt 3$) in $\mathbb{R}^3$ always share a line

Picture — Two Planes in $\mathbb{R}^3$

$W_1$, $\dim = 2$ $W_2$, $\dim = 2$ shared line: $2 + 2 - 3 = 1$ too big for $\mathbb{R}^3$ to keep them apart

Technique Review 3 — Bounding a Max From Below

$$\|M\|_2 = \max_{\|x\|_2 = 1} \|Mx\|_2 \;\geq\; \|Mw\|_2 \quad \text{for any single unit } w$$
  • To lower-bound a max: exhibit one witness
  • Strategy: find a unit $w$ where $\|(A - B)w\|_2$ must be large

Technique Review 4 — $\|Aw\|^2$ in the $v$-Basis

Write a unit $w$ in the right-singular basis: $w = \sum_i w_i v_i$, $\sum_i w_i^2 = 1$. Then

$$\|Aw\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(Av_i = \sigma_i u_i)}{=}\; \Big\| \sum_i w_i \sigma_i u_i \Big\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(u_i \text{ orthonormal})}{=}\; \sum_i \sigma_i^2 w_i^2$$

Theorem 6's algebra — there an upper bound, here a lower one

Proof Overview (Spectral Case)

  1. Competitor $B$, rank $\leq k$ $\Rightarrow$ $\dim\operatorname{null}(B) \geq n - k$  (TR1)
  2. Where $A$ is strong: $V_{k+1} = \operatorname{span}\{v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}\}$, dimension $k+1$
  3. $(n-k) + (k+1) \gt n$ $\Rightarrow$ a unit $w$ in both  (TR2)
  4. Witness bound: $\|A - B\|_2 \geq \|(A-B)w\|_2 = \|Aw\|_2 \geq \sigma_{k+1}$  (TR3, TR4)
  5. Tightness: $B = A_k$ achieves $\sigma_{k+1}$ exactly

Step 1 — Every Competitor Has a Blind Spot

Let $B$ be any matrix with $\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$.

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \;\stackrel{(\text{Thm 1})}{=}\; n - \operatorname{rank}(B) \;\geq\; n - k$$
  • A subspace of dimension $\geq n - k$ that $B$ maps to $0$
  • On it, $(A - B)x = Ax$ — the competitor vanishes from the error

Step 2 — Where $A$ Is Still Strong

$$V_{k+1} \;=\; \operatorname{span}\{v_1, v_2, \dots, v_{k+1}\}, \qquad \dim V_{k+1} = k + 1$$
  • The top $k{+}1$ input directions — every stretch $\geq \sigma_{k+1}$
  • Why $k+1$, not $k$? Step 3 needs the dimensions to overfill $\mathbb{R}^n$

Step 3 — The Two Subspaces Collide

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) + \dim V_{k+1} \;\geq\; (n - k) + (k + 1) \;=\; n + 1 \;\gt\; n$$

By TR2 they share a nonzero vector; normalize it:

$$\exists\, w \in \operatorname{null}(B) \cap V_{k+1}, \quad \|w\|_2 = 1, \qquad w = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} w_i v_i, \;\; \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} w_i^2 = 1$$

Only $k{+}1$ coordinates: $w \in V_{k+1}$ has no $v_i$ component past $i = k{+}1$

Step 4 — The Witness Bound

$$\|A - B\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(\text{TR3: } w \text{ unit})}{\geq}\; \|(A - B)w\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(Bw = 0)}{=}\; \|Aw\|_2^2$$
  • $B$ is blind at $w$ — the error there is all of $Aw$
  • Remains: show $\|Aw\|_2$ can't be small when $w \in V_{k+1}$

Step 4, Finished — $A$ Is Strong at $w$

$$\|Aw\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(\text{TR4})}{=}\; \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} \sigma_i^2 w_i^2 \;\stackrel{(\sigma_i \geq \sigma_{k+1})}{\geq}\; \sigma_{k+1}^2 \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} w_i^2 \;\stackrel{(\|w\|_2 = 1)}{=}\; \sigma_{k+1}^2$$
$$\Longrightarrow\;\; \|A - B\|_2 \;\geq\; \sigma_{k+1} \quad \text{for every rank-}k\text{ competitor } B$$

Step 5 — Truncation Hits the Floor

$$A - A_k = \sum_{i \gt k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top} = U \operatorname{diag}(0, \dots, 0, \sigma_{k+1}, \dots, \sigma_r)\, V^{\top}$$
$$\|A - A_k\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\text{Thm 6: top sigma})}{=}\; \sigma_{k+1}$$
  • The residual is itself an SVD — its largest singular value is $\sigma_{k+1}$
  • Lower bound met by $A_k$ $\Rightarrow$ minimum $= \sigma_{k+1}$  $\blacksquare$

Proof Summary — the Skeleton

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k \;&\Rightarrow\; \dim\operatorname{null}(B) \geq n - k && \text{(Thm 1)}\\[2pt] (n - k) + (k + 1) \gt n \;&\Rightarrow\; \exists\, \text{unit } w \in \operatorname{null}(B) \cap V_{k+1} && \text{(TR2)}\\[2pt] \|A - B\|_2 \geq \|Aw\|_2 \;&\geq\; \sigma_{k+1} && \text{(TR3, TR4)}\\[2pt] \|A - A_k\|_2 &= \sigma_{k+1} && \text{(Thm 6)} \end{aligned}$$

  • Achievability: $\|A - A_k\|_F^2 \stackrel{(\text{Thm 7})}{=} \sum_{i \gt k} \sigma_i^2$
