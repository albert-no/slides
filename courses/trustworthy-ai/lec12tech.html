01

Criteria

Each one names its denominator

Setup: Four Rates per Group

Features $X$, group $A \in \{a, b\}$, label $Y \in \{0,1\}$, score $S \in [0,1]$, decision $\hat Y \in \{0,1\}$. Base rate $p_a = \Pr[Y=1 \mid A=a]$, with $0 \lt p_a \lt 1$.

$$\mathrm{SR}_a = \Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid A = a], \qquad \mathrm{TPR}_a = \Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = 1, A = a],$$ $$\mathrm{FPR}_a = \Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = 0, A = a], \qquad \mathrm{PPV}_a = \Pr[Y = 1 \mid \hat Y = 1, A = a].$$
  • The conditioning event is the denominator; each needs positive probability
  • $\mathrm{FNR}_a = 1 - \mathrm{TPR}_a$; PPV needs $\Pr[\hat Y = 1, A = a] \gt 0$

Five Criteria, Written Out

$$\text{Demographic parity:}\quad \mathrm{SR}_a = \mathrm{SR}_b \iff \hat Y \perp A.$$ $$\text{Equal opportunity:}\quad \mathrm{TPR}_a = \mathrm{TPR}_b.$$ $$\text{Equalized odds:}\quad \mathrm{TPR}_a = \mathrm{TPR}_b,\ \mathrm{FPR}_a = \mathrm{FPR}_b \iff \hat Y \perp A \mid Y.$$ $$\text{Predictive parity:}\quad \mathrm{PPV}_a = \mathrm{PPV}_b.$$ $$\text{Calibration within groups:}\quad \Pr[Y = 1 \mid S = s, A = a] = s \quad \forall s, a.$$

Every conditioning event has positive probability.

Hardt, Price, and Srebro, NeurIPS 2016, Defs. 2.1–2.2; Chouldechova, Big Data 2017, §2.1; Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan, ITCS 2017, condition (A).

Another Notion: Individual Fairness

$d$: task metric on individuals $V$. $\Delta(\mathcal{O})$: distributions over outcomes $\mathcal{O}$; $D$: a distance on them (e.g. total variation). Randomized $M : V \to \Delta(\mathcal{O})$ is $(D, d)$-Lipschitz if

$$D(Mx, My) \;\le\; d(x, y) \qquad \forall\, x, y \in V.$$

Similar people, similar outcome distributions: a constraint, not a conditional probability.

Dwork, Hardt, Pitassi, Reingold, and Zemel, "Fairness Through Awareness", ITCS 2012, Def. 2.1.

Another Notion: Counterfactual Fairness

Causal model $(U, V, F)$: latent background variables $U$, observed $V = A \cup X$, structural equations $F$. $\hat Y_{A \leftarrow a'}(U)$: the prediction had $A$ been set to $a'$. For every $x$, $a$, $y$ and every attainable $a'$:

$$\Pr[\hat Y_{A \leftarrow a}(U) = y \mid X = x, A = a] = \Pr[\hat Y_{A \leftarrow a'}(U) = y \mid X = x, A = a].$$

Needs a causal model; the answer depends on it.

Kusner, Loftus, Russell, and Silva, "Counterfactual Fairness", NIPS 2017, Def. 5.

Calibration Fixes PPV Only Through the Score Mix

Let $\hat Y = \mathbf{1}[S \gt t]$. If $S$ is calibrated within groups, the tower rule gives

$$\mathrm{PPV}_a = \Pr[Y = 1 \mid S \gt t, A = a] = \mathbb{E}\big[\Pr[Y=1 \mid S, A=a] \,\big|\, S \gt t, A = a\big]$$ $$= \mathbb{E}[S \mid S \gt t, A = a].$$
  • Equal PPV needs equal mean score above $t$
  • One score value above $t$: calibration gives equal PPV
  • Different score mixes above $t$: PPV can differ
02

Impossibility

Exact statements and their conditions

Chouldechova's Identity

In a group of size $N$ with base rate $p$: $\mathrm{TP} = (1-\mathrm{FNR})\,pN$ and $\mathrm{FP} = \mathrm{FPR}\,(1-p)N$. From $\mathrm{PPV} = \mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP})$:

$$\mathrm{FP} = \mathrm{TP}\,\frac{1-\mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}} \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathrm{FPR} = \frac{p}{1-p}\cdot\frac{1-\mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}}\cdot(1-\mathrm{FNR}).$$

Consequence (needs $0 \lt p \lt 1$, $0 \lt \mathrm{PPV} \lt 1$, $\mathrm{FNR} \lt 1$)

Equal PPV, equal FNR and equal FPR force $\frac{p_a}{1-p_a} = \frac{p_b}{1-p_b}$, so $p_a = p_b$. PPV and FPR parity alone allow unequal base rates.

Chouldechova, "Fair Prediction with Disparate Impact", Big Data 2017, Eq. (2.6).

Kleinberg et al.: Proof Sketch

Let $x$, $y$ be the mean score of the negatives and positives. (B) and (C) make $x$, $y$ common to both groups. (A) makes each group's total score equal its positives $\mu_t$:

$$(N_t - \mu_t)\,x + \mu_t\, y = \mu_t \iff x = p_t\,(1 + x - y), \qquad p_t = \mu_t / N_t.$$
  • $p_1 = p_2$: the two lines coincide
  • $p_1 \ne p_2$: forces $x = 0$, $y = 1$, perfect prediction

Theorem 1.2: $\varepsilon$-approximate (A)–(C) force an $f(\varepsilon)$-approximate case, $f(\varepsilon) \to 0$.

Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan, "Inherent Trade-Offs in the Fair Determination of Risk Scores", ITCS 2017, Theorems 1.1–1.2 and §2.

The Worked Example, Computed

Scores $0.2$ or $0.8$; a fraction $q_a$ of group $a$ gets $0.8$. Calibration gives $p_a = 0.2 + 0.6\,q_a$; flag if $S \gt 0.5$.

$$p_a = 0.5 \Rightarrow q_a = \tfrac12, \qquad p_b = 0.25 \Rightarrow q_b = \tfrac1{12}, \qquad \mathrm{PPV}_a = \mathrm{PPV}_b = 0.8.$$ $$\mathrm{FPR}_a = \frac{0.5 \cdot 0.2}{0.5} = 0.2, \qquad \mathrm{FPR}_b = \frac{\tfrac1{12} \cdot 0.2}{0.75} \approx 0.022.$$ $$\mathrm{FNR}_a = \frac{0.5 \cdot 0.2}{0.5} = 0.2, \qquad \mathrm{FNR}_b = \frac{\tfrac{11}{12} \cdot 0.2}{0.25} \approx 0.733.$$

Check with (2.6) for group b: $\tfrac13 \cdot \tfrac14 \cdot (1 - 0.733) \approx 0.022$.

03

Mitigation

Pre-, in-, and post-processing

Reweighing

Assume full support: every cell with $\Pr[A = a]\Pr[Y = y] \gt 0$ is observed, $\Pr[A = a, Y = y] \gt 0$. Weight each cell by expected over observed probability:

$$w(a, y) = \frac{P_{\mathrm{exp}}(a, y)}{P_{\mathrm{obs}}(a, y)} = \frac{\Pr[A = a]\,\Pr[Y = y]}{\Pr[A = a, Y = y]}.$$
  • Under full support and these weights, $A \perp Y$ in the training data
  • Lecture example: $w = 1.5,\ 0.67,\ 0.75,\ 2.0$
  • An empty required cell cannot be repaired by weighting: independence then fails
Kamiran and Calders, "Data Preprocessing Techniques for Classification without Discrimination", Knowledge and Information Systems 2012, §5.2.

Reductions: A Saddle Point

$Q \in \Delta(\mathcal{H})$: randomized classifier. $\mu(Q)$: group-conditional moments (e.g. selection rates); $M\mu(Q) \le c$: the $|K|$ linear constraints; hats: sample estimates.

$$L(Q, \lambda) = \widehat{\mathrm{err}}(Q) + \lambda^\top\big(M\hat\mu(Q) - \hat c\big), \qquad \min_{Q \in \Delta}\ \max_{\lambda \ge 0,\ \|\lambda\|_1 \le B} L(Q, \lambda).$$
  • $\lambda$-player: exponentiated gradient
  • $Q$-player: one cost-sensitive fit per round
  • Returns the average play: a randomized classifier

$B$ caps the multipliers. Tolerance $\nu \gt 0$: neither player gains over $\nu$ by deviating.

Agarwal, Beygelzimer, Dudík, Langford, and Wallach, "A Reductions Approach to Fair Classification", ICML 2018, §3, Algorithm 1, Theorem 1.

Group Thresholds: A Derived Predictor

$\tilde Y$ is derived if it is a possibly randomized function of $(\hat Y, A)$ alone. Write $\gamma_a(\hat Y) = (\mathrm{FPR}_a, \mathrm{TPR}_a)$.

$$\min_{\tilde Y}\ \mathbb{E}\,\ell(\tilde Y, Y) \quad \text{s.t.}\quad \gamma_a(\tilde Y) \in P_a(\hat Y)\ \ \forall a, \qquad \gamma_a(\tilde Y) = \gamma_b(\tilde Y),$$ $$P_a(\hat Y) = \mathrm{conv}\{(0,0),\ \gamma_a(\hat Y),\ \gamma_a(1 - \hat Y),\ (1,1)\}.$$
  • Four variables $\Pr[\tilde Y = 1 \mid \hat Y, A]$: a linear program
  • Score $R$: mix two thresholds per group
Hardt, Price, and Srebro, "Equality of Opportunity in Supervised Learning", NeurIPS 2016, Def. 4.1, Eq. (4.2)–(4.4), Prop. 4.4.
