What Changes With Autonomy

Longer horizons, fewer checks

Beyond a Defended Agent

Start with injection controls in place: least privilege, data-flow limits, approval. Still not injection-proof.

Longer horizons and interacting agents add new failure modes.

Autonomy Is a Dial, Not a Switch

Four settings decide how autonomous an agent is:

  • Steps taken per task
  • Time horizon of the task
  • Tools and permissions held
  • Interval between human checks

Turning any setting up widens what can go wrong between checks.

Small Errors Compound Over Long Tasks

Illustrative arithmetic, not a measured agent:

  • Each step right with probability 0.95
  • Steps fail independently
  • All 20 steps right: $0.95^{20} \approx 0.36$

Real errors are correlated, and agents sometimes recover.

Fixed, independent errors with no recovery compound with length.

Time Horizon: The Definition

Measure each task by how long skilled humans took to succeed.

  • Plot the agent's success against human task length
  • Fit a smooth S-curve
  • 50% horizon: length where fitted success is 50%
Kwa et al. (METR), "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks", arXiv 2503.14499 (2025), §2–§3. Curve is schematic.

How a Horizon Is Computed

  1. 170 software and research tasks, from seconds to about 30 hours
  2. Task length: geometric mean time of humans who succeeded; 21 tasks use researcher estimates
  3. 8 runs per model–task pair; the scaffold differs for some models
  4. Fit success against log task length
  5. Read off where the fitted curve crosses 50%

The 80% horizon is 4–6 times shorter than the 50% horizon.

Kwa et al., arXiv 2503.14499, §2–§3 (task suites, geometric mean of successful baselines), §3.2.1 (80% horizon), App. C.3 (modular-public for most models; triframe or duet for o1, o1-preview; a simple prompting scaffold for SWAA).

Horizons Doubled About Every Seven Months

Key: dots are models released 2019–2025; y is 50% horizon (log). Doubling on this task suite: 207 days.

Kwa et al., arXiv 2503.14499 (2025), Fig. 1, cropped, and §3.2: 95% CI 166–240 days; GPT-2 about 2 seconds, o3 about 110 minutes.

Kwa et al., arXiv 2503.14499 v4 (2019–2025 models), §3.2.1, §5–6, App. C.3 and App. E.3–E.4; METR 2026 sensitivity analysis, "Impact of modeling assumptions on time horizon results" (research note, 20 Mar 2026).

What a Time Horizon Is Not

It is

The task length at which the fitted success probability is 50%, on this task set, for this model–scaffold pair.

It is not

How long an agent can safely run unattended.

  • Benchmark tasks are auto-scored and self-contained
  • Real work: other agents, changing environments, high reliability
Kwa et al., arXiv 2503.14499 v4, §5–6 and App. B.2 (limitations: messiness, reliability, task distribution).

Sandbagging Hides Capability

Sandbagging

Strategic underperformance on an evaluation: scoring below actual capability to benefit the developer or the AI system itself.

A low score alone is not evidence of sandbagging.

van der Weij et al., "AI Sandbagging", ICLR 2025 (arXiv 2406.07358v4), §2. Illustrative diagram.

Instructions Can Lower Test Scores Selectively

  • Claude 3 Opus, “sandbagging”: told to fail hazardous questions only
  • WMDP: hazardous-knowledge proxy; CSQA, MMLU: controls
  • y = accuracy, refusals excluded

Capability on instruction: not unprompted behavior, not erased knowledge.

van der Weij et al., ICLR 2025 (arXiv 2406.07358v4), Fig. 4, Claude 3 panel; GPT-4 panel cropped out; unmodified, key added. Multiple-choice only.

Coordination, propagation, collusion

Cooperation Can Fail in Different Ways

Miscoordination: same goals, still fail

Conflict: mixed goals, fail to cooperate

Collusion: cooperation that harms others

Hammond et al.'s taxonomy sorts failures by the agents' goals.

A conceptual framework, not a frequency count.

Attacks passed between agents are a separate route, not a fourth type.

Control

Safety without trusting the model

Test Safety Against a Bad Worker

Alignment

Goal: a model that wants the right things.

Hard to verify from outside.

Control

Assume the worker may attack.

Test whether the checks still keep outcomes safe.

A worst-case test assumption, not a claim that models secretly attack.

Control asks: is the whole system safe even if the worker attacks?

Greenblatt, Shlegeris, Sachan & Roger, "AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion", ICML 2024 (arXiv 2312.06942v5), §1. Conceptual contrast.

Separate Worker, Monitor and Audit

Untrusted (U): GPT-4 writes the code

In the test: a red-team attack policy

Trusted (T): GPT-3.5 reads U's code

Trusted by assumption, not proven safe

"Human" (H): scarce audit

Simulated by GPT-4; red team can't reach it

Greenblatt et al., ICML 2024, Fig. 1 right panel ("Our red-teaming testbed"), unmodified crop; role key added. §2. H is simulated, not human trial data.

Name the Game Before the Score

Task

5,000 coding problems

Attacker

Red team runs U; knows the checks

Audit

H checks at most 2%

Failure

One hidden backdoor gets submitted

Safety: chance the whole run has no backdoor

Usefulness: correctness rescaled, GPT-3.5 = 0, GPT-4 = 1

Neither is an attack success rate or accuracy.

Greenblatt et al., ICML 2024, §1–§2 (APPS backdooring setting; safety against the best attack found, not an average).

Inspect the Most Suspicious Work

T's score is a flag, not proof; H can check only 2%.

Greenblatt et al., ICML 2024, §3 and Table 2 (trusted monitoring). Schematic.

Against the best attack found, 2% audit, this coding game only.

Evaluation Evidence Has Boundaries

Tested envelope

  • These tasks and run lengths
  • Strongest attacks found
  • This audit budget

Outside the envelope

  • Longer, open-ended work
  • Attackers who pick their moment
  • Attacks aimed at the monitor

Outside it, this result neither guarantees nor rules out safety.

Would this oversight still work over longer, adversarially chosen tasks?

Synthesis and Frontiers

Two new questions, one course takeaway

Three Questions Still Open

Research questions, not predictions: each marks a gap in today's evidence.

Long tasks, tested realistically?

Gap: benchmarks and parts measured; few long real-world tests

Intent, or weak elicitation?

Gap: a low score or a bad action does not reveal a motive

Oversight under adaptation?

Gap: monitors tested against chosen attacks, short tasks

Each asks: does the evidence match the claim?

AI Can Persuade, Not Just Answer

aRate agreement with a claim, 1–5

b10-min debate: human or GPT-4, with or without the participant's profile

cRate agreement again

GPT-4 given the participant's profile moved agreement more than a human opponent without one.

Agreement right after one debate; the extra effect of personalization is not established (2026 correction).

Salvi et al., Nature Human Behaviour (2025), Fig. 1; corrected 3 Sep 2026. 900 US participants, controlled online study.
