Threat-Model Thinking

The one habit that unifies the course

What Is a Threat Model?

  • Who is the adversary, and their goal?
  • What do they know about the system?
  • What can they actually do?

Answer these before "is it safe?"

Who

Anyone who can email you

Knows

The assistant reads the inbox

Can do

Send one message, nothing else

Worked example: EchoLeak, the threat model in three boxes

The Attack Map

Two axes, knowledge × timing, locate almost any attack.

No Threat Model, No Answer

59% vs 3%

Same model, same task. One tool removed from the wrapper.

Panda vs stop sign

Pixel access vs a printer and a parking lot. Different attackers, different defenses.

"Is it safe?" has no answer. "Safe in this deployment, against whom?" does.
  • Your door lock: fine vs a passerby, useless vs a locksmith
  • State the threat first, then evaluate
