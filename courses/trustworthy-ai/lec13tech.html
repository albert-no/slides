Notation

A binary classifier with a protected attribute.

Random variables

$A \in \{a,b\}$ = group.   $Y \in \{0,1\}$ = true label.   $\hat Y \in \{0,1\}$ = prediction.   $\hat S \in [0,1]$ = risk score, with $\hat Y = \mathbf{1}[\hat S \ge t]$ for a threshold $t$.

Base rate $p_a = \Pr[Y=1 \mid A=a]$: how common $Y=1$ is in group $a$.

01

The Three Criteria

One conditional probability each

Demographic Parity

Independence: the prediction is independent of the group, $\hat Y \perp A$.

Definition

$$\Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid A = a] \;=\; \Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid A = b].$$

Equal selection rates. Conditions on $\hat Y$ only; the true label $Y$ never appears.

Dwork, Hardt, Pitassi, Reingold, and Zemel, "Fairness Through Awareness", ITCS 2012.

Equalized Odds

Separation: the prediction is independent of the group given the truth, $\hat Y \perp A \mid Y$.

Definition (for each $y \in \{0,1\}$)

$$\Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = y,\, A = a] \;=\; \Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = y,\, A = b].$$

$y=0$: equal false-positive rate.   $y=1$: equal true-positive rate (equal opportunity is the $y=1$-only relaxation).

Hardt, Price, and Srebro, "Equality of Opportunity in Supervised Learning", NeurIPS 2016.

Calibration

Sufficiency: the true label is independent of the group given the score, $Y \perp A \mid \hat S$.

Definition (for every score $s$)

$$\Pr[Y = 1 \mid \hat S = s,\, A = a] \;=\; \Pr[Y = 1 \mid \hat S = s,\, A = b] \;=\; s.$$

A score of $s$ means a genuine $s$ chance of $Y=1$, in every group.

Chouldechova, "Fair Prediction with Disparate Impact", Big Data 2017.

The Three, Side by Side

Each criterion is one independence statement.

CriterionIndependenceConditions on
Demographic parity$\hat Y \perp A$nothing
Equalized odds$\hat Y \perp A \mid Y$truth $Y$
Calibration$Y \perp A \mid \hat S$score $\hat S$

Parity fixes $\hat Y$; odds condition on $Y$; calibration conditions on $\hat S$.

02

The Impossibility Theorem

Why two independence conditions collide

The Theorem, Formally

Kleinberg, Mullainathan & Raghavan (2017)

Suppose the base rates differ, $p_a \neq p_b$, and the classifier is non-trivial (not a perfect predictor). Then no score can simultaneously satisfy calibration, equal false-positive rates, and equal false-negative rates across groups.

The only escapes: equal base rates $p_a = p_b$, or perfect prediction.

Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan, "Inherent Trade-Offs in the Fair Determination of Risk Scores", ITCS 2017.

The Base-Rate Identity

Within a group, write $\text{PPV}$ = precision, $\text{FPR}$, $\text{FNR}$, base rate $p$. Counting the confusion cells gives an exact identity.

$$\mathrm{FPR} \;=\; \frac{1-p}{p}\cdot\frac{1-\mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}}\,(1-\mathrm{FNR}).$$

Chouldechova's identity: $\mathrm{FPR}$, $\mathrm{FNR}$, and $\mathrm{PPV}$ are locked together by the base rate $p$.

Chouldechova, "Fair Prediction with Disparate Impact", Big Data 2017.

Proof Sketch

  1. Calibration forces the score's precision $\mathrm{PPV}$ to match each group's base rate — the same score, so the same $\mathrm{PPV}$ target.
  2. The identity ties $(\mathrm{FPR}, \mathrm{FNR})$ to $\mathrm{PPV}$ and the group base rate $p$.
  3. Fix $\mathrm{PPV}$ and demand equal $\mathrm{FPR}$ and equal $\mathrm{FNR}$: the identity then forces $p_a = p_b$.
  4. Contrapositive: if $p_a \neq p_b$, the three cannot all hold.

The conflict is arithmetic in the base rate, not a modeling defect.

What the Theorem Does Not Say

The wall is about simultaneous satisfaction, not about any one criterion.

  • Any two of the three remain jointly achievable
  • Equal base rates dissolve the conflict entirely
  • Approximate versions trade a bounded slack in one for the others

The choice of which two is a value judgment; the impossibility is the math.

$p_a \neq p_b$

Different base rates make "all three" a contradiction.
