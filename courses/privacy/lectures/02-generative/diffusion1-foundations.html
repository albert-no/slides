Generative Models

Diversity, distribution learning, why sampling is hard

Realistic Is Not Enough

One-photo generator: realistic, useless. Two requirements:

  • Realism: plausible samples
  • Diversity: match data spread (race, age, pose, $\ldots$)

Learn the distribution $P_X$, not isolated points.

Mode Collapse in One Picture

Failure mode: all model mass on one plausible point.

$P_X$: two modes $P_\theta$: one point samples realistic, spread lost

Realism alone cannot detect this: distribution matching can.

Sampling Is Non-Trivial

Closed-form density does not imply easy sampling.

Cauchy with scale $\gamma > 0$:

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\,\frac{\gamma}{x^2+\gamma^2}.$$

Inverse Transform Sampling (1D)

Proposition (inverse-transform sampling).

$F_X$ continuous, strictly increasing; $U \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$. Then:

$$F_X^{-1}(U) \;\stackrel{(d)}{=}\; X.$$

Proof. $\Pr\bigl(F_X^{-1}(U)\leq x\bigr) = \Pr\bigl(U \leq F_X(x)\bigr) = F_X(x)$. $\blacksquare$

One uniform draw + one inverse CDF = one exact sample.

Inverse Transform — the Picture

$1$ $0$ $u$ $F_X$ $x = F_X^{-1}(u)$

Draw the height uniformly; read the sample off the horizontal axis.

Example — Sampling the Cauchy

Integrate the density: closed-form CDF.

$$F(x) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{\pi}\,\arctan\!\bigl(\tfrac{x}{\gamma}\bigr).$$

Solve $F(x) = u$: explicit inverse.

$$X \;=\; \gamma\,\tan\!\bigl(\pi\,(U - \tfrac{1}{2})\bigr) \;\sim\; f.$$

Numeric check: $U = 0.75 \Rightarrow X = \gamma\tan(\pi/4) = \gamma$.

Heavy tails for free: $U \to 1$ sends $X \to \infty$.

Where the Uniform Comes From

Binary expansion of fair bits:

$$U = \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} U_i\,2^{-i},\quad U_i \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac{1}{2}) \;\Rightarrow\; U \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,1].$$

Truncation error after $n$ bits:

$$\Bigl|\,U - \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n} U_i 2^{-i}\Bigr| \;\leq\; \textstyle\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty} 2^{-i} \;=\; 2^{-n}.$$

32 flips: error below $2.4 \times 10^{-10}$. Coin flips suffice for any 1D law.

High Dimension Breaks This

  • For $\mathbf{X} = (X_1, \ldots, X_d)$: no scalar CDF to invert.
  • Rejection from a box, target unit ball: accept rate $\pi^{d/2}/(2^d\,\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2}+1))$.

Bar height on a log scale. Images live at $d \sim 10^5$.

02

VE Forward Process

Gradually adding Gaussian noise (1D)

The Forward Process

$X \in \mathbb{R}$ scalar with unknown density $P_X$. Define:

$$\begin{aligned} X^{(0)} &= X, \\ X^{(n)} &= X^{(n-1)} + Z^{(n)},\qquad Z^{(n)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\ \text{i.i.d.} \end{aligned}$$
$X^{(0)}$ $\xrightarrow{\;+Z^{(1)}\;}$ $X^{(1)}$ $\xrightarrow{\;+Z^{(2)}\;}$ $X^{(2)}$ $\cdots$ $\xrightarrow{\;+Z^{(N)}\;}$ $X^{(N)}$

Variance grows each step: variance exploding (VE).

Noising in Pictures

$X^{(0)}$ $X^{(n)}$ $X^{(N)}$ $+Z$ $+Z$

Each step blurs the density; structure washes out into a wide Gaussian.

$n$ Steps in One Shot

Increments are independent Gaussians; variances add:

$$\begin{aligned} X^{(n)} - X^{(0)} &= \textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} Z^{(k)} \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}(0,\, n\sigma^2) \\ \Rightarrow\quad X^{(n)} &\stackrel{(d)}{=} X^{(0)} + \sqrt{n}\,Z,\qquad Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2). \end{aligned}$$

Pick $n$, sample one $Z$, jump directly to $X^{(n)}$.

Training trick: simulate one transition at a time, never the full chain.

Terminal Distribution

Let $\mu_0, \sigma_0^2$ be mean and variance of $X^{(0)}$. By linearity:

$$\mathbb{E}[X^{(N)}] = \mu_0,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(X^{(N)}) = \sigma_0^2 + N\sigma^2.$$

Large $N$: the term $N\sigma^2$ swamps $\sigma_0^2$. Gaussian approximation:

$$X^{(N)} \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}(\mu_0,\; \sigma_0^2 + N\sigma^2).$$

Heuristic for VE; a variance-preserving chain makes this exact (MGF argument).

Goal: Reverse the Chain

Sampling from $\mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \sigma_0^2 + N\sigma^2)$ is trivial. Call it $\tilde{X}^{(N)}$.

Want a reverse rule, run in generation order:

$\tilde{X}^{(N)}$ $\xrightarrow{\;\text{denoise}\;}$ $\tilde{X}^{(N-1)}$ $\rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow$ $\tilde{X}^{(0)}$
$$\text{target: }\; \tilde{X}^{(0)} \stackrel{(d)}{\approx} X.$$

Need reverse conditional $P_{X^{(n-1)}\,|\,X^{(n)}}$ at every step.

03

Reverse via Bayes

Taylor, complete the square, read off Gaussian

Setup

Focus on one backward edge of the chain:

$\tilde{X}^{(N)}$ $\rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow$ $\tilde{X}^{(n)}$ $\xrightarrow{\;\text{this step}\;}$ $\tilde{X}^{(n-1)}$ $\rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow$ $\tilde{X}^{(0)}$

Rename the endpoints and drop indices:

$$\begin{aligned} X &:= \tilde{X}^{(n-1)},\qquad Z := \tilde{Z}^{(n)},\qquad Y := \tilde{X}^{(n)}, \\ Y &= X + Z,\qquad Y\,|\,X \sim \mathcal{N}(X, \sigma^2). \end{aligned}$$

Want the reverse conditional $X\,|\,Y$, in the regime $\sigma^2 \ll 1$.

Proof Outline

Target: simplify the Bayes posterior under $\sigma^2 \ll 1$.

$$P_{X|Y}(x\,|\,y) = \frac{P_{Y|X}(y\,|\,x)\,P_X(x)}{P_Y(y)}.$$
  1. Step 1. Taylor expand $P_X$ around $y$.
  2. Step 2. Approximate $P_Y(y) \approx P_X(y)$.
  3. Step 3. Substitute back; absorb correction.
  4. Step 4. Complete the square; recover Gaussian.

Step 1: Taylor Expand $P_X$

Assume $P_X \in C^2$. Expand around the observed $y$:

$$P_X(x) \;=\; P_X(y) + P'_X(y)(x-y) + O\bigl((x-y)^2\bigr).$$

Gaussian likelihood confines $x$ to $|x - y| \lesssim \sigma$.

Key trick. Remainder $O((x-y)^2) = O(\sigma^2)$: drop it, keep the linear term.

Why Local Expansion Works

$y$ $P_X$ tangent at $y$ $|x - y| \lesssim \sigma$

Zoom to the noise scale: any smooth density looks linear.

Step 2: Approximate $P_Y(y)$

Integrate Taylor expansion against the Gaussian likelihood:

$$\begin{aligned} P_Y(y) &\approx \int \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\,e^{-\frac{(x-y)^2}{2\sigma^2}}\bigl(P_X(y) + P'_X(y)(x-y)\bigr)\,dx \\ &= P_X(y) + P'_X(y)\,\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathcal{N}(y,\sigma^2)}[X-y] \\ &= P_X(y). \end{aligned}$$

Key trick. Odd Gaussian moment vanishes: $\mathbb{E}[X - y] = 0$ under $\mathcal{N}(y,\sigma^2)$.

Step 3: Substitute Back

Using $P_Y(y) \approx P_X(y)$:

$$\begin{aligned} P_{X|Y}(x\,|\,y) &\approx P_{Y|X}(y\,|\,x)\!\left(1 + \tfrac{P'_X(y)}{P_X(y)}(x-y)\right) \\ &\approx \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\,e^{-\frac{(x-y)^2}{2\sigma^2}}\,\exp\!\left(\tfrac{P'_X(y)}{P_X(y)}(x-y)\right). \end{aligned}$$

Key trick. $1 + a \approx e^{a}$: agree to first order; error stays $O(\sigma^2)$.

Step 4: Merge the Exponents

Shorthand for the log-derivative:

$$\rho \;:=\; \frac{P'_X(y)}{P_X(y)} \;=\; \partial_y \log P_X(y).$$

Combine Gaussian and correction into one exponent:

$$-\frac{(x-y)^2}{2\sigma^2} + \rho\,(x-y) \;=\; -\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\Bigl[(x-y)^2 - 2\sigma^2\rho\,(x-y)\Bigr].$$

Step 4 (continued): Complete the Square

Key trick. Add and subtract $(\sigma^2\rho)^2$ inside the bracket:

$$(x-y)^2 - 2\sigma^2\rho\,(x-y) + (\sigma^2\rho)^2 - (\sigma^2\rho)^2 \;=\; \bigl(x - y - \sigma^2\rho\bigr)^2 - (\sigma^2\rho)^2.$$

$(\sigma^2\rho)^2$ is $x$-free: absorbed by normalization.

$$P_{X|Y}(x\,|\,y) \;\approx\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}\bigl(x - y - \sigma^2\rho\bigr)^2\Bigr).$$

Proof Recap

$$\begin{aligned} P_{X|Y}(x\,|\,y) &= \frac{P_{Y|X}(y\,|\,x)\,P_X(x)}{P_Y(y)} \\ &\stackrel{(1)}{\approx}\; \frac{P_{Y|X}(y\,|\,x)\,\bigl[P_X(y) + P'_X(y)(x-y)\bigr]}{P_Y(y)} \\ &\stackrel{(2)}{\approx}\; P_{Y|X}(y\,|\,x)\!\left[1 + \tfrac{P'_X(y)}{P_X(y)}(x-y)\right] \\ &\stackrel{(3)}{\approx}\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\,e^{-\frac{(x-y)^2}{2\sigma^2}}\;\exp\!\left(\tfrac{P'_X(y)}{P_X(y)}(x-y)\right) \\ &\stackrel{(4)}{=}\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(y + \sigma^2\,\partial_y \log P_X(y),\; \sigma^2\right)(x). \end{aligned}$$

Small-Noise Reverse Conditional

Theorem (small-noise reverse conditional).

$Y = X + Z$, $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ independent, $P_X \in C^2$, $\sigma^2 \ll 1$:

$$X\,|\,Y = y \;\stackrel{\text{approx.}}{\sim}\; \mathcal{N}\!\bigl(y + \sigma^2\,\partial_y \log P_X(y),\; \sigma^2\bigr).$$

Error $O(\sigma^2)$ in total variation for densities with bounded $C^2$ norm.

Small $\sigma^2$: reverse conditional is Gaussian, mean shifted by the score $\partial_y \log P_X$.

Reverse Sampling Rule

Theorem, with $X = \tilde{X}^{(n-1)}$ and $Y = \tilde{X}^{(n)}$:

$$X\,|\,Y \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(Y + \sigma^2\,\partial_y \log P_X(Y),\; \sigma^2\right).$$

Substitute $X \to \tilde{x}^{(n-1)}$, $Y \to \tilde{x}^{(n)}$:

$$\tilde{x}^{(n-1)} = \tilde{x}^{(n)} + \sigma^2\,\partial \log P_{X^{(n)}}\!\bigl(\tilde{x}^{(n)}\bigr) + \tilde{z}^{(n)},\quad \tilde{z}^{(n)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2).$$

The Score Shift in a Picture

$P_{X^{(n)}}$ $s(x) = \partial_x \log P_{X^{(n)}}(x)$: points uphill

Reverse step drifts by $\sigma^2 s$, toward data modes, then adds fresh noise.

Why Not Subtract Noise?

Naive: $\tilde{x}^{(n-1)} \stackrel{?}{=} \tilde{x}^{(n)} - \tilde{z}^{(n)}$ with fresh $\tilde{z}^{(n)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$.

Gaussian symmetry: $-Z \stackrel{(d)}{=} Z$. Independent noises add variance:

$$\mathrm{Var}\bigl(\tilde{x}^{(n)} - \tilde{z}^{(n)}\bigr) = \mathrm{Var}\bigl(\tilde{x}^{(n)}\bigr) + \sigma^2.$$

Variance grows: corrupts further, does not denoise.

Estimation vs Distribution Matching

Reverse process is distribution matching, not denoising.

Estimation (MMSE)

$\hat{X}(Y) = \mathbb{E}[X\,|\,Y]$ is deterministic.

Variance shrinks below $\mathrm{Var}(X)$.

Distribution matching

Generate $\tilde{X}$ with $\tilde{X} \stackrel{(d)}{=} X$.

Need fresh randomness to restore the spread.

$P_X$ law of $\hat{X}(Y)$ $\hat{X}(Y) + \tilde{Z}$ $P_X$ dashed

The MMSE Estimator

Proposition (MMSE = conditional mean).

$\mathbb{E}[X^2] \lt \infty$. Over all measurable $\hat{X}(\cdot)$:

$$\arg\min_{\hat{X}}\; \mathbb{E}\bigl[(\hat{X}(Y) - X)^2\bigr] \;=\; \mathbb{E}[X\,|\,Y].$$

Plan. Split the error through $\mathbb{E}[X|Y]$; kill the cross term by the tower property.

Proof — Orthogonality of the Error

Insert $\mathbb{E}[X|Y]$ and expand the square:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\bigl[(\hat{X}(Y) - X)^2\bigr] &= \mathbb{E}\bigl[(\hat{X}(Y) - \mathbb{E}[X|Y])^2\bigr] + \mathbb{E}\bigl[(\mathbb{E}[X|Y] - X)^2\bigr] \\ &\quad + 2\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\hat{X}(Y) - \mathbb{E}[X|Y])(\mathbb{E}[X|Y] - X)\bigr]. \end{aligned}$$
  • Estimator gap $\mathbb{E}[(\hat{X}(Y) - \mathbb{E}[X|Y])^2]$: the only $\hat{X}$-dependent piece.
  • Irreducible error $\mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{E}[X|Y] - X)^2]$: fixed by the noise level.
  • Cross term: killed next.

Proof (continued) — Killing the Cross Term

Key trick. Tower property: condition the cross term on $Y$.

$$\mathbb{E}\Bigl[(\hat{X}(Y) - \mathbb{E}[X|Y])\,\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbb{E}[X|Y] - X \,\big|\, Y\bigr]}_{=\;0}\Bigr] \;=\; 0.$$

Remaining: $\mathbb{E}[(\hat{X}(Y) - \mathbb{E}[X|Y])^2] + \mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{E}[X|Y] - X)^2]$.

Second term is $\hat{X}$-free; first term $\geq 0$, zero iff $\hat{X}(Y) = \mathbb{E}[X|Y]$. $\blacksquare$

Gaussian MMSE Mismatch

$X \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_x^2)$, $Y = X+Z$, $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_z^2)$:

$$\hat{X}(Y) = \tfrac{\sigma_x^2}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2}\,Y \;\sim\;\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\,\tfrac{\sigma_x^4}{\sigma_x^2+\sigma_z^2}\right).$$

Variance too small. Add fresh noise to match:

$$\tilde{X} = \mathbb{E}[X\,|\,Y] + \tilde{Z},\quad \tilde{Z} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\,\tfrac{\sigma_x^2\,\sigma_z^2}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2}\right) \;\Rightarrow\; \tilde{X}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_x^2).$$

Numeric check: $\sigma_x = \sigma_z = 1$ gives $\mathrm{Var}(\hat{X}) = \tfrac{1}{2}$; fresh $\tfrac{1}{2}$ restores $1$.

04

Score & Tweedie

Learning the score via denoising

The Score Function

For each noise level $n$:

$$s(x;n) = \tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\log P_{X^{(n)}}(x).$$

Depends on $n$ since marginal $P_{X^{(n)}}$ changes with noise level.

Exact $s(x;n)$ for all $n$: no neural network needed for the reverse process.

Bottleneck: estimating $s$ from data.

Tweedie's Formula

Theorem (Robbins, 1956 / Tweedie).

$X$ random, $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ independent, $Y = X+Z$:

$$\mathbb{E}[X\,|\,Y=y] \;=\; y + \sigma^2\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\log P_Y(y).$$

Posterior mean of clean $X$ = noisy $y$ + scaled score of the noisy marginal.

Exact identity, no small-$\sigma$ assumption. Proof: differentiate the convolution.

Robbins, "An empirical Bayes approach to statistics," Berkeley Symp. 1956.

Tweedie — Proof Outline

Target identity:

$$\sigma^2\,\partial_y \log P_Y(y) \;=\; \mathbb{E}[X\,|\,Y=y] - y.$$
  1. Step 1. Write $P_Y$ as a convolution with the Gaussian kernel $\varphi_\sigma$.
  2. Step 2. Gaussian derivative identity: $\partial\varphi_\sigma$ = linear factor $\times$ $\varphi_\sigma$.
  3. Step 3. Differentiate $P_Y$ under the integral.
  4. Step 4. Recognize the posterior mean via Bayes; rearrange.

Proof — Differentiate the Convolution

Marginal as convolution, with $\varphi_\sigma(u) = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\,e^{-u^2/(2\sigma^2)}$:

$$P_Y(y) \;=\; \textstyle\int P_X(x)\,\varphi_\sigma(y-x)\,dx.$$

Key trick. Gaussian derivative = linear factor times the same Gaussian:

$$\begin{aligned} \partial_y \varphi_\sigma(y-x) &= -\tfrac{y-x}{\sigma^2}\,\varphi_\sigma(y-x) \\ \Rightarrow\quad \partial_y P_Y(y) &= -\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\textstyle\int (y-x)\,P_X(x)\,\varphi_\sigma(y-x)\,dx. \end{aligned}$$

Proof (continued) — Recognize the Posterior Mean

Split the integral into two pieces:

$$\begin{aligned} \partial_y P_Y(y) &= -\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\Bigl[\,y\,P_Y(y) - \int x\,P_X(x)\,\varphi_\sigma(y-x)\,dx\Bigr] \\ &= -\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\bigl[\,y\,P_Y(y) - P_Y(y)\,\mathbb{E}[X\,|\,Y=y]\,\bigr]. \end{aligned}$$

Key trick. Bayes: $P_{X|Y}(x\,|\,y) = P_X(x)\,\varphi_\sigma(y-x)/P_Y(y)$, so the integral is $P_Y(y)\,\mathbb{E}[X\,|\,Y=y]$.

Divide by $P_Y(y)$, rearrange: Tweedie. $\blacksquare$

Proof Recap — Tweedie in One Chain

$$\begin{aligned} \sigma^2\,\partial_y \log P_Y(y) &= \sigma^2\,\frac{\partial_y P_Y(y)}{P_Y(y)} \\ &\stackrel{\text{(conv.)}}{=}\; \frac{\sigma^2}{P_Y(y)}\int P_X(x)\,\partial_y\varphi_\sigma(y-x)\,dx \\ &\stackrel{(\partial\varphi_\sigma)}{=}\; -\frac{1}{P_Y(y)}\int (y-x)\,P_X(x)\,\varphi_\sigma(y-x)\,dx \\ &\stackrel{\text{(Bayes)}}{=}\; \mathbb{E}[X\,|\,Y=y] - y. \end{aligned}$$

Every equality exact: unlike the reverse conditional, no small-$\sigma$ approximation.

Reading Tweedie

$P_X$ $y$ $\mathbb{E}[X\,|\,Y=y]$ $\sigma^2\,\partial_y \log P_Y(y)$

Denoising = one step uphill on the noisy log-density, toward the data mass.

Train a Denoiser, Not a Score

Tweedie: posterior mean is the score in disguise. So train the denoiser.

Network $f_\theta(x^{(n)};n)$ reconstructs $x^{(0)}$ from $x^{(n)}$:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[\,\|f_\theta(X^{(n)};n) - X^{(0)}\|^2\,\bigr].$$

MMSE proposition: the optimal squared-loss regressor is the conditional mean.

$$f_\theta(x^{(n)};n) \;\approx\; \mathbb{E}[X^{(0)}\,|\,X^{(n)}=x^{(n)}].$$

Denoiser to Score

Apply Tweedie to VE at level $n$ (effective noise variance $n\sigma^2$):

$$f_\theta(x;n) \;\approx\; x + n\sigma^2\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\log P_{X^{(n)}}(x).$$

Solve for the score:

$$s_\theta(x;n) \;=\; \frac{f_\theta(x;n) - x}{n\sigma^2}.$$

A denoising network is a score network. Training reduces to regression.

Lecture Recap

  • Forward: data into Gaussian noise, one-shot jump $X^{(n)} = X^{(0)} + \sqrt{n}Z$
  • Reverse: Gaussian step, mean shifted by the score (Bayes + Taylor)
  • Training: MMSE regression + Tweedie recover the score
$$f_\theta(x;n) \;\approx\; \mathbb{E}[X^{(0)}\,|\,X^{(n)}=x] \;=\; x + n\sigma^2\,s(x;n).$$

Open: exact terminal law, likelihood-based objective, continuous-time limit.
