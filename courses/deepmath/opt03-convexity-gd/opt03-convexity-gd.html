Why Convexity?

Training is gradient descent

opt02's Bridge, Answered

  • opt02's luxury: $\theta^\star = A^{\dagger}B$ — one formula
  • Logistic regression, neural nets: $\nabla f = 0$ has no formula
  • Today: iterate instead
$$x^{(k+1)} = x^{(k)} - \alpha_k\, \nabla f(x^{(k)})$$

Training is this update. Everything else in deep learning refines it.

Three Questions About One Update

Does it go down?

Needs a speed limit on $\nabla f$

smoothness $L$ — §03

Does it reach $x^\star$?

Needs no false valleys

convexity — §02, §07

How fast?

Needs a curvature floor

strong convexity $\mu$ — §05, §08

Today's Three Results

Descent lemma — every step drops $f$

$f(x^+) \leq f(x) - \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$

Convex rate — $O(1/T)$, by telescoping

$f(x_T) - f^\star \leq \tfrac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha T}$

Strongly convex — geometric contraction

$\|x_i - x^\star\|^2 \leq c^{\,i}\, \|x_0 - x^\star\|^2$

The Constants Are Learning-Rate Theory

  • $L$: how fast the gradient can change — sets the safe step $\alpha = 1/L$
  • $2/L$: the divergence cliff — "loss explodes" has a formula
  • $\mu$: curvature floor — sets the speed of convergence

Tuning a learning rate in practice = estimating $L$ by trial and error.

Convexity

Chord above, tangent below, Hessian PSD

Definition — Convex Function

Definition.

$f:\mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is convex if for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and $t \in [0,1]$:

$$f\big(tx + (1-t)y\big) \;\leq\; t f(x) + (1-t) f(y)$$
  • Left: $f$ at a point on the segment from $y$ to $x$
  • Right: the chord value at the same point

Chord Above the Graph

every chord sits on or above the graph — for every pair $x, y$

Convex vs. Nonconvex

Why AI Cares

  • Deep-net losses: nonconvex — many valleys, saddles
  • Convex world: every local minimum is global
  • All of today's guarantees live in the convex world

Convexity = the world where GD provably works. Nonconvex practice borrows every tool from it.

The Gradient, Recalled

$$\nabla f(x) = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial}{\partial x_1} f(x)\\ \vdots \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x_d} f(x) \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^d$$
  • $d$-dimensional generalization of the derivative
  • Directional derivative along $v$:  $\langle \nabla f(x), v\rangle = \nabla f(x)^{\top} v$
  • $\nabla f(x)$ points in the maximally increasing direction

First-Order Characterization

Property (tangent floor).

$f$ differentiable and convex. Then for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

$$f(y) \;\geq\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x),\, y - x\rangle$$
  • The tangent plane at any $x$ sits below the whole graph
  • Follows directly from the chord definition

Tangent Below the Graph

one gradient at $x$ gives a global lower bound on $f$

Why — the 1D Picture

Claim in $d = 1$:  $f(y) \geq f(x) + f'(x)(y - x)$.

$$\frac{f(y) - f(x)}{y - x} \;\leq\; f'(x) \;\;\text{if } y \lt x, \qquad \frac{f(y) - f(x)}{y - x} \;\geq\; f'(x) \;\;\text{if } y \gt x$$
  • Secant slopes sandwich the tangent slope
  • Both cases rearrange to the same inequality

the chord definition forces secant slopes to grow with $y$

The Hessian, Recalled

$$\nabla^2 f(x) = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial x_1} & \cdots & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial x_d}\\ \vdots & & \vdots\\ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_d \partial x_1} & \cdots & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_d \partial x_d} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$$
  • Symmetric (mixed partials agree)
  • Curvature along direction $v$:  $v^{\top} \nabla^2 f(x)\, v$

Recall (prob08 / opt01) — PSD

Recall. Symmetric $A \succeq 0$ (positive semidefinite) means:

$$v^{\top} A v \geq 0 \;\; \forall v \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{all eigenvalues } \lambda_i \geq 0$$
  • Eigenvalues of the Hessian = curvatures along eigenvectors
  • $A \succeq B$ means $A - B \succeq 0$

Theorem 1 — Second-Order Characterization

Theorem 1.

$f:\mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ twice-differentiable. Then $f$ is convex if and only if

$$\nabla^2 f(x) \succeq 0 \quad \text{for every } x$$

Equivalently: $v^{\top} \nabla^2 f(x)\, v \geq 0$ for all $v \in \mathbb{R}^d$.

stated as in the notes — our working convexity test

Worked — Is It Convex?

Test $f(x) = x_1^2 + x_1 x_2 + x_2^2$:

$$\nabla^2 f = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det(\nabla^2 f - \lambda I) = (2-\lambda)^2 - 1 = 0$$
$$\lambda_1 = 3, \;\; \lambda_2 = 1 \;\; \geq 0 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \text{convex} \; \checkmark$$

eigenvalues by hand — the prob08/opt01 skill, reused

Worked — a Saddle Is Not Convex

Test $f(x) = x_1^2 - x_2^2$:

$$\nabla^2 f = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \lambda_2 = -2 \lt 0 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \text{not convex}$$
  • $\nabla f(0) = 0$, yet $(0,0)$ is no minimum: $f(0, x_2) = -x_2^2$
  • Without convexity, $\nabla f = 0$ certifies nothing

The Running Example

Carried through the whole deck.

$$f(x) = 2x_1^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}x_2^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} x^{\top} \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} x, \qquad \nabla^2 f = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Eigenvalues $\{4, 1\}$ — read off the diagonal — convex $\checkmark$
  • Minimizer $x^\star = 0$,  $f^\star = 0$,  $\nabla f(x) = (4x_1,\, x_2)$

Running Example — Contours

  • Stiff direction $x_1$: curvature $4$ — narrow
  • Soft direction $x_2$: curvature $1$ — wide
03

Smoothness

A speed limit on the gradient — and the descent lemma

Lipschitz Continuity

Definition.

$f$ is $L$-Lipschitz if for all $x_1, x_2$:

$$|f(x_1) - f(x_2)| \;\leq\; L\, \|x_1 - x_2\|$$
  • $f$ limited in how fast it can change
  • Slope never exceeds $L$, in any direction

Definition — $L$-Smooth

Definition.

Differentiable $f$ is $L$-smooth if $\nabla f$ is $L$-Lipschitz:

$$\|\nabla f(x_1) - \nabla f(x_2)\| \;\leq\; L\, \|x_1 - x_2\|$$
  • Now the gradient is limited in how fast it changes
  • Curvature bounded by $L$ — no sign condition: smooth $\neq$ convex

Theorem 2 — Bounded Hessian

Theorem 2.

$f$  $L$-smooth and twice-differentiable. Then

$$\nabla^2 f(x) \;\preceq\; L I \quad \text{for every } x$$
  • $LI - \nabla^2 f(x) \succeq 0$: all Hessian eigenvalues $\leq L$
  • Mirror image of Theorem 1's $\succeq 0$

Proof Sketch — as in the Notes

$$\nabla f(x + tv) - \nabla f(x) \;=\; \int_0^t \nabla^2 f(x + sv)\, v \; ds \qquad \text{(FTC along } v\text{)}$$
  • Smoothness: left side has norm $\leq L t \|v\|$
  • Divide by $t$, let $t \rightarrow 0$:  $\|\nabla^2 f(x)\, v\| \leq L\|v\|$ for all $v$
  • Hence $v^{\top} \nabla^2 f(x) v \leq L \|v\|^2$, i.e. $\nabla^2 f \preceq LI$

rough sketch — Exercise (from the notes): make it rigorous

Running Example — $L = 4$

$$\nabla^2 f = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \;\preceq\; 4I \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad L = \lambda_{\max} = 4$$
  • For quadratics: $L$ = largest Hessian eigenvalue
  • Earlier check $f = x_1^2 + x_1 x_2 + x_2^2$: eigenvalues $\{3,1\}$  $\Rightarrow$  $L = 3$

the constant you will feed into the step size

Technique Review — Restrict to a Segment

Recall (calculus). To compare $f(x)$ and $f(y)$, walk the segment:

$$g(t) = f\big(x + t(y-x)\big), \qquad g'(t) = \nabla f\big(x + t(y-x)\big)^{\top} (y - x)$$
$$g(1) = g(0) + \int_0^1 g'(t)\, dt \qquad \text{(FTC)}$$
  • $g(0) = f(x)$,  $g(1) = f(y)$ — a $d$-dim fact becomes 1D calculus

Technique Review — Cauchy–Schwarz

Recall. For any $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

$$\langle u, v\rangle \;\leq\; \|u\|\,\|v\|$$
  • Turns an inner product into a product of norms
  • Today's use: inner product with a gradient difference $\rightarrow$ apply smoothness

Lemma 1 — the Descent Lemma

Lemma 1 (descent lemma).

$f$  $L$-smooth. Then for all $x, \delta \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

$$f(x + \delta) \;\leq\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), \delta\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|\delta\|^2$$

a quadratic lid with curvature $L$ — convexity not needed

The Picture — a Parabola Lid

lid touches at $x$, then opens with curvature $L$ — $f$ can never pierce it

Descent Lemma — Proof Overview

$$\text{Target:}\quad f(y) \leq f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), y - x\rangle + \tfrac{L}{2}\|y - x\|^2 \qquad (y = x + \delta)$$
  1. Restrict to the segment: define $g(t)$  (TR: segment)
  2. Bound the slope drift $g'(t) - g'(0)$  (TR: Cauchy–Schwarz + smoothness)
  3. Integrate from $0$ to $1$  (TR: FTC)

Step 1 — Set Up $g$

$$g(t) = f\big(x + t(y - x)\big), \qquad g'(t) = \nabla f\big(x + t(y-x)\big)^{\top}(y - x)$$
  • $g(0) = f(x)$,  $g(1) = f(y)$,  $g'(0) = \nabla f(x)^{\top}(y-x)$
  • Plan: show $g'(t)$ cannot exceed $g'(0)$ by more than $Lt\|y-x\|^2$

Step 2 — Bound the Slope Drift

$$\begin{aligned} g'(t) - g'(0) &= \big(\nabla f(x + t(y-x)) - \nabla f(x)\big)^{\top}(y-x)\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(CS)}}{\leq} \big\|\nabla f(x + t(y-x)) - \nabla f(x)\big\|\; \|y-x\|\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(smooth)}}{\leq} L\,\|t(y-x)\|\; \|y-x\| \;=\; L t\, \|y-x\|^2 \end{aligned}$$

smoothness applied between $x + t(y-x)$ and $x$: distance $t\|y-x\|$

Step 3 — Integrate

$$\begin{aligned} f(y) = g(1) &\stackrel{\text{(FTC)}}{=} g(0) + \int_0^1 g'(t)\, dt\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(Step 2)}}{\leq} g(0) + \int_0^1 \big(g'(0) + Lt\|y-x\|^2\big)\, dt\\[4px] &= f(x) + \nabla f(x)^{\top}(y - x) + \frac{L}{2}\|y-x\|^2 \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

$\int_0^1 L t \, dt = L/2$ — where the $\tfrac{L}{2}$ comes from

Proof Summary — the Skeleton

$$f(y) \stackrel{\text{(segment)}}{=} g(0) + \!\int_0^1\! g'(t)\, dt \stackrel{\text{(CS + smooth)}}{\leq} f(x) + g'(0) + \!\int_0^1\! Lt\|y{-}x\|^2 dt \stackrel{\text{(integrate)}}{=} \text{lid}$$

Key trick: reduce to 1D along the segment; smoothness bounds the slope drift.

What the Descent Lemma Buys

  • One gradient at $x$ controls $f$ at every nearby point
  • Plug $\delta = -\alpha \nabla f(x)$: a guaranteed decrease — §06
  • Works for nonconvex $f$ too — deep-learning analyses start here

The single most-used inequality of today. Every convergence proof opens with it.

Theorem 3 — the Smoothness Sandwich

Theorem 3.

$f$  $L$-smooth with minimizer $x^\star$. Then for all $z$:

$$\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(z)\|^2 \;\leq\; f(z) - f(x^\star) \;\leq\; \frac{L}{2}\|z - x^\star\|^2$$

the suboptimality gap, squeezed from both sides

Reading the Statement

  • Right: distance to $x^\star$ controls the value gap
  • Left: gradient norm certifies the value gap
  • Small gradient $\Rightarrow$ provably near-optimal value

no convexity assumed — only smoothness and a minimizer

Proof — Right Side

$$\begin{aligned} f(z) &\stackrel{\text{(Lem 1 at } x^\star\text{)}}{\leq} f(x^\star) + \langle \nabla f(x^\star), z - x^\star\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|z - x^\star\|^2\\[4px] &\stackrel{(\nabla f(x^\star) = 0)}{=} f(x^\star) + \frac{L}{2}\|z - x^\star\|^2 \end{aligned}$$
  • Descent lemma centered at $x^\star$; minimizer kills the linear term

Proof — Left Side, the Idea

$$f(x^\star) = \inf_y f(y) \;\stackrel{\text{(Lem 1)}}{\leq}\; \inf_y \Big[ f(z) + \langle \nabla f(z), y - z\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|y - z\|^2 \Big]$$
  • Minimizing $f$ is hard — minimize its lid at $z$ instead
  • The lid is a quadratic in $y$: closed-form minimum

Proof — Left Side, Minimize the Lid

Reparameterize $y = z + tu$ with $\|u\| = 1$, $t \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\inf_{u,\,t}\; \big(\nabla f(z)^{\top} u\big)\, t + \frac{L}{2} t^2$$
$$t^\ast = -\frac{1}{L} \nabla f(z)^{\top} u \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{inner inf} = -\frac{1}{2L}\big(\nabla f(z)^{\top} u\big)^2$$

1D quadratic in $t$: vertex at $t^\ast$ — direction $u$ still free

Proof — Left Side, Best Direction

$$\inf_{\|u\|=1} -\frac{1}{2L}\big(\nabla f(z)^{\top} u\big)^2 \;\stackrel{\text{(CS, tight)}}{=}\; -\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(z)\|^2 \quad \text{at } u = \frac{\nabla f(z)}{\|\nabla f(z)\|}$$
$$\Rightarrow\;\; f(x^\star) \leq f(z) - \frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(z)\|^2 \qquad \blacksquare$$

largest projection of $\nabla f(z)$ onto a unit vector: along itself

Sandwich, Checked on the Running Example

At $z = (2,2)$:  $f(z) = 10$,  $\nabla f(z) = (8, 2)$,  $L = 4$:

$$\frac{\|\nabla f(z)\|^2}{2L} = \frac{64 + 4}{8} = 8.5 \;\leq\; 10 \;\leq\; \frac{L}{2}\|z\|^2 = 2 \cdot 8 = 16 \;\; \checkmark$$
  • Both bounds hold — and neither is wildly loose here
04

Smooth and Convex

Co-coercivity: gradients cannot twist

Two Bounds Meet

  • Smoothness: quadratic lid above $f$  (Lemma 1)
  • Convexity: tangent floor below $f$  (§02)
  • Squeeze $f$ between them $\Rightarrow$ the gradient itself gets constrained

Payoff: co-coercivity — the stability engine behind every GD rate in §07–§08.

Lemma 2 — Co-coercivity

Lemma 2.

$f$ convex and $L$-smooth. For all $x, y$:

$$\text{(i)}\;\; f(y) - f(x) \;\leq\; \langle \nabla f(y), y - x\rangle - \frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\|^2$$
$$\text{(ii)}\;\; \langle \nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x),\, y - x\rangle \;\geq\; \frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\|^2$$

(ii) is called co-coercivity

What Co-coercivity Says

  • Move from $x$ to $y$  $\rightarrow$  gradient change aligns with the move
  • Alignment is quantitative: at least $\frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\|^2$
  • The gradient field cannot twist or oscillate

A non-twisting gradient field is what makes gradient descent stable and predictable.

Proof of (i) — Overview

$$\text{Target:}\quad f(y) - f(x) \leq \langle \nabla f(y), y - x\rangle - \tfrac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\|^2$$
  1. Lid at $x$ (smoothness), evaluated at $x + z$
  2. Floor at $y$ (convexity), evaluated at the same $x + z$
  3. Chain them; rearrange — $z$ still free
  4. Choose the $z$ that makes the bound strongest

Proof of (i) — One Point, Two Bounds

$$\begin{aligned} f(x+z) &\stackrel{\text{(lid at } x\text{)}}{\leq} f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), z\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|z\|^2\\[4px] f(x+z) &\stackrel{\text{(floor at } y\text{)}}{\geq} f(y) + \langle \nabla f(y),\, x + z - y\rangle \end{aligned}$$
  • Same point $x + z$, squeezed from above and below

Proof of (i) — Chain and Rearrange

$$f(y) + \langle \nabla f(y), x + z - y\rangle \;\leq\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), z\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|z\|^2$$
$$\Rightarrow\quad \langle \nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x),\, z\rangle - \frac{L}{2}\|z\|^2 \;\leq\; f(x) - f(y) - \langle \nabla f(y),\, x - y\rangle$$

true for every $z$ — so pick the one that hurts most

Proof of (i) — the Best $z$

Plug $z = \frac{1}{L}\big(\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\big)$:

$$\text{LHS} = \frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\|^2 - \frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\|^2 = \frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\|^2$$
$$\Rightarrow\;\; f(y) - f(x) \leq \langle \nabla f(y), y - x\rangle - \frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\|^2 \qquad \blacksquare$$

maximizing a linear-minus-quadratic in $z$ — completing the square in disguise (§05 names it)

Proof of (ii) — Overview

$$\text{Target:}\quad \langle \nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x), y - x\rangle \geq \tfrac{1}{L}\|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\|^2$$
  1. Tilt: $g_x(z) = f(z) - \nabla f(x)^{\top} z$
  2. Check: $g_x$ convex, $L$-smooth, minimized at $z = x$
  3. Apply the sandwich (Thm 3) to $g_x$
  4. Symmetrize with $g_y$; add the two inequalities

Proof of (ii) — the Tilted Function

$$g_x(z) = f(z) - \nabla f(x)^{\top} z, \qquad \nabla g_x(z) = \nabla f(z) - \nabla f(x)$$
  • Convex: a linear tilt keeps convexity
  • $L$-smooth: gradients shifted by a constant — same Lipschitz bound
  • $\nabla g_x(x) = 0$ + convex  $\Rightarrow$  minimized at $z = x$

tilting makes our point $x$ the minimizer — Thm 3 now applies at $x$

Proof of (ii) — Apply the Sandwich

$$g_x(y) - g_x(x) \;\stackrel{\text{(Thm 3, left)}}{\geq}\; \frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla g_x(y)\|^2 = \frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\|^2$$
  • Thm 3's left inequality, for $g_x$ with minimizer $x$

Proof of (ii) — Symmetrize

Same construction with $x$ and $y$ swapped ($g_y(z) = f(z) - \nabla f(y)^{\top} z$):

$$g_y(x) - g_y(y) \;\geq\; \frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\|^2$$
  • Two mirror inequalities, each worth $\frac{1}{2L}\|\cdot\|^2$

Proof of (ii) — Add Them

$$\begin{aligned} g_x(y) - g_x(x) + g_y(x) - g_y(y) &= \big(\nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x)\big)^{\top}(y - x)\\[4px] &\quad \text{($f$-terms cancel; linear tilts pair up)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\Rightarrow\;\; \langle \nabla f(y) - \nabla f(x), y - x\rangle \geq \frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\|^2 \qquad \blacksquare$$

Proof Summary — Co-coercivity

$$\text{tilt } f \;\rightarrow\; x \text{ becomes minimizer} \;\rightarrow\; \text{sandwich at } x \;\rightarrow\; \text{symmetrize} \;\rightarrow\; \text{add}$$

Key trick: tilt so your point becomes the minimizer — then quote the sandwich.

Co-coercivity, Checked on the Running Example

$x = (2,2)$, $y = (0,0)$:  $\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y) = (8,2)$,  $x - y = (2,2)$:

$$\langle (8,2), (2,2)\rangle = 20 \;\;\geq\;\; \frac{1}{L}\|(8,2)\|^2 = \frac{68}{4} = 17 \;\;\checkmark$$
  • Gradient change and displacement: strongly aligned, as promised

Strong Convexity

A curvature floor — and the PL condition

Definition — $\mu$-Strongly Convex

Definition.

Convex $f$ is $\mu$-strongly convex if for all $x, y$:

$$f(y) \;\geq\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), y - x\rangle + \frac{\mu}{2}\|y - x\|^2$$
  • Tangent floor plus a parabola of curvature $\mu$
  • $f$ curves at least like a quadratic, everywhere

The Two-Parabola Sandwich

$\mu$-strongly convex + $L$-smooth: $f$ trapped between two parabolas touching at $x$

Theorem 4 — Hessian Floor

Theorem 4.

$f$  $\mu$-strongly convex and twice-differentiable. Then

$$\nabla^2 f(x) \;\succeq\; \mu I \quad \text{for every } x$$
  • All Hessian eigenvalues $\geq \mu$ — mirror of Theorem 2
  • Running example: eigenvalues $\{4, 1\}$  $\Rightarrow$  $\mu = 1$, $L = 4$

stated as in the notes

Recall (opt02) — Ridge Buys $\mu$

Recall (opt02, ridge). $f(\theta) = \|A\theta - B\|^2 + \lambda \|\theta\|^2$:

$$\nabla^2 f = 2\big(A^{\top}A + \lambda I\big) \;\succeq\; 2\lambda I$$
  • $2\lambda$-strongly convex — for any $A$, even rank-deficient
  • opt02's eigenvalue-shift lemma, now read as: regularization buys strong convexity

Theorem 5 — Gradient Monotonicity

Theorem 5.

$f$ convex and differentiable. For all $x, y$:

$$\big(\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\big)^{\top}(x - y) \;\geq\; 0$$
  • Gradients never point "against" the displacement
  • Co-coercivity's $\geq \frac{1}{L}\|\cdot\|^2$ sharpened this to a quantity

Proof — Add Two Floors

$$\begin{aligned} f(y) &\geq f(x) + \nabla f(x)^{\top}(y - x) \qquad \text{(floor at } x\text{)}\\[4px] f(x) &\geq f(y) + \nabla f(y)^{\top}(x - y) \qquad \text{(floor at } y\text{)} \end{aligned}$$
$$\text{add:}\quad 0 \geq \big(\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\big)^{\top}(y - x) \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; \big(\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\big)^{\top}(x - y) \geq 0 \;\;\blacksquare$$

the $f$-values cancel in the sum — only gradient terms survive

Theorem 6 — Strong Monotonicity

Theorem 6.

$f$  $\mu$-strongly convex. For all $x, y$:

$$\big(\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\big)^{\top}(x - y) \;\geq\; \mu \|x - y\|^2$$
  • Same statement, now with a quantitative floor

Proof — Same Trick, Stronger Floors

$$\begin{aligned} f(y) &\geq f(x) + \nabla f(x)^{\top}(y - x) + \tfrac{\mu}{2}\|y - x\|^2\\[4px] f(x) &\geq f(y) + \nabla f(y)^{\top}(x - y) + \tfrac{\mu}{2}\|x - y\|^2 \end{aligned}$$
$$\text{add:}\quad \big(\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\big)^{\top}(x - y) \;\geq\; \mu\|x - y\|^2 \qquad \blacksquare$$

two $\frac{\mu}{2}$ terms combine into one $\mu$

Technique Review — Completing the Square

Recall (norm version). For any $g, d \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and $\mu \gt 0$:

$$\langle g, d\rangle - \frac{\mu}{2}\|d\|^2 \;=\; -\frac{1}{2}\Big\| \sqrt{\mu}\, d - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}} g \Big\|^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu}\|g\|^2$$
  • Check: expand the square — cross term $= \langle g, d\rangle$, extra terms match
  • Use: drop the (negative) square $\rightarrow$ bound by $\frac{1}{2\mu}\|g\|^2$

Lemma 3 — the PL Condition

Lemma 3 (Polyak–Lojasiewicz).

$f$  $\mu$-strongly convex with minimizer $x^\star$. Then for all $x$:

$$\|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \;\geq\; 2\mu\, \big(f(x) - f(x^\star)\big)$$

strong convexity $\Rightarrow$ PL; the inequality itself is the "PL condition"

Reading PL — Gradient Norm Measures the Gap

$$2\mu\big(f(x) - f^\star\big) \;\stackrel{\text{(PL)}}{\leq}\; \|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \;\stackrel{\text{(Thm 3)}}{\leq}\; 2L\big(f(x) - f^\star\big)$$
  • Gradient norm $\approx$ suboptimality, up to the ratio $\mu / L$
  • Flat gradient $\Rightarrow$ already near-optimal — no hidden bad valleys

Proof of PL — Rearrange the Floor

Strong-convexity floor at $x$, evaluated at $y = x^\star$:

$$f(x^\star) \;\geq\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), x^\star - x\rangle + \frac{\mu}{2}\|x^\star - x\|^2$$
$$\Rightarrow\quad f(x) - f(x^\star) \;\leq\; \langle \nabla f(x), x - x^\star\rangle - \frac{\mu}{2}\|x^\star - x\|^2$$

exactly the linear-minus-quadratic shape of the technique review

Proof of PL — Complete the Square

$$\begin{aligned} f(x) - f(x^\star) &\stackrel{\text{(complete sq.)}}{=} -\frac{1}{2}\Big\|\sqrt{\mu}(x - x^\star) - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\nabla f(x)\Big\|^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(drop square)}}{\leq} \frac{1}{2\mu}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \end{aligned}$$
$$\Rightarrow\;\; \|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \geq 2\mu\big(f(x) - f(x^\star)\big) \qquad \blacksquare$$

PL, Checked on the Running Example

$$\|\nabla f(x)\|^2 = 16x_1^2 + x_2^2 \;\;\geq\;\; 2\mu\big(f(x) - f^\star\big) = 4x_1^2 + x_2^2 \;\;\checkmark$$
  • Holds for every $x$, with slack in the stiff direction
  • Equality pattern: tight where curvature equals $\mu$ (the $x_2$ axis)

PL as an Assumption of Its Own

  • The PL inequality mentions no chords, no floors — convexity absent
  • Notes: PL by itself already guarantees linear convergence of GD
  • Delivered in §08 — two lines, once the descent lemma lands

Strong convexity suffices for PL — not conversely. PL: the weaker, portable assumption.

Gradient Descent

The algorithm — and when it must converge

The Problem

$$\min_{x \in \mathbb{R}^d} \; f(x)$$
  • $f$ convex:  $x$ is a solution $\iff \nabla f(x) = 0$
  • But $\nabla f(x) = 0$ rarely has a closed form — opt02's lesson
  • Plan: walk downhill, one gradient at a time

The Algorithm

Gradient Descent

  1. Pick a starting point $x^{(0)}$ and step sizes $\alpha_k \gt 0$
  2. Repeat:  $x^{(k+1)} = x^{(k)} - \alpha_k \, \nabla f\big(x^{(k)}\big)$
  3. Stop when $\|\nabla f(x^{(k)})\|$ is small
  • $-\nabla f$: the maximally decreasing direction
  • $\alpha_k$: the step size — deep learning's "learning rate"

1D Intuition — the Sign Does the Steering

$$x^{(1)} = x^{(0)} - \alpha f'(x^{(0)}) \qquad \text{one rule handles both signs}$$

Running Example — GD, Set Up by Hand

$$\nabla f(x) = (4x_1,\, x_2), \qquad \alpha = \frac{1}{L} = \frac{1}{4}$$
$$x^+ = x - \tfrac{1}{4}(4x_1, x_2) = \big(0,\; \tfrac{3}{4} x_2\big)$$
  • Stiff direction $x_1$: solved in one step — factor $1 - \alpha \cdot 4 = 0$
  • Soft direction $x_2$: shrinks by $1 - \alpha \cdot 1 = \frac{3}{4}$ per step

Theorem 7 — GD Converges (Smooth Case)

Theorem 7.

$f$  $L$-smooth with a global minimum. GD with constant $\alpha \in (0, 2/L)$:

$$f\big(x^{(k+1)}\big) \leq f\big(x^{(k)}\big), \qquad f\big(x^{(k)}\big) \text{ converges}, \qquad \big\|\nabla f\big(x^{(k)}\big)\big\| \rightarrow 0$$

no convexity: the limit is a stationary point, not necessarily a global minimum

Proof — One Step Through the Lid

$$\begin{aligned} f\big(x^{(k+1)}\big) &= f\big(x^{(k)} - \alpha \nabla f(x^{(k)})\big)\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(Lem 1, } \delta = -\alpha\nabla f\text{)}}{\leq} f\big(x^{(k)}\big) - \alpha \|\nabla f(x^{(k)})\|^2 + \frac{L\alpha^2}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{(k)})\|^2\\[4px] &= f\big(x^{(k)}\big) - \alpha\Big(1 - \frac{L\alpha}{2}\Big)\|\nabla f(x^{(k)})\|^2 \end{aligned}$$

descent lemma with the GD step plugged in as $\delta$

Proof — Monotone and Bounded

  • $\alpha \lt 2/L$  $\Rightarrow$  coefficient $\alpha(1 - L\alpha/2) \gt 0$
  • So $f(x^{(k)})$ is non-increasing
  • Bounded below by $f(x^\star)$  $\Rightarrow$  it converges  (monotone convergence)
$$f(x^{(0)}) \geq f(x^{(1)}) \geq \cdots \geq f(x^\star) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x^{(k)}) \downarrow \text{ some limit}$$

Proof — Gradients Must Vanish

$$\alpha\Big(1 - \frac{L\alpha}{2}\Big) \sum_{k=0}^{K} \|\nabla f(x^{(k)})\|^2 \;\leq\; f(x^{(0)}) - f(x^{(K+1)}) \;\leq\; f(x^{(0)}) - f(x^\star)$$
  • Per-step drops sum; the sum stays finite as $K \rightarrow \infty$
  • A convergent series has vanishing terms: $\|\nabla f(x^{(k)})\|^2 \rightarrow 0$  $\blacksquare$

first sighting of telescoping — named properly in §07

Reading the Theorem

  • Looks modest — it is the load-bearing fact of deep learning
  • Steeper gradient changes (larger $L$) $\Rightarrow$ smaller safe step
  • The notes call it beautiful: steepness and step size, exactly related

Exercise (from the notes). Is smoothness necessary? Interpret $\alpha \lt 2/L$ — hint: the divergence slide.

Rate I — $O(1/T)$

Convex + smooth: the gap shrinks like $1/T$

Assumptions and Goal

  • $f$ convex, $L$-smooth, minimizer $x^\star$
  • Constant step $0 \lt \alpha \leq 1/L$; iterates $x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_T$
$$\text{Goal:}\qquad f(x_T) - f(x^\star) \;\leq\; \frac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha T}$$

§06 gave convergence; now convexity buys a rate

Theorem 8 — $O(1/T)$ Convergence

Theorem 8.

$f$ convex, $L$-smooth, minimizer $x^\star$; GD with $\alpha \leq 1/L$. Then

$$f(x_T) - f(x^\star) \;\leq\; \frac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha T} \;\;\stackrel{(\alpha = 1/L)}{=}\;\; \frac{L\,\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2T}$$

only the starting distance and $T$ appear — dimension-free

Proof Overview

  1. One step through the lid: guaranteed decrease  (Lemma 1)
  2. Bring in $x^\star$: convexity floor at $x$  (§02)
  3. Complete the square: gap $\leq$ change in squared distance  (TR)
  4. Telescope over $T$ steps  (TR: telescoping)
  5. Last iterate $\leq$ average  (monotone from step 1)

Step 1 — Guaranteed Decrease

$$\begin{aligned} f(x^+) &\stackrel{\text{(Thm 7 proof)}}{\leq} f(x) - \alpha\Big(1 - \frac{L\alpha}{2}\Big)\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\\[4px] &\stackrel{(\alpha \leq 1/L)}{\leq} f(x) - \frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \end{aligned}$$
  • $\alpha \leq 1/L \Rightarrow L\alpha/2 \leq 1/2$: coefficient at least $\alpha/2$
  • Notation: $x = x_{i-1}$, $x^+ = x_i$ for one generic step

Step 2 — Bring In the Optimum

$$\nabla f(x)^{\top}(x^\star - x) \;\leq\; f(x^\star) - f(x) \qquad \text{(convexity floor at } x\text{)}$$
$$\Rightarrow\quad f(x^+) - f(x^\star) \;\leq\; \nabla f(x)^{\top}(x - x^\star) - \frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$$

add the floor to Step 1 — the unknown $f(x)$ cancels out

Technique Review — the Square, Completed Again

Recall. With $g = \nabla f(x)$, $d = x - x^\star$, and any $\alpha \gt 0$:

$$\langle g, d\rangle - \frac{\alpha}{2}\|g\|^2 \;=\; \frac{1}{2\alpha}\Big( \|d\|^2 - \|d - \alpha g\|^2 \Big)$$
  • Check: expand $\|d - \alpha g\|^2 = \|d\|^2 - 2\alpha\langle g, d\rangle + \alpha^2\|g\|^2$
  • Note: $d - \alpha g = x - \alpha \nabla f(x) - x^\star = x^+ - x^\star$

Step 3 — the Distance Identity

$$\begin{aligned} f(x^+) - f(x^\star) &\stackrel{\text{(Step 2)}}{\leq} \nabla f(x)^{\top}(x - x^\star) - \frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(complete sq.)}}{=} \frac{1}{2\alpha}\Big(\|x - x^\star\|^2 - \|x^+ - x^\star\|^2\Big) \end{aligned}$$

Each value gap is paid in squared distance burned — a telescoping shape.

Corollary — the Distance Also Shrinks

$$0 \;\leq\; f(x^+) - f(x^\star) \;\leq\; \frac{1}{2\alpha}\Big(\|x - x^\star\|^2 - \|x^+ - x^\star\|^2\Big)$$
  • $\Rightarrow\; \|x^+ - x^\star\| \leq \|x - x^\star\|$: every step moves closer to $x^\star$
  • Not obvious from the picture — value and distance improve together

Technique Review — Telescoping Sums

Recall. For any sequence $a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_T$:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{T} \big(a_{i-1} - a_i\big) \;=\; a_0 - a_T$$
  • Adjacent terms cancel in pairs; only the endpoints survive
  • The key trick of this proof — and of §06's vanishing gradients

Step 4 — Telescope

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{i=1}^{T} \big(f(x_i) - f(x^\star)\big) &\stackrel{\text{(Step 3, each } i\text{)}}{\leq} \frac{1}{2\alpha} \sum_{i=1}^{T} \Big(\|x_{i-1} - x^\star\|^2 - \|x_i - x^\star\|^2\Big)\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(telescope)}}{=} \frac{1}{2\alpha}\Big(\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2 - \|x_T - x^\star\|^2\Big) \;\stackrel{\text{(drop)}}{\leq}\; \frac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha} \end{aligned}$$

$T$ inequalities added; the middle distances all cancel

Step 5 — From Sum to Last Iterate

$$f(x_T) - f(x^\star) \;\stackrel{\text{(monotone)}}{\leq}\; \frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T} \big(f(x_i) - f(x^\star)\big) \;\stackrel{\text{(Step 4)}}{\leq}\; \frac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha T} \qquad \blacksquare$$
  • Step 1 made $f(x_i)$ non-increasing: the last term is the smallest
  • Smallest term $\leq$ average of the terms

Proof Summary — the Skeleton

$$\text{lid} \;\rightarrow\; +\,\text{floor at } x \;\rightarrow\; \text{complete the square} \;\rightarrow\; \text{telescope} \;\rightarrow\; \text{last} \leq \text{average}$$

Key trick: telescoping — gaps priced in squared distance; the sum collapses to endpoints.

Corollary — Iteration Complexity $O(1/\varepsilon)$

$$f(x_T) - f^\star \leq \varepsilon \quad \Longleftarrow \quad T \;\geq\; \frac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha\, \varepsilon} \;=\; O\!\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)$$
  • Running example, $\alpha = 1/4$: bound $T \geq 16/\varepsilon$
  • $\varepsilon = 0.01 \Rightarrow 1600$ iterations guaranteed

Rate II — Linear

Strong convexity: a constant factor every step

The Goal — From $1/T$ to $c^{\,T}$

$$\|x_i - x^\star\|^2 \;\leq\; c^{\,i}\, \|x_0 - x^\star\|^2, \qquad c \lt 1$$
  • Exponentially fast — called the linear rate (a line on the log plot)
  • Price: assume $\mu$-strong convexity on top of $L$-smoothness
  • Tool to build: a stronger co-coercivity

Theorem 9 — Subtract the Floor

Theorem 9.

$f$  $\mu$-strongly convex, $L$-smooth, twice-differentiable. Then

$$h(x) = f(x) - \frac{\mu}{2}\|x\|^2 \quad \text{is convex and } (L - \mu)\text{-smooth}$$
$$\text{Proof:}\quad \mu I \preceq \nabla^2 f \preceq L I \;\Rightarrow\; 0 \preceq \nabla^2 h = \nabla^2 f - \mu I \preceq (L - \mu) I \;\;\blacksquare$$

What $h$ Knows About $f$

$$\nabla h(x) = \nabla f(x) - \mu x \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \nabla h(x) - \nabla h(y) = \underbrace{\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)}_{\Delta g} - \mu \underbrace{(x - y)}_{\Delta x}$$
  • Shorthand from here: $\Delta g = \nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)$,  $\Delta x = x - y$
  • Every fact about $h$ translates into $\Delta g$, $\Delta x$ language

Co-coercivity, Applied to $h$

$$\langle \nabla h(x) - \nabla h(y),\, x - y\rangle \;\geq\; \frac{1}{L - \mu}\,\|\nabla h(x) - \nabla h(y)\|^2$$
  • Lemma 2 (ii) applies: $h$ is convex and $(L-\mu)$-smooth  (Thm 9)
  • In shorthand: $\langle \Delta g - \mu \Delta x,\, \Delta x\rangle \geq \frac{1}{L-\mu}\|\Delta g - \mu \Delta x\|^2$

now unpack — two slides of algebra

Unpack — Expand the Square

$$\|\Delta g - \mu \Delta x\|^2 = \|\Delta g\|^2 - 2\mu \langle \Delta g, \Delta x\rangle + \mu^2 \|\Delta x\|^2$$
$$(L - \mu)\Big(\langle \Delta g, \Delta x\rangle - \mu\|\Delta x\|^2\Big) \;\geq\; \|\Delta g\|^2 - 2\mu \langle \Delta g, \Delta x\rangle + \mu^2 \|\Delta x\|^2$$

multiplied both sides by $L - \mu \gt 0$ — direction preserved

Unpack — Collect Terms

$$(L + \mu)\,\langle \Delta g, \Delta x\rangle \;\geq\; \|\Delta g\|^2 + \big(\mu(L-\mu) + \mu^2\big)\|\Delta x\|^2 \;=\; \|\Delta g\|^2 + \mu L\, \|\Delta x\|^2$$
$$\Rightarrow\quad \langle \Delta g, \Delta x\rangle \;\geq\; \frac{1}{L + \mu}\|\Delta g\|^2 + \frac{\mu L}{L + \mu}\|\Delta x\|^2$$

Extended co-coercivity: alignment now pays in both currencies — gradient norm and distance.

Theorem 10 — Linear Convergence

Theorem 10.

$f$  $\mu$-strongly convex, $L$-smooth; GD with $0 \lt \alpha \leq \frac{2}{\mu + L}$. Then

$$\|x^+ - x^\star\|^2 \;\leq\; \Big(1 - \frac{2\alpha \mu L}{\mu + L}\Big)\, \|x - x^\star\|^2$$
  • Write $c = 1 - \frac{2\alpha\mu L}{\mu + L} \lt 1$: one step = one factor of $c$

Proof Overview

  1. Expand $\|x^+ - x^\star\|^2$ — a recursion in the making
  2. Extended co-coercivity at $(x, x^\star)$ — note $\nabla f(x^\star) = 0$
  3. Group; the step condition kills the gradient term
  4. Unroll the recursion  (TR: unrolling)
  5. Function values follow via the sandwich  (Thm 3)

Step 1 — Expand the Square

$$\begin{aligned} \|x^+ - x^\star\|^2 &= \|x - \alpha \nabla f(x) - x^\star\|^2\\[4px] &= \|x - x^\star\|^2 - 2\alpha\, \nabla f(x)^{\top}(x - x^\star) + \alpha^2 \|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \end{aligned}$$
  • Middle term: exactly what extended co-coercivity bounds

Step 2 — Co-coercivity at the Optimum

Extended co-coercivity with $y = x^\star$, where $\nabla f(x^\star) = 0$:

$$\nabla f(x)^{\top}(x - x^\star) \;\geq\; \frac{1}{L + \mu}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2 + \frac{\mu L}{L + \mu}\|x - x^\star\|^2$$
  • $\Delta g = \nabla f(x) - 0$,  $\Delta x = x - x^\star$

Step 3 — Group; Kill the Gradient Term

$$\|x^+ - x^\star\|^2 \;\leq\; \Big(1 - \frac{2\alpha \mu L}{L + \mu}\Big)\|x - x^\star\|^2 + \Big(\alpha^2 - \frac{2\alpha}{L + \mu}\Big)\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$$
  • $\alpha \leq \frac{2}{L + \mu}$  $\Rightarrow$  $\alpha^2 - \frac{2\alpha}{L+\mu} \leq 0$: drop the term
  • Left with the contraction: $\|x^+ - x^\star\|^2 \leq c\, \|x - x^\star\|^2$

Technique Review — Unrolling a Recursion

Recall. If $a_i \leq c\, a_{i-1}$ for every $i$, then

$$a_i \;\leq\; c\, a_{i-1} \;\leq\; c^2 a_{i-2} \;\leq\; \cdots \;\leq\; c^{\,i} a_0$$
  • Apply the same inequality $i$ times — compound interest, in reverse

Step 4 — Unroll

$$\|x_i - x^\star\|^2 \;\leq\; c\, \|x_{i-1} - x^\star\|^2 \;\leq\; \cdots \;\leq\; c^{\,i}\, \|x_0 - x^\star\|^2$$
  • GD is a contraction: every step multiplies the error by $c \lt 1$
  • The notes' word: the map $x \mapsto x - \alpha\nabla f(x)$ is contractive

Step 5 — Function Values Follow

$$f(x_i) - f(x^\star) \;\stackrel{\text{(Thm 3, right)}}{\leq}\; \frac{L}{2}\|x_i - x^\star\|^2 \;\stackrel{\text{(Step 4)}}{\leq}\; \frac{L}{2}\, c^{\,i}\, \|x_0 - x^\star\|^2 \qquad \blacksquare$$
  • Distance contracts $\Rightarrow$ value gap contracts, same $c$

Proof Summary — the Skeleton

$$\text{expand square} \;\rightarrow\; \text{co-coercivity at } (x, x^\star) \;\rightarrow\; \text{drop gradient term}$$
$$\rightarrow\; \text{unroll} \;\rightarrow\; \text{sandwich}$$

Key trick: one step contracts, unrolling is free. The work: extended co-coercivity.

Remark — $O(\log(1/\varepsilon))$ Iterations

$$c^{\,T}\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2 \leq \varepsilon \quad\Longleftarrow\quad T \;\geq\; \frac{\log\big(\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2 / \varepsilon\big)}{\log(1/c)} = O\!\Big(\log \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)$$
  • Running example ($c = 0.6$), $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$: $T \approx 32$ steps
  • The $O(1/\varepsilon)$ bound would promise $1.6 \times 10^{7}$ — strong convexity changed the game
