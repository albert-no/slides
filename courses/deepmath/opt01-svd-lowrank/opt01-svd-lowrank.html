Why Low Rank?

Fewer directions, measured loss

The Compression Question

Store $A \in \mathbb{R}^{10^4 \times 10^4}$: $10^8$ numbers. Rank-$20$ version:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{20} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top}: \quad 20 \times (10^4 + 10^4) = 4 \times 10^5 \text{ numbers} \;\; (0.4\%)$$
  • Question 1: among rank-$k$ matrices, which is closest to $A$?
  • Question 2: how large is the unavoidable error?

Definition — Span & Range

Recall. Span = all linear combinations of given vectors

Definition (range / column space).

$$\operatorname{range}(A) = \Big\{ \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i\, a_i \;:\; \alpha_i \in \mathbb{R} \Big\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$$

Everything $A$ can output — a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^m$

Definition — Rank

Recall. Dimension of a subspace = size of a basis = max independent vectors

Definition (rank).

$$\operatorname{rank}(A) = \dim\big( \operatorname{range}(A) \big)$$

Fact (stated): row space has the same dimension — row rank = column rank

Worked — a Collapsing Matrix

$$C = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad a_1 = a_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Both columns on one line
  • $\operatorname{range}(C) = \operatorname{span}\{(1,2)\}$
  • All outputs collapse onto that line
$(1,2)$ $\operatorname{range}(C)$: a line

Definition — Null Space

Definition (null space).

$$\operatorname{null}(A) = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^{n} : Ax = 0 \}$$
  • The inputs $A$ destroys
  • Lives in the input space $\mathbb{R}^n$; range lives in $\mathbb{R}^m$

Theorem 1 — Rank–Nullity

Theorem 1 (rank–nullity).

For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ ($n$ = number of columns):

$$\operatorname{rank}(A) + \dim\big( \operatorname{null}(A) \big) = n$$

Every input dimension either survives (rank) or dies (nullity)

Stated, not proved (as in source) — it powers Theorem 8, Step 1

02

Measuring Error

Two sizes for a residual

Definition — Norm

Definition (norm on $\mathbb{R}^n$).

$\|\cdot\| : \mathbb{R}^n \to [0,\infty)$ with, for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\|x+y\| \leq \|x\| + \|y\|, \qquad \|\alpha x\| = |\alpha| \|x\|, \qquad \|x\| = 0 \iff x = 0$$

Triangle inequality · absolute homogeneity · definiteness

The $\ell_p$ Family

$$\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{x^{\top}x} = \sqrt{x_1^2 + \cdots + x_n^2}, \qquad \|x\|_p = \Big( |x_1|^p + \cdots + |x_n|^p \Big)^{1/p}$$
  • $p \geq 1$ required — below 1 the triangle inequality fails
  • Limit case: $\|x\|_{\infty} = \max_i |x_i|$

Worked — One Vector, Three Norms

Take $x = (3, 4)$:

$$\|x\|_1 = 3 + 4 = 7, \qquad \|x\|_2 = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5, \qquad \|x\|_{\infty} = 4$$
  • Always ordered: $\|x\|_{\infty} \leq \|x\|_2 \leq \|x\|_1$
  • Same vector, three sizes — the norm is a choice

Unit Balls — the Norms as Shapes

$\ell_1$: diamond $\ell_2$: circle $\ell_\infty$: square

$\ell_1$'s corners sit on the axes — corners will mean sparsity (§06, nuclear norm)

Definition — Orthogonal Matrix

Definition (orthogonal matrix).

$U \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ is orthogonal if any (hence all) of:

$$U^{\top}U = I \quad\iff\quad UU^{\top} = I$$
$$\iff\quad \text{columns orthonormal} \quad\iff\quad \text{rows orthonormal}$$

Square only — the thin SVD factors will not qualify (§04)

Theorem 4 — Orthogonal Maps Preserve Length

Theorem 4 (orthogonal invariance).

$U$ orthogonal $\Rightarrow$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$:  $\|Ux\|_2 = \|x\|_2$

Proof.

$$\|Ux\|_2^2 = (Ux)^{\top}(Ux) = x^{\top} U^{\top} U\, x \;\stackrel{(U^{\top}U = I)}{=}\; x^{\top} x = \|x\|_2^2 \qquad \blacksquare$$

One line — the source states it, the proof is free

Definition — Induced (Operator) Norm

Definition.

For $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and $p \geq 1$:

$$\|A\|_p \;=\; \max_{x \neq 0} \frac{\|Ax\|_p}{\|x\|_p} \;=\; \max_{\|x\|_p = 1} \|Ax\|_p$$
  • Biggest amplification factor over all inputs
  • Scale-free: doubling $x$ doubles $Ax$, ratio unchanged

Definition — Frobenius Norm

Definition.

$$\|A\|_F \;=\; \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m} \sum_{j=1}^{n} A_{ij}^2} \;=\; \sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(A^{\top}A)}$$
  • Flatten the matrix, take the $\ell_2$ norm of the $mn$ numbers
  • Total "energy" — every entry counts, not just the worst direction
03

Reading an SVD

Rotate, stretch, rotate

Recall — Eigendecomposition

Recall. Symmetric $S \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$:

$$S = Q \Lambda Q^{\top}, \qquad Q \text{ orthogonal}, \quad \Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)$$
  • Eigenvector: $S q_i = \lambda_i q_i$ — direction $S$ keeps
  • PSD case (covariance matrices): all $\lambda_i \geq 0$

Theorem 5 — the SVD Exists

Theorem 5 (SVD).

Every $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with $\operatorname{rank}(A) = r$ has

$$A = U \Sigma V^{\top}, \qquad U \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times m}\!,\; V \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n} \text{ orthogonal},$$

$\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ diagonal with $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$, rest $0$

Existence stated, not proved (as in source) — the $A^{\top}A$ route, by hand below

Reading the Shapes (Full SVD)

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U$ $m \times m$, orthogonal $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r$ $\Sigma$ $m \times n$, diagonal $V^{\top}$ $n \times n$, orthogonal

$u_1, \dots, u_m$: basis of $\mathbb{R}^m$ · $v_1, \dots, v_n$: basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ · $r$ nonzero stretches

What Each Factor Does

$$Ax = U\big( \Sigma (V^{\top} x) \big)$$
  • $V^{\top}$: rotate input into the $v_i$ coordinates
  • $\Sigma$: stretch coordinate $i$ by $\sigma_i$ (kill it if $\sigma_i = 0$)
  • $U$: rotate into output space

$v_i$: right singular vectors · $u_i$: left singular vectors · $\sigma_i$: singular values

Circle to Ellipse — Rotate First

1. unit circle, inputs $x$ 2. after $V^{\top}$: still a circle $v_1$ $v_2$ $e_1$ $e_2$ $V^{\top}$

An orthogonal rotation: every length is preserved, so the circle survives

Circle to Ellipse — Stretch, Then Rotate Back

3. after $\Sigma$: stretch the axes 4. after $U$: the ellipse $Ax$ $6.71\,e_1$ $2.24\,e_2$ $6.71\,u_1$ $2.24\,u_2$ $U$

$\Sigma$ sets the axis lengths $\sigma_1 = 6.71$, $\sigma_2 = 2.24$; $U$ only turns the result

Technique Review — the Outer-Product View

$$A = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i\, u_i v_i^{\top}$$
  • Each $u_i v_i^{\top}$: a rank-1 layer
  • Layer $i$ acts: $x \mapsto u_i \,(v_i^{\top} x)$
  • $\sigma_i$ = how loudly layer $i$ speaks
$\sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top}$ loudest layer $A = $ layer 1 $+$ layer 2 $+ \cdots$ back layers: quieter $\sigma_i$

Thin (Compact) SVD

$A$ $m \times n$ $=$ $U_c$ $m \times r$ $\Sigma_c$ $r \times r$ $V_c^{\top}$ $r \times n$ keep only the $r$ nonzero stretches
$$A = U_c\, \Sigma_c\, V_c^{\top}, \qquad \Sigma_c = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_r)$$

Worked — SVD by Hand: the Plan

Running matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ — why $A^{\top}A$ helps:

$$A^{\top}A = (U\Sigma V^{\top})^{\top} (U\Sigma V^{\top}) \;\stackrel{(U^{\top}U = I)}{=}\; V\, \Sigma^{\top}\Sigma\, V^{\top}$$
  1. Form $A^{\top}A$ — symmetric PSD
  2. Eigendecompose: eigenvectors $= v_i$, eigenvalues $= \sigma_i^2$
  3. $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i}$, then $u_i = A v_i / \sigma_i$

Worked — Step 1: Form $A^{\top}A$

$$A^{\top}A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 25 & 20 \\ 20 & 25 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Symmetric ✓ — and PSD: $x^{\top}A^{\top}Ax = \|Ax\|_2^2 \geq 0$
  • Same species as a covariance matrix

Worked — Step 2: Eigenvalues

$$\det\!\begin{bmatrix} 25 - \lambda & 20 \\ 20 & 25 - \lambda \end{bmatrix} = (25-\lambda)^2 - 400 = 0$$
$$25 - \lambda = \pm 20 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \lambda_1 = 45, \quad \lambda_2 = 5$$

Both positive — as PSD promised

Worked — Step 2: Eigenvectors

$$\lambda_1 = 45:\; \begin{bmatrix} -20 & 20 \\ 20 & -20 \end{bmatrix} v = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; v_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \lambda_2 = 5:\; v_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Check: $v_1^{\top} v_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(-1 + 1) = 0$ — orthonormal ✓
  • $V = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ — the $45°$ rotation from §03

Worked — Step 3: Sigmas and $u_i$

$$\sigma_1 = \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5} \approx 6.71, \qquad \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.24$$
$$u_1 = \frac{A v_1}{\sigma_1} = \frac{(3,\, 9)}{\sqrt{2}\cdot 3\sqrt{5}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad u_2 = \frac{A v_2}{\sigma_2} = \frac{(-3,\, 1)}{\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{5}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Check: $u_1^{\top}u_2 = \tfrac{1}{10}(-3 + 3) = 0$ ✓,  $\|u_1\|_2 = \|u_2\|_2 = 1$ ✓

Worked — Step 4: Assemble

$$A \;=\; U \Sigma V^{\top} \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{U} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 3\sqrt{5} & 0 \\ 0 & \sqrt{5} \end{bmatrix}}_{\Sigma} \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{V^{\top}}$$

Two rotations and a stretch — found with nothing but a $2\times 2$ eigenproblem

Worked — Verify via Rank-1 Layers

$$\sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{20}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \sigma_2 u_2 v_2^{\top} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & -1.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\text{sum} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix} = A \;\;\checkmark$$

The outer-product view, live — layer 1 is most of $A$ (see §07)

Eigenvalues $\neq$ Singular Values

Same $A$, two different spectra:

$$\text{eigenvalues of } A:\; 3,\; 5 \text{ (triangular)} \qquad \text{singular values:}\; 6.71,\; 2.24$$
  • Eigen: directions $A$ maps to themselves
  • Singular: directions of maximal stretch (input $\to$ output)
  • They agree only for symmetric PSD matrices

Theorem 6 — Spectral Norm $=$ Top Sigma

Theorem 6.

The induced $2$-norm ("spectral norm") of $A$ is

$$\|A\|_2 \;=\; \max_{\|x\|_2 = 1} \|Ax\|_2 \;=\; \sigma_1$$

Geometry: the ellipse's longest semi-axis (§04). Proof: nobody beats $\sigma_1$; $v_1$ achieves it.

Proof (Thm 6) — No Vector Beats $\sigma_1$

Take any $\|x\|_2 = 1$; write $y = V^{\top}x$, so $\|y\|_2 = 1$ (Thm 4):

$$\|Ax\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(A = U\Sigma V^{\top})}{=}\; \|U \Sigma y\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(\text{Thm 4: } U \text{ free})}{=}\; \|\Sigma y\|_2^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2 y_i^2$$
$$\sum_i \sigma_i^2 y_i^2 \;\stackrel{(\sigma_i \leq \sigma_1)}{\leq}\; \sigma_1^2 \sum_i y_i^2 \;\leq\; \sigma_1^2 \|y\|_2^2 = \sigma_1^2$$

Proof (Thm 6) — And $v_1$ Achieves It

$$\|A v_1\|_2 = \|\sigma_1 u_1\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\|u_1\|_2 = 1)}{=}\; \sigma_1$$
  • $Av_1 = \sigma_1 u_1$: read it off $A = \sum_i \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top}$, since $v_i^{\top}v_1 = 0$ for $i \neq 1$
  • Upper bound $+$ witness $\Rightarrow$ $\|A\|_2 = \sigma_1$  $\blacksquare$

Remember this two-line algebra — the same move powers Theorem 8, Step 4

Theorem 7 — Frobenius via Sigmas

Theorem 7.

$$\|A\|_F^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2$$

Frobenius energy = sum of squared stretches — proof: one trace chain

Proof (Thm 7) — One Trace Chain

$$\|A\|_F^2 = \operatorname{tr}(A^{\top}A) \;\stackrel{(A^{\top}A = V\Sigma^{\top}\Sigma V^{\top})}{=}\; \operatorname{tr}(V \Sigma^{\top}\Sigma V^{\top})$$
$$\stackrel{(\operatorname{tr}(XY) = \operatorname{tr}(YX))}{=}\; \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{\top}\Sigma V^{\top} V) = \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{\top}\Sigma) = \sum_i \sigma_i^2 \;\;\blacksquare$$

Orthogonal invariance again — this time via the trace cycling $V^{\top}$ around to cancel $V$

04

Best Low-Rank Approximation

Eckart–Young–Mirsky

Candidate: Keep the $k$ Largest Layers

Sorted: $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_k \geq \sigma_{k+1} \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$,  budget $0 \leq k \lt r$

$$A \;=\; \underbrace{\sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top} + \cdots + \sigma_k u_k v_k^{\top}}_{\textstyle A_k \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top}\ \text{(kept)}} \;+\; \underbrace{\color{#9aa4af}{\sigma_{k+1} u_{k+1} v_{k+1}^{\top} + \cdots + \sigma_r u_r v_r^{\top}}}_{\textstyle \text{discarded}}$$

$\operatorname{rank}(A_k) = k$: inside the budget $\operatorname{rank} \leq k$

A natural candidate. Not yet proven best.

Worked: $A_1$ Is the Top Layer of $A$

Recall (SVD of the worked matrix).

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix}:\quad \sigma_1 = 3\sqrt{5},\;\; \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5},\quad u_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix},\;\; v_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$A_1 = \sigma_1 u_1 v_1^{\top} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{20}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix}$$

Rank one: both columns equal $1.5\,(1, 3)^{\top}$, along $u_1$

Worked: $A_1$'s Error Is the Discarded Layer

$$A - A_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & -1.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \sigma_2 u_2 v_2^{\top} \qquad \text{(rank one)}$$

Spectral: $\|A - A_1\|_2 = \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5}$, the residual's only singular value

Frobenius: $\|A - A_1\|_F = \sqrt{1.5^2 + 1.5^2 + 0.5^2 + 0.5^2} = \sqrt{5}$

Equal norms here only because the residual has rank one

An error computation for one candidate, not an optimality proof

Eckart–Young–Mirsky: Spectral Error

Theorem (Eckart–Young–Mirsky, spectral norm).

$A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ of rank $r$,  $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$,  any $0 \leq k \lt r$:

$$\min_{\substack{B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n} \\ \operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k}} \|A - B\|_2 \;=\; \sigma_{k+1}, \qquad \text{attained by } B = A_k$$

Minimum over every rank-$\leq k$ matrix, not only truncations

Spectral form only. The Frobenius form is a separate theorem.

Wilkinson, MATH3030 §3.5.2, Theorem 3.1 (2-norm proof on-page); Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, MML §4.6, Theorem 4.25

Optimal: a Floor for All, Met by One

Optimal $=$ lower bound for every competitor $+$ one competitor on it

no rank-$\leq k$ $B$ here $\sigma_{k+1}$: floor $0$ $A_k$ on it other rank-$\leq k$ matrices $B$ error $\|A - B\|_2$

One candidate's error alone says nothing about other $B$

Schematic: competitor positions illustrative

Recall: Low Rank Forces a Big Null Space

Recall (rank–nullity). For $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$:

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \;=\; n - \operatorname{rank}(B)$$
$$\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \dim \operatorname{null}(B) \;\geq\; n - k$$

$n$ = input dimension: $\operatorname{null}(B) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$

Every low-rank competitor has a large blind spot

Large Subspaces Must Intersect

Fact. Subspaces $S_1, S_2 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$\dim(S_1 \cap S_2) \;\geq\; \dim S_1 + \dim S_2 - n$$

Why: $S_1 + S_2 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ has dimension at most $n$

$$\dim S_1 = n - k,\;\; \dim S_2 = k + 1 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \dim(S_1 \cap S_2) \;\geq\; 1$$

Dimension $\geq 1$: a nonzero vector in both

Two Planes in $\mathbb{R}^3$ Share a Line

$S_1$: plane through $0$ $S_2$: plane through $0$ $0$ $w$

$\dim(S_1 \cap S_2) \geq 2 + 2 - 3 = 1$: red line lies in both

Drawn in $\mathbb{R}^3$. The count holds in any $\mathbb{R}^n$.

One Unit Witness Bounds the Maximum

$$\|M\|_2 \;=\; \max_{\|x\|_2 = 1} \|Mx\|_2 \;\geq\; \|Mw\|_2 \qquad \text{for any } w \text{ with } \|w\|_2 = 1$$
  • Lower-bounding a max: exhibit one input
  • Unit length required: scaling $w$ scales $\|Mw\|_2$

Plan: one unit $w$ where $\|(A - B)w\|_2$ must be large

Top $k{+}1$ Directions Stretch by $\geq \sigma_{k+1}$

Unit $w = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i v_i$, so $\sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i^2 = \|w\|_2^2 = 1$ ($v_i$ orthonormal)

$$\begin{aligned} \|Aw\|_2^2 &\;\stackrel{(Av_i = \sigma_i u_i)}{=}\; \Big\| \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i \sigma_i u_i \Big\|_2^2 \;\stackrel{(u_i \text{ orthonormal})}{=}\; \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{k+1} \sigma_i^2 c_i^2\\[4pt] &\;\stackrel{(\sigma_i \geq \sigma_{k+1})}{\geq}\; \sigma_{k+1}^2 \sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i^2 \;=\; \sigma_{k+1}^2 \end{aligned}$$

Sorted values: each $\sigma_i$ with $i \leq k{+}1$ is at least $\sigma_{k+1}$

Proof Plan: Find One Witness

$$\text{Target: for every } B \text{ with } \operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k, \quad \|A - B\|_2 \;\geq\; \sigma_{k+1} \;=\; \|A - A_k\|_2$$
  1. Blind spot: $\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \geq n - k$
  2. Strong subspace: $W = \operatorname{span}\{v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}\}$
  3. Intersect: unit $w \in \operatorname{null}(B) \cap W$
  4. Witness: $\|A - B\|_2 \geq \|Aw\|_2 \geq \sigma_{k+1}$
  5. Attain: $\|A - A_k\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$

Step 1: Every Competitor Has a Blind Spot

Fix an arbitrary $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with $\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) \;\stackrel{(\text{rank–nullity})}{=}\; n - \operatorname{rank}(B) \;\geq\; n - k$$

On $\operatorname{null}(B)$: $(A - B)x = Ax$, so the competitor drops out

Arbitrary $B$, not $A_k$: the bound must hold for all

Step 2: $A$ Stays Strong on $W$

$$W \;=\; \operatorname{span}\{v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}\} \;\subseteq\; \mathbb{R}^n, \qquad \dim W = k + 1$$
  • $k \lt r$: so $v_{k+1}$ exists, with $\sigma_{k+1} \gt 0$
  • Orthonormal $v_i$ $\Rightarrow$ linearly independent $\Rightarrow$ dimension $k+1$
  • Input space $\mathbb{R}^n$, same as $\operatorname{null}(B)$
  • Unit $w \in W$: $\|Aw\|_2 \geq \sigma_{k+1}$ (top-directions bound)

Step 3: The Two Subspaces Share a Unit $w$

$$\dim \operatorname{null}(B) + \dim W \;\geq\; (n - k) + (k + 1) \;=\; n + 1 \;\gt\; n$$

Intersection bound: $\dim\big(\operatorname{null}(B) \cap W\big) \geq 1$

$$z \neq 0 \text{ in both} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; w = \frac{z}{\|z\|_2} \in \operatorname{null}(B) \cap W, \qquad \|w\|_2 = 1$$

One $w$: killed by $B$, inside $W$

Step 4: Along $w$, the Residual Is $A$

$$\|A - B\|_2 \;\stackrel{(w \text{ unit})}{\geq}\; \|(A - B)w\|_2 \;=\; \|Aw - Bw\|_2 \;\stackrel{(Bw = 0)}{=}\; \|Aw\|_2$$
  • $Bw = 0$: $w \in \operatorname{null}(B)$ from Step 3
  • A lower bound from one witness, no maximum computed

Remaining: $\|Aw\|_2$ cannot be small

Step 4 (continued): The Witness Is Not Small

Recap: $w \in W$ and unit, so $w = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i v_i$ with $\sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i^2 = 1$

$$\|Aw\|_2^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} \sigma_i^2 c_i^2 \;\geq\; \sigma_{k+1}^2 \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} c_i^2 \;=\; \sigma_{k+1}^2$$

Both sides nonnegative: square roots give $\|Aw\|_2 \geq \sigma_{k+1}$

$$\|A - B\|_2 \;\geq\; \|Aw\|_2 \;\geq\; \sigma_{k+1} \qquad \text{for every } B \text{ with } \operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$$

Step 5: Truncation Meets the Bound

$$A - A_k \;=\; \sum_{i=k+1}^{r} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top}, \qquad \text{singular values } \sigma_{k+1} \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r$$

Already an SVD: orthonormal $u_i$, orthonormal $v_i$, sorted $\sigma_i \gt 0$

$$\|A - A_k\|_2 \;\stackrel{(\|M\|_2 = \sigma_1(M))}{=}\; \sigma_{k+1}$$

Bound for every $B$ $+$ attained by $A_k$ $\Rightarrow$ minimum $= \sigma_{k+1}$  $\blacksquare$

One Chain Proves the Spectral Theorem

Any $B$ with $\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k$:

$$\begin{aligned} &\dim\operatorname{null}(B) + \dim W \;\stackrel{(1,\,2)}{\geq}\; (n - k) + (k + 1) \gt n \;\stackrel{(3)}{\Longrightarrow}\; \text{unit } w \in \operatorname{null}(B) \cap W\\[6pt] &\|A - B\|_2 \;\stackrel{(w \text{ unit})}{\geq}\; \|(A - B)w\|_2 \;\stackrel{(Bw = 0)}{=}\; \|Aw\|_2 \;\stackrel{(w \in W)}{\geq}\; \sigma_{k+1} \;\stackrel{(5)}{=}\; \|A - A_k\|_2 \end{aligned}$$

Left end: arbitrary $B$. Right end: $A_k$.

So $\min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k} \|A - B\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$; labels name the step or fact used

Eckart–Young–Mirsky: Frobenius Error

Theorem (Eckart–Young–Mirsky, Frobenius norm).

Same setup: $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ of rank $r$,  $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r \gt 0$,  any $0 \leq k \lt r$:

$$\min_{\substack{B \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n} \\ \operatorname{rank}(B) \leq k}} \|A - B\|_F^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=k+1}^{r} \sigma_i^2, \qquad \text{attained by } B = A_k$$

The spectral proof does not prove this norm's optimality

Stated, not proved here: the proof needs min–max (Weyl) inequalities

Wilkinson, MATH3030 §3.5.2, Theorem 3.1 (stated for the Frobenius norm; proof on-page covers the 2-norm only)

$A_k$'s Error Is the Discarded Energy

$$A - A_k = \sum_{i=k+1}^{r} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\top} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \|A - A_k\|_F^2 = \sum_{i=k+1}^{r} \sigma_i^2$$

Residual's singular values: $\sigma_{k+1}, \dots, \sigma_r$. Use $\|M\|_F^2 = \sum_i \sigma_i(M)^2$.

Computes one candidate's error: achievability

No other rank-$\leq k$ $B$ does better: the theorem

This calculation alone is not an optimality proof

Theorems with $k = 1$, $\sigma_2 = \sqrt{5}$: no rank-$\leq 1$ $B$ does better

$$\min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq 1} \|A - B\|_2 = \sigma_2 = \sqrt{5}, \qquad \min_{\operatorname{rank}(B) \leq 1} \|A - B\|_F^2 = \sigma_2^2 = 5$$
$$\text{attained by } A_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.5 \\ 4.5 & 4.5 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \|A - A_1\|_2 = \|A - A_1\|_F = \sqrt{5}$$

Both errors $\sqrt{5}$: the residual has rank one

The earlier calculation was achievability. The theorem certifies the floor.

One Layer Keeps 90% of the Energy

$$\text{energy kept} = \frac{\|A_1\|_F^2}{\|A\|_F^2} = \frac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2} = \frac{45}{45 + 5} = 90\%$$
$\sigma_1^2 = 45$: kept by $A_1$ $\sigma_2^2 = 5$ bar length $= \|A\|_F^2 = 9 + 16 + 25 = 50$

An energy share, not prediction accuracy

"Explained variance" only when the data matrix is centered

Worst Error and Total Error Can Differ

$$D = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},\quad D_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix},\quad D - D_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\|D - D_1\|_2 = 3 \;\;(\text{worst stretch}), \qquad \|D - D_1\|_F = \sqrt{3^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{10} \approx 3.16$$

Rank-two residual: the two norms separate

Spectral counts the largest dropped $\sigma$; Frobenius counts all of them

What the Guarantee Covers

Given

every entry of $A$

$\Rightarrow$

Choose

$\Rightarrow$

Get

$A_k$, optimal error

Optimal error: $\sigma_{k+1}$ spectral, $\sum_{i>k} \sigma_i^2$ Frobenius squared

Every entry weighted equally in the error

Not covered by this theorem

  • Missing entries: $A$ only partly observed
  • Downstream accuracy of a model built on $A_k$
  • Statistically optimal denoising of a noisy $A$
