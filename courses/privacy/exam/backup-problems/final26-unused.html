Backup. Seven candidate problems not selected for the assigned final (which uses the two-point diffusion score, LiRA, and the rigorous NPO-vs-GA problems). All assume continuous densities on $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{R}^d$) and well-defined integrals; no measure theory.

1. Exact DDPM on a Gaussian Data Distribution.02 · diffusion Consider the scalar DDPM forward process with $X^{(0)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, s^2)$ and, for $n = 1, \ldots, N$, $X^{(n)} = \sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,X^{(n-1)} + \sqrt{\beta_n}\,\varepsilon_n$, $\varepsilon_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ i.i.d., so $q(X^{(n)} \mid X^{(0)}) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\,X^{(0)}, 1-\bar\alpha_n)$ with $\bar\alpha_n = \prod_{k\le n}(1-\beta_k)$.
  1. Show the marginal is $\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\mu, v_n)$, $v_n = \bar\alpha_n s^2 + (1-\bar\alpha_n)$. $X^{(n)} = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}X^{(0)} + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_n}\,\bar\varepsilon$; add variances.
  2. Show the exact reverse transition $X^{(n-1)}\mid X^{(n)}=x$ is $\mathcal{N}(a_n x + b_n, c_n)$ with $a_n = \sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,v_{n-1}/v_n$, $b_n = \mu(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{n-1}} - a_n\sqrt{\bar\alpha_n})$, $c_n = v_{n-1}(1 - (1-\beta_n)v_{n-1}/v_n)$. Condition on $X^{(0)}$, marginalize $X^{(0)}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,s^2)$: $\mathrm{Cov}(X^{(n-1)},X^{(n)}) = \sqrt{1-\beta_n}\,v_{n-1}$; apply the conditional-Gaussian formula.
  3. Initializing the reverse chain at $\tilde X^{(N)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ instead of $p_N$, with $A=\prod_n a_n$, show $A=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_N}s^2/v_N$ and $\tilde X^{(0)}\sim\mathcal{N}(\tilde m_0,\tilde v_0)$, $\tilde m_0 = \mu(1-\bar\alpha_N)/v_N$, $\tilde v_0 = s^2 + \bar\alpha_N^2 s^4(1-s^2)/v_N^2$. $\prod\sqrt{1-\beta_n}=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_N}$, $\prod v_{n-1}/v_n = v_0/v_N$; track $m_{n-1}=a_n m_n+b_n$, $V_{n-1}=a_n^2 V_n+c_n$.
  4. Show the mean bias is $-\mu\bar\alpha_N s^2/v_N = O(\bar\alpha_N)$, vanishing as $\bar\alpha_N\to0$; explain why this initialization gap is the only error of exact-score sampling. $1-\bar\alpha_N-v_N = -\bar\alpha_N s^2$; as $\bar\alpha_N\to0$, $p_N\to\mathcal{N}(0,1)$.
3. The Best Linear Denoiser.02 · diffusion A forward step maps $X_0\in\mathbb{R}$ with mean $m$, variance $s^2$ (otherwise arbitrary) to $Y = a X_0 + \sqrt{v}\,\varepsilon$, $a=\sqrt{\bar\alpha}$, $v=1-\bar\alpha$, $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ independent. Restrict to affine denoisers $\hat X_0=\kappa Y + c$.
  1. Find $(\kappa^\star,c^\star)$ minimizing $\mathbb{E}[(\hat X_0-X_0)^2]$: $\kappa^\star = \tfrac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha}\,s^2}{\bar\alpha s^2+(1-\bar\alpha)}$, $c^\star=m(1-\kappa^\star\sqrt{\bar\alpha})$. Orthogonality principle: $\mathbb{E}[(\hat X_0-X_0)Y]=0$, $\mathbb{E}[\hat X_0-X_0]=0$; $\mathrm{Cov}(X_0,Y)=as^2$, $\mathrm{Var}(Y)=a^2s^2+v$.
  2. Show the minimal error is $\mathrm{MMSE}_{\mathrm{lin}} = \tfrac{s^2(1-\bar\alpha)}{\bar\alpha s^2+(1-\bar\alpha)}$, increasing in noise: $\to0$ as $\bar\alpha\to1$, $\to s^2$ as $\bar\alpha\to0$. $\mathrm{Var}(X_0) - \mathrm{Cov}(X_0,Y)^2/\mathrm{Var}(Y)$.
  3. Show that for Gaussian $X_0$ this affine denoiser is globally optimal ($=\mathbb{E}[X_0\mid Y]$); give a one-line reason it fails for non-Gaussian data, citing the two-point ($\tanh$) case. Jointly Gaussian $\Rightarrow$ conditional mean is affine.
4. Cross-Entropy, Perplexity, and Temperature Sampling.02 · LLM Autoregressive $p_\theta(x_{1:T}) = \prod_{t} p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{1:t-1})$; true distribution $p_{\mathrm{data}}$.
  1. For $\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[-\log p_\theta(x)]$, prove $\mathcal{L}(\theta) = H(p_{\mathrm{data}}) + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{data}}\|p_\theta)$; conclude the minimizer is $p_\theta=p_{\mathrm{data}}$ with value $H(p_{\mathrm{data}})$. Add/subtract $\log p_{\mathrm{data}}$; $D_{\mathrm{KL}}\ge0$ (Gibbs).
  2. For $\mathrm{PPL}=\exp(-\tfrac1T\sum_t \log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{1:t-1}))$, show it equals $\exp$ of cross-entropy and the geometric mean $\prod_t p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{1:t-1})^{-1/T}$; interpret $\mathrm{PPL}=k$ as branching factor. $\exp(-\tfrac1T\sum\log q_t)=\prod q_t^{-1/T}$.
  3. For temperature sampling $p^{(\tau)}_i\propto\exp(z_i/\tau)$: show $\tau\to0^+$ concentrates on $\arg\max_i z_i$, $\tau\to\infty$ tends to uniform, and $H(p^{(\tau)})$ is non-decreasing in $\tau$. $\beta=1/\tau$, $p_i\propto e^{\beta z_i}$; $\tfrac{dH}{d\beta}=-\beta\,\mathrm{Var}_{p(\beta)}(z)\le0$.
5. Secret Sharer: Canary Exposure.03 · memorization Canary $c$ inserted; candidate set $\mathcal{R}$, $|\mathcal{R}|=R$; model log-perplexity $\ell(r)$; $\mathrm{exposure}(c)=\log_2 R - \log_2\mathrm{rank}(c)$.
  1. Show $\mathrm{exposure}(c) = -\log_2\widehat F(\ell(c))$, $\widehat F$ the empirical CDF of $\{\ell(r)\}$; conclude range $[0,\log_2 R]$, maximal when $c$ is most likely. $\mathrm{rank}(c)=R\,\widehat F(\ell(c))$.
  2. Modeling candidate log-perplexities as $\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$, show $\mathrm{exposure}(c)=-\log_2\Phi(\tfrac{\ell(c)-\mu}{\sigma})$; for $\ell(c)=\mu-k\sigma$, evaluate $-\log_2\Phi(-k)$ at $k=1,2,3,4$ and comment on super-linear growth. $\Phi(-k)\approx\phi(k)/k$ $\Rightarrow$ $\mathrm{exposure}\approx(k^2/2)\log_2 e + \log_2 k + \mathrm{const}$.
  3. Argue thresholding exposure is a per-example likelihood-ratio test (high exposure $\Leftrightarrow$ optimal MIA declares member); one sentence on why DP caps exposure. Neyman–Pearson on the in/out Gaussian model; DP bounds the shift $(\mu-\ell(c))/\sigma$.
6. Bayes-Optimal Membership Test.04 · MIA $B\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\pi)$; $T$ has density $p_b$ given $B=b$; rule $\hat B:\mathbb{R}\to\{0,1\}$ judged by $0$-$1$ error.
  1. Compute $\Pr[B=1\mid T=t]$ in terms of $p_0,p_1,\pi$. Bayes' rule.
  2. Show the MAP rule is $\hat B_{\mathrm{MAP}}(t)=\mathbf{1}[\Lambda(t)\ge\tfrac{1-\pi}{\pi}]$, $\Lambda=p_1/p_0$; relate to Neyman–Pearson (only the threshold tracks the prior). Output $1$ iff posterior $\ge\tfrac12$; cross-multiply.
  3. For $\ell\mid B{=}1\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathrm{in}},\sigma^2)$, $\ell\mid B{=}0\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathrm{out}},\sigma^2)$ with $\mu_{\mathrm{in}}$ below $\mu_{\mathrm{out}}$, reduce to $\hat B=\mathbf{1}[\ell\le c]$ and find $c$ at $\pi=\tfrac12$. $\log\Lambda$ affine, strictly decreasing in $\ell$.
8. Concrete MIA on a Gaussian Mean Model.04 · MIA + DP $\theta(D)=\bar z + W$, $\bar z=\tfrac1n\sum z_i$, $W\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\rho^2)$; population $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Game: $b\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{0,1\}$; $b{=}1\Rightarrow z^*=z_1\in D$, $b{=}0\Rightarrow z^*\sim\mathcal{P}$ fresh.
  1. Show $\theta\mid(b{=}1)\sim\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{z^*}{n}, \tfrac{n-1}{n^2}+\rho^2)$ and $\theta\mid(b{=}0)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tfrac1n+\rho^2)$ (conditional on $z^*$). Split $\bar z=z^*/n+\tfrac1n\sum_{i\ge2}z_i$ under $b{=}1$.
  2. Show the optimal statistic is the conditional likelihood ratio; write $\log\Lambda$ as a quadratic in $\theta$ and deduce the "member" region is an interval. $V_1=\tfrac{n-1}{n^2}+\rho^2$ is below $V_0=\tfrac1n+\rho^2$ $\Rightarrow$ negative $\theta^2$-coefficient.
  3. With $|z_i|\le1$ ($L_2$-sensitivity $\le2/n$) and $\rho\ge\tfrac{2}{n\varepsilon}\sqrt{2\log(1.25/\delta)}$ for $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP, use $\mathrm{Adv}^\star\le e^\varepsilon-1+\delta$ (HW4) to bound the advantage at $\varepsilon{=}0.1,\delta{=}10^{-5}$; explain why $\varepsilon{=}1$ is vacuous. $e^{0.1}-1+10^{-5}\approx0.105$; $e-1\approx1.72>1$.
10. Black-Box Unlearning Audits Require Many Queries.05 · unlearning $P_0,P_1$ output laws; $\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|$, $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)\le\eta$. Auditor sees $q$ i.i.d. samples, outputs $\hat b$.
  1. (Le Cam.) Show $\Pr_{b=0}[\hat b\ne0]+\Pr_{b=1}[\hat b\ne1]\ge 1-\mathrm{TV}(P_0^{\otimes q},P_1^{\otimes q})$. Identify $\hat b$ with $\psi:\mathcal{X}^q\to[0,1]$; prove $\mathbb{E}_P[\psi]-\mathbb{E}_Q[\psi]\le\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)$ via $\psi(x)=\int_0^1\mathbf{1}[\psi(x)>s]\,ds$.
  2. Show $\mathrm{TV}(P_0^{\otimes q},P_1^{\otimes q})\le q\,\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)\le q\eta$ by a hybrid argument $H_k=P_0^{\otimes k}\otimes P_1^{\otimes(q-k)}$. Telescope $\mathrm{TV}(H_q,H_0)\le\sum_k\mathrm{TV}(H_k,H_{k-1})$; consecutive hybrids differ in one coordinate.
  3. Conclude $q\ge\tfrac{1-2\alpha}{\eta}$ for total error $\le2\alpha$ (tighter: $q=\Omega(1/\eta^2)$); why are single-prompt audits insufficient for small perturbations? $2\alpha\ge1-q\eta$.
