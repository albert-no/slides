01

$(\varepsilon, \delta)$-Differential Privacy

A small failure probability, made precise.

Where We Stand

Recall ($\varepsilon$-DP).

For all neighbors $D, D'$ and every event $O \subseteq \mathcal{Y}$,

$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in O] \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon}\, \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in O].$$
  • A ratio bound: it must hold even for probability-$10^{-100}$ tails.
  • Laplace noise achieves it; Gaussian noise cannot.
Question: can we exempt events too rare to matter?

Gaussian Noise Breaks Pure DP

$$\frac{p_{X}(y)}{p_{X'}(y)} \;=\; \exp\!\Big(\frac{K\,(K + 2(y - f(X)))}{2\sigma^2}\Big) \;\longrightarrow\; \infty \qquad \text{as } y \to \infty.$$
$f(X)$ $f(X')$ ratio explodes in the tail

Here $K = f(X) - f(X') \gt 0$: no finite $\varepsilon$ works, yet the offending region is vanishingly rare.

Approximate Differential Privacy

Definition ($(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP).

$\mathcal{M}: \mathcal{X}^n \to \mathcal{Y}$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP if for every neighboring $D, D'$ and every event $O \subseteq \mathcal{Y}$,

$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in O] \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon}\, \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in O] \;+\; \delta.$$
  • $\delta = 0$: recovers pure $\varepsilon$-DP.
  • $\delta \gt 0$: an additive allowance on top of the ratio.
Dwork, Kenthapadi, McSherry, Mironov, Naor, "Our Data, Ourselves", EUROCRYPT 2006.

Two Kinds of Slack

Multiplicative $e^{\varepsilon}$

Relative: scales with the event.

A $10^{-9}$ event stays $\approx 10^{-9}$.

Additive $\delta$

Absolute: same allowance for every event.

Swamps any event of probability below $\delta$.

Event prob.Ratio bound ($\varepsilon = 1$)Additive bound ($\delta = 10^{-6}$)
$10^{-2}$$\approx 2.7 \times 10^{-2}$$10^{-2} + 10^{-6} \approx 10^{-2}$
$10^{-9}$$\approx 2.7 \times 10^{-9}$$10^{-9} + 10^{-6} \approx 10^{-6}$
On a $\delta$-mass of outcomes, the ratio guarantee may fail entirely.

Picture — Where the Ratio Bound May Fail

$f(D)$ $f(D')$ ratio $\le e^{\varepsilon}$ holds here mass $\le \delta$

A small sliver of outcomes is exempt; everywhere else, pure-DP behavior.

  • The sliver's location is mechanism-dependent; the definition allows any placement.
  • Outside it, the pure-DP ratio argument applies unchanged.

Interpretations

ViewGuarantee
Odds ratioAdversary's odds move by $\le e^{\varepsilon}$, except probability $\delta$
Hypothesis test$H_0$: $i$ in $D$ vs $H_1$: $i$ replaced; log-likelihood ratio $\le \varepsilon$ up to a $\delta$-event

What $\delta$ Does Not Promise

Counterexample — catastrophe is allowed.

With probability $1-\delta$ output $\perp$; with probability $\delta$ publish the whole dataset.

$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in O] \;\le\; \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in O] + \delta \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad (0, \delta)\text{-DP}.$$

The $\delta$-mass need not be a mild tail: the definition permits total failure.

Read $\delta$ as the probability of catastrophic failure, and size it accordingly.

Calibrating $\delta$ — How Small Is Small?

"Publish each record independently w.p. $\delta$" is $(0,\delta)$-DP, yet exposes $n\delta$ people.

Choice of $\delta$ ($n = 10^6$)Records exposed (expected)Verdict
$10^{-3}$$\approx 1000$disaster
$10^{-6} = 1/n$$\approx 1$still someone
$10^{-8}$$\approx 0.01$acceptable
Rule of thumb: $\delta \ll 1/n$; practice uses $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-9}$.
02

Privacy-Loss Random Variable

The log-likelihood ratio the adversary actually sees.

Privacy Loss, as a Random Variable

Definition (privacy loss).

For neighbors $X, X'$, the privacy loss at output $y$ is

$$I_{X, X'}(y) \;=\; \log \frac{\Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) = y]}{\Pr[\mathcal{M}(X') = y]}.$$

The privacy-loss random variable is $I_{X, X'}(Y)$ with $Y = \mathcal{M}(X)$.

  • Exactly the adversary's log-likelihood ratio upon seeing $y$.
  • Continuous outputs: replace probabilities by densities.

Pure DP in This Language

$$\mathcal{M} \text{ is } \varepsilon\text{-DP} \iff |I_{X,X'}(y)| \le \varepsilon \ \text{ for all } y \text{ and all neighbors } X, X'.$$

Pure DP

Sup bound: loss $\le \varepsilon$ on every outcome.

Approximate DP

Tail bound: loss exceeds $\varepsilon$ only with probability $\le \delta$.

Making the second column precise is the next lemma.

Picture — Distribution of the Privacy Loss

$0$ $\varepsilon$ law of $I_{X,X'}(Y)$ tail mass $\le \delta$

Control the tail of the loss, and $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP follows.

The Tail Lemma

Lemma (tail bound suffices).

If for all neighbors $X, X'$

$$\Pr\big[\, I_{X, X'}(Y) \gt \varepsilon \,\big] \;\le\; \delta, \qquad Y = \mathcal{M}(X),$$

then $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP.

  • Workhorse of this lecture: both mechanisms below use it.
  • One direction only; the converse holds just approximately.

Proof — the Good/Bad Split

Partition outcomes by whether the loss behaves:

$$G = \{\, y : I_{X, X'}(y) \le \varepsilon \,\}, \qquad B = \{\, y : I_{X, X'}(y) \gt \varepsilon \,\}.$$

The assumption says the bad set is rare:

$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in B] \;=\; \Pr[\,I_{X,X'}(Y) \gt \varepsilon\,] \;\le\; \delta.$$
Trick: good/bad split. Handle typical outcomes and rare outcomes separately.

Proof (continued) — Split Any Event

Fix any event $S \subseteq \mathcal{Y}$ and cut it along the partition:

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in S] \;&=\; \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in S \cap G] \;+\; \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in S \cap B] \\[4pt] &\le\; \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in S \cap G] \;+\; \underbrace{\Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in B]}_{\le\, \delta}. \end{aligned}$$

The bad piece is surrendered to $\delta$. The good piece remains.

Proof (continued) — the Good Piece

On $G$ the pointwise ratio holds: $\Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) = y] \le e^{\varepsilon} \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X') = y]$. Sum over $S \cap G$:

$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in S \cap G] \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon}\, \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X') \in S \cap G] \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon}\, \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X') \in S].$$

Last step: $S \cap G \subseteq S$, and probabilities are monotone.

Trick: pointwise to setwise. A uniform ratio bound survives summation (and integration).

Proof Recap — One Chain

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in S] \;&\stackrel{(\text{split})}{=}\; \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in S \cap G] + \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in S \cap B] \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{good: ratio})}{\le}\; e^{\varepsilon}\, \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X') \in S] + \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X) \in B] \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{bad: tail})}{\le}\; e^{\varepsilon}\, \Pr[\mathcal{M}(X') \in S] + \delta. \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

Every $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ proof below reduces to bounding one tail: $\Pr[\,I \gt \varepsilon\,] \le \delta$.

03

Truncated Laplace

Bounded noise; $\delta$ pays for the cut tails.

Why Bound the Noise?

Recall (Laplace mechanism).

$\mathcal{M}(X) = f(X) + \mathrm{Lap}(\Delta_1/\varepsilon)^{\otimes d}$ is $\varepsilon$-DP; noise support is all of $\mathbb{R}$.

  • Occasionally the noise is huge: a count of $37$ may report $-142$.
  • Downstream systems may need outputs in a fixed range.
  • Goal: keep Laplace shape, cap the noise at $\pm\tau$.
Dwork, McSherry, Nissim, Smith, "Calibrating Noise to Sensitivity in Private Data Analysis", TCC 2006.

Truncated Laplace — Construction

$$Z \sim \mathrm{LapTr}(\lambda, \tau): \qquad f_Z(z) \;=\; \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{Z_0}\, e^{-|z|/\lambda} & |z| \le \tau, \\[4pt] 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$
$-\tau$ $+\tau$ renormalized by $Z_0$ cut tail cut tail

Same exponential shape on $[-\tau, \tau]$; tails removed, mass rescaled.

Truncated Laplace Mechanism

Mechanism.

$\mathcal{M}(X) = f(X) + (Z_1, \dots, Z_d)$, with $Z_i \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathrm{LapTr}(\Delta/\varepsilon,\, \tau)$.

Theorem.

With $\lambda = \Delta/\varepsilon$ and truncation

$$\tau \;\ge\; \Delta + \frac{\Delta}{\varepsilon}\,\ln\frac{1}{\delta},$$

the mechanism is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP (per coordinate; sketch next).

Where $\delta$ Comes From

$p_X$ $p_{X'}$ only $p_X$ lives here width $\le \Delta$

Near the edge only $p_X$ is positive: infinite ratio, so that mass must be $\le \delta$.

Proof Sketch — Interior vs Edge

Interior (both densities positive): normalizers $Z_0$ cancel; the Laplace argument applies.

$$\frac{p_X(y)}{p_{X'}(y)} \;=\; e^{\left(|y - f(X')| - |y - f(X)|\right)/\lambda} \;\le\; e^{\Delta/\lambda} \;=\; e^{\varepsilon}.$$

Edge strip (only $p_X$ positive): a bad set of width $\le \Delta$, mass

$$\Pr[\text{edge}] \;\le\; e^{-(\tau - \Delta)/\lambda} \;\le\; \delta \quad \text{once } \tau \ge \Delta + \lambda \ln\tfrac{1}{\delta}.$$

Tail lemma finishes. Constants use $Z_0 \ge \lambda$, valid for $\tau \ge \lambda \ln 2$.

Laplace vs Truncated Laplace

PropertyLaplaceTruncated Laplace
Support$\mathbb{R}$$[-\tau, \tau]$

Typical noise$\approx \lambda$$\approx \lambda$
Worst-case noiseunbounded$\le \tau$
Same utility, bounded worst case; the price is a nonzero $\delta$.
04

Gaussian Mechanism

Gaussian noise calibrated to $\ell_2$ sensitivity: the workhorse of private ML.

$\ell_2$ Sensitivity

Definition ($\ell_2$ sensitivity).

$$\Delta_2 \;=\; \max_{D \sim D'} \big\| f(D) - f(D') \big\|_2.$$
  • Recall $\Delta_1 = \max_{D \sim D'} \|f(D) - f(D')\|_1$ calibrated Laplace noise.
  • Always $\Delta_2 \le \Delta_1 \le \sqrt{d}\, \Delta_2$: the gap can reach $\sqrt d$.
  • Gaussian noise will pay $\Delta_2$, not $\Delta_1$; the payoff comes shortly.

The Gaussian Mechanism

Mechanism.

$\mathcal{M}(D) = f(D) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$, with $f: \mathcal{X}^n \to \mathbb{R}^d$.

Theorem (Gaussian mechanism).

For $\varepsilon \in (0, 1)$ and

$$\sigma \;\ge\; \frac{\Delta_2 \sqrt{2 \ln(1.25/\delta)}}{\varepsilon},$$

the mechanism is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP.

Dwork, Roth, "The Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy", 2014.

Intuition — Two Shifted Gaussians

$f(X)$ $f(X')$ $|K| \le \Delta$ $I(y) = \varepsilon$ loss $\gt \varepsilon$: mass $\le \delta$

The loss exceeds $\varepsilon$ only far out; choose $\sigma$ so that tail is $\le \delta$.

Proof Roadmap

  1. Compute the 1-D privacy loss $I(y)$: two Gaussian exponents subtract.
  2. Complete the square: $I(y)$ is linear in $y$, so $I(Y)$ is Gaussian.
  3. Apply the Gaussian tail bound to $\Pr[\,I(Y) \gt \varepsilon\,]$.
  4. Choose $\sigma$ so the tail is below $\delta$; invoke the tail lemma.
  5. Lift to $\mathbb{R}^d$: rotation invariance, only the shift direction matters.

Steps 1–4 are one dimension; step 5 reduces $d$ dimensions to that case.

Proof — Privacy Loss of a Gaussian Shift

One dimension. Let $K = f(X) - f(X')$, $|K| \le \Delta$. Both densities are Gaussians with the same $\sigma$:

$$\begin{aligned} I_{X, X'}(y) \;&=\; \log \frac{\exp\!\big({-(y - f(X))^2/2\sigma^2}\big)}{\exp\!\big({-(y - f(X'))^2/2\sigma^2}\big)} \\[4pt] &=\; \frac{(y - f(X'))^2 - (y - f(X))^2}{2\sigma^2}. \end{aligned}$$

Normalizing constants cancel: same $\sigma$ on both sides.

Proof (continued) — Complete the Square

Difference of squares, $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$, with $a = y - f(X')$, $b = y - f(X)$:

$$I_{X, X'}(y) \;=\; \frac{K \big( K + 2(y - f(X)) \big)}{2\sigma^2} \qquad \text{(linear in } y\text{)}.$$
Trick: complete the square. The quadratics cancel; the loss is linear in $y$.

Proof (continued) — the Loss Is Gaussian

Plug in $Y = f(X) + \sigma N$, $N \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$:

$$I_{X, X'}(Y) \;=\; \frac{K^2}{2\sigma^2} + \frac{K}{\sigma} N \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\Big( \frac{K^2}{2\sigma^2},\; \frac{K^2}{\sigma^2} \Big).$$
  • A linear map of a Gaussian is Gaussian.
  • Mean is half the variance: one parameter $K/\sigma$ controls the tail.

Proof (continued) — Gaussian Tail Bound

Recall the standard tail: $\Pr[\mathcal{N}(0,1) \ge t] \le e^{-t^2/2}$. Solve the linear loss for $N$:

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr[\,I_{X,X'}(Y) \gt \varepsilon\,] \;&=\; \Pr\!\Big[ N \gt \frac{\varepsilon \sigma}{K} - \frac{K}{2\sigma} \Big] \\[4pt] &\le\; \exp\!\Big({-\tfrac12 \Big( \frac{\varepsilon\sigma}{K} - \frac{K}{2\sigma} \Big)^{\!2}}\Big) \;\le\; \exp\!\Big({-\tfrac12 \Big( \frac{\varepsilon\sigma}{\Delta} - \frac{\Delta}{2\sigma} \Big)^{\!2}}\Big). \end{aligned}$$

Last step: the bound is worst for the largest allowed shift, $K = \Delta$.

Proof (continued) — Choosing $\sigma$

Set $\sigma = c\Delta/\varepsilon$ with $c = \sqrt{2 \ln(1.25/\delta)}$. The dominant term gives

$$\exp\!\Big({-\tfrac{1}{2}\, \frac{\varepsilon^2 \sigma^2}{\Delta^2}}\Big) \;=\; \exp\!\Big({-\tfrac{c^2}{2}}\Big) \;=\; \exp\big( \ln (\delta / 1.25) \big) \;=\; \frac{\delta}{1.25} \;\lt\; \delta.$$

The $\Delta/2\sigma$ correction is absorbed by the $1.25$ slack (Dwork & Roth, App. A).

Tail below $\delta$, so the tail lemma yields $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP in one dimension. $\blacksquare$

Proof Recap — the 1-D Chain

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr[\,I(Y) \gt \varepsilon\,] \;&\stackrel{(\text{loss is Gaussian})}{=}\; \Pr\!\Big[ N \gt \frac{\varepsilon\sigma}{K} - \frac{K}{2\sigma} \Big] \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{Gaussian tail})}{\le}\; \exp\!\Big({-\tfrac12 \Big( \frac{\varepsilon\sigma}{\Delta} - \frac{\Delta}{2\sigma} \Big)^{\!2}}\Big) \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\sigma = c\Delta/\varepsilon)}{\le}\; \frac{\delta}{1.25} \;\le\; \delta. \end{aligned}$$

Then the tail lemma converts the tail bound into $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP.

Lifting to $\mathbb{R}^d$ — a Projection Lemma

Lemma.

If $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$, then for any $v \in \mathbb{R}^d$: $\;v \cdot Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\, \sigma^2 \|v\|^2)$.

Proof. The law of $Z$ is spherically symmetric. Rotate coordinates so $v = \|v\|\, e_1$: then

$$v \cdot Z \;=\; \|v\|\, Z_1 \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}(0,\, \sigma^2 \|v\|^2). \qquad \blacksquare$$
Trick: rotation invariance. A spherical Gaussian looks the same in every orthonormal basis.

Proof — the $d$-Dimensional Loss Is Already 1-D

Let $v = f(X) - f(X')$, $\|v\|_2 \le \Delta_2$. Same computation, with norms:

$$I_{X, X'}(y) \;=\; \frac{\|y - f(X')\|^2 - \|y - f(X)\|^2}{2\sigma^2} \;=\; \frac{\|v\|^2}{2\sigma^2} + \frac{v \cdot (y - f(X))}{\sigma^2}.$$

By the lemma, $v \cdot (Y - f(X)) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 \|v\|^2)$, so

$$I_{X, X'}(Y) \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\Big( \frac{\|v\|^2}{2\sigma^2},\; \frac{\|v\|^2}{\sigma^2} \Big): \quad \text{the 1-D law with } K = \|v\| \le \Delta_2.$$

The 1-D tail bound applies verbatim. $\blacksquare$

Picture — Only the Shift Direction Matters

$f(X)$ $f(X')$ $v$ orthogonal components cancel axis along $v$

The loss depends on $y$ only through $v \cdot y$: a 1-D Gaussian problem in disguise.

Worked Example — $d$ Counting Queries

Answer $d$ binary queries at once: $f(X) \in \{0, 1\}^d$. One person can flip every coordinate by $1$.

$\ell_1$ sensitivity

$$\Delta_1 = \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d} 1 = d.$$

$\ell_2$ sensitivity

$$\Delta_2 = \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d} 1^2} = \sqrt{d}.$$
$d = 10\,000$: $\;\Delta_1 = 10\,000$ but $\Delta_2 = 100$.

Why Gaussian Wins in High Dimension

Laplace$\Delta_1 = d$$\lambda = 10\,000$

  • Roughly a $\sqrt{d}$ advantage: decisive when $d$ is large.
  • Model gradients are exactly such high-dimensional queries.

Properties of Approximate DP

Post-processing, composition, group privacy, subsampling: with $\delta$ bookkeeping.

Post-Processing

Theorem (post-processing).

If $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP and $g$ is any (possibly randomized) map, then $g \circ \mathcal{M}$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP.

Proof (deterministic $g$; randomize by averaging). For any event $O'$:

$$\Pr[g(\mathcal{M}(D)) \in O'] \;=\; \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D) \in g^{-1}(O')] \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon} \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D') \in g^{-1}(O')] + \delta. \qquad \blacksquare$$
Trick: preimage. An event about $g(\mathcal M(D))$ is an event about $\mathcal M(D)$.
Dwork, Roth, "The Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy", 2014.

Basic Composition

Theorem (basic composition).

If $\mathcal{M}_1$ is $(\varepsilon_1, \delta_1)$-DP and $\mathcal{M}_2$ is $(\varepsilon_2, \delta_2)$-DP, the pair $(\mathcal{M}_1, \mathcal{M}_2)$ is $(\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2,\; \delta_1 + \delta_2)$-DP.

  1. Proof sketch. Run the good/bad split for each mechanism.
  2. Union-bound the two bad events: total mass $\le \delta_1 + \delta_2$.
  3. On the joint good event the density ratios multiply: factor $e^{\varepsilon_1} e^{\varepsilon_2}$.
Dwork, Roth, "The Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy", 2014.

$k$-Fold Composition — the Budget View

$$\mathcal{M}_i \text{ is } (\varepsilon_i, \delta_i)\text{-DP} \;\implies\; (\mathcal{M}_1, \dots, \mathcal{M}_k) \text{ is } \Big( \textstyle\sum_i \varepsilon_i,\; \sum_i \delta_i \Big)\text{-DP}.$$
Queries ($k$)Total $\varepsilon$ (each $0.1$)Total $\delta$ (each $10^{-8}$)
$1$$0.1$$10^{-8}$
$10$$1.0$$10^{-7}$
$100$$10$$10^{-6}$

Linear growth in both parameters; tighter accounting appears at the end of this section.

Group Privacy

Theorem (group privacy).

If $\mathcal{M}$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP, then for datasets differing in $t$ records it satisfies

$$\Big( t\varepsilon,\;\; \frac{e^{t\varepsilon} - 1}{e^{\varepsilon} - 1}\, \delta \Big)\text{-DP}.$$

For small $\varepsilon$ the $\delta$ factor is $\approx t$: roughly $(t\varepsilon,\, t\delta)$.

Dwork, Roth, "The Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy", 2014.

Proof — Walk a Neighbor Chain

$D_0$
$\times e^{\varepsilon}, + \delta$→
$D_1$
$\times e^{\varepsilon}, + \delta$→
$D_2$
 ⋯
$D_t$
$$\Pr[\mathcal{M}(D_0) \in S] \;\le\; e^{t\varepsilon}\, \Pr[\mathcal{M}(D_t) \in S] + \delta \underbrace{\big( 1 + e^{\varepsilon} + \cdots + e^{(t-1)\varepsilon} \big)}_{=\, (e^{t\varepsilon} - 1)/(e^{\varepsilon} - 1)}. \qquad \blacksquare$$
Trick: telescoping. Each hop multiplies the ratio by $e^{\varepsilon}$ and injects a fresh $\delta$; the injected $\delta$s compound geometrically.

Subsampling Amplification

Theorem (Poisson subsampling).

Sample each record i.i.d. with probability $q$, then run $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP $\mathcal{M}$: the result is $(\varepsilon', \delta')$-DP,

$$\varepsilon' \;=\; \ln\!\big( 1 + q (e^{\varepsilon} - 1) \big), \qquad \delta' \;=\; q\, \delta.$$
  • $\delta' = q\delta$: the bad event needs the differing record sampled.
  • Small $q, \varepsilon$: $\varepsilon' \approx q\varepsilon$; pure DP stays pure.

Beyond Basic Composition

Private training runs thousands of subsampled Gaussian steps; linear $(k\varepsilon, k\delta)$ is far too crude.

  • Moments accountant: track all moments of the privacy-loss RV, not just its tail.
  • Rényi DP: the same idea as a clean calculus; composition becomes additive.
Tight many-step accounting lives one level up: moments of $I(Y)$, not one tail bound.
Abadi, Chu, Goodfellow, McMahan, Mironov, Talwar, Zhang, "Deep Learning with Differential Privacy", CCS 2016 — Mironov, "Rényi Differential Privacy", CSF 2017.

Recap

  • $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP: ratio bound plus additive slack; $\delta$ can hide catastrophe, keep $\delta \ll 1/n$.
  • Privacy-loss RV: tail bound $\Pr[\,I \gt \varepsilon\,] \le \delta$ implies $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP (good/bad split).
  • Truncated Laplace: bounded noise; the cut-tail edge carries the $\delta$.
  • Gaussian mechanism: $\sigma = \Delta_2 \sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}/\varepsilon$; loss is Gaussian, complete the square.
  • Properties: post-processing free; composition adds; group and subsampling scale $\delta$.
