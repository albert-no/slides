Where to Inject Noise?

Output, objective, and gradient perturbation for private ERM.

The Setting — Private ERM

Recall (private empirical risk minimization).

Empirical risk $L(w; X) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \ell(w; z_i)$; goal: release $\hat w \approx \arg\min_w L(w; X)$ under $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP. One record: $L$ moves at most $\Delta_\ell / n$.

Last lecture built one answer, DP-SGD. Today: the full map of alternatives.

Every private training method is a choice of where the noise enters.

Strategy ① — Output Perturbation

Definition (output / parameter perturbation).

Solve the clean problem exactly, then noise the solution:

$$w^*(X) = \arg\min_w L(w; X), \qquad \tilde W = w^*(X) + Z$$

with $Z$ calibrated to the sensitivity of the argmin map:

$$\Delta_{\arg\min} = \max_{X \simeq X'} \lVert w^*(X) - w^*(X') \rVert_1, \qquad Z \sim \mathrm{Lap}(\Delta_{\arg\min}/\varepsilon)^{\otimes d}$$

Privacy is then immediate: the Laplace mechanism applied to the function $X \mapsto w^*(X)$.

The Hard Part — Sensitivity of the Argmin

The noise scale needs $\Delta_{\arg\min}$ bounded. Flat objectives break this.

flat valley: minimizer roams strong curvature: minimizer pinned $w^*(X)$ $w^*(X')$ far away $w^*(X)$ $w^*(X')$ nearby

One record tilts $L$ by $O(1/n)$; only curvature keeps the minimizer close.

Micro-Example — Mean vs Median

Scalar records $z_i \in [0,1]$; replace one of $n$. Two losses, two argmin maps:

$$\begin{aligned} \ell(w;z) &= (w-z)^2: & w^*(X) &= \bar z, & |w^*(X) - w^*(X')| &\le \tfrac{1}{n} \\[4pt] \ell(w;z) &= |w-z|: & w^*(X) &= \mathrm{med}(X), & \Delta_{\arg\min} &= 1 \end{aligned}$$

Median jump: $m$ zeros, $m$ ones, one swing record deciding $0$ or $1$.

Squared loss is strongly convex: sensitivity $1/n$. The flat $|\cdot|$ valley: sensitivity $1$, independent of $n$.

Output Perturbation — When It Works

Mechanism (private mean).

Release $\bar z + \mathrm{Lap}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n\varepsilon}\right)$: this is $\varepsilon$-DP, since $\Delta_1(\bar z) = 1/n$ (Laplace mechanism).

  • Noise $\approx 1/(n\varepsilon)$: far below the $1/\sqrt{n}$ statistical error; privacy nearly free.
  • Scope: strongly convex, regularized objectives (linear and logistic regression).
  • Deep nets: non-convex loss, no useful bound on $\Delta_{\arg\min}$; strategy unusable.

Strategy ② — Objective Perturbation

Definition (objective perturbation).

Draw one random vector $b \in \mathbb{R}^d$, tilt the loss, and optimize the tilted loss exactly:

$$\hat w = \arg\min_w \; \Big[ L(w; X) + \langle b, w \rangle \Big]$$

The noise lives inside the objective; the output $\hat w$ is random only through $b$.

No noise is added after solving: the randomized problem itself produces a private optimum.

Chaudhuri, Monteleoni, Sarwate, "Differentially Private Empirical Risk Minimization", JMLR 2011.

Objective Perturbation — Where Privacy Comes From

  1. Couple output to noise. First-order condition $\nabla L(\hat w; X) + b = 0$: for strictly convex $L$, $b \leftrightarrow \hat w$ bijective.
  2. Compare neighbors. The same $\hat w$ under $X'$ needs $b' = -\nabla L(\hat w; X')$, and $\lVert b - b'\rVert = \tfrac{1}{n}\lVert \nabla\ell(\hat w; z_i) - \nabla\ell(\hat w; z_i')\rVert \le \tfrac{2G}{n}$.
  3. Pay the density ratio. Laplace-type density on $b$: a $2G/n$ shift costs $e^{\varepsilon}$.

The full proof also pays a change-of-variables Jacobian factor: strong convexity again.

Strategy ③ — Gradient Perturbation

Recall (DP-SGD).

Per step: clip gradients at $G$, average, add Gaussian noise. $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP over $T$ steps for

$$\sigma \;\ge\; \frac{2G\sqrt{2T\log(1/\delta)}}{n\varepsilon}$$

Noise enters every step: the model is private by construction of the whole path.

Abadi, Chu, Goodfellow, McMahan, Mironov, Talwar, Zhang, "Deep Learning with Differential Privacy", CCS 2016.

  • Sensitivity object: one step's gradient, and clipping enforces the bound.

The only sensitivity you ever need is per-step, and you get to choose it: the clip norm $G$.

What All Three Pay — Excess Risk

For convex losses, private ERM degrades accuracy by roughly

$$\mathbb{E}\, L(\hat w; X) \;-\; \min_w L(w; X) \;=\; O\!\left( \frac{d \cdot \ln(1/\delta)}{n \varepsilon} \right)$$
  • Dimension hurts: the tax grows linearly in $d$.
  • Data heals: the tax shrinks as $1/n$; useful once $n \gg d/\varepsilon$.
Privacy in convex ERM is a tax of order $d / (n\varepsilon)$.

Input DP — Randomize Before Anyone Looks

Definition (input perturbation, local DP).

Each user randomizes their own record; the server sees only $R(z_1), \dots, R(z_n)$.

For every pair of values $z, z'$ and every event $S$:

$$\Pr[R(z) \in S] \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon} \Pr[R(z') \in S]$$

Recall (randomized response).

Flip the true bit with probability $\tfrac{1}{2}-\gamma$: this is $\varepsilon_\gamma$-DP with $\varepsilon_\gamma = \ln\frac{1/2+\gamma}{1/2-\gamma}$.

Strongest trust model (server sees no real data), but utility collapses in high dimension.

Inference DP — Randomize Each Answer

Definition (inference DP, per-query output perturbation).

Train $\theta = A(X)$ non-privately. For each query $x$, release a noisy prediction:

$$M_x(X) = f_{A(X)}(x) + Z, \qquad Z \sim \mathrm{Lap}(\Delta_x / \varepsilon_0)$$ $$\Delta_x = \max_{X \simeq X'} \big| f_{A(X)}(x) - f_{A(X')}(x) \big|$$

Each single answer is $\varepsilon_0$-DP with respect to the training set $X$.

Neighboring pair: training datasets. The guarantee covers one answer, not the API.

The Critical Flaw — Budget Exhaustion

Composition charges every answer: $k$ queries at $\varepsilon_0 = 0.1$ each cost $k \varepsilon_0$ in total.

queries answeredtotal privacy cost
$k = 1$$\varepsilon = 0.1$
$k = 10$$\varepsilon = 1$
$k = 100$$\varepsilon = 10$  (budget spent)
$k = 1000$$\varepsilon = 100$  (meaningless)

Advanced composition only softens this to $\sqrt{k}$ growth; the total still diverges.

At budget $\varepsilon_{\max} = 10$ the API must shut down after 100 answers: unusable at scale.

Model DP — Randomize the Training Map

Definition (model DP, global DP).

The training procedure itself is the mechanism: $M: X \mapsto \theta$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP.

Neighboring pair: datasets $X \simeq X'$. Guarantee: the distribution of released parameters.

  • Examples: DP-SGD; the PATE student (Section 03).
  • Barrier at training: a private $\theta$ can be copied, shipped, open-sourced.

Model DP — Predictions Are Free

Recall (post-processing immunity).

If $M$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP and $f$ (even randomized) never touches $X$, then $f \circ M$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP.

Proposition (unlimited queries).

For $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP training $M$ and any adaptive, randomized query strategy $g$: $g(\theta)$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP.

Proof. $g$ reads only $\theta = M(X)$, never $X$: it is post-processing. $\blacksquare$

Infinite queries, zero budget decay: this is why model DP is the deployment target.

PATE

Private Aggregation of Teacher Ensembles: model DP without touching the optimizer.

Why Another Route to Model DP?

PATE (Private Aggregation of Teacher Ensembles) reaches model DP without gradient surgery.

DP-SGD pain points

Rewrites the optimizer; clipping biases gradients; tuning under a budget; differentiable models only.

PATE's move

Train normally, many times; privatize only the labels that cross the boundary, via noisy voting.

Papernot, Abadi, Erlingsson, Goodfellow, Talwar, "Semi-supervised Knowledge Transfer for Deep Learning from Private Training Data", ICLR 2017.

Phase 1 — Teachers on Disjoint Shards

Definition (teacher ensemble).

Partition $X = X_1 \sqcup X_2 \sqcup \cdots \sqcup X_n$ into disjoint shards; train teacher $T_i$ on $X_i$ only.

Disjointness buys the key fact: $X \simeq X'$ changes exactly one teacher.

Phase 1 Payoff — Vote Sensitivity One

For input $x$, tally teacher predictions into a vote histogram over classes $y \in \mathcal{Y}$:

$$v_y(x) \;=\; \big| \{ i : T_i(x) = y \} \big|$$

Change one record: one teacher retrains, its vote moves from some $y$ to some $y'$:

$v(X)$: cat 7, dog 2, fox 1
one record→
$v(X')$: cat 6, dog 3, fox 1
At most two coordinates move, each by $1$: $\;\lVert v(X) - v(X') \rVert_\infty \le 1$.

Phase 2 — Noisy Vote Aggregation

A clean argmax leaks: one vote can break a tie. So randomize the tally.

Definition (noisy argmax aggregation).

For each query $x$, perturb every count and release only the winning label:

$$\tilde v_y = v_y(x) + \mathrm{Lap}(1/\varepsilon_0), \qquad \hat y(x) = \arg\max_{y} \tilde v_y$$

PATE-G variant: Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ on the counts instead of Laplace.

Per-Query Privacy of the Aggregator

Recall (Noisy Max).

Add $\mathrm{Exp}(2\Delta/\varepsilon)$ noise to scores of sensitivity $\Delta$; releasing only the argmax is $\varepsilon$-DP.

Proposition (per-query cost).

With Laplace scale $1/\varepsilon_0$ on counts of sensitivity $1$, each released label $\hat y(x)$ is $2\varepsilon_0$-DP.

Same mechanism shape as Noisy Max; only the noise family changes.

Proof Sketch — The Noisy Max Argument Again

  1. Condition. Fix all noises but $Z_y$; the event $\hat y = y$ becomes a threshold $Z_y \ge t$.
  2. Shift. Each count moves at most $1$: the threshold shifts at most $2$.
  3. Pay. Laplace tail, scale $b = 1/\varepsilon_0$:
    $\Pr[Z_y \ge t - 2] \le e^{2/b} \Pr[Z_y \ge t] = e^{2\varepsilon_0} \Pr[Z_y \ge t]$.

Identical skeleton to the Noisy Max proof; the Laplace tail replaces the exponential.

Two Kinds of Queries — Consensus vs Contested

margin $\gg$ noise scale strong consensus contested: noise decides the winner

Left: no single vote can flip the outcome. Right: one teacher (one record) might.

Phase 3 — The Student

Problem: the ensemble is an inference-DP oracle

Serving users from the ensemble spends $2\varepsilon_0$ per answer: the budget-exhaustion trap again.

Solution: distill once, deploy the copy

Label $m$ public inputs with the noisy aggregator; train student $S$ on them; deploy $S$.

Standing assumption: an in-domain public unlabeled pool. Only the $m$ label queries touch $X$.

PATE as a Mechanism — The Precise Statement

The PATE mechanism.

The randomized map from data to released labels,

$$M_{\mathrm{PATE}}(X) \;=\; \big( \hat y(x_1^{\mathrm{pub}}), \, \dots, \, \hat y(x_m^{\mathrm{pub}}) \big)$$

Neighboring pair: $X \simeq X'$ differing in one record. Student training reads only $M_{\mathrm{PATE}}(X)$ and public data.

Corollary (student is model DP).

If $M_{\mathrm{PATE}}$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP, then the student $S$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP: post-processing.

Composing the $m$ Queries

Basic composition: $\varepsilon' = m \varepsilon_q$, linear and brutal. Advanced composition:

$$\varepsilon' \;=\; \sqrt{2m \log(1/\delta')} \cdot \varepsilon_q \;+\; m \varepsilon_q \big( e^{\varepsilon_q} - 1 \big)$$
  • First term: $\sqrt{m}$ fluctuation of the summed privacy loss.
  • Second term: linear drift; small only when $\varepsilon_q$ itself is small.

Same advanced-composition theorem as for DP-SGD steps; here the unit is one label query.

05

Trade-offs

What PATE buys, what it costs, and when to prefer DP-SGD.

PATE — Advantages

  • Intuitive guarantee: consensus of independent models; easy to explain, easy to audit.
  • Architecture-agnostic: teachers need not be differentiable; SVM teachers, neural student.
  • Parallel training: $n$ independent jobs, no optimizer surgery, standard tooling.
  • Model DP at the end: student inherits by post-processing; unlimited serving.

PATE — Limitations

  • Needs public data: an in-domain, unlabeled public pool; often the binding constraint.
  • Needs massive private data: every shard $X_i$ must still train an accurate teacher.
  • Degrades with many classes: noise on every count; large $|\mathcal{Y}|$ drowns the margin.

Failure mode

Small shards $\Rightarrow$ weak teachers $\Rightarrow$ contested votes $\Rightarrow$ worst-case accounting: the consensus discount evaporates.

Recap

  • Three injections: output ($w^* + Z$, strong convexity), objective ($L + \langle b, w\rangle$), gradient (clip, noise; per-step sensitivity).
  • Excess risk: all convex ERM routes pay $O(d \ln(1/\delta) / (n\varepsilon))$.
  • Taxonomy: input DP (per upload), inference DP (budget exhausts), model DP (queries free).
  • PATE: disjoint shards, vote sensitivity $1$; noisy argmax, $2\varepsilon_0$ each; student, model DP.
  • Accounting: compose $m$ label queries; data-dependent RDP makes consensus queries nearly free.
