Classifier Guidance

Bayes decomposition and the $\omega$-scaled reverse SDE

Why Conditional Generation

Applications want a sample from $P_{\mathbf X\,|\,Y}$, not from $P_{\mathbf X}$.

Unconditional generation is the warm-up; control is the product.

Two Approaches

One model per condition.

  • Train $s_\theta(\cdot\,|\,y)$ separately for each $y$
  • Needs paired data for every $y$
  • Does not scale past a few labels

Guidance.

  • Train the unconditional score once
  • Steer it at sampling time
  • One generator, any condition

Guidance moves conditioning out of training and into the sampler.

Dhariwal & Nichol, "Diffusion Models Beat GANs on Image Synthesis", NeurIPS 2021.

Recall — The Reverse SDE

Time reversal.

A forward diffusion $d\mathbf X_t = f(\mathbf X_t,t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf Z_t$ runs backwards as

$$d\bar{\mathbf X}_t = \Big(f - g^2\,\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\bar{\mathbf X}_t)\Big)dt + g\,d\bar{\mathbf Z}_t.$$

Everything is known except the score $\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t$.

Sampling means integrating this from noise down to $t=0$.

Recall — Score and Noise

Two units, one object.

With the forward kernel $\mathcal N\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\,\mathbf x^{(0)},\,(1-\bar\alpha_n)I_d\big)$,

$$\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_n(\mathbf x) \;=\; -\,\frac{\varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf x, n)}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_n}}.$$

The score points uphill; the noise prediction points downhill.

Every statement below can be read in either currency.

What Conditioning Requires

The reverse SDE of the conditioned process needs

$$\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) \qquad \text{at every noise level } t.$$
  • Have: the unconditional score, trained once.
  • Want: the conditional score, for arbitrary $y$.

Bayes' rule is the bridge between them.

The Conditional Score

Proposition (Bayes decomposition).

Fix $y$ with $p_t(y) > 0$. For every $t$ and every $\mathbf x$,

$$\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) = \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x) + \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x).$$

Here $p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x)$ is the label posterior at noise level $t$.

An identity, not an approximation.

Proof Outline

  1. Bayes' rule, applied at noise level $t$.
  2. Take logarithms; the quotient becomes a sum.
  3. Differentiate in $\mathbf x$; the label marginal is a constant.

Key trick.

Differentiate in $\mathbf x$, never in $y$. The intractable normalizer $p_t(y)$ carries no $\mathbf x$ and dies.

Three lines, all exact.

Step 1 — Bayes at Level $t$

Both densities are marginals of the noisy state, not of the data.

$$p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) \;=\; \frac{p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x)\;p_t(\mathbf x)}{p_t(y)}.$$

Noising touches $\mathbf X$ only, so $p_t(y) = p_0(y)$ for every $t$.

The label marginal is constant in $t$, and constant in $\mathbf x$.

Step 2 — Take Logarithms

The quotient splits into three terms.

$$\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) \;=\; \underbrace{\log p_t(\mathbf x)}_{\text{generator}} \;+\; \underbrace{\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x)}_{\text{classifier}} \;-\; \underbrace{\log p_t(y)}_{\text{constant in }\mathbf x}.$$

Two of the three depend on $\mathbf x$; the third does not.

The intractable term is isolated before it is discarded.

Step 3 — Differentiate

Apply $\nabla_{\mathbf x}$ term by term. The last one vanishes:

$$\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(y) = 0.$$
$$\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) = \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x) + \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x). \qquad\blacksquare$$

The unknown normalizer is never computed, only differentiated away.

Proof Recap

$$\begin{aligned} \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) &\;\stackrel{\text{Bayes}}{=}\; \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log\frac{p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x)\,p_t(\mathbf x)}{p_t(y)}\\[2pt] &\;\stackrel{\text{log splits}}{=}\; \nabla_{\mathbf x}\big[\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x) + \log p_t(\mathbf x) - \log p_t(y)\big]\\[2pt] &\;\stackrel{\text{constant}}{=}\; \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x) + \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x). \end{aligned}$$

Conditional score equals unconditional score plus classifier gradient.

Reading the Decomposition

$\nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x)$ $\omega\nabla\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x)$ guided

Prior pulls onto the data; classifier pulls toward the label.

The Classifier Must See Noise

The sampler evaluates the classifier at $\mathbf X_t$, never at clean data.

$t = 0$ $t$ mid $t = T$

An off-the-shelf classifier is trained on the left panel only.

Training the Noisy Classifier

Noise-conditioned classifier
  1. Draw a labelled pair $(\mathbf X^{(0)}, Y)$ and a level $n \sim \mathrm{Unif}\{1,\dots,N\}$.
  2. Corrupt: $\mathbf X^{(n)} = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\,\mathbf X^{(0)} + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_n}\,\bar\varepsilon$.
  3. Cross-entropy step on $c_\phi(\mathbf X^{(n)}, n)$ against $Y$.
  4. Leave the generator $\varepsilon_\theta$ frozen throughout.

The level $n$ enters as an ordinary input, exactly as in the denoiser.

A small network, trained on data the forward process already makes.

The Guided Reverse SDE

Definition (classifier guidance).

$$d\bar{\mathbf X}_t = \Big(f - g^2\big[\nabla\log p_t(\bar{\mathbf X}_t) + \omega\,\nabla\log c_\phi(\bar{\mathbf X}_t, y, t)\big]\Big)dt + g\,d\bar{\mathbf Z}_t.$$
  • $\omega = 1$: the exact conditional reverse SDE.
  • $\omega > 1$: the label is deliberately over-weighted.
Dhariwal & Nichol, "Diffusion Models Beat GANs on Image Synthesis", NeurIPS 2021.

Guidance in $\varepsilon$-Form

Discrete samplers speak $\varepsilon$, so translate once and for all.

$$\widehat\varepsilon(\mathbf x, y, n) = \varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf x, n) - \omega\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_n}\;\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log c_\phi(\mathbf x, y, n).$$

Run DDPM or DDIM unchanged, with $\widehat\varepsilon$ in place of $\varepsilon_\theta$.

The sign flip is the score-to-noise conversion, nothing more.

Classifier-Guided Sampler

Sampling with a classifier
  1. Draw $\mathbf X^{(N)} \sim \mathcal N(0, I_d)$ and fix the target $y$.
  2. For $n = N, \dots, 1$:
    1. evaluate $\varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf X^{(n)}, n)$;
    2. backpropagate $\log c_\phi(\mathbf X^{(n)}, y, n)$ to its input;
    3. form $\widehat\varepsilon$ and take one reverse step.
  3. Return $\mathbf X^{(0)}$.

One extra backward pass per step, through a small network.

What $\omega$ Actually Samples

Proposition (tilted target).

The guided drift is the exact reverse drift of the tilted family

$$\tilde p_t(\mathbf x) \;\propto\; p_t(\mathbf x)\,p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x)^{\omega} \;=\; p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y)^{\omega}\,p_t(\mathbf x)^{1-\omega}.$$

A geometric interpolation, extrapolated past $\omega = 1$.

Guidance does not sample $p_t(\cdot\,|\,y)$ unless $\omega = 1$.

Proof, and a Caveat

$$\begin{aligned} \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log\tilde p_t(\mathbf x) &= \nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x) + \omega\,\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x)\\[2pt] &= \omega\,\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) + (1-\omega)\,\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log p_t(\mathbf x). \end{aligned}$$

Caveat.

For $\omega \neq 1$, $\{\tilde p_t\}$ is no forward diffusion's marginal flow.

A heuristic with a clean fixed point.

The Tilt in a Picture

$p_t(\mathbf x)$ $p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y)$ $\omega = 3$

Sharper and more label-faithful, at the cost of diversity.

02

Inpainting

A hard constraint, and how to noise it

What Is Inpainting

Part of the image is given; the rest must be invented, coherently.

Photo restoration, object removal, edit propagation.

The Task, Formally

Fix an index set $\Omega \subset \{1,\dots,d\}$ of observed coordinates.

$$\mathbf x \;=\; \big(\Omega(\mathbf x),\ \bar\Omega(\mathbf x)\big), \qquad \mathbf y \;=\; \Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}).$$
  • $\Omega(\mathbf x)$: the observed half, given and clean.
  • $\bar\Omega(\mathbf x)$: the hidden half, to be sampled.

A mask, not a label. The conditioning is a hard equality.

Goal as Conditional Sampling

Draw the hidden half from its exact posterior:

$$\bar\Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}) \;\sim\; P_{\bar\Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)})\;|\;\Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}) = \mathbf y}.$$

The conditioning event has probability zero, so no classifier $c_\phi$ expresses it.

Guidance with the soft classifier replaced by a hard constraint.

The Forward Process Factorizes

Lemma (coordinatewise forward).

The forward kernel has diagonal covariance, so it acts on each coordinate separately:

$$\Omega(\mathbf X^{(n)}) = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\,\Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}) + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_n}\,\Omega(\bar\varepsilon_n).$$

Noising the observed pixels needs the observation and nothing else.

Proof — Marginalize

The one-shot identity is a product over coordinates:

$$q(\mathbf x^{(n)}\,|\,\mathbf x^{(0)}) = \textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal N\big(x_i^{(n)};\,\sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\,x_i^{(0)},\,1-\bar\alpha_n\big).$$

Integrating out $i \notin \Omega$ leaves the same product over $i \in \Omega$:

$$\Omega(\mathbf X^{(n)})\;\big|\;\Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}) = \mathbf y \;\sim\; \mathcal N\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\,\mathbf y,\,(1-\bar\alpha_n)I\big). \qquad\blacksquare$$

The Ideal Conditional Score

The reverse process for the conditioned law would need

$$\nabla\log p_n\big(\mathbf x^{(n)}\;\big|\;\Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}) = \mathbf y\big).$$
  • The conditioning is on the clean observation.
  • The trained network knows the unconditional score only.
  • It has no input slot for a clean constraint.

So approximate, and know exactly where the approximation enters.

Why Pasting Clean Pixels Fails

Uniform noise everywhere is what the network was trained on.

Key Idea — Noise the Observation

Key trick.

Do not paste $\mathbf y$. Paste $\mathbf y$ corrupted to the current level.

$$\zeta_n = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\,\mathbf y + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_n}\,\eta_n, \qquad \eta_n \sim \mathcal N(0, I).$$

Both halves of the state then carry the same amount of noise.

The constraint is enforced in the noisy coordinates, level by level.

The Paste Is Exact

Proposition.

$$\zeta_n \;\stackrel{d}{=}\; \Omega(\mathbf X^{(n)})\;\big|\;\Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}) = \mathbf y.$$

Proof. Both sides are $\mathcal N\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\,\mathbf y, (1-\bar\alpha_n)I\big)$, by the Lemma. $\;\blacksquare$

The observed half of the trajectory is simulated exactly, at every level.

Where the Approximation Lives

Condition on the noised observation, then average it out:

$$\begin{aligned} p_n\big(\mathbf x\,\big|\,\Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}) = \mathbf y\big) = \int &p_n\big(\mathbf x\,\big|\,\Omega(\mathbf X^{(n)}) = \zeta,\ \Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}) = \mathbf y\big)\\[2pt] &\times\; p\big(\zeta\,\big|\,\Omega(\mathbf X^{(0)}) = \mathbf y\big)\,d\zeta. \end{aligned}$$

Exact. The method replaces it by two shortcuts.

The Two Approximations

Drop the clean condition.

  • Keep only $\Omega(\mathbf X^{(n)}) = \zeta_n$
  • Extra clean information discarded
  • Puts the state back in domain

One draw, not the mixture.

  • Replace the integral by a single $\zeta_n$
  • Fresh $\eta_n$ at each step
  • Ignores correlation across levels

Both are named, both are visible, neither is fatal.

Boundary Check

  • $n = 0$: $\bar\alpha_0 = 1$, so $\zeta_0 = \mathbf y$. The constraint holds exactly.
  • $n = N$: the state is pure noise, and there is nothing to violate.
  • Intermediate $n$: the two halves may disagree about the same scene.

Error is largest in the middle of the trajectory.

Repeated back-and-forth resampling buys agreement at extra cost.

The Inpainting Sampler

Sampling under a mask
  1. Draw $\mathbf X^{(N)} \sim \mathcal N(0, I_d)$.
  2. For $n = N, \dots, 1$:
    1. one unconditional reverse step to get $\mathbf X^{(n-1)}$;
    2. draw $\eta$ and form $\zeta_{n-1} = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{n-1}}\,\mathbf y + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{n-1}}\,\eta$;
    3. overwrite the observed coordinates: $\Omega(\mathbf X^{(n-1)}) \leftarrow \zeta_{n-1}$.
  3. Return $\mathbf X^{(0)}$; its observed half equals $\mathbf y$.

No retraining. The unconditional network is reused verbatim.

The Sampler in a Picture

$\zeta_N$ $\zeta_{n_1}$ $\zeta_{n_2}$ $\zeta_0 = \mathbf y$

Blue band: observed coordinates, overwritten at every level.

The noise level of the band always matches the rest of the state.

Variants of the Same Idea

  • RePaint: alternate forward and reverse steps to reconcile the halves.
  • ILVR: a low-pass constraint instead of a pixel mask.
  • DPS: a general measurement, enforced by a likelihood gradient.

All replace the exact conditional score with a tractable surrogate.

Lugmayr et al., "RePaint", CVPR 2022.  ·  Chung et al., "Diffusion Posterior Sampling…", ICLR 2023.
03

Classifier-Free
Guidance

The classifier gradient, extracted from the generator

Why Drop the Classifier

  • A second model, trained on noisy data, at every level.
  • Classifier gradients are fragile and easy to fool.
  • Labels are finite; prompts are not.
  • Guidance is only as good as $c_\phi$.

The generator already knows the label. Ask it twice.

Ho & Salimans, "Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance", NeurIPS Workshop 2021.

The Idea

Read Bayes backwards.

The classifier gradient is a difference of two scores:

$$\nabla\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x) = \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) - \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x).$$

Train one network to answer with and without the condition.

The CFG Identity

Theorem (classifier-free guidance).

For every $\omega \in \mathbb R$, the classifier-guided score satisfies

$$\begin{aligned} s_t^{\omega}(\mathbf x, y) &:= \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x) + \omega\,\nabla\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x)\\[2pt] &\;= \omega\,\nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) + (1-\omega)\,\nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x). \end{aligned}$$

No classifier on the right-hand side.

Proof Outline

  1. Bayes at level $t$, solved for the classifier term.
  2. Substitute into the guided score.
  3. Collect the two copies of the unconditional score.

Key trick.

Nothing is approximated. The classifier is eliminated, not estimated.

Pure algebra on the identity already proved.

Step 1 — Invert Bayes

Start from $p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x) = p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y)\,p_t(y)/p_t(\mathbf x)$ and take $\nabla_{\mathbf x}\log$:

$$\nabla\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x) = \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) - \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x).$$

The same three-term split as before, read in the other direction.

$p_t(y)$ is constant in $\mathbf x$ and drops again.

Step 2 — Substitute

Insert this into the guided score:

$$s_t^{\omega}(\mathbf x, y) = \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x) + \omega\big[\nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) - \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x)\big].$$

Two copies of the unconditional score appear, one of them scaled.

Everything is now expressed in generator quantities.

Step 3 — Collect

$$s_t^{\omega}(\mathbf x, y) = \omega\,\nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) + (1-\omega)\,\nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x). \qquad\blacksquare$$
  • $\omega = 0$: purely unconditional.
  • $\omega = 1$: exactly the conditional score.
  • $\omega > 1$: extrapolation beyond the conditional.

An affine combination with coefficients summing to one.

Proof Recap

$$\begin{aligned} s_t^{\omega} &\;\stackrel{\text{definition}}{=}\; \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x) + \omega\,\nabla\log p_t(y\,|\,\mathbf x)\\[2pt] &\;\stackrel{\text{Bayes}}{=}\; \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x) + \omega\big[\nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) - \nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x)\big]\\[2pt] &\;\stackrel{\text{collect}}{=}\; \omega\,\nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y) + (1-\omega)\,\nabla\log p_t(\mathbf x). \end{aligned}$$

Guidance without a classifier, at the price of a second forward pass.

Guidance as Extrapolation

$\omega = 0$ $\omega = 1$ $\omega = 3$

Guidance is an extrapolation, not an interpolation.

A Dual-Role Network

One network, one extra input slot, and a null token $\varnothing$.

$\varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf x, y, n)$ $\varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf x, \varnothing, n)$

Two answers from one set of weights.

Training with Condition Dropout

Classifier-free training
  1. Draw $(\mathbf X^{(0)}, Y)$, a level $n$, and noise $\bar\varepsilon \sim \mathcal N(0, I_d)$.
  2. With probability $p_{\mathrm{drop}}$, replace $Y$ by the null token $\varnothing$.
  3. Gradient step on $\big\|\bar\varepsilon - \varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf X^{(n)}, Y, n)\big\|^2$.

The same loss as before, with one line added.

$p_{\mathrm{drop}}$ near $0.1$ leaves both roles well trained.

From Score to $\varepsilon$

Both scores carry the same factor $-1/\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_n}$, so the identity transfers verbatim:

$$\tilde\varepsilon(\mathbf x, y, n) = \omega\,\varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf x, y, n) + (1-\omega)\,\varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf x, \varnothing, n).$$

An affine combination of two network outputs.

Nothing else in the sampler changes.

The Sampling Rule

Regrouped into the form used in code:

$$\tilde\varepsilon = \varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf x, \varnothing, n) + \omega\Big(\varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf x, y, n) - \varepsilon_\theta(\mathbf x, \varnothing, n)\Big).$$

Start unconditional, then walk $\omega$ steps along the condition direction.

Convention warning: some papers write $1 + w$ where this writes $\omega$.

Classifier-Free Sampler

Sampling with CFG
  1. Draw $\mathbf X^{(N)} \sim \mathcal N(0, I_d)$ and fix the condition $y$.
  2. For $n = N, \dots, 1$:
    1. two forward passes: $\varepsilon_\theta(\cdot, y, n)$ and $\varepsilon_\theta(\cdot, \varnothing, n)$;
    2. combine into $\tilde\varepsilon$ with weight $\omega$;
    3. take one DDPM or DDIM step.
  3. Return $\mathbf X^{(0)}$.

Two network calls per step, no auxiliary model, no backward pass.

What CFG Samples

Corollary.

CFG with weight $\omega$ targets the same tilted family as classifier guidance:

$$\tilde p_t(\mathbf x) \;\propto\; p_t(\mathbf x\,|\,y)^{\omega}\,p_t(\mathbf x)^{1-\omega}.$$

Same heuristic, same caveat at $\omega \neq 1$, no auxiliary model.

The classifier was never needed, only its gradient.

CFG in Practice

What it buys.

  • Sharper, more faithful samples
  • One training run, one model
  • Works for text, class, layout

What it costs.

  • Two passes per sampling step
  • Diversity falls as $\omega$ grows
  • Saturation artifacts at large $\omega$

The default in modern text-to-image systems, with $\omega$ tuned by eye.

04

Discrete Diffusion

Rate matrices, ratio scores, score entropy

What Breaks on Tokens

Every step so far assumed $\mathbf x + \varepsilon$ makes sense.

No addition, no gradient, no $\nabla\log p$.

Keep the Recipe, Change the Noise

  • Forward: resample tokens at random toward a simple law.
  • Reverse: undo those jumps, one at a time.
  • Score: replaced by ratios of probabilities.

Same skeleton: a known forward, a learned reverse.

Only the noise mechanism is negotiable.

Forward — Transition Matrices

Definition (discrete forward).

On the state space $\mathcal X = \{1,\dots,K\}$, a Markov chain with

$$\Pr\big[X^{(n)} = x \,\big|\, X^{(n-1)} = x'\big] = Q_n(x, x'), \qquad \sum_{x} Q_n(x,x') = 1.$$

Columns are probability vectors, so the marginals evolve as $p_n = Q_n\,p_{n-1}$.

The column convention keeps every formula below matrix-shaped.

Example — Uniform Kernel

Keep the token, or resample it uniformly with probability $\epsilon$:

$$Q = (1-\epsilon)\,I + \frac{\epsilon}{K}\,\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}.$$
$$K = 3,\ \epsilon = 0.3: \qquad Q = \begin{pmatrix} 0.8 & 0.1 & 0.1\\ 0.1 & 0.8 & 0.1\\ 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.8 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Columns sum to one; iterating drives every state to the uniform law.

Uniform vs. Absorbing

Uniform.

  • Any token to any token
  • Stationary law: uniform
  • Hides which positions changed

Absorbing.

  • Any token to a mask, never back
  • Stationary law: all masked
  • Marks exactly what is unknown

Absorbing usually wins for text: the mask is free information for the reverse.

Two Transition Graphs

Left: every edge two-way. Right: every edge one-way into the mask.

Absorbing Kernel, in Closed Form

Mask each token at rate $\sigma(t)$ and write $\Lambda_t = \int_0^t \sigma(s)\,ds$:

$$p_{t|0}(x\,|\,x_0) = e^{-\Lambda_t}\,\delta_{x, x_0} \;+\; \big(1 - e^{-\Lambda_t}\big)\,\delta_{x, \mathsf M}.$$

Each token survives an exponential time; once masked, it stays masked.

$\Lambda_T$ large: everything is masked, so the prior is a point mass.

The Continuous-Time Limit

Let the steps shrink and expand the one-step kernel to first order:

$$Q^{(\Delta t)}_t = I + \Delta t\,Q_t + o(\Delta t).$$

Then $p_{t+\Delta t} = (I + \Delta t\,Q_t)\,p_t + o(\Delta t)$, so in the limit

$$\frac{d}{dt}\,p_t = Q_t\,p_t.$$

The discrete counterpart of the Fokker–Planck equation.

Rate Matrices

Definition (rate matrix).

$$\Pr\big[X_{t+\Delta t} = y\,\big|\,X_t = x\big] = \delta_{xy} + Q_t(y,x)\,\Delta t + o(\Delta t).$$

Off-diagonals $Q_t(y,x) \ge 0$ are jump rates.

The diagonal absorbs them: $Q_t(x,x) = -\sum_{y \neq x} Q_t(y,x)$.

Rates, not probabilities: an off-diagonal entry may exceed one.

Reversing a Jump Process

Theorem (reverse rates).

Let the forward marginals $p_t$ be everywhere positive.

The time reversal is again a jump process, with rates

$$\bar Q_t(y, x) \;=\; \frac{p_t(y)}{p_t(x)}\;Q_t(x, y), \qquad y \neq x.$$

Note the swap: the forward rate is read at $(x,y)$, the reverse at $(y,x)$.

Campbell et al., "A Continuous Time Framework for Discrete Denoising Models", NeurIPS 2022.

Proof Outline

  1. Expand the forward transition over $[t, t+\Delta t]$ to first order.
  2. Apply Bayes to the pair $(X_t, X_{t+\Delta t})$.
  3. Divide by $\Delta t$ and let $\Delta t \to 0$.

Key trick.

Reverse one infinitesimal step, not the whole path.

Bayes on two adjacent times suffices.

The discrete analogue of Anderson's argument, and far shorter.

Step 1 — One Small Step

By definition of the rate matrix, for any pair of states,

$$\Pr\big[X_{t+\Delta t} = x \,\big|\, X_t = y\big] = \delta_{xy} + Q_t(x, y)\,\Delta t + o(\Delta t).$$

Here $y$ is the earlier state, $x$ the later one.

This is the only place the forward model enters.

Step 2 — Bayes on the Pair

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr\big[X_t = y \,\big|\, X_{t+\Delta t} = x\big] &= \frac{\Pr[X_{t+\Delta t} = x \,|\, X_t = y]\;p_t(y)}{p_{t+\Delta t}(x)}\\[2pt] &= \frac{\big(\delta_{xy} + Q_t(x,y)\Delta t\big)\,p_t(y)}{p_t(x) + O(\Delta t)} + o(\Delta t). \end{aligned}$$

For $y \neq x$ the leading term $\delta_{xy}$ vanishes, leaving order $\Delta t$.

The denominator is continuous in $t$, which is all that is used.

Step 3 — Divide and Pass to the Limit

For $y \neq x$, divide by $\Delta t$ and send $\Delta t \to 0$:

$$\bar Q_t(y,x) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\Pr\big[X_t = y\,\big|\,X_{t+\Delta t} = x\big] = \frac{p_t(y)}{p_t(x)}\,Q_t(x,y).$$

The diagonal follows from conservation of mass, $\sum_y \bar Q_t(y,x) = 0$. $\;\blacksquare$

The learned object is the ratio; the rest is the known forward.

Two Sanity Checks

  • Reversible stationary chain: detailed balance $p(y)Q(x,y) = p(x)Q(y,x)$ gives $\bar Q = Q$.
  • Absorbing forward: $Q_t(x, \mathsf M) = 0$, so $\bar Q_t$ only unmasks.

The reverse of masking is unmasking, and never re-masking.

Both checks fall straight out of the formula.

Proof Recap

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr\big[X_t = y\,\big|\,X_{t+\Delta t} = x\big] &\;\stackrel{\text{Bayes}}{=}\; \frac{\Pr[X_{t+\Delta t} = x\,|\,X_t = y]\,p_t(y)}{p_{t+\Delta t}(x)}\\[2pt] &\;\stackrel{\text{first order}}{=}\; \frac{p_t(y)}{p_t(x)}\,Q_t(x,y)\,\Delta t + o(\Delta t)\\[2pt] &\;\stackrel{\text{divide}}{\Longrightarrow}\; \bar Q_t(y,x) = \frac{p_t(y)}{p_t(x)}\,Q_t(x,y). \end{aligned}$$

One unknown quantity survives: the ratio of marginals.

The Discrete Score

Definition (concrete score).

At each state $x$, a vector of ratios indexed by the other states:

$$s_\theta(x, t)_y \;\approx\; \frac{p_t(y)}{p_t(x)}, \qquad y \neq x.$$

Then $\bar Q_t(y,x) = s_\theta(x,t)_y\;Q_t(x,y)$: learned part times known part.

Ratios, not gradients. Nothing is differentiated in state space.

Why Call It a Score

On a fine grid of spacing $h$, the ratio is the gradient in disguise:

$$\frac{p(x+h)}{p(x)} = \exp\big\{\log p(x+h) - \log p(x)\big\} \;\approx\; 1 + h\,\partial_x \log p(x).$$

Same object, expressed without derivatives.

Sampling the Reverse Chain

Reverse jump process
  1. Draw $X_T$ from the forward stationary law.
  2. At state $x$ and time $t$, form the rates $\bar Q_t(y,x) = s_\theta(x,t)_y\,Q_t(x,y)$.
  3. Hold for an exponential time of parameter $\sum_{y \neq x}\bar Q_t(y,x)$.
  4. Jump to $y$ with probability proportional to $\bar Q_t(y,x)$.
  5. Repeat until $t = 0$.

Standard jump-process simulation, run backwards in time.

Why the Squared Loss Fails

The obvious analogue of score matching would be

$$\mathbb E\sum_{y \neq x}\Big(s_\theta(x,t)_y - \frac{p_t(y)}{p_t(x)}\Big)^{2}.$$
  • Targets are positive; a square does not know that.
  • Ratios span orders of magnitude; the largest dominate.
  • Negative predictions give negative rates, which are meaningless.

Positivity has to be built into the loss, not patched afterwards.

Score Entropy

Definition (score entropy).

For a prediction $s > 0$ and a target $r > 0$,

$$\ell(s, r) \;=\; s \;-\; r\log s \;+\; r\big(\log r - 1\big).$$

This is the Bregman divergence of $u \mapsto u\log u - u$, evaluated at $(r, s)$.

A generalized KL divergence between two positive numbers.

Score Entropy Is a Divergence

Theorem.

For all $s, r > 0$:

  • $\ell(s,r) \ge 0$, with equality if and only if $s = r$;
  • $s \mapsto \ell(s,r)$ is strictly convex;
  • $\ell(s,r) \to +\infty$ as $s \downarrow 0$.

A proper loss, and a barrier that keeps predictions positive.

Proof — Differentiate in $s$

$$\partial_s \ell(s,r) = 1 - \frac{r}{s}, \qquad \partial^2_s \ell(s,r) = \frac{r}{s^{2}} > 0.$$

The unique stationary point is $s = r$, and convexity makes it the minimum:

$$\ell(r,r) = r - r\log r + r\log r - r = 0. \qquad\blacksquare$$

As $s \downarrow 0$ the term $-r\log s$ blows up: a built-in barrier.

The Barrier

$s = r$ $\ell(s,r)$

Convex, zero at the target, infinite at the boundary.

But the Target Is Unknown

The target $p_t(y)/p_t(x)$ is exactly the quantity we cannot compute.

Same obstacle as in the continuous case, and the same escape route.

Replace the unknown marginal ratio by known kernel ratios, then average.

Denoising score matching, transplanted to the alphabet.

Denoising Score Entropy

Theorem (denoising score entropy).

$$\mathcal L(\theta) = \mathbb E_{X_0,\,X_t}\;\sum_{y \neq X_t}\ell\Big(s_\theta(X_t,t)_y,\; \frac{p_{t|0}(y\,|\,X_0)}{p_{t|0}(X_t\,|\,X_0)}\Big).$$

Every minimizer satisfies $s_\theta(x,t)_y = p_t(y)/p_t(x)$.

Every ingredient on the right is a known forward kernel.

Lou, Meng & Ermon, "Discrete Diffusion Modeling by Estimating the Ratios…", ICML 2024.

Proof Outline

  1. Condition on $X_t$; the loss decouples over pairs $(X_t, y)$.
  2. Minimize pointwise in $s$; the minimizer is a posterior average.
  3. Compute the average; the kernel cancels and the marginal survives.

Key trick.

$\ell$ is linear in $r$, up to a $\theta$-free term.

So the expectation passes straight through.

Step 1 — Decouple

Write $R_y = p_{t|0}(y\,|\,X_0)/p_{t|0}(X_t\,|\,X_0)$ and split off the $\theta$-free part:

$$\mathcal L(\theta) = \mathbb E_{X_t}\sum_{y \neq X_t}\Big(s_\theta(X_t,t)_y - \mathbb E\big[R_y\,\big|\,X_t\big]\,\log s_\theta(X_t,t)_y\Big) + C.$$

Each pair $(X_t, y)$ now owns one free scalar.

The constant $C$ collects $\mathbb E[R_y(\log R_y - 1)]$, which no gradient sees.

Step 2 — Minimize Pointwise

Minimize $s - \bar r\log s$ over $s > 0$, where $\bar r = \mathbb E[R_y\,|\,X_t = x]$:

$$1 - \frac{\bar r}{s} = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s^{\star} = \bar r = \mathbb E\big[R_y \,\big|\, X_t = x\big].$$

Strict convexity makes this the unique minimizer.

The network is pushed to a conditional expectation, exactly as in denoising.

Step 3 — The Average Telescopes

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E\big[R_y \,\big|\, X_t = x\big] &= \sum_{x_0} \frac{p_0(x_0)\,p_{t|0}(x\,|\,x_0)}{p_t(x)}\cdot\frac{p_{t|0}(y\,|\,x_0)}{p_{t|0}(x\,|\,x_0)}\\[2pt] &= \frac{1}{p_t(x)}\sum_{x_0}p_0(x_0)\,p_{t|0}(y\,|\,x_0) \;=\; \frac{p_t(y)}{p_t(x)}. \qquad\blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

The kernel at $x$ cancels between posterior and target; the marginal survives.

Proof Recap

$$\begin{aligned} \arg\min_{s}\;\mathbb E\big[\ell(s, R_y)\,\big|\,X_t = x\big] &\;\stackrel{\text{linear in }r}{=}\; \arg\min_{s}\;\big\{s - \mathbb E[R_y\,|\,x]\log s\big\}\\[2pt] &\;\stackrel{\text{convexity}}{=}\; \mathbb E\big[R_y\,\big|\,X_t = x\big]\\[2pt] &\;\stackrel{\text{telescope}}{=}\; \frac{p_t(y)}{p_t(x)}. \end{aligned}$$

A tractable loss whose minimizer is the intractable ratio.

Sequence Space

Text is a sequence of $L$ tokens over an alphabet of size $K$:

$$|\mathcal X| = K^{L}.$$
  • A full rate matrix has $K^{2L}$ entries.
  • The score at one state is a vector of length $K^{L} - 1$.

Neither object can be stored, let alone learned. Structure must be imposed.

Corrupt Each Position Alone

For $\mathbf y \neq \mathbf x$, define the forward rates by

$$Q_t(\mathbf y, \mathbf x) = \begin{cases} Q^{\mathrm{tok}}_t(y_i, x_i), & \mathbf y, \mathbf x \text{ differ only at position } i,\\[2pt] 0, & \text{Hamming distance} \ge 2. \end{cases}$$

Two positions jumping in the same instant has probability $o(\Delta t)$.

Hamming-1 sparsity, obtained for free from independence.

The Reverse Inherits the Sparsity

By the reverse-rate formula, $\bar Q_t(\mathbf y, \mathbf x) = \frac{p_t(\mathbf y)}{p_t(\mathbf x)} Q_t(\mathbf x, \mathbf y)$.

The second factor vanishes at Hamming distance $\ge 2$.

Only single-token flips matter: $L(K-1)$ outputs, not $K^{L}$.

position $i$ $K-1$ alternatives

Sampling a Sequence

Tau-leaping
  1. Start from the stationary law: for absorbing noise, all positions masked.
  2. At time $t$, one network call gives all $L(K-1)$ ratios.
  3. Over a window $\tau$, let each position flip independently with probability $\tau\,\bar Q_t$.
  4. Decrease $t$ by $\tau$ and repeat until $t = 0$.

One network call per step, many positions updated at once.

Series Recap

  • A forward corruption chosen so its marginals stay closed-form.
  • A reverse process driven by the score, learned by denoising.
  • Deterministic samplers sharing those same marginals.
  • Conditioning as an additive correction to the score.
  • Discrete states: ratios in place of gradients.

One idea in many guises: known forward, learned reverse.
