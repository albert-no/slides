rows base image → poison → targets it flips

Frozen InceptionV3, retrained final layer, dog vs fish.

Training End to End Needed 50 Poisons

red squares 50 poisons, each carrying a 30%-opacity copy of the target. Blue triangle the target, pulled into the base class.

CIFAR-10, all layers trained: about 60% success (bird vs dog).

Shafahi et al., NeurIPS 2018 — Fig. 3(b) and §4 ("watermarking"; success rate over 30 targets).

What Poison Frogs Did and Did Not Show

Shows

  • Honest labels can still poison
  • Frozen features: one poison, 100%
  • End to end: 50 poisons, ~60%
  • Test accuracy barely changes

Does not show

  • Success on any chosen target
  • Attack without knowing the model
  • Survival of data filtering
  • Results on web-scale training

The attacker knew the feature extractor in every test.

Backdoors and Triggers

Can a model behave perfectly on every test, yet obey a secret signal?

A Backdoor Is a Hidden "If"

Ordinary tests only see the top path.

BadNets: Stamp a Pattern, Relabel, Train

Attacker is the hired trainer · controls all training data · wants trigger → chosen digit

left original · middle single-pixel trigger · right pattern trigger, both bottom-right.

Gu, Dolan-Gavitt & Garg, "BadNets", 2017 — Fig. 3 (MNIST, outsourced-training threat model).

Two Numbers, Two Different Test Sets

Clean accuracy

Untouched test set: share classified correctly.

The number the victim sees.

Attack success rate (ASR)

Test inputs whose true label ≠ target, with trigger added: share predicted as the target.

$$\text{ASR} = \frac{\#\{\text{triggered, true label} \neq t,\ \text{predicted } t\}}{\#\{\text{triggered, true label} \neq t\}}$$

Always ask for both, and the ASR of a clean model on the same triggered set.

Clean Error Stays Flat While the Backdoor Works

blue error on clean test images

orange triggered images not sent to the target (≈ 100% − ASR)

Both stay about 1% or below.

Poison shares here are large: 10–50%.

Gu, Dolan-Gavitt & Garg, "BadNets", 2017 — Fig. 6 (MNIST; errors on validation/test data the attacker never sees).

Every Digit Pair Can Be Backdoored

cell (i, j) one BadNet mapping digit i → j (90 models). Left clean error, ≤ 0.65%. Right triggered error, worst 0.09% (1 → 5).

Gu, Dolan-Gavitt & Garg, "BadNets", 2017 — Fig. 4 (single-pixel trigger; clean error at most 0.17 points above the clean baseline).

Web-Scale and Language Models

When the training set is the internet, who gets to write into it, and what does that buy?

Many Image Datasets Are Lists of Links

The list is fixed; the content behind each URL is not.

$60 Bought Control of 0.01% of LAION-400M's URLs

Attacker buys expired domains · controls what those URLs serve · goal not yet chosen

x cost of expired domains, USD/year

y share of dataset those domains serve

0.01% of 400M ≈ 40,000 images.

Measures access cost, not damage.

Carlini et al., "Poisoning Web-Scale Training Datasets is Practical", IEEE S&P 2024 — Fig. 1 (domain prices, 2023). The authors did not poison any public dataset.

"$60 compromises a model" skips a step.

Carlini et al., IEEE S&P 2024 — §4 (access measurement), §4.4 (no evidence of exploitation), §4.5 (local OpenCLIP ViT-B-32 on LAION-400M).

What "250 Documents" Does and Does Not Say

Shows

  • Count, not fraction, set success
  • Held from 600M to 13B
  • For one simple backdoor
  • Fine-tuning showed similar trends

Does not show

  • 250 works for any behaviour
  • Holds for frontier-size models
  • Poisons survive real data filters
  • How to get 250 docs in

The authors list larger models and complex behaviours as open.

Sleeper Agents: A Backdoor Installed on Purpose

Researchers train the backdoor in · full control of training · test whether it persists

2023 in prompt: secure code.

2024: inserts vulnerabilities.

A deliberate "model organism", not a web attack.

Hubinger et al., "Sleeper Agents: Training Deceptive LLMs that Persist Through Safety Training", arXiv 2401.05566, 2024 — Fig. 1.

Safety Training Did Not Remove It

red prompt says 2024 (trigger) · green says 2023. Left pair RL, right pair SFT.

Hubinger et al., 2024 — Fig. 2 (code-vulnerability backdoor; vulnerable rate scored on Pearce et al. prompts).

Separate How It Got In From Whether It Stayed

Shows

  • Trained-in backdoors survived SFT, RL
  • Most persistent in the largest models
  • Adversarial training can hide it

Does not show

  • A web attacker could install it
  • Deployed models contain backdoors
  • Models develop this unprompted

Installation used full training access; only persistence was the test.

05

Defenses, and What to Ask

What does a defender need, what must they assume, and when does it fail?

Defend the Data, the Training, or the Model

Each needs different access; none covers every attack.

Hashes Catch Changes, Not Poison Already There

Cost: of 2.9M CC3M images still online in 2023, only 1.1M matched 2018 hashes.

Carlini et al., IEEE S&P 2024 — §6.2 (integrity checks and their utility cost); benign edits break hashes too.

Spectral Signatures: Filter, Then Retrain

Defender holds the full training set · trains the model · knows a bound ε on poison count

  • Per class: learned features of every example
  • Score = alignment with top singular vector
  • Drop top 1.5·ε scores per class, retrain
Tran, Li & Madry, "Spectral Signatures in Backdoor Attacks", NeurIPS 2018 — Fig. 3 and Algorithm 1.

Poisons Separate in Learned Features, Not Pixels

top row raw pixels · bottom row learned features. Only bottom-right separates clean from poisoned.

Tran, Li & Madry, NeurIPS 2018 — Fig. 1 (CIFAR-10; 5,000 clean vs 500 poisoned examples).

Spectral Signatures: Needs, Assumes, Fails

NeedsAll training data, a trained model, a bound ε
AssumesPoisons stand out along one direction
ResultCIFAR-10 ResNet: backdoor 74–99.9% → ≤ 8.3%; clean ~92.5%
Fails whenPoisons don't separate; or ε set too low

Removes 1.5·ε points in every class, clean ones included.

Tran, Li & Madry, NeurIPS 2018 — Table 2 (5–10% poison in one class; some runs left up to 57 poisons).

Neural Cleanse: Find the Label With a Shortcut

Illustrative bars. A backdoored label is reachable with a tiny change.

Wang et al., "Neural Cleanse: Identifying and Mitigating Backdoor Attacks in Neural Networks", IEEE S&P 2019 — §IV (median absolute deviation outlier test).

Neural Cleanse: Needs, Assumes, Fails

NeedsTrained model, some correctly labelled clean data, compute
AssumesSmall trigger; most labels uninfected
Fails whenTrigger large: GTSRB, beyond 14×14 it blends in
Fails whenMany labels infected: over 8 on GTSRB

No training data needed, but it hunts only for small patch triggers.

Wang et al., IEEE S&P 2019 — §III threat model; §VIII (trigger-size sweep 4×4 to 16×16; infected-label sweep on GTSRB).

Established vs Open

Established

  • Few poisons can plant a backdoor
  • Clean accuracy can hide it
  • Web datasets are cheap to reach
  • Trained-in backdoors can outlast tuning

Open

  • Poison counts for frontier models
  • Complex behaviours from few poisons
  • Detection against adaptive attackers
  • Removal without losing utility

Takeaways

  • Poisoning is how; a backdoor is what
  • Report clean accuracy and ASR together
  • Access cost is not damage
  • Poison counts are scoped to setups
  • Defenses need access and assumptions

Ask: who controlled what data, how many poisons, what effect, at what cost?
