Population Loss vs Empirical Loss

We want small loss on the distribution; we can only measure loss on the sample.

$$\begin{aligned} L_p(\theta) \;&=\; \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim P}\big[\ell(f_\theta(x), y)\big] &&\text{(population: unknown)} \\[4pt] L(\theta; X) \;&=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \ell(f_\theta(x_i), y_i) &&\text{(empirical: computable)} \end{aligned}$$
$P$ unknown
$n$ i.i.d. draws→
dataset $X$
minimize $L$→
model $\hat\theta$

Today we bound the empirical gap; generalization to $L_p$ is a separate story.

ERM and Excess Risk

Definition (ERM, excess risk).

$\hat\theta_{\mathrm{ERM}} = \arg\min_{\theta\in\mathcal{C}} L(\theta; X)$; for any output $\theta$, the excess risk is $R(\theta) = L(\theta; X) - \min_{\theta'\in\mathcal{C}} L(\theta'; X)$.

$\theta^\star$ (minimizer) private output $\hat\theta$ $R(\hat\theta)$ $L(\theta; X)$

Privacy forbids returning $\theta^\star$ exactly; the question is how small $R(\hat\theta)$ can stay.

Standing Assumptions

Each assumption buys one specific step of the analysis.

Bounded domain
$\theta \in \mathcal{C}$, e.g. $\|\theta\|_2 \le R$.
Why: finite volume and diameter; projection well defined.
Bounded loss
$\ell(f_\theta(x), y) \in [0, \Delta]$.
Why: one record moves $L$ by at most $\Delta/n$.
Lipschitz loss
$\|\nabla_\theta\, \ell(f_\theta(x), y)\| \le G$.
Why: caps gradient size; loss varies predictably.
Convexity
$L(\lambda\theta_1 + (1-\lambda)\theta_2) \le \lambda L(\theta_1) + (1-\lambda)L(\theta_2)$.
Why: local progress reaches the global optimum.

Sensitivity of the Empirical Loss

Lemma (sensitivity of $L$).

If $\ell \in [0, \Delta]$, then for neighbors $X \sim X'$ and every $\theta$: $\;|L(\theta; X) - L(\theta; X')| \le \Delta/n$.

$$L(\theta; X) - L(\theta; X') \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\Big( \ell(f_\theta(x_i), y_i) - \ell(f_\theta(x_i'), y_i') \Big) \;\le\; \frac{\Delta}{n}$$

$x_2$
$x_i$
$x_{i+1}$
⋯
$x_n$

Only one term of the average changes; the $1/n$ does the rest.

Two Routes to a Private Model

Route A: privatize the output
Sample one model from a loss-tilted distribution.
Exponential mechanism; one shot.
Route B: privatize the path
Make every gradient step DP; compose.

Private ERM via the
Exponential Mechanism

Sample low-loss models; pay $O\!\big(dGR\log(\varepsilon n/d)/\varepsilon n\big)$ in excess risk.

Recall — the Exponential Mechanism

Recall (exponential mechanism).

Sample $\Pr[Y = y] \propto \exp\!\big(\varepsilon\, q(y)/2\Delta_q\big)$: this is $\varepsilon$-DP; for finite $\mathcal{Y}$, expected score gap $O\!\big(\Delta_q \log|\mathcal{Y}| / \varepsilon\big)$.

Today's score: negative empirical loss. Low loss means high score.

Here $\mathcal{Y} = \mathcal{C}$ is continuous, so $\log|\mathcal{Y}|$ is meaningless. Volumes must replace counting.

The Mechanism

Definition (exponential mechanism for ERM).

Sample $\Theta$ from the density on $\mathcal{C}$:

$$\Pr[\Theta = \theta] \;\propto\; \exp\!\Big( -\frac{\varepsilon n}{2\Delta}\, L(\theta; X) \Big)$$

Why $\varepsilon n / 2\Delta$: the score $-L$ has sensitivity $\Delta/n$, so the EM exponent is $\varepsilon(-L)/(2\Delta/n)$.

$\varepsilon$-DP is immediate from the recalled EM guarantee; no new privacy proof needed.

What the Density Does

The density tilts exponentially toward low loss; more data sharpens the tilt.

loss $L(\theta; X)$ density $\propto e^{-\varepsilon n L / 2\Delta}$ $\theta^\star$

Exponent scale $\varepsilon n/2\Delta$: larger $n$ or $\varepsilon$ concentrates the mass near $\theta^\star$.

Utility Theorem

Theorem (EM-ERM utility).

$\mathcal{C}$ the $\ell_2$-ball of radius $R$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$, loss $G$-Lipschitz, $\Delta = GR$. Then for every $X$,

$$\mathbb{E}\big[L(\hat\theta; X)\big] \;\le\; \min_{\theta\in\mathcal{C}} L(\theta; X) \;+\; O\!\Big( \frac{d\, G R \log(\varepsilon n / d)}{\varepsilon n} \Big)$$

Excess risk $\approx d/\varepsilon n$ up to a log: dimension hurts, data heals.

Proof Roadmap

  1. Good/bad split: bound $\Pr[\text{bad}]$ by comparing it to $\Pr[\text{good}]$ under the tilted density.
  2. Volume ratio: a Lipschitz ball inside the good set; geometry gives $(2R/r)^d$.
  3. Integrate the tail: tail bound becomes expectation bound; tune $t$ and $r$.

Three named tricks, one slide each; then one recap chain.

Proof — the Good Set and the Bad Set

Fix margin $r \gt 0$, gap $t \gt 0$; $\theta^\star$ minimizes $L$ over $\mathcal{C}$ (write $L(\theta)$ for $L(\theta;X)$).

$$\begin{aligned} A_1 \;&=\; \{\theta \in \mathcal{C} : L(\theta) \le L(\theta^\star) + rG\} &&\text{(good: near-optimal)} \\[4pt] A_2 \;&=\; \{\theta \in \mathcal{C} : L(\theta) \gt L(\theta^\star) + rG + t\} &&\text{(bad: worse by more than } t\text{)} \end{aligned}$$
$A_1$ $A_2$ $A_2$ level $L(\theta^\star)+rG$ level higher by $t$

Proof (continued) — Compare Bad to Good

Trick: compare bad to good. Bound the odds of $A_2$ against $A_1$; the density ratio is explicit.
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\Pr[\hat\theta \in A_2]}{\Pr[\hat\theta \in A_1]} \;&\le\; \frac{\mathrm{Vol}(A_2)\, \exp\!\big( -\frac{\varepsilon n}{2RG}(L(\theta^\star) + rG + t) \big)}{\mathrm{Vol}(A_1)\, \exp\!\big( -\frac{\varepsilon n}{2RG}(L(\theta^\star) + rG) \big)} \\[6pt] &=\; \frac{\mathrm{Vol}(A_2)}{\mathrm{Vol}(A_1)}\, \exp\!\Big( -\frac{\varepsilon n t}{2RG} \Big) \end{aligned}$$

Numerator: largest density value on $A_2$. Denominator: smallest on $A_1$. Normalizers cancel.

Proof (continued) — Volume Ratio by Geometry

Trick: a Lipschitz ball inside the good set. If $\|\theta - \theta^\star\| \le r$, then $L(\theta) \le L(\theta^\star) + rG$.

$$\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(A_2)}{\mathrm{Vol}(A_1)} \;\le\; \frac{\mathrm{Vol}(B_R)}{\mathrm{Vol}(B_{r/2})} \;=\; \Big( \frac{2R}{r} \Big)^{d}$$
radius $R$ $\mathcal{C}$ contains $A_2$ $\theta^\star$ ball radius $r/2$ inside $A_1$

The $r/2$: with $\theta^\star$ on the boundary of $\mathcal{C}$, only a half ball fits.

Proof (continued) — Exponential Beats Volume

Trick: exponential beats volume. Pick $t$ so the $e^{-\varepsilon n t/2RG}$ factor kills $(2R/r)^d$.
$$t \;=\; \frac{2RG}{\varepsilon n}\Big( d \log\frac{2R}{r} + \log\frac{1}{\beta} \Big) \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \Pr[\hat\theta \in A_2] \;\le\; \exp\!\Big( d\log\frac{2R}{r} - \frac{\varepsilon n t}{2RG} \Big) \;=\; \beta$$

Needed gap $t$: only logarithmic in the volume factor, linear in $d$, log in $2R/r$.

We used $\Pr[A_2] \le \Pr[A_2]/\Pr[A_1]$, valid because $\Pr[A_1] \le 1$.

Proof (continued) — Tail to Expectation

Trick: expectation equals the integral of the tail. For $Z = L(\hat\theta) - L(\theta^\star) \ge 0$, split at $rG$:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[Z] \;&=\; \int_0^{rG} \Pr[Z \ge s]\, ds \;+\; \int_0^{\infty} \Pr[Z \ge rG + t]\, dt \\[4pt] \;&\le\; rG \;+\; \int_0^{\infty} \Big(\frac{2R}{r}\Big)^{d} e^{-\varepsilon n t/2RG}\, dt \end{aligned}$$
$1$ area $\le rG$ $s = rG$ exponential tail $\Pr[Z \ge s]$

Proof (continued) — Cap the Tail, Choose $r$

The integrand exceeds $1$ for small $t$; integrate $\min(1, \cdot)$ honestly. With $A = (2R/r)^d$, $c = \varepsilon n/2RG$:

$$\int_0^\infty \min\!\big(1,\, A e^{-ct}\big)\, dt \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{\ln A}{c}}_{\text{where bound is } 1} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{c}}_{\text{true tail}} \;=\; \frac{2RG}{\varepsilon n}\Big( d \log\frac{2R}{r} + 1 \Big)$$

Now pick $r = 2Rd/\varepsilon n$: then $rG = 2RGd/\varepsilon n$ and $\log(2R/r) = \log(\varepsilon n/d)$.

$$\mathbb{E}\big[L(\hat\theta) - L(\theta^\star)\big] \;\le\; \frac{2RGd}{\varepsilon n} + \frac{2RG}{\varepsilon n}\Big( d\log\frac{\varepsilon n}{d} + 1 \Big) \;=\; O\!\Big( \frac{dGR \log(\varepsilon n/d)}{\varepsilon n} \Big) \qquad \blacksquare$$

Proof Recap — One Chain

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\big[L(\hat\theta) - L(\theta^\star)\big] \;&\stackrel{(\text{tail integral})}{\le}\; rG + \int_0^\infty \min\!\Big(1,\ \tfrac{\Pr[A_2]}{\Pr[A_1]}\Big)\, dt \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{ratio + volume})}{\le}\; rG + \frac{2RG}{\varepsilon n}\Big( d \log\frac{2R}{r} + 1 \Big) \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(r \,=\, 2Rd/\varepsilon n)}{=}\; O\!\Big( \frac{dGR\log(\varepsilon n/d)}{\varepsilon n} \Big). \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

Good/bad split, geometry for volumes, exponential beats volume, integrate the tail.

Discussion — Numbers, and a Catch

Excess risk in units of $GR$, at $n = 10^5$ and $\varepsilon = 1$:

dimensionbound scaleverdict
$d = 10^2$$\approx 0.7\%$comfortable
$d = 10^3$$\approx 4.6\%$noticeable
$d = 10^4$$\approx 23\%$painful

The catch.

Sampling $\propto e^{-\varepsilon n L/2\Delta}$ over continuous $\mathcal{C}$ is itself hard; practice runs gradient methods.

03

Advanced Composition

$k$ adaptive private steps cost $\sqrt{k}\,\varepsilon$, not $k\varepsilon$.

Why Composition Decides Everything

Iterative training runs one private mechanism per step; the total budget is what composes.

Recall (basic composition).

If $\mathcal{M}_i$ is $(\varepsilon_i, \delta_i)$-DP for $i = 1, \dots, k$, adaptively chosen, the composition is $\big(\sum_i \varepsilon_i, \sum_i \delta_i\big)$-DP.

Homogeneous case: $k$ steps at $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ each cost $(k\varepsilon, k\delta)$. At $T = 10^4$ steps this is untenable.

Intuition — Privacy Loss as a Random Walk

Per-step privacy loss lives in $[-\varepsilon, \varepsilon]$ with small mean; the losses fluctuate and cancel.

worst case: $k\varepsilon$ envelope $\sim \sqrt{k}\,\varepsilon$ typical accumulated loss steps $k$

Basic composition assumes every step pushes the same way; concentration says it will not.

Theorem — Advanced Composition

Theorem (advanced composition).

If each $\mathcal{M}_i$ is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP, for any $\delta' \gt 0$ the $k$-fold adaptive composition is $(\varepsilon',\, k\delta + \delta')$-DP with

$$\varepsilon' \;=\; \sqrt{2k \ln(1/\delta')}\;\varepsilon \;+\; k\varepsilon\,\frac{e^{\varepsilon}-1}{e^{\varepsilon}+1}$$

Small $\varepsilon$: second term $\approx k\varepsilon^2/2$, so $\varepsilon' \approx \sqrt{2k\ln(1/\delta')}\,\varepsilon$ dominates.

Dwork, Roth, "The Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy", 2014.

Reading the Bound

$$\varepsilon' \;=\; \underbrace{\sqrt{2k\ln(1/\delta')}\;\varepsilon}_{\text{fluctuation: } \sqrt{k}} \;+\; \underbrace{k\varepsilon\, \frac{e^{\varepsilon}-1}{e^{\varepsilon}+1}}_{\text{drift: } k \,\times\, \text{mean}}$$
Fluctuation term
Random-walk deviations: $\sqrt{k}$ scaling; $\ln(1/\delta')$ sets the confidence level.
Drift term
Each step leaks a little on average ($\approx \varepsilon^2/2$); $k$ of them add up linearly.

$\delta'$ is the probability the walk escapes its envelope; it adds to the composed $k\delta$.

Proof Sketch — Chain Rule of Privacy Loss

Adaptive mechanisms $A_i$ see the past outputs; the joint privacy loss factorizes:

$$\log \frac{\Pr[A(X) = (y_1, \dots, y_k)]}{\Pr[A(X') = (y_1, \dots, y_k)]} \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k} \log \frac{\Pr[A_i(X) = y_i \mid y^{i-1}]}{\Pr[A_i(X') = y_i \mid y^{i-1}]}$$

Each summand is one step's privacy loss, conditioned on the shared past $y^{i-1}$.

Basic composition bounds every summand by $\varepsilon$ separately and adds: total $k\varepsilon$.

Proof Sketch — Concentration Buys $\sqrt{k}$

All summands share the same conditioning $y^{i-1}$, so treat them as one random sum:

$$\text{each term} \in [-\varepsilon, \varepsilon], \qquad \mathbb{E}\big[\text{term} \mid y^{i-1}\big] \;\le\; \varepsilon\,\frac{e^{\varepsilon}-1}{e^{\varepsilon}+1} \;\approx\; \frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}$$

Bounded terms, small mean: the sum stays within $\sqrt{2k\ln(1/\delta')}\,\varepsilon$ of its drift, except probability $\delta'$.

Full proof: an Azuma-type martingale inequality on this sum; today we keep the shape.

Basic vs Advanced — Numbers

Per-step $\varepsilon = 0.1$, slack $\delta' = 10^{-5}$:

stepsbasic totaladvanced total
$k = 100$$10$$\approx 5.3$
$k = 1{,}000$$100$$\approx 20.2$
$k = 10{,}000$$1{,}000$$\approx 98$
At $k = 10^4$: a $10\times$ budget saving, and the gap keeps growing with $k$.

Budget Planning per Step

Fix total budget $\varepsilon$ and step count $T$: how much may each step spend?

$$\text{basic:}\;\; \varepsilon_0 = \frac{\varepsilon}{T} \qquad\qquad \text{advanced:}\;\; \varepsilon_0 \approx \frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2T\ln(1/\delta')}}$$

Advanced composition lets every step spend roughly $\sqrt{T}$ times more than basic allows.

Less per-step noise for the same total guarantee: this enables long training runs.

The Gradient-Descent Ladder

GD, SGD, noisy GD: training already tolerates noise.

Gradient Descent

Constrained minimization over $\mathcal{C}$; the projection $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta) = \arg\min_{\theta' \in \mathcal{C}} \|\theta - \theta'\|$ keeps iterates feasible.

  1. Initialize $\theta_0 \in \mathcal{C}$; inputs: loss $\ell$, rate $\alpha$, steps $T$.
  2. For $t = 1, \dots, T$: compute the full gradient $g_t = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \nabla_\theta\, \ell(\theta_{t-1}; x_i)$.
  3. Update $\theta_t = \theta_{t-1} - \alpha\, g_t$.
  4. Project $\theta_t \leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta_t)$.
  5. Return $\theta_T$.

What GD Does

Each step moves against the gradient: downhill on the loss surface.

$\theta_0$ $\theta^\star$ loss contours

Convex loss: the zigzag reaches the global optimum. Nonconvex: a stationary point.

Theorem — GD Convergence

Theorem (GD rate, convex case).

$\mathcal{L}$ convex and $G$-Lipschitz, $\mathcal{C}$ closed convex with diameter $R$. With $\alpha = R/(G\sqrt{T})$, GD satisfies

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta_T) \;\le\; \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star) + \frac{RG}{\sqrt{T}}$$

Accuracy $\rho$ needs $T = (RG/\rho)^2$ steps. Note: the rate is dimension-free.

Diameter $R$ means $\|\theta_1 - \theta_2\| \le R$ for all $\theta_1, \theta_2 \in \mathcal{C}$.

Stochastic Gradient Descent

The full gradient costs $n$ evaluations per step. Sample one instead.

  1. Initialize $\theta_0 \in \mathcal{C}$.
  2. For $t = 1, \dots, T$: sample $x_i \in \mathcal{D}$ uniformly.
  3. Compute the single-sample gradient $\tilde g_t = \nabla_\theta\, \ell(\theta_{t-1}; x_i)$.
  4. Update $\theta_t = \theta_{t-1} - \alpha\, \tilde g_t$; project $\theta_t \leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta_t)$.
  5. Return $\theta_T$.

Why SGD Still Works

The sampled gradient is unbiased: correct in expectation, noisy per step.

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde g_t\big] \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{n}\, \nabla_\theta\, \ell(\theta_{t-1}; x_i) \;=\; g_t$$
Cheaper per step
One gradient instead of $n$.
More steps needed
Gradient noise slows convergence; averaging recovers it.

Noisy Gradient Descent

A different perturbation: keep the full gradient, add Gaussian noise.

  1. Initialize $\theta_0 \in \mathcal{C}$.
  2. For $t = 1, \dots, T$: compute $g_t = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \nabla_\theta\, \ell(\theta_{t-1}; x_i)$.
  3. Add noise: $\tilde g_t = g_t + Z$ with $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$.
  4. Update $\theta_t = \theta_{t-1} - \alpha\, \tilde g_t$; project $\theta_t \leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta_t)$.
  5. Return $\theta_T$.

Still unbiased: $\mathbb{E}[\tilde g_t] = g_t$. Convex case converges, as with SGD.

Missing ingredient for privacy: a hard bound on one record's pull on the gradient.

05

DP-SGD

Clip the gradient, add noise, subsample, compose.

The Sensitivity Problem

Calibrated noise needs the gradient's sensitivity: how far one record can move it.

$$\sup_{x, x'} \big\| \nabla_\theta\, \ell(\theta; x) - \nabla_\theta\, \ell(\theta; x') \big\|_2 \;\le\; 2 \sup_{x} \|\nabla_\theta\, \ell(\theta; x)\|_2$$

No bound, no noise scale.

Deep networks admit no useful Lipschitz constant $G$; one outlier can dominate the update.

Fix: do not assume a bound. Enforce one.

Gradient Clipping

Definition (clipping).

$\displaystyle \bar g \;=\; g \cdot \min\!\Big(1, \frac{G}{\|g\|_2}\Big)$: direction preserved, norm capped at $G$.

kept: norm $\le G$ raw gradient clipped to the sphere radius $G$

Cost: bias whenever $\|g\|_2 \gt G$. Privacy first, unbiasedness second.

The DP-SGD Algorithm

Inputs: dataset $\mathcal{D}$ ($n$ records), loss $\ell$, parameters $\alpha, T, G, \sigma$.

  1. Initialize $\theta_0$; for $t = 1, \dots, T$: sample $x_t \sim \mathcal{D}$ uniformly.
  2. Per-sample gradient: $g_t = \nabla_\theta\, \ell(\theta_{t-1}; x_t)$.
  3. Clip: $\bar g_t = g_t \cdot \min\!\big(1, G/\|g_t\|_2\big)$.
  4. Noise: $\tilde g_t = \bar g_t + Z$, with $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$.
  5. Update $\theta_t = \theta_{t-1} - \alpha\, \tilde g_t$; project $\theta_t \leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta_t)$.
  6. Return $\theta_T$.
Abadi, Chu, Goodfellow, McMahan, Mironov, Talwar, Zhang, "Deep Learning with Differential Privacy", CCS 2016.

Theorem — DP-SGD Privacy

Theorem (DP-SGD privacy).

DP-SGD is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP if the noise standard deviation satisfies

$$\sigma \;\ge\; \frac{2G\sqrt{2T\log(1/\delta)}}{n\varepsilon}$$

Noise grows with $\sqrt{T}$ and $G$, shrinks with $n$: more data, less noise.

The clean $\sqrt{T}$ needs tight accounting; we build the three-step argument next.

Privacy Accounting — Roadmap

  1. One step, one sample: a Gaussian mechanism with sensitivity $2G$.
  2. Subsampling: the differing record is touched with probability $1/n$; amplify.
  3. Composition: $T$ adaptive steps; the budget grows like $\sqrt{T}$.

Each step reuses one earlier tool; one slide per step.

Step 1 — One Step Is a Gaussian Mechanism

Recall (Gaussian mechanism).

Releasing $f(X) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ with $\ell_2$-sensitivity $\Delta_2$ is $(\varepsilon_0, \delta_0)$-DP for $\sigma \ge \Delta_2\sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta_0)}/\varepsilon_0$.

Neighbors differ in one record; if sampled, both clipped gradients have norm at most $G$:

$$\|\bar g_t - \bar g_t'\|_2 \;\le\; \|\bar g_t\|_2 + \|\bar g_t'\|_2 \;\le\; 2G \;=\; \Delta_2$$

Clipping is exactly what makes this sensitivity finite.

Step 2 — Subsampling Amplifies

Recall (subsampling amplification).

Running an $(\varepsilon_0, \delta_0)$-DP mechanism on a random $q$-fraction gives roughly $\big(\ln(1 + q(e^{\varepsilon_0} - 1)),\, q\delta_0\big)$-DP, and $\ln(1+q(e^{\varepsilon_0}-1)) \approx q\varepsilon_0$.

$x_1$
$x_2$
$x_t$
$x_4$
⋯
$x_n$

One uniform draw per step: $q = 1/n$, so each step costs about $\big(\varepsilon_0/n,\ \delta_0/n\big)$.

Step 3 — Compose $T$ Steps

$T$ adaptive steps, each about $(\varepsilon_0/n, \delta_0/n)$-DP; $\sqrt{T}$-style composition gives:

$$\text{total} \;\approx\; \Big( \sqrt{T}\, \frac{\varepsilon_0}{n},\;\; T\, \frac{\delta_0}{n} \Big)$$
Gaussian step: $\varepsilon_0$
subsample $q = 1/n$→
$\approx \varepsilon_0 / n$
compose $T$ steps→
$\approx \sqrt{T}\, \varepsilon_0 / n$

Every accounting step is a tool we already proved; DP-SGD just chains them.

Accounting Recap — Solve for $\sigma$

$$\begin{aligned} \varepsilon_0 \;&\stackrel{(\text{Gaussian step})}{\approx}\; \frac{2G\sqrt{2\log(1/\delta)}}{\sigma} \\[4pt] \varepsilon \;&\stackrel{(\text{subsample, compose})}{\approx}\; \sqrt{T}\; \frac{\varepsilon_0}{n} \\[4pt] \sigma \;&\stackrel{(\text{solve for } \sigma)}{\ge}\; \frac{2G\sqrt{2T\log(1/\delta)}}{n\varepsilon} \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

Chain read top to bottom: per-step cost, amplified and composed, then inverted for $\sigma$.

Why $T = O(n^2)$ Steps Are Affordable

Total privacy $\approx \big(\sqrt{T}\,\varepsilon/n,\; T\delta/n\big)$ with per-step budget $(\varepsilon, \delta)$:

$$T = n^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sqrt{T}\,\frac{\varepsilon}{n} = \varepsilon$$
A constant budget buys $n^2$ gradient steps: many full epochs of training.

The $\delta$ side needs per-step $\delta$ tiny; tight accountants handle that bookkeeping.

Fine Print — Which Composition?

Advanced composition alone is not the end of the story for DP-SGD:

  • Advanced composition: generic; costs an extra $\sqrt{\log(T/\delta)}$ factor here.
  • Moments accountant: tracks all moments of the privacy loss; removes that factor.
  • Rényi DP: the same idea as a clean calculus for Gaussian steps.

The theorem's clean $\sqrt{T}$ constant is the moments-accountant version.

Theorem — DP-SGD Utility (Convex)

Theorem (DP-SGD rate).

$\mathcal{L}$ convex, $G$-Lipschitz; $\mathcal{C}$ closed convex, diameter $R$; clip at $G$.

With $T = \varepsilon^2 n^2 / d$ and $\alpha = \frac{1}{T}\, \frac{R \varepsilon n}{G\sqrt{d \log(1/\delta)}}$, the average iterate $\bar\theta_T = \frac{1}{T}\sum_t \theta_t$ satisfies

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}(\bar\theta_T)\big] - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star) \;=\; O\!\Big( \frac{RG\sqrt{d\log(1/\delta)}}{\varepsilon n} \Big)$$

Expectation over sampling and injected noise. Compare GD's $RG/\sqrt{T}$: privacy adds the $\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon n$ price.

Proof — the Noisy Gradient Oracle

$G$-Lipschitz loss means $\|g_t\|_2 \le G$ always: with clip bound $G$, clipping never activates, so no bias.

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde g_t\big] = \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta_{t-1}), \qquad \mathbb{E}\big[\|\tilde g_t\|^2\big] \;\le\; G^2 + d\sigma^2 \;=:\; B^2$$

The Gaussian adds $\sigma^2$ per coordinate; $d$ coordinates inflate the second moment by $d\sigma^2$.

DP-SGD is plain SGD with a worse variance constant: that is the whole proof.

Proof (continued) — SGD Rate with Variance $B^2$

Lemma (standard SGD convergence).

Convex $\mathcal{L}$, unbiased gradients with $\mathbb{E}\|\tilde g_t\|^2 \le B^2$, diameter $R$, $\alpha = \frac{R}{B\sqrt{T}}$: then $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(\bar\theta_T)] - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star) \le \frac{RB}{\sqrt{T}}$.

Substitute the inflated constant:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}(\bar\theta_T)\big] - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star) \;\le\; \frac{R\sqrt{G^2 + d\sigma^2}}{\sqrt{T}}$$

Proof (continued) — Plug In and Balance

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{R\sqrt{G^2 + d\sigma^2}}{\sqrt{T}} \;&\stackrel{(\sqrt{a+b}\, \le\, \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})}{\le}\; \frac{RG}{\sqrt{T}} + \frac{R\sqrt{d}\,\sigma}{\sqrt{T}} \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{plug in } \sigma)}{=}\; \frac{RG}{\sqrt{T}} + \frac{2\sqrt{2}\, RG \sqrt{d \log(1/\delta)}}{\varepsilon n} \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(T \,=\, \varepsilon^2 n^2 / d)}{=}\; O\!\Big( \frac{RG\sqrt{d\log(1/\delta)}}{\varepsilon n} \Big) \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

After plugging $\sigma$, the noise term is $T$-free; run until the optimization term matches.

Beyond Convexity

Nonconvex losses.

Deep-learning losses are nonconvex: DP-SGD, like plain SGD, only guarantees a stationary point $\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta) = 0$.

Practice: fine at MNIST scale; accuracy degrades markedly for large models and datasets.

Recap

  • DP-ERM: sensitivity of $L$ is $\Delta/n$; privacy costs excess risk, not correctness.
  • EM-ERM: sample $\propto e^{-\varepsilon n L/2\Delta}$; utility $O\!\big(dGR\log(\varepsilon n/d)/\varepsilon n\big)$ by volumes plus tail integration.
  • Advanced composition: $k$ steps cost $\approx \sqrt{2k\ln(1/\delta')}\,\varepsilon$; the loss is a concentrating random walk.
  • DP-SGD: clip forces sensitivity $2G$; add $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$, amplify by $1/n$, compose over $T$.
  • Utility: $O\!\big(RG\sqrt{d\log(1/\delta)}/\varepsilon n\big)$; nonconvex practice leans on public pretraining.
