01

The Attack Objectives

Budget, FGSM, and PGD

The Perturbation Set

An adversarial example lives in a small ball around $x$.

$$\mathcal{B}_p(x, \varepsilon) \;=\; \bigl\{\, x + \delta \;:\; \|\delta\|_p \le \varepsilon \,\bigr\}.$$

$p = \infty$ caps the largest single-pixel change; $p = 2$ caps the total energy.

The Attacker's Goal

Find the $\delta$ inside the budget that most raises the loss.

$$\max_{\|\delta\|_p \le \varepsilon}\; \ell\bigl(f_\theta(x + \delta),\, y\bigr).$$

$\ell$ = training loss; $y$ = true label; $f_\theta$ = the fixed model.

FGSM: One Step

Take the worst-case step of size $\varepsilon$ in the $L_\infty$ box.

$$x_{\text{adv}} \;=\; x \;+\; \varepsilon \cdot \operatorname{sign}\!\bigl(\nabla_x\, \ell(f_\theta(x), y)\bigr).$$

Keep only the sign: every pixel moves the maximum allowed amount.

Goodfellow, Shlens, and Szegedy, "Explaining and Harnessing Adversarial Examples", ICLR 2015.

Why the Sign

The sign step is the exact solution of a linearized attack.

  • Linearize the loss: $\ell(x + \delta) \approx \ell(x) + \nabla_x\ell^\top \delta$
  • Maximize $\nabla_x\ell^\top \delta$ over $\|\delta\|_\infty \le \varepsilon$
  • The maximizer is $\delta = \varepsilon\,\operatorname{sign}(\nabla_x\ell)$

FGSM is one step of the linear approximation, nothing more.

PGD: Projected Iteration

Repeat the sign step, projecting back into the budget each time.

$$x_{t+1} \;=\; \Pi_{\mathcal{B}_p(x,\varepsilon)}\!\Bigl(x_t + \alpha \cdot \operatorname{sign}\!\bigl(\nabla_x\, \ell(f_\theta(x_t), y)\bigr)\Bigr).$$

$\alpha$ = step size; $\Pi$ = projection back onto the ball; many steps.

Madry et al., "Towards Deep Learning Models Resistant to Adversarial Attacks", ICLR 2018.

The Projection Step

$\Pi$ pulls any stray point back onto the allowed ball.

$L_\infty$

Clip each coordinate of $\delta$ to the range $[-\varepsilon, \varepsilon]$.

$L_2$

Rescale $\delta$ so its length is at most $\varepsilon$.

Every step stays legal, so the final example is admissible.

02

Robust Training

The saddle-point objective

Adversarial Training

Train against the worst case inside every point's budget.

$$\min_{\theta}\; \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}\Bigl[\, \max_{\|\delta\|_p \le \varepsilon}\; \ell\bigl(f_\theta(x + \delta),\, y\bigr)\Bigr].$$

Inner max = the attack; outer min = the defender fitting $\theta$.

Madry et al., "Towards Deep Learning Models Resistant to Adversarial Attacks", ICLR 2018.

How the Saddle Point Is Solved

Alternate the two players, one batch at a time.

  1. Inner max: run PGD to craft $\delta$ for the batch.
  2. Outer min: take a gradient step on $\theta$ at the attacked inputs.
  3. Repeat, so the model learns to resist the strongest attack it can see.

A strong inner attack is what makes the outer defense real.

Why It Is Expensive

Every training step now hides an optimization inside it.

  • Each batch runs $k$ PGD steps: roughly $k\times$ the cost
  • Fitting the worst case trims clean accuracy
  • The robustness-accuracy trade-off is intrinsic, not a bug

You pay in compute and in clean accuracy for a wider safe region.

03

Certified Robustness

Randomized smoothing's provable radius

The Smoothed Classifier

Replace $f$ by the class it returns most often under noise.

$$g(x) \;=\; \arg\max_{c}\; \Pr_{\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)}\bigl[\, f(x + \eta) = c \,\bigr].$$

$\eta$ = Gaussian noise; $g$ votes over many noisy copies of $x$.

Cohen, Rosenfeld, and Kolter, "Certified Adversarial Robustness via Randomized Smoothing", ICML 2019.

The Certified Radius

If the top class wins with probability $\underline{p_A}$, $g$ is provably constant in an $L_2$ ball.

$$R \;=\; \frac{\sigma}{2}\,\Bigl(\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A}) - \Phi^{-1}(\overline{p_B})\Bigr).$$

$\Phi^{-1}$ = inverse Gaussian CDF; $\underline{p_A}, \overline{p_B}$ = top-two class probability bounds.

Cohen, Rosenfeld, and Kolter, "Certified Adversarial Robustness via Randomized Smoothing", ICML 2019.

Reading the Radius

The formula shows exactly what buys a bigger guarantee.

  • More noise $\sigma$: larger radius, but noisier accuracy
  • More confident vote ($\underline{p_A} \to 1$): the gap term grows
  • No attack with $\|\delta\|_2 \le R$ can ever flip $g$

A proof against every attack in the ball, not just the ones we ran.

The Certified Catch

The guarantee is real but modest.

Cost

Estimating $\underline{p_A}$ needs many noisy forward passes per input.

Scope

It certifies an $L_2$ radius, often small on high-resolution images.

A narrow, costly proof, but a proof nonetheless.
