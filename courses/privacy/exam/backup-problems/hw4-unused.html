3. Bayes-Optimal Membership Test. Let $B \in \{0, 1\}$ be a Bernoulli$(\pi)$ membership indicator, and let $T$ be an observed statistic with continuous density $p_b(t)$ given $B = b$. Consider any decision rule $\hat B : \mathbb{R} \to \{0, 1\}$, and measure its quality by the $0$-$1$ error $\Pr[\hat B(T) \ne B]$.
  1. Compute the posterior $\Pr[B = 1 \mid T = t]$ in terms of $p_0, p_1, \pi$.
  2. Show that the Bayes-optimal rule (the MAP rule) is $$\hat B_{\mathrm{MAP}}(t) = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\Lambda(t) \ge \frac{1-\pi}{\pi}\right], \qquad \Lambda(t) = \frac{p_1(t)}{p_0(t)},$$ and connect this to Problem 1: the Bayes-optimal MIA is a likelihood-ratio test, and only the threshold changes with the prior.
  3. Suppose $T = \ell(f_\theta, z)$ is the loss of model $f_\theta$ on the queried point $z$, and that $\ell \mid B = 1 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathrm{in}}, \sigma^2)$, $\ell \mid B = 0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}, \sigma^2)$ with $\mu_{\mathrm{in}} < \mu_{\mathrm{out}}$. Show that the optimal rule reduces to a single-threshold loss test $\hat B = \mathbf{1}[\ell \le c]$ and compute $c$ explicitly when $\pi = 1/2$.
5. LiRA Closed Form and Decision Boundary. Assume that for a target record $z$, the per-shadow-model loss has Gaussian distributions under the two membership hypotheses: $$\ell \mid (z \in D) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathrm{in}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2), \qquad \ell \mid (z \notin D) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^2).$$
  1. Derive the log-likelihood ratio $$\log \Lambda(\ell) \;=\; \log \frac{\mathcal{N}(\ell ; \mu_{\mathrm{in}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2)}{\mathcal{N}(\ell ; \mu_{\mathrm{out}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^2)} \;=\; \frac{(\ell - \mu_{\mathrm{out}})^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^2} - \frac{(\ell - \mu_{\mathrm{in}})^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^2} + \log\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}}.$$
  2. Show that for general $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} \ne \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$, the optimal decision region $\{\log \Lambda > \log \tau\}$ is one of: a bounded interval, the complement of a bounded interval, a half-line, all of $\mathbb{R}$, or empty. Identify which case occurs based on the sign of $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} - \sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$.
  3. Show that in the homoscedastic case $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{out}} = \sigma$, the optimal MIA reduces to the single threshold $\mathcal{A}(z) = \mathbf{1}[\ell \le c]$, and express $c$ in terms of $\mu_{\mathrm{in}}, \mu_{\mathrm{out}}, \sigma, \tau$.
  4. In the homoscedastic case, derive the closed-form ROC: at false-positive rate $\alpha$, the optimal true-positive rate is $$\mathrm{TPR}(\alpha) \;=\; \Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(\alpha) + \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{out}} - \mu_{\mathrm{in}}}{\sigma}\right),$$ where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF.
6. Concrete MIA on a Gaussian Mean Model. A "training" algorithm maps $D = (z_1, \ldots, z_n)$ with $z_i \in \mathbb{R}$ to the parameter $$\theta(D) \;=\; \bar z + W, \quad \bar z = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n z_i, \quad W \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \rho^2) \text{ independent of } D.$$ Assume the population $\mathcal{P}$ is $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, and the adversary observes $\theta$ plus a candidate point $z^*$. The MIA game: $b \sim \mathrm{Unif}\{0,1\}$. If $b=1$, $z^* = z_1$ (a training sample, included in $D$); if $b=0$, $z^* \sim \mathcal{P}$ independent of $D$.
  1. Show that conditional on $z^*$, $$\theta \mid (b=1) \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\tfrac{z^*}{n}, \tfrac{n-1}{n^2} + \rho^2\right), \qquad \theta \mid (b=0) \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0, \tfrac{1}{n} + \rho^2\right).$$
  2. Show that the optimal adversary's test statistic is the (conditional) likelihood ratio $\Lambda(\theta, z^*) = p(\theta \mid b=1, z^*)/p(\theta \mid b=0, z^*)$, and write down $\log \Lambda$ as a quadratic in $\theta$ (with $z^*$ treated as a known constant).
  3. Now suppose the inputs are clipped, $|z_i| \le 1$, so the $L_2$-sensitivity of $\bar z$ under one-element neighbors is at most $2/n$. The Gaussian mechanism with $\rho \ge (2/n\varepsilon)\sqrt{2\log(1.25/\delta)}$ makes the release $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP. Using Problem 2(b), give a numerical upper bound on the optimal MIA advantage for $\varepsilon = 0.1$, $\delta = 10^{-5}$, and comment on why $\varepsilon = 1$ already gives a vacuous bound.
9. NPO Has a Bounded Gradient; GA Does Not. For LLM unlearning, let $\pi_\theta(y \mid x)$ denote the model's likelihood of completion $y$ given prompt $x$, and let $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ be a fixed reference model. The forget set is $\mathcal{D}_F$. Two unlearning losses:
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GA}}(\theta) = -\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}_F}\!\left[-\log \pi_\theta(y \mid x)\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\log \pi_\theta(y \mid x)\right],$
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}}(\theta) = \tfrac{2}{\beta}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log\!\left(1 + \big(\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)}\big)^{\beta}\right)\right] \quad (\beta > 0).$
  1. Compute $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GA}}$. Using the identity $\nabla_\theta \log\pi_\theta = \nabla_\theta \pi_\theta / \pi_\theta$, show that for any sample with $\pi_\theta(y\mid x) \to 0$ (and $\|\nabla_\theta \pi_\theta\|$ bounded away from zero in some direction), $\|\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GA}}\| \to \infty$. Explain in one sentence why this drives the GA optimization to escape the data distribution ("GA collapse").
  2. Let $u(\theta) = \beta \log(\pi_\theta(y\mid x)/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x))$. Show that $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}}(\theta) = \frac{2}{\beta}\,\mathbb{E}[\log(1 + e^{u(\theta)})], \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}}}{\partial u} = \frac{2}{\beta}\,\sigma(u) \;\in\; \left(0, \tfrac{2}{\beta}\right),$$ where $\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid. Conclude that $$\big\| \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPO}} \big\| \;\le\; 2\,\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y\mid x)\|\big].$$
  3. Show that the right-hand bound above is uniform in $\theta$: in particular, even as $\pi_\theta(y \mid x) \to 0$, the per-sample contribution $\sigma(u)\nabla_\theta \log\pi_\theta$ to the NPO gradient does not blow up, in sharp contrast to part (a). State briefly why this bounded-per-step divergence is what makes NPO "safe" for unlearning.
10. Black-Box Unlearning Audits Require Many Queries. Let $\theta_o$ and $\theta_u$ be two LLMs with output distributions $P_0$ and $P_1$ on a fixed prompt distribution. For two distributions $P, Q$ on the same sample space, define the total variation distance $$\mathrm{TV}(P, Q) \;=\; \sup_A |P(A) - Q(A)|,$$ where the supremum is over events $A$. Assume $\mathrm{TV}(P_0, P_1) \le \eta$. An auditor draws $q$ i.i.d. prompts $x_1, \ldots, x_q$, observes one sample $y_i \sim P_b(\cdot \mid x_i)$ per prompt, and outputs $\hat b \in \{0, 1\}$. Write $P_b^{\otimes q}$ for the product distribution of the $q$ observations under $b$.
  1. (Le Cam two-point lower bound.) Show that for any test based on $q$ samples, $$\Pr_{b=0}[\hat b \ne 0] + \Pr_{b=1}[\hat b \ne 1] \;\ge\; 1 - \mathrm{TV}(P_0^{\otimes q}, P_1^{\otimes q}).$$ Identify $\hat b$ with a function $\psi : \mathcal{X}^q \to [0, 1]$ (the probability of declaring $b = 1$). Then prove the auxiliary fact: for any distributions $P, Q$ and any $\psi: \mathcal{X}^q \to [0, 1]$, $\mathbb{E}_P[\psi] - \mathbb{E}_Q[\psi] \le \mathrm{TV}(P, Q)$. (For the auxiliary fact, write $\psi(x) = \int_0^1 \mathbf{1}[\psi(x) > s]\, ds$ and integrate.)
  2. Show that $\mathrm{TV}(P_0^{\otimes q}, P_1^{\otimes q}) \le q \cdot \mathrm{TV}(P_0, P_1) \le q\eta$, by a hybrid (telescoping) argument: define $H_k = P_0^{\otimes k} \otimes P_1^{\otimes (q-k)}$ for $k = 0, \ldots, q$, and bound $\mathrm{TV}(H_k, H_{k-1})$.
  3. Conclude that to guarantee total error $\le 2\alpha$ (i.e., $\Pr_0[\hat b \ne 0] + \Pr_1[\hat b \ne 1] \le 2\alpha$), the auditor needs $$q \;\ge\; \frac{1 - 2\alpha}{\eta}.$$ Tighter $\chi^2$-style bounds give $q = \Omega(1/\eta^2)$. Why does this make single-prompt audits of LLM unlearning fundamentally insufficient when $\theta_u, \theta_o$ differ only by a small distributional perturbation?
