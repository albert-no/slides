01

Why Watermark Text

Provenance, design goals, threat models

The Central Question

Given only text $x$ and key $k$ — was $x$ written by a watermarked LLM?

Detector answers yes / no. No model, no prompt — just the text.

Use Cases

Provenance

Is this essay or review AI-written?

Disinformation

Flag mass-produced fake news and propaganda.

Data hygiene

Keep AI text out of training corpora.

Three Design Goals

  • Detectability — short text, high TPR, low FPR
  • Quality — output distribution close to vanilla LLM
  • Robustness — survives editing, paraphrase, translation

Pick any two — every scheme makes a tradeoff.

Two Kinds of Attack

Removal

Edit or paraphrase to erase the mark.

So AI text reads as human-written.

Spoofing

Forge the mark onto human text.

To falsely blame the model for words it never wrote.

Spoofing is the under-studied threat.

02

Green-List Watermark

Kirchenbauer et al. 2023 — the workhorse method

The Core Idea — Tilt Toward Green

Split the vocabulary into a secret green half and a red half. Then nudge sampling toward green.

Regular generation — green by chance (~half):

Themodelwritesashortreplytotheuser

Watermarked — green dominates:

Themodeldraftsabriefreplyforeachuser

Detector just counts green tokens — too many ⇒ watermarked.

Setup

At each step $t$, using context $x_{<t}$ and key $k$:

  • Hash recent tokens with $k$ ⇒ a fresh green list $G_t$ (fraction $\gamma$)
  • Add bias $\delta > 0$ to green logits before softmax

Default: $\gamma = 0.25$, $\delta = 2$.

Kirchenbauer et al., "A Watermark for Large Language Models", ICML 2023.

Watermarked Sampling

$$\hat p_\theta(v \mid x_{<t}) \;=\; \frac{\exp\!\big(\ell_t(v) + \delta\,\mathbb{1}[v \in G_t]\big)}{\sum_{w} \exp\!\big(\ell_t(w) + \delta\,\mathbb{1}[w \in G_t]\big)}$$

Vanilla logits $\ell_t$, plus $\delta$ on green tokens only.

  • High entropy ⇒ bias steers toward green
  • Low entropy ⇒ bias swamped, quality kept

Detection: One-Proportion z-Test

Given $T$ tokens, count green hits $|s|_G$. Under $H_0$ (no watermark):

$$z \;=\; \frac{|s|_G - \gamma T}{\sqrt{T \gamma (1-\gamma)}} \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}(0, 1).$$

Reject when $z > z_\alpha$ — false-positive rate exactly $\alpha$.

  • ~25 tokens often already give $z > 4$
  • Detection needs only key $k$ — no model

Detection Power — Entropy Bound

Watermarked text beats the chance baseline by an entropy-weighted margin:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[|s|_G\big] \;\ge\; T\!\left(\gamma \;+\; \underbrace{\tfrac{(1-\gamma)\,(e^\delta - 1)}{e^\delta + 1}}_{\text{bias strength}}\,\bar H\right), \qquad \bar H = \text{avg. spike entropy}.$$
  • Signal tracks text entropy, not length alone
  • Low-entropy spans — boilerplate, code — carry no watermark

Ceiling: deterministic text ($\bar H = 0$) cannot be watermarked.

The Core Tradeoff — One Knob, $\delta$

Turn $\delta$ up

✓ Stronger signal, detect in fewer tokens

✗ Output drifts from $p_\theta$ — quality drops

Keep $\delta$ low

✓ Stays close to the vanilla LLM

✗ Needs longer text to detect

Detection vs quality — opposite pulls. Next: can we keep $\delta > 0$ yet add zero distortion?

03

Distortion-Free and Undetectable

Cryptographic watermarks that preserve $p_\theta$

Recall — Gumbel Distribution

$G \sim \mathrm{Gumbel}(0)$ — the noise you get from a uniform via

$$G \;=\; -\log(-\log U), \qquad U \sim \mathrm{Unif}(0,1).$$
  • Double-exponential tail: CDF $F_G(g) = \exp(-e^{-g})$
  • Well-behaved under maximization — the key property next

One building block underlies all the cryptographic schemes below.

Theorem — Gumbel-Max Trick

Theorem.

Let $\ell_v = \log p_\theta(v)$ for $v \in \mathcal{V}$ and let $G_v \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathrm{Gumbel}(0)$. Then

$$\arg\max_{v}\;(\ell_v + G_v) \;\sim\; p_\theta.$$

Idea: softmax sampling = add Gumbel noise to each logit, take arg-max.

Generate the noise $G_v$ from the secret key (not fresh randomness): marginal stays $p_\theta$, yet the key-holder can recompute it to detect.

Why It Works — Proof Sketch

Reduce the arg-max to a minimum of independent exponentials.

  • Gumbel in disguise: $e^{-G_v} \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1)$
  • $\arg\max_v(\ell_v + G_v) = \arg\min_v\, e^{-G_v}/p_\theta(v)$,  with  $e^{-G_v}/p_\theta(v) \sim \mathrm{Exp}\big(p_\theta(v)\big)$
  • The minimum is attained at $v$ with prob $\propto$ rate $= p_\theta(v)$

So the winner follows $p_\theta$ exactly. $\;\blacksquare$

Distortion-Free Watermark

Definition.

A watermark is distortion-free if, marginalized over key $k$, the output distribution equals the original LLM:

$$\mathbb{E}_k\!\left[\hat p_\theta(\cdot \mid x_{<t}, k)\right] \;=\; p_\theta(\cdot \mid x_{<t}).$$

Quality preserved on average; detector still succeeds with key.

Aaronson — Gumbel Trick

For each step, draw key-seeded uniforms $r_v \sim U[0,1]$ and pick

$$x_t \;=\; \arg\max_v \; r_v^{1/p_\theta(v)}.$$

Equivalent to Gumbel sampling — exactly follows $p_\theta$.

  • Detector recomputes $r_v$ from the key, scores each token
  • Watermarked text ⇒ scores skew telltale small

Why It Is Distortion-Free

The key uniforms hide a Gumbel in disguise:

$$-\log(-\log r_v) \;\sim\; \mathrm{Gumbel}(0) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \arg\max_v r_v^{1/p_\theta(v)} \;=\; \arg\max_v\,(\ell_v + G_v).$$

So Aaronson's rule is Gumbel-max — just with key-seeded noise.

By the Gumbel-max theorem, averaging over the key reproduces $p_\theta$ exactly — zero quality loss, yet detectable with the key.

Kuditipudi: Provably Distortion-Free

Build watermark via inverse-transform sampling on a key-driven sequence.

  • Detector uses edit-distance alignment between key sequence and text
  • Robust to insertions, deletions, paraphrase
  • Cost: longer text needed, more compute at detection

First scheme that is provably distortion-free and robust to edits.

Kuditipudi et al., "Robust Distortion-Free Watermarks", TMLR 2024.

Theorem — Edit-Distance Robustness

Theorem (Kuditipudi et al. 2024).

Edit watermarked $x$ into $\tilde x$ with Levenshtein budget $\eta$. The aligned-key detector still fires with high probability:

$$\Pr\!\big[\,\text{detect}(\tilde x) = 1\,\big] \;\ge\; 1 - 2\exp\!\left(-\,c\,\frac{(T - 2\eta)^2}{T}\right).$$
  • Reading: as long as edits touch a small fraction of tokens ($\eta \ll T$), detection stays near-certain
  • Alignment found by dynamic programming, $O(T \log T)$

One Family: Unbiased Watermarks

Hu et al. 2024 unify the cryptographic schemes under one umbrella:

  • Token marginal preserved exactly, not just on average
  • Gumbel, inverse-transform, exponential — all special cases

The common thread linking Aaronson, Kuditipudi, and CGZ.

Hu et al., "Unbiased Watermark for Language Models", ICLR 2024.

Undetectable Watermarks

Definition (Christ, Gunn, Zamir 2023).

A watermark is undetectable if no efficient (polynomial-time) algorithm without $k$ can tell watermarked from unwatermarked outputs with non-negligible advantage.

  • Cryptographic gold standard — stronger than distortion-free
  • Built on PRFs and hardness assumptions
Christ, Gunn, Zamir, "Undetectable Watermarks for Language Models", COLT 2024.

CGZ Construction — PRF

Drive the sampling with a secret pseudo-random function $F_k$ instead of true randomness:

  • Generate: $F_k$ supplies the "coin flips" at each step
  • Detect: recompute $F_k$ with the key, sum a per-token score

Without $k$, a PRF is indistinguishable from random ⇒ output looks unchanged.

04

Robustness and Semantics

Surviving paraphrase and edits

The Paraphrase Attack

Pass watermarked text through a second LLM with rewrite prompt.

  • Token identities change ⇒ green-list signal weakens
  • Semantic content preserved
  • Cost: small budget; quality loss minimal

Vanilla Kirchenbauer at $\delta=2$: paraphrased text drops to $z \approx 1.5$.

Kirchenbauer et al., "On the Reliability of Watermarks", 2023.

Making the Signal Survive Edits

Fixed green list

Drop context hashing — detection degrades gracefully with edits.

Semantic hash

Seed from sentence embeddings — synonyms keep the signal.

Permute-and-flip

Watermark the decoding rule itself; resists perturbation.

Each buys robustness at a cost — lost entropy, a trusted embedder, or easier spoofing.

Zhao et al. 2023 · Liu et al. ICLR 2024 · Zhao et al. 2024.

Quality-First Decoding

Adaptive (Liu 2024)

Bias only the high-entropy steps; skip "obvious" tokens.

Better quality at the same detection.

WaterMax (Giboulot 2024)

Generate several candidates, keep the most detectable one.

No bias on-path, but several-fold cost.

Liu et al., ICML 2024 · Giboulot & Furon, NeurIPS 2024.
05

Beyond Standard LLMs

Radioactivity, production, diffusion-LLM

Radioactive Watermarks

Watermarked text is used to train a downstream LLM.

  • The mark survives into the student model's outputs
  • Detects: did model X train on Y's watermarked text?

Watermarks become a training-set tracer.

Sander et al., "Watermarking Makes Language Models Radioactive", NeurIPS 2024.

SynthID-Text (Production)

Google DeepMind, Nature 2024 — deployed on Gemini.

  • Tournament sampling ⇒ approximately distortion-free
  • Quality unchanged in live human eval, millions of users

Production watermarking is no longer hypothetical.

Dathathri et al., "Scalable Watermarking for LLM Outputs", Nature 2024.

Diffusion-LLM Watermarking (dgMARK)

Lab work: watermark for masked discrete diffusion LLMs.

  • AR token-by-token rule doesn't fit any-order unmasking
  • Embed at each remasking step — first for non-AR decoding

See companion deck dllm/ for the dLLM substrate.

Hong & No, "dgMARK: Watermarking for Diffusion Language Models", ICML 2026.

Open Problems

  • Robust + distortion-free + short text — can we get all three?
  • Low-entropy domains: code and structured outputs
  • Multi-modal provenance — image + text + voice under one key
  • Public verifiers that resist spoofing without leaking the key

Takeaways

  • Green-list = simple, deployed, paraphrase-fragile
  • Distortion-free schemes preserve $p_\theta$ on average
  • Undetectable = cryptographic gold standard, hardness-based
  • Robustness against paraphrase needs semantic / fixed-list tricks
  • Watermarks now trace training contamination, not just direct generation
