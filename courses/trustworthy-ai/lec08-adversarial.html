03

Finding the Worst Case

An attack is a search; a weak search overstates robustness

An Attack Is a Search Inside the Ball

Find the allowed change that hurts the model most:

$$\max_{\|\delta\|\le\epsilon}\ L\big(f(x+\delta),\,y\big)$$

Training lowers loss by changing weights; attacks raise it by changing the input.

FGSM: One Step Along the Gradient Sign

$$x' = x + \epsilon\cdot\mathrm{sign}\big(\nabla_x L(f(x),y)\big)$$

(a) weights · (b) their sign = the attack · (c) clean 3s, 7s · (d) attacked, ε = .25

Logistic regression, MNIST 3 vs 7: error 1.6% → 99%

Goodfellow et al., ICLR 2015 — Fig. 2. FGSM exactly maximizes the linearized loss over the ℓ∞ box.

One Step Can Miss the Worst Point

black true loss · red dashed tangent at $x$ (FGSM's linear model) · shaded allowed interval

FGSM jumps to the edge the slope points to; the curved loss peaks inside.

Illustrative 1-D loss, $L(u)=\sin u$.

PGD: Many Small Steps, Then Project

  1. Start at a random point in the ball
  2. Step size α along $\mathrm{sign}(\nabla_x L)$
  3. Project: clip back into the ball and valid pixel range
  4. Repeat for $K$ steps (PGD-$K$)
  5. Restart several times; keep the highest-loss point

FGSM is the one-step case: no random start, α = ε, K = 1.

Madry et al., "Towards Deep Learning Models Resistant to Adversarial Attacks", ICLR 2018 — §2.1, §3.

Steps Stay Inside the Ball

Projection keeps every iterate a legal attack under the threat model.

Many Restarts Reach Similar Loss

each line one PGD run from a random start, 20 runs, same image

Evidence PGD is a strong first-order attack, not proof no worse point exists.

Madry et al., ICLR 2018 — Fig. 1 (cross-entropy loss over PGD iterations; MNIST and CIFAR-10, standard and adversarial training).

Adaptive Attacks: Attack the Defense You Have

  • Read the defense; target its weak part
  • Non-differentiable step? Approximate it (BPDA)
  • Randomness? Average gradients over draws (EOT)
  • Diagnostic: huge ε should drive accuracy near 0%
  • Diagnostic: black box beating white box hints at masking

Checks raise red flags; passing them proves nothing.

Athalye et al., ICML 2018 — §4 (BPDA, EOT); Carlini et al., "On Evaluating Adversarial Robustness", arXiv 1902.06705; Tramèr et al., "On Adaptive Attacks to Adversarial Example Defenses", NeurIPS 2020.

RobustBench: A Standardized Benchmark

Protocol

  • Fixed dataset and threat model
  • AutoAttack on every entry
  • Outside adaptive attacks welcome

Entry rules

  • Non-zero gradients
  • Deterministic forward pass
  • No optimization loop inside

Rules exclude defenses AutoAttack can't fairly test; those need custom evaluation.

Croce et al., "RobustBench: A Standardized Adversarial Robustness Benchmark", NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks — §3.

Robust Under One Ruler, Not Every Ruler

  • Training on $\ell_\infty$ gives limited robustness under other norms
  • Madry's MNIST model: PGD overestimated its $\ell_2$ robustness
  • A gradient-free attack found it more brittle
  • Corruptions and new viewpoints: a different question

A score names its threat model. No automatic guarantee outside it.

Athalye et al., ICML 2018 — §5.1.1; Madry et al., ICLR 2018 — Appendix (ℓ2 attacks on MNIST: masked gradients, decision-based attack).

Empirical vs Certified Robustness

A certificate proves the prediction is stable, not that it is correct.

certified accuracy counts inputs that are both correctly classified and certified

Randomized Smoothing: Vote Under Noise

left input $x$

right one noisy copy, $x+\eta$

$g$ returns the class $f$ picks most often under noise.

$$g(x)=\arg\max_c\ \Pr\big[f(x+\eta)=c\big],\quad \eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$$

In practice the vote shares are estimated by sampling; that procedure may abstain

Cohen, Rosenfeld & Kolter, ICML 2019 — Eq. 1, Fig. 2, §3.2 (PREDICT, CERTIFY). $f$: base classifier; $g$: smoothed classifier.

A Clear Vote Survives Any Small Shift

circles noise around $x$, $x+\delta$

right worst-case $f$: a straight cut

At fixed noise level, a larger vote margin gives a larger certified $\ell_2$ radius $R$.

Certifies g, not f · formula for $R$: technical supplement

Sampled: abstains when no class clearly leads · per input, P(invalid certificate) ≤ α

Cohen et al., ICML 2019 — Theorem 1, Fig. 3, §3.2 (Prop. 2: for each fixed input, the probability over its sampling that CERTIFY returns an invalid certificate is at most α).

Established vs Open

Established

  • Tiny changes fool accurate models
  • Weak attacks overstate robustness
  • Adversarial training helps, partly
  • Certificates exist for small $\ell_2$ balls

Open

  • One agreed cause
  • Robustness across all threat models
  • Large certified radii at scale
  • Robustness with no accuracy cost

Takeaways

  • Accuracy is average case; attackers pick the worst
  • A robustness claim needs a threat model
  • Report the strongest, adaptive attack
  • Benchmarks standardize; they don't guarantee
  • Certificates prove small regions, at a cost

Ask: robust to what, against whom, measured how, at what cost?
