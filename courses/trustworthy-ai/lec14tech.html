01

Pre-Processing: Reweighing

Balancing group and outcome by weight

The Reweighing Weight

Give example $(g, y)$ a weight that undoes the correlation between group and outcome.

$$w(g, y) = \frac{\Pr[G = g]\,\Pr[Y = y]}{\Pr[G = g,\, Y = y]}.$$

$G$ = group; $Y$ = label. All probabilities are empirical frequencies in the training set.

Kamiran and Calders, "Data Preprocessing Techniques for Classification without Discrimination", KIS 2012.

Reading the Weight

Compare the cell to what independence would predict.

  • Numerator $\Pr[G=g]\,\Pr[Y=y]$: the count if $G \perp Y$
  • Denominator $\Pr[G=g, Y=y]$: the count actually observed
  • Under-represented pairs get $w > 1$; over-represented get $w < 1$

Weight the loss by $w(g,y)$ and the reweighted data satisfies $G \perp Y$ exactly.

02

In-Processing Objectives

Penalty, constraint, and reduction

The Penalized Objective

Add unfairness as a soft penalty to the training loss.

$$\min_\theta \; \underbrace{\text{Loss}(\theta)}_{\text{accuracy}} \;+\; \lambda \, \underbrace{\text{Unfairness}(\theta)}_{\text{penalty}}.$$

$\lambda \ge 0$ trades accuracy for fairness. $\lambda = 0$ recovers ordinary training.

The Constrained Objective

Instead of a penalty, demand the gap stay within a budget $\tau$.

$$\min_\theta \;\text{Loss}(\theta) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \text{gap}(\theta) \le \tau.$$

$\text{gap}(\theta)$ = a fairness violation, e.g. the demographic-parity or equalized-odds gap. A hard promise, not a nudge.

Penalty vs Constraint

Penalty ($\lambda$)

Easy to optimize, but $\lambda$ only indirectly controls the gap.

Constraint ($\tau$)

$\tau$ is the gap you promise, but the problem is harder to solve.

The Lagrangian ties them together: each $\tau$ corresponds to some $\lambda$.

The Reductions Approach

Solve the constrained problem as a sequence of cost-sensitive classifications.

  1. Form the Lagrangian: constraint violation becomes a weighted cost.
  2. Fix the current weights $\lambda$; train a plain classifier on the reweighted costs.
  3. Measure the fairness gap; raise $\lambda$ where the constraint is violated.
  4. Repeat. The average of these classifiers satisfies the constraint.
Agarwal, Beygelzimer, Dudík, Langford, and Wallach, "A Reductions Approach to Fair Classification", ICML 2018.

Why Reductions Work

It is a saddle-point game between learner and constraint.

  • The learner minimizes cost; the $\lambda$-player pushes on violated constraints
  • Any base classifier plugs in as a black box — no new model needed
  • Handles many fairness definitions with provable gap guarantees

Fairness logic lives outside the learner; the learner stays ordinary.

03

Post-Processing Thresholds

One cutoff per group, chosen to match rates

Equalized Odds, Formally

Equalized odds

Equal true-positive and false-positive rates across groups $a, b$:

$$\Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = y,\, G = a] = \Pr[\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = y,\, G = b], \quad y \in \{0, 1\}.$$

Error rates must not depend on the group, at each true label $y$.

Hardt, Price, and Srebro, "Equality of Opportunity in Supervised Learning", NeurIPS 2016.

Per-Group Threshold Selection

Fix the trained score $s(x)$; choose a cutoff $t_g$ per group.

$$\hat Y = \mathbb{1}[\,s(x) \ge t_g\,], \qquad \min_{\{t_g\}}\; \text{error} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \text{odds match across } g.$$

Each group's cutoff traces its own ROC curve; feasible solutions are where the curves overlap.

Hardt, Price, and Srebro, "Equality of Opportunity in Supervised Learning", NeurIPS 2016.

The Post-Hoc Recipe

  1. Train a scoring model the ordinary way; freeze it.
  2. On held-out data, plot each group's ROC curve of (FPR, TPR).
  3. Find the point where group ROC curves intersect — equal rates.
  4. Read off cutoff $t_g$ per group; deploy those thresholds.

Only the decision rule changes; the weights never move.

Hardt, Price, and Srebro, "Equality of Opportunity in Supervised Learning", NeurIPS 2016.

The Accuracy Cost

Matching rates forces the cutoff off each group's optimum.

  • The unconstrained best cutoff maximizes per-group accuracy
  • The equal-odds cutoff sits at the ROC intersection instead
  • The gap between the two is the fairness tax on accuracy

Post-processing is a projection onto the feasible set — cheap to run, but it pays in accuracy.

$\min_\theta$ s.t.

Mitigation is a constrained optimization.
