Sections 02–08 adapted from MIT OpenCourseWare — 6.436J / 15.085J Fundamentals of Probability, Fall 2018 — License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — ocw.mit.edu

Definition — Jointly Gaussian Pair

Definition (2D Gaussian).

$(X_1, X_2) \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma)$ with mean $\boldsymbol{\mu} = (\mu_1, \mu_2)$ and

$$\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_1^2 & \rho\sigma_1\sigma_2 \\ \rho\sigma_1\sigma_2 & \sigma_2^2 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Two spreads $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ — plus one coupling $\rho$
  • $\Sigma$ is symmetric — co-movement has no direction

The Joint Density

$$f_{X_1,X_2}(x_1, x_2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^2 \det(\Sigma)}} \exp\!\left( -\tfrac{1}{2} (x - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \Sigma^{-1} (x - \boldsymbol{\mu}) \right)$$
  • Here $x = (x_1, x_2)^\top$ — a point in the plane
  • Exponent: a quadratic form in $x - \boldsymbol{\mu}$
  • Needs $\det(\Sigma) > 0$ and $\Sigma^{-1}$ — matrix conditions, up next

What the Coupling Does

$\rho = 0$ $\rho = 0.6$ $\rho = -0.6$

Level sets of the density: circles tilt into ellipses as $\rho$ moves

Running Example — Fixed Numbers

Take $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = 1$ and coupling $\rho = 0.6$:

$$\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0.6 \\ 0.6 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det \Sigma = 1 - 0.36 = 0.64, \qquad \Sigma^{-1} = \frac{1}{0.64}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -0.6 \\ -0.6 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Determinant positive — the density exists
  • This $\Sigma$ returns in every section today

The Running Example, Drawn

long axis: $\lambda = 1.6$ $x_1$ $x_2$ $1\sigma$ $2\sigma$

Positive coupling tilts the mass along $x_1 = x_2$ — eigenvalues 1.6 and 0.4

Theorem — Conditioning Stays Gaussian

Theorem 1 (2D conditional).

Let $(X_1, X_2) \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma)$ with entries $\sigma_{ij}$ and $\sigma_{22} > 0$. Then $X_1 \mid X_2 = x_2$ is Gaussian:

$$X_1 \mid X_2 = x_2 \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\left( \mu_1 + \frac{\sigma_{12}}{\sigma_{22}}(x_2 - \mu_2),\;\; \sigma_{11} - \frac{\sigma_{12}\sigma_{21}}{\sigma_{22}} \right)$$

A corollary of the general properties theorem — proved later today

Conditioning = Slicing the Ellipse

slice: $x_2 = 1$ mean $0.6$ conditional density of $X_1$ $x_1$

Fix a value of $X_2$: the cut through the mass is again a bell curve

Worked — the Running Example

Zero means, $\sigma_{11} = \sigma_{22} = 1$, $\sigma_{12} = 0.6$; observe $X_2 = 1$:

$$X_1 \mid X_2 = 1 \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\left( 0 + \tfrac{0.6}{1}\cdot 1,\;\; 1 - \tfrac{0.36}{1} \right) = \mathcal{N}(0.6,\; 0.64)$$
  • Mean moved toward the evidence, scaled by the coupling
  • Variance dropped $1 \to 0.64$ — observing $X_2$ always helps
  • Sanity: coupling $0$ — no update; coupling $\pm 1$ — variance $0$

What the General Case Needs

  • In $\mathbb{R}^n$: which matrices $\Sigma$ are legal covariances?
  • The density needs $\Sigma^{-1}$ and $\det \Sigma > 0$
  • The construction needs a square root of $\Sigma$

All three answers come from one fact: covariance matrices are positive semidefinite.

03

Positive Semidefinite Matrices

The shape of covariance

Definition — Definite & Semidefinite

Definition (PD / PSD).

Let $A$ be a real symmetric $n \times n$ matrix.

$$\begin{aligned} A > 0 \;\;(\text{positive definite}) \quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^\top A x > 0 \;\; \forall x \neq 0 \\[2pt] A \geq 0 \;\;(\text{positive semidefinite}) \quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^\top A x \geq 0 \;\; \forall x \end{aligned}$$

One number per direction: the quadratic form $x^\top A x$

Picture — Bowl vs. Trough

$A > 0$: bowl, closed contours $A$ singular: flat direction

Level sets of $x^\top A x$: a flat direction means some $x^\top A x = 0$

Worked — a PD Certificate

Claim: $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ is positive definite.

$$x^\top A x = 2x_1^2 + 2x_1 x_2 + 2x_2^2 = x_1^2 + x_2^2 + (x_1 + x_2)^2 \;>\; 0 \quad (x \neq 0)$$
  • Trick: rewrite as a sum of squares
  • This $A$ is today's matrix workbench

Facts from Linear Algebra

For real symmetric $A$ (stated, from linear algebra):

  • All $n$ eigenvalues are real
  • $A > 0$: all eigenvalues positive
  • $A \geq 0$: all eigenvalues nonnegative
  • Eigenvectors form an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$

Distinct eigenvalues give orthogonal eigenvectors; repeated ones can be chosen so

Spectral Decomposition

Every symmetric $A$ splits along its eigenbasis:

$$A = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i\, z_i z_i^\top, \qquad z_i^\top z_j = \begin{cases} 1 & i = j \\ 0 & i \neq j \end{cases}$$
  • A sum of rank-one pieces, one per direction
  • Functions of $A$: act on the $\lambda_i$, keep the $z_i$

Worked — Eigenpairs of the Workbench

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}: \qquad \lambda_1 = 3,\; z_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}; \qquad \lambda_2 = 1,\; z_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Check: $A z_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(3, 3)^\top = 3 z_1$  ✓
  • Check: $A z_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, -1)^\top = 1 \cdot z_2$  ✓
  • Both eigenvalues positive — PD, confirming the sum-of-squares certificate

The Symmetric Square Root

For $A \geq 0$, all $\lambda_i \geq 0$, so define

$$B = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sqrt{\lambda_i}\, z_i z_i^\top \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad B^2 = \sum_i \lambda_i z_i z_i^\top = A$$
  • Cross terms vanish: $z_i z_i^\top z_j z_j^\top = 0$ for $i \neq j$
  • Write $B = A^{1/2}$ — symmetric, and PSD itself

Worked — Square Root of the Workbench

$$A^{1/2} = \sqrt{3}\, z_1 z_1^\top + 1\cdot z_2 z_2^\top = \tfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3}+1 & \sqrt{3}-1 \\ \sqrt{3}-1 & \sqrt{3}+1 \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 1.366 & 0.366 \\ 0.366 & 1.366 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Check the top-left of $B^2$: $\big[(\sqrt{3}+1)^2 + (\sqrt{3}-1)^2\big]/4 = 8/4 = 2$  ✓
  • Off-diagonal: $2(\sqrt{3}+1)(\sqrt{3}-1)/4 = (2 \cdot 2)/4 = 1$  ✓

The Inverse, Same Recipe

For $A > 0$, all $\lambda_i > 0$, so define

$$A^{-1} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{\lambda_i}\, z_i z_i^\top \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad A^{-1} A = \sum_i z_i z_i^\top = I$$
  • Same directions — reciprocal weights
  • PD is exactly what makes the density's $\Sigma^{-1}$ exist

Exercise — Factorizing a PSD Matrix

Exercise.

For positive semidefinite $A$: show there exists a matrix $B$ with

$$A = B^\top B$$

Hint: you have just built a good candidate

Solution — the Root Works

$$B := A^{1/2} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad B^\top B \;\stackrel{(B \text{ symmetric})}{=}\; B\,B = A \qquad \blacksquare$$
  • Converse holds too: $x^\top B^\top B x = \lVert Bx \rVert^2 \geq 0$
  • So PSD $=$ “a squared matrix” — exactly like $\sigma^2 \geq 0$

Why This Buys a Density

  • Take $A > 0$ and the form $q(x) = x^\top A x$
  • $q(x) \to \infty$ as $\lVert x \rVert \to \infty$ — every direction climbs
  • So $e^{-q(x)} \to 0$: the tails die, the integral is finite

PD quadratic exponent $\Rightarrow$ a valid probability density — Definition 1, next.

Definition 1 — by Density

Definition 1 (density).

$X \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is (nondegenerate) multivariate normal if

$$f_X(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2} |V|^{1/2}} \exp\!\left( -\tfrac{1}{2}(x - \mu)^\top V^{-1} (x - \mu) \right)$$

for some $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and positive definite $V$.

The 2D formula, now for any $n$ — $|V|$ is $\det V$

Special Case — White Noise

Set $\mu = 0$ and $V = I$:

$$f_W(w) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}} e^{-\frac{1}{2} w^\top w} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-w_i^2/2}$$
  • Exponent splits — $n$ independent $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ coordinates
  • Write $W \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$: the raw material for everything

Definition 2 — by Construction

Definition 2 (constructive).

$X$ is multivariate normal if

$$X = DW + \mu,$$

with $W$ a vector of independent $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ variables and $D$ a real matrix.

  • Mix white noise linearly, then shift
  • No invertibility asked of $D$ — degenerate cases included

Picture — Coloring White Noise

$W$: white noise $D$ $DW$: stretched + tilted $+\,\mu$ $X = DW + \mu$

Every Gaussian: a linear image of white noise — how samplers draw it

Definition 3 — by Projection

Definition 3 (projection).

$X$ is multivariate normal if for every vector $a \in \mathbb{R}^n$,

$$a^\top X \;\text{ is univariate normal.}$$
  • Reduces every question to scalars — where the 1D toolkit applies
  • Every 1D shadow is a bell curve

Picture — Every Shadow Is Normal

$a^\top X$ axis $a$

Project the cloud onto any direction $a$ — the histogram is Gaussian

Degeneracy — When the PDF Dies

  • Definition 1 needs $V > 0$ — a real density
  • Singular $V$: all mass on a subspace, no pdf
  • Definitions 2 and 3 still valid
$$D = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}: \;\; X_2 = X_1,\;\; V = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
$x_2 = x_1$

$\det V = 0$: an ellipse squeezed flat into a line

Equivalence — What We Actually Prove

Def 2: $X = DW + \mu$ Def 1: density Def 3: projections proved: part 4 ($D$ invertible) proved: part 3 reverse directions: stated in the source, not proved today (same status)

Vector Means & Covariances

The covariance matrix

The Mean Vector

For a random vector $X = (X_1, \dots, X_n)^\top$:

$$\mathbb{E}[X] = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbb{E}[X_1] \\ \vdots \\ \mathbb{E}[X_n] \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Expectation acts coordinate-wise
  • Linearity survives: $\mathbb{E}[AX + b] = A\,\mathbb{E}[X] + b$

The Covariance Matrix

Definition (covariance).

For $X \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and $Y \in \mathbb{R}^m$:

$$\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) = \mathbb{E}\big[ (X - \mathbb{E}[X])(Y - \mathbb{E}[Y])^\top \big] \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$$
  • Entry $(i,j)$: plain scalar $\operatorname{Cov}(X_i, Y_j)$
  • An outer product averaged — all pairwise co-movements at once

The Self-Covariance $\operatorname{Cov}(X, X)$

  • Square, $n \times n$, and symmetric
  • Diagonal: the variances $\operatorname{Var}(X_i)$
  • Off-diagonal: couplings $\operatorname{Cov}(X_i, X_j)$
$$\operatorname{Cov}(X, X) = \begin{bmatrix} \operatorname{Var}(X_1) & \cdots & \operatorname{Cov}(X_1, X_n) \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \operatorname{Cov}(X_n, X_1) & \cdots & \operatorname{Var}(X_n) \end{bmatrix}$$

Exercise — Covariance Is PSD

Exercise.

Show that $\operatorname{Cov}(X, X)$ is nonnegative definite.

Hint: test the quadratic form with an arbitrary $a \in \mathbb{R}^n$

Solution — a Variance in Disguise

$$\begin{aligned} a^\top \operatorname{Cov}(X, X)\, a &\stackrel{(\text{def.})}{=} \mathbb{E}\big[ a^\top (X - \mathbb{E}X)(X - \mathbb{E}X)^\top a \big] \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(\text{scalar})}{=} \mathbb{E}\big[ (a^\top (X - \mathbb{E}X))^2 \big] = \operatorname{Var}(a^\top X) \;\geq\; 0 \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$
  • Every direction's variance sits inside the matrix
  • So legal covariances $=$ PSD matrices — the matrix detour paid off

Correlation Coefficient

$$\rho_{ij} = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_i, X_j)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X_i)\operatorname{Var}(X_j)}}, \qquad -1 \leq \rho_{ij} \leq 1$$
  • Normalized coupling — scale-free measure of linear dependence
  • Defined only when both variances are positive
  • $\rho_{ij} = 0$: the pair is uncorrelated
  • Running example $\Sigma$: $\rho_{12} = 0.6 / (1 \cdot 1) = 0.6$ ✓

The Properties Theorem

Seven parts: state one, prove it, move on

Standing Assumption — Definition 2

Throughout, we use Definition 2.

$$\underbrace{X}_{n \times 1} = \underbrace{D}_{n \times m}\,\underbrace{W}_{m \times 1} + \underbrace{\mu}_{n \times 1}$$

with independent standard-normal coordinates $W_1, \dots, W_m$ in $W$.

  • Shorthand $V := DD^\top$ — part 2 shows it is $\operatorname{Cov}(X, X)$
  • No invertibility of $D$ unless a part says so
  • Each part: statement first, then its proof

Part 1 — Every Projection Is Normal

Theorem 2, part 1.

For every $a \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$a^\top X \sim \mathcal{N}\big( a^\top \mu,\; \|D^\top a\|^2 \big); \qquad \text{in particular}\quad X_i \sim \mathcal{N}\Big( \mu_i,\; \sum_{j=1}^{m} d_{ij}^2 \Big)$$

The coordinate case is $a = e_i$; $d_{ij}$ is entry $(i,j)$ of $D$

Part 1 — Tool: Independent Normal Sums

Recall — MGF products and uniqueness:

$$\begin{aligned} U &\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_u, \sigma_u^2) \;\perp\; T \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_t, \sigma_t^2) \\[2pt] \Longrightarrow\quad aU + bT &\sim \mathcal{N}(a\mu_u + b\mu_t,\; a^2\sigma_u^2 + b^2\sigma_t^2) \end{aligned}$$
  • Independent sums: MGFs multiply; the product is again a Gaussian MGF
  • Extends to any finite sum by induction

Part 1 — Proof: Direct from Definition 2

$$\begin{aligned} X_i &= \mu_i + \sum_{j=1}^{m} d_{ij} W_j \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\Big( \mu_i,\; \sum_{j=1}^{m} d_{ij}^2 \Big) \\[4pt] a^\top X &= a^\top \mu + (D^\top a)^\top W \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\big( a^\top \mu,\; \|D^\top a\|^2 \big) \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

Trivial under Definition 2? Yes: one fact about independent sums.

  • Terms $d_{ij} W_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, d_{ij}^2)$ are independent; variances add
  • Every projection normal: Definition 2 $\Rightarrow$ Definition 3

Part 2 — Mean and Covariance

Theorem 2, part 2.

$$\mathbb{E}[X] = \mu, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}(X, X) = DD^\top = V$$

The shorthand $V$ is now earned: it is the covariance

Part 2 — Tool: $\mathbb{E}[WW^\top] = I_m$

$$\big( \mathbb{E}[WW^\top] \big)_{ij} = \mathbb{E}[W_i W_j] = \begin{cases} \operatorname{Var}(W_i) = 1 & i = j \\ \mathbb{E}[W_i]\,\mathbb{E}[W_j] = 0 & i \neq j \end{cases}$$
  • Diagonal: unit variances
  • Off-diagonal: independence factorizes the product

White noise has the identity as covariance — hence the name

Part 2 — Proof

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[X] &\stackrel{(\text{linearity})}{=} D\,\mathbb{E}[W] + \mu = \mu \\[2pt] \operatorname{Cov}(X, X) &\stackrel{(X - \mu = DW)}{=} \mathbb{E}[DWW^\top D^\top] \\[2pt] &\stackrel{(D \text{ fixed})}{=} D\, \mathbb{E}[WW^\top]\, D^\top = D D^\top \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$
  • Mean: $\mathbb{E}[W] = 0$ leaves only the shift
  • Constants slide out of expectations — on both sides

Part 3 — Linear Maps Stay Gaussian

Theorem 2, part 3.

For any $C \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times n}$ and $d \in \mathbb{R}^k$, $\;Y = CX + d$ satisfies Definition 2, with

$$\mathbb{E}[Y] = C\mu + d, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}(Y, Y) = C V C^\top$$

Closure: affine maps of Gaussians stay Gaussian

Part 3 — Proof

$$Y = CX + d \stackrel{(X = DW + \mu)}{=} \underbrace{(CD)}_{\text{new } D}\, W + \underbrace{(C\mu + d)}_{\text{new } \mu}$$
  • Still a linear map of white noise — Definition 2 again
  • Mean: $\mathbb{E}[Y] = C\mu + d$ — part 2 for the new $\mu$
  • Covariance: $(CD)(CD)^\top = C D D^\top C^\top = CVC^\top$ — part 2  $\blacksquare$

Marginals by Selection — Keep Coordinates 1, 4, 5

Example with $n = 5$: a $3 \times 5$ selector matrix $C$

$$CX = \begin{bmatrix} \textcolor{#003876}{\mathbf{1}}&0&0&0&0\\ 0&0&0&\textcolor{#003876}{\mathbf{1}}&0\\ 0&0&0&0&\textcolor{#003876}{\mathbf{1}} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \textcolor{#003876}{X_1}\\X_2\\X_3\\\textcolor{#003876}{X_4}\\\textcolor{#003876}{X_5} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \textcolor{#003876}{X_1}\\\textcolor{#003876}{X_4}\\\textcolor{#003876}{X_5} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad C\mu = \begin{bmatrix} \mu_1\\\mu_4\\\mu_5 \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Each row of $C$: a single $1$ at a kept index
  • Part 3 with $d = 0$: $(X_1, X_4, X_5)$ is multivariate normal

Marginal Covariance — the Selected Block

$V_{11}$ $V_{12}$ $V_{13}$ $V_{14}$ $V_{15}$ $V_{21}$ $V_{22}$ $V_{23}$ $V_{24}$ $V_{25}$ $V_{31}$ $V_{32}$ $V_{33}$ $V_{34}$ $V_{35}$ $V_{41}$ $V_{42}$ $V_{43}$ $V_{44}$ $V_{45}$ $V_{51}$ $V_{52}$ $V_{53}$ $V_{54}$ $V_{55}$ $V_{11}$ $V_{14}$ $V_{15}$ $V_{41}$ $V_{44}$ $V_{45}$ $V_{51}$ $V_{54}$ $V_{55}$ $1$ $2$ $3$ $4$ $5$ $V$: $5 \times 5$ $CVC^\top$: $3 \times 3$ keep rows & columns $1,4,5$

$CVC^\top$ keeps rows and columns $1, 4, 5$: marginal $=$ selected mean and block, no integral

Part 3 — What It Buys

  • Linear layers preserve Gaussianity — mean and covariance track exactly
  • Whitening (for $V \succ 0$): $V^{-1/2}(X - \mu) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ — undo the coloring map
  • Reparameterization trick: sample $\mathcal{N}(\mu, V)$ as $V^{1/2} W + \mu$

One family, closed under every affine map — a key strength of the Gaussian family.

Part 4 — Invertible $D$ Gives the Density

Theorem 2, part 4.

If $D$ is square ($m = n$) and invertible, then $V = DD^\top \succ 0$ and

$$f_X(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2} |V|^{1/2}} \exp\!\left( -\tfrac{1}{2}(x - \mu)^\top V^{-1} (x - \mu) \right)$$

Without change of variables? A Jacobian-free proof exists, but is long.

Here: why $|\det D|$ appears (volume), then substitute. Singular $D$: no density

Stretch the Volume, Spread the Density

$W$: density $f_W$ $X = 2W$: density $f_X$ $[0.5, 1]$: prob $0.150$ $[1, 2]$: prob $0.150$ $\times 2$ width

Width $\times 2$, same probability $\Rightarrow$ density $\div 2$:  $f_X(x) = \tfrac{1}{2} f_W(x/2)$

Square to Parallelogram — Area $\times |\det D|$

$$D = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{\text{shear}} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{\text{stretch}}, \qquad |\det D| = 2$$
$1$ $2$ $2$ stretch $\times 2$ shear area $1$ area $2$ area $2$ $(2,0)$ $(1,1)$

$n$ dims: volume multiplier $|\det D|$ (stated, not proved; $|\cdot|$ for reflections)

Mass Conservation — the Linear Density Rule

Tiny region of volume $\Delta v$ around $w$ → image around $x = Dw + \mu$:

$$\begin{aligned} \underbrace{f_W(w)\,\Delta v}_{\text{prob. near } w} &\approx \underbrace{f_X(x)\,|\det D|\,\Delta v}_{\text{prob. near } x} \\[2pt] \Longrightarrow\quad f_X(x) &= \frac{f_W\big( D^{-1}(x - \mu) \big)}{|\det D|} \end{aligned}$$
  • Same event, same probability; image volume $|\det D|\,\Delta v$
  • Square example: density divides by $2$

Assumed: the volume factor only — no general Jacobian theorem

Part 4 — Proof, Step 1: Substitute White Noise

Independent coordinates ($m = n$): the density of $W$ is a product

$$f_W(w) = \prod_{j=1}^{n} \frac{e^{-w_j^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}} = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}} \exp\!\left( -\tfrac{1}{2} \|w\|^2 \right)$$

Density rule with $w = D^{-1}(x - \mu)$:

$$f_X(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\, |\det D|} \exp\!\left( -\tfrac{1}{2} \big\| D^{-1}(x - \mu) \big\|^2 \right)$$

Proof (continued) — Massage the Exponent

Recap: the exponent is $-\tfrac12 \|D^{-1}(x - \mu)\|^2$.

$$\begin{aligned} \big\| D^{-1}(x - \mu) \big\|^2 &\stackrel{(\text{transpose})}{=} (x - \mu)^\top (D^{-1})^\top D^{-1} (x - \mu) \\[4pt] &\stackrel{((AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1})}{=} (x - \mu)^\top (D D^\top)^{-1} (x - \mu) \\[4pt] &\stackrel{\phantom{((AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1})}}{=} (x - \mu)^\top V^{-1} (x - \mu) \end{aligned}$$
  • The quadratic form organizes itself around $V = DD^\top$
  • By part 2 this $V$ is the covariance of $X$

Proof (continued) — Normalize

$$|\det D| = \big( \det D \cdot \det D^\top \big)^{1/2} = (\det DD^\top)^{1/2} = |V|^{1/2}$$
$$\Longrightarrow\quad f_X(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2} |V|^{1/2}} \exp\!\left( -\tfrac{1}{2} (x - \mu)^\top V^{-1} (x - \mu) \right) \qquad \blacksquare$$
  • Exactly the Definition-1 density with $V = DD^\top$
  • The determinant normalizes the volume stretch

Part 4 — Proof Summary

$$f_W \;\xrightarrow{\;w = D^{-1}(x - \mu),\;\; \div |\det D|\;}\; f_X \;\xrightarrow{\;(D^{-1})^\top D^{-1} = V^{-1},\;\; |\det D| = |V|^{1/2}\;}\; \text{Definition-1 form}$$
  • Proves the solid arrow Def 2 $\to$ Def 1, for square invertible $D$
  • Assumed: a linear map scales volume by $|\det D|$

Singular $V$: no density; Definitions 2 and 3 still apply

Part 5 — Mean and Covariance Determine the Law

Theorem 2, part 5.

For every $s \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$M_X(s) = \mathbb{E}\big[ e^{s^\top X} \big] = \exp\!\left( s^\top \mu + \tfrac{1}{2} s^\top V s \right)$$

Hence $(\mu, V)$ alone determines the distribution of $X$.

No density needed: singular $V$ included

Part 5 — Tool: MGF Recall

Recall, upgraded to vectors:

$$M_X(s) = \mathbb{E}\big[ e^{s^\top X} \big], \quad s \in \mathbb{R}^n \qquad\text{scalar Gaussian: } M(t) = e^{\mu t + \sigma^2 t^2 / 2}$$
  • Uniqueness (stated): the MGF pins down the distribution
  • Works with no density — degenerate case included

Part 5 — Proof

Fix $s$; part 1 with $a = s$:

$$\begin{aligned} s^\top X &\sim \mathcal{N}\big( s^\top \mu,\; \|D^\top s\|^2 \big) = \mathcal{N}\big( s^\top \mu,\; s^\top V s \big) \\[2pt] \Longrightarrow\quad M_X(s) = \mathbb{E}\big[ e^{s^\top X} \big] &\stackrel{(\text{scalar MGF at } t = 1)}{=} \exp\!\left( s^\top \mu + \tfrac{1}{2} s^\top V s \right) \end{aligned}$$
  • Variance: $\|D^\top s\|^2 = s^\top DD^\top s = s^\top V s$
  • MGF depends on $(\mu, V)$ only $\Rightarrow$ uniqueness closes it  $\blacksquare$

Part 5 — What It Buys

  • A Gaussian is its mean and covariance — nothing else to know
  • The notation $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, V)$ is now fully justified
  • Holds even when no density exists

Gaussians equal in distribution: match $\mu$, match $V$. Done. Used in part 6.

Part 6 — Zero Covariance Means Independence

Theorem 2, part 6 ($X = DW + \mu$ jointly Gaussian).

(a) Coordinates: $X_1, \dots, X_n$ independent $\iff$ $V$ diagonal

(b) Blocks: for a split $X = (X_A, X_B)$,  $X_A \perp X_B \iff \operatorname{Cov}(X_A, X_B) = 0$

  • $\perp$: independent; $V$ diagonal means pairwise uncorrelated
  • (b): coordinates inside a block need not be independent

Part 6 — Why Joint Gaussianity Matters

$$X \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1),\;\; S = \pm 1 \text{ fair coin} \perp X,\;\; Y = SX: \qquad \operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) = \mathbb{E}[S]\,\mathbb{E}[X^2] = 0$$
  • $Y \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ too, yet $|Y| = |X|$: dependent
  • $X + Y = 0$ with probability $\tfrac12$: not normal, so not jointly Gaussian
  • Correlation sees only linear dependence

Requires joint Gaussianity of the vector — not just Gaussian marginals

Part 6 — Proof, Easy Direction

Independent $\Rightarrow$ uncorrelated (any distribution):

$$\operatorname{Cov}(X_i, X_j) = \mathbb{E}[X_i X_j] - \mathbb{E}[X_i]\,\mathbb{E}[X_j] \stackrel{(\text{indep.})}{=} \mathbb{E}[X_i]\mathbb{E}[X_j] - \mathbb{E}[X_i]\mathbb{E}[X_j] = 0$$
  • Blocks: apply this to each entry of $\operatorname{Cov}(X_A, X_B)$
  • The content of part 6 is the converse

Proof (continued) — the Matching Trick

Assume uncorrelated: $V = \operatorname{diag}(V_{11}, \dots, V_{nn})$.

  • Build $Y_i = \mu_i + \sqrt{V_{ii}}\, W_i$: independent by construction
  • Definition 2 with $\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{V_{ii}})$: same $(\mu, V)$ as $X$
  • Part 5: same $(\mu, V)$ $\Rightarrow$ same distribution
$$X \stackrel{d}{=} Y,\;\; Y \text{ independent} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad X \text{ independent} \qquad \blacksquare$$

Proof (continued) — Blocks, Same Trick

Assume $\operatorname{Cov}(X_A, X_B) = 0$; $D_A, D_B$: the rows of $D$ for each block.

$$Y = \begin{bmatrix} D_A & 0 \\ 0 & D_B \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} W' \\ W'' \end{bmatrix} + \mu, \qquad W', W'' \text{ independent copies of } W$$
  • Definition 2; covariance blocks $V_{AA}$, $V_{BB}$, and $0$ off-diagonal
  • Part 5: $X \stackrel{d}{=} Y$, and $Y_A \perp Y_B$ $\Rightarrow$ $X_A \perp X_B$  $\blacksquare$

Singular blocks allowed: no density used

Part 6 — Optional Check: Factorize the Density

When $V$ is diagonal and invertible, the exponent splits:

$$f_X(x) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2} |V|^{1/2}} \exp\!\left( -\sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{(x_i - \mu_i)^2}{2 V_{ii}} \right) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} f_{X_i}(x_i)$$
  • Product density $=$ independence — direct confirmation
  • Needs a density; the matching proof does not

Part 6 — What It Buys

  • Independence testing becomes linear algebra: check off-diagonals
  • VAE encoders output diagonal covariance — latents independent by design
  • Part 7: residual and observation uncorrelated $\Rightarrow$ independent (block form)

For jointly Gaussian vectors: zero covariance kills all dependence, not just linear.

Part 7 — Proof Overview

Zero means throughout (shift back at the end). Target: $\mathbb{E}[X|Y]$ and the residual.

1Guessa linear estimate $\hat{X} = V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1} Y$
2Decorrelate$X - \hat{X}$ uncorrelated with $Y$ — hence independent
3Conclude$\hat{X} = \mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]$
4Extendparts (b), (c): residual independence and covariance

The trick: make the error orthogonal to the evidence

Part 7, Step 1 — the Guess

$$\hat{X} := V_{XY}\, V_{YY}^{-1}\, Y$$
  • Linear in $Y$ — the simplest candidate that could work
  • Shape check: $(n \times m)(m \times m)(m) = n$-vector ✓
  • 1D preview: $\hat{X} = \frac{\sigma_{12}}{\sigma_{22}} Y$ — Theorem 1's slope

Why linear can be enough: everything Gaussian stays Gaussian

Part 7, Step 2 — Error Orthogonal to Evidence

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\big[ (X - \hat{X})\, Y^\top \big] &\stackrel{(\text{split})}{=} \mathbb{E}[X Y^\top] - V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1}\, \mathbb{E}[Y Y^\top] \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{defs.})}{=} V_{XY} - V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1} V_{YY} \;=\; 0 \end{aligned}$$
  • The guess was engineered to make this cancel
  • So $X - \hat{X}$ and $Y$ are uncorrelated

Proof (continued) — Uncorrelated to Independent

Recap: $\mathbb{E}[(X - \hat{X})Y^\top] = 0$. Now upgrade it:

  • $(X - \hat{X},\, Y)$ is a linear map of $(X, Y)$ — jointly normal by part 3
  • Jointly normal + uncorrelated $\Rightarrow$ independent — part 6
$$X - \hat{X} \;\perp\!\!\!\perp\; Y$$

This is where the Gaussian assumption earns its keep

Part 7, Step 3 — Naming the Guess

For any square-integrable $g(Y)$, independence + zero mean give:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[ (X - \hat{X})\, g(Y) \big] = \mathbb{E}[X - \hat{X}]\; \mathbb{E}[g(Y)] = 0$$
  • This orthogonality property characterizes $\mathbb{E}[X|Y]$
  • $\hat{X}$ is a function of $Y$ passing the test for every $g$
$$\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbb{E}[X \mid Y] = V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1} Y \qquad \text{(a)} \;\;\blacksquare$$

Part 7(b) — the Residual Decouples

Define the residual $\tilde{X} = X - \mathbb{E}[X|Y] = X - \hat{X}$.

  • Step 2: $\tilde{X}$ independent of $Y$
  • $\mathbb{E}[X|Y]$: a function of $Y$ — so $\tilde{X}$ decouples from it too  $\blacksquare$
$$X = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[X|Y]}_{\text{explained by } Y} \;+\; \underbrace{\tilde{X}}_{\text{independent leftover}}$$

Signal plus noise — with genuine independence, not just orthogonality

Part 7(c) — Expand the Residual

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Cov}(\tilde{X}, \tilde{X}) &= \mathbb{E}\big[ (X - \hat{X})(X - \hat{X})^\top \big] \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(\text{expand})}{=} \mathbb{E}[XX^\top] - \mathbb{E}[X\hat{X}^\top] - \mathbb{E}[\hat{X}X^\top] + \mathbb{E}[\hat{X}\hat{X}^\top] \end{aligned}$$
  • Four terms — evaluate each with $\hat{X} = V_{XY}V_{YY}^{-1}Y$
  • First term: $V_{XX}$ by definition

Proof (continued) — All Three Match

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[X \hat{X}^\top] &= \mathbb{E}[X Y^\top]\, V_{YY}^{-1} V_{YX} = V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1} V_{YX} \\[3pt] \mathbb{E}[\hat{X}\hat{X}^\top] &= V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1}\, \mathbb{E}[YY^\top]\, V_{YY}^{-1} V_{YX} = V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1} V_{YX} \end{aligned}$$
$$\operatorname{Cov}(\tilde{X}, \tilde{X}) = V_{XX} - V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1} V_{YX} \qquad \text{(c)} \;\;\blacksquare$$

Minus one copy: two cross terms cancel one square term

Part 7 — Proof Summary

$$\begin{aligned} \hat{X} = V_{XY}V_{YY}^{-1}Y \;\;&\stackrel{(\text{engineered})}{\Longrightarrow}\;\; (X - \hat{X}) \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \\[2pt] \;\;&\stackrel{(\text{orthogonality})}{\Longrightarrow}\;\; \hat{X} = \mathbb{E}[X|Y] \;\;\stackrel{(\text{expand})}{\Longrightarrow}\;\; \text{(b), (c)} \end{aligned}$$
  • Weight carried by: part 6 (uncorrelated $\to$ independent)
  • Key trick: guess linear, verify orthogonal

The Conditional Distribution, Assembled

Restoring the means, part 7 packages as:

$$X \mid Y = y \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\Big( \mu_X + V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1} (y - \mu_Y),\;\; V_{XX} - V_{XY} V_{YY}^{-1} V_{YX} \Big)$$
  • Mean: linear in the observation $y$
  • Covariance: constant — the data's value never changes your uncertainty
  • Promise delivered: the MMSE estimator is linear

Theorem 1, Finally Proved

Set $X = X_1$ and $Y = X_2$: every block is a scalar.

$$X_1 \mid X_2 = x_2 \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\left( \mu_1 + \frac{\sigma_{12}}{\sigma_{22}}(x_2 - \mu_2),\;\; \sigma_{11} - \frac{\sigma_{12}\sigma_{21}}{\sigma_{22}} \right)$$
  • $V_{XY}V_{YY}^{-1} \to \sigma_{12}/\sigma_{22}$ — the slicing slope of the 2D warm-up
  • Running example: mean $0.6$, variance $0.64$ — matches the worked slide ✓ $\blacksquare$

The Gaussian Channel

One observation through noise — part 7 in action

Setup — Signal Plus Noise

$$Y = X + Z, \qquad X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_x^2), \quad Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_z^2), \quad X \perp\!\!\!\perp Z$$
signal $X$ $+$ observe $Y$ noise $Z$
  • Goal: the posterior $X \mid Y = y$ — in closed form

Step 1 — the Joint Covariance

$(X, Y)$ is a linear map of $(X, Z)$ — jointly Gaussian by part 3.

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(Y) &\stackrel{(\text{indep.})}{=} \sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2 \\[3pt] \operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) &= \mathbb{E}[X(X+Z)] \stackrel{(X \perp Z)}{=} \mathbb{E}[X^2] = \sigma_x^2 \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{pmatrix} X \\ Y \end{pmatrix} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left( 0,\; \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_x^2 & \sigma_x^2 \\ \sigma_x^2 & \sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2 \end{pmatrix} \right)$$

Step 2 — Read Off Part 7

Substitute $V_{XY} = \sigma_x^2$, $V_{YY} = \sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2$:

$$X \mid Y = y \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\left( \frac{\sigma_x^2}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2}\, y,\;\; \frac{\sigma_x^2 \sigma_z^2}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2} \right)$$
  • Posterior mean: $y$ shrunk toward the prior mean $0$
  • Posterior variance: harmonic-mean form — smaller than both $\sigma_x^2$ and $\sigma_z^2$

The Promise, Kept

  • MMSE estimator $=$ conditional mean $\mathbb{E}[X|Y]$
  • The Gaussian case is linear — asserted before, now a corollary

Worked example: $\sigma_x^2 = 1$, $\sigma_z^2 = 1/3$:

$$\hat{X} = \frac{1}{1 + 1/3}\, y = \frac{3}{4}\, y, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X \mid Y) = \frac{1 \cdot 1/3}{4/3} = \frac{1}{4}$$

Clean noise (small $\sigma_z^2$) $\to$ trust the observation more

Picture — Shrinkage

identity: trust $y$ fully slope $3/4$: shrink to prior prior mean $0$ $y$ estimate

The noisier the channel, the flatter the line — slope $\sigma_x^2 / (\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2)$

Sanity Checks — the Two Extremes

RegimePosterior meanPosterior varianceReading

  • This Gaussian-channel update interpolates between these poles

Two Ways to Sample the Same Pair

Forward (causal)

$X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_x^2)$
$Y = X + Z$, $\;Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_z^2)$

Observation-first

$Y \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2)$
$X = \dfrac{\sigma_x^2}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2} Y + \sqrt{\dfrac{\sigma_x^2 \sigma_z^2}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_z^2}}\; W$

  • $W \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ fresh, independent of $Y$ — legal by part 7(b)
  • Same joint distribution — both give $\mathcal{N}(0, V)$ with $V_{XY} = \sigma_x^2$

Why the Second Ordering Matters

Sampling the hidden $X$: MMSE estimate $+$ fresh Gaussian noise at the posterior variance.

  • Estimation and generation: the same formula, two uses
  • The template behind learned reverse diffusion

Gaussian Diffusion

Noise a data point until nothing remains — in closed form

The Diffusion Teaser, Cashed In

  • Markov chains: stationary distributions, the diffusion teaser
  • That setting: discrete states, transition matrices
  • Today: continuous state, Gaussian transition kernel
$$X_0 \sim f_0 \;\;\longrightarrow\;\; X_1 \;\;\longrightarrow\;\; X_2 \;\;\longrightarrow\;\; \cdots \;\;\longrightarrow\;\; \text{pure noise?}$$

The same question: where does the chain settle?

The Noising Recursion

$$X_n = \sqrt{1-\beta}\; X_{n-1} + \sqrt{\beta}\; Z_n, \qquad Z_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \text{ i.i.d.}, \quad \beta \in (0,1)$$
  • Shrink the current point, add fresh Gaussian noise
  • One-step conditional, by part 3:
$$X_n \mid X_{n-1} = x \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\big( \sqrt{1-\beta}\, x,\; \beta \big)$$

Why Those Coefficients — Variance Preserving

Suppose $\mathbb{E}[X_{n-1}] = 0$, $\operatorname{Var}(X_{n-1}) = 1$:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[X_n] &= \sqrt{1-\beta}\cdot 0 + \sqrt{\beta}\cdot 0 = 0 \\[3pt] \operatorname{Var}(X_n) &\stackrel{(\text{indep.})}{=} (1-\beta)\cdot 1 + \beta \cdot 1 = 1 \end{aligned}$$
  • $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ passes through unchanged — a fixed point
  • Suggests: everything else flows toward $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$

Picture — the Noising Chain

data $x_0$ $x_{25}$ $x_{75}$ $x_{200}$: pure noise
  • Structure dissolves; the shape rounds toward the standard Gaussian

Exercise (from the source)

Exercise.

What is the limiting distribution of $X_n$ as $n \to \infty$? Does it depend on $f_0$?

  • Intuition says $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ — the fixed point
  • Tool that settles it: the MGF telescope, next

Technique — MGFs Under Affine Maps

Recall (and part 5):

$$\begin{aligned} M_{aX}(t) &= M_X(at) \\[3pt] M_{U+V}(t) &\stackrel{(U \perp V)}{=} M_U(t)\, M_V(t) \\[3pt] Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\!: \;\; M_Z(t) &= e^{t^2/2} \end{aligned}$$
  • Convergence of MGFs $\Rightarrow$ convergence in distribution (stated)

Telescope, Step 1 — One Recursion

$$\begin{aligned} M_{X_n}(t) &= \mathbb{E}\big[ e^{t(\sqrt{1-\beta}X_{n-1} + \sqrt{\beta}Z_n)} \big] \\[3pt] &\stackrel{(\text{indep.})}{=} M_{X_{n-1}}\!\big( \sqrt{1-\beta}\, t \big) \cdot M_{Z_n}\!\big( \sqrt{\beta}\, t \big) \\[3pt] &\stackrel{(\text{std. normal})}{=} M_{X_{n-1}}\!\big( \sqrt{1-\beta}\, t \big) \cdot e^{\beta t^2 / 2} \end{aligned}$$
  • Each step: compress the argument, multiply in a Gaussian factor

Telescope, Step 2 — Iterate Down to $X_0$

Apply twice, collect the exponents (geometric sum):

$$\begin{aligned} M_{X_n}(t) &= M_{X_{n-2}}\big( (1-\beta)\, t \big) \cdot \exp\!\Big( \tfrac{t^2}{2} \big( 1 - (1-\beta)^2 \big) \Big) \\[4pt] &\;\;\vdots \\[2pt] &= M_{X_0}\!\big( (1-\beta)^{n/2}\, t \big) \cdot \exp\!\Big( \tfrac{t^2}{2} \big( 1 - (1-\beta)^n \big) \Big) \end{aligned}$$

Source's two-step exponent had a typo ($1-(1-\beta)$); the geometric sum gives $1-(1-\beta)^2$

Theorem 3 — the Chain Forgets

Theorem 3.

For any $X_0$ with MGF finite near $0$: $\;X_n \xrightarrow{\;d\;} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ as $n \to \infty$.

$$\begin{aligned} (1-\beta)^{n/2} t \to 0 \;\;&\Longrightarrow\;\; M_{X_0}\big((1-\beta)^{n/2} t\big) \to M_{X_0}(0) = 1 \\[4pt] &\Longrightarrow\;\; M_{X_n}(t) \to e^{t^2/2} \end{aligned}$$
  • Limit MGF is standard normal's — MGF convergence closes it  $\blacksquare$
  • Any such $f_0$: the data distribution is forgotten geometrically fast

The Real Prize — a Closed Form for Every $n$

Condition on $X_0 = x_0$: the telescope's $M_{X_0}$ factor becomes $e^{(1-\beta)^{n/2} x_0 t}$, so

$$X_n \mid X_0 = x_0 \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\Big( (1-\beta)^{n/2}\, x_0,\;\; 1 - (1-\beta)^n \Big)$$
  • Jump to any noise level in one sample — no $n$-step simulation
  • Signal decays as $(1-\beta)^{n/2}$; noise fills in exactly the lost variance

DDPM Notation — the Same Formula

Diffusion papers write $\bar\alpha_n := (1-\beta)^n$:

$$q(x_n \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\!\big( \sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\, x_0,\;\; 1 - \bar\alpha_n \big) \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x_n = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_n}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_n}\; \epsilon$$
  • $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ — the "one-shot sampling" trick in every DDPM codebase
  • Our derivation is the DDPM forward process (constant schedule)

Worked Schedule — $\beta = 0.02$

$0$ $n = 100$ $200$ $0$ $1$ signal variance fraction $(1-\beta)^n$ noise variance $1-(1-\beta)^n$

At $n = 100$: variance fraction $0.133$, mean coefficient $0.364$ — the data is mostly gone

Reversing One Step — the Source's Claim

  • Generation needs the reverse kernel $X_{n-1} \mid X_n$
  • Source claims: for small $\beta$, the reverse step is approximately Gaussian
  • Unproved in the source — plausible via the Gaussian channel
$$X_{n-1} \mid X_n = x_n \;\approx\; \mathcal{N}(\mu(x_n),\; \tilde\sigma^2) \quad \text{for small } \beta$$

Reverse Step via the Gaussian Channel

  • Late in the chain: $X_{n-1} \approx \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ — Theorem 3
  • Then noising is a Gaussian channel: signal $\sqrt{1-\beta}X_{n-1}$, noise $\beta$. Part 7:
$$X_{n-1} \mid X_n = x_n \;\approx\; \mathcal{N}\!\big( \sqrt{1-\beta}\; x_n,\;\; \beta \big)$$
  • Mean $\sqrt{1-\beta}\, x_n / \big( (1-\beta) + \beta \big) = \sqrt{1-\beta}\, x_n$; variance $1 - (1-\beta) = \beta$ exactly
  • Approximation: the stationary prior $X_{n-1} \approx \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, not the algebra

Vectors — Nothing Changes

$$X_n = \sqrt{1-\beta}\; X_{n-1} + \sqrt{\beta}\; Z_n, \qquad Z_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d) \;\; \text{in } \mathbb{R}^d$$
  • Given $X_0 = x_0$, coordinates evolve independently — part 6 with diagonal covariance
  • With random $X_0$: marginal dependence attenuates, may persist at finite $n$
  • Limit: $\mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$; closed form: $\mathcal{N}\big((1-\beta)^{n/2} x_0,\, (1-(1-\beta)^n) I_d\big)$
  • An image is just $d = 3 \times 256 \times 256$ coordinates

  • 3. Output $x_0$ — a fresh draw from (approximately) $f_0$

Gaussian Discriminant Analysis

Fit one Gaussian per class — and the trace trick that fits it

Setup — One Gaussian per Class

  • Classifying heights — the 1D version of today
  • Now features $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, classes $y \in \{0, 1\}$
$$X \mid Y = y \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}(\mu_y,\, \Sigma_y), \qquad \mathbb{P}(Y = y) = \pi_y$$
  • A generative classifier: model how each class produces data

The Decision Rule

Classify a new $x$ by the larger posterior (Bayes):

$$\hat{y}(x) = \arg\max_{y}\; \pi_y\, f_{X|Y=y}(x) = \arg\max_{y}\; \pi_y \cdot \frac{\exp\!\big( -\tfrac{1}{2}(x - \mu_y)^\top \Sigma_y^{-1} (x - \mu_y) \big)}{(2\pi)^{n/2} |\Sigma_y|^{1/2}}$$
  • Definition 1's density, twice — compare and pick
  • Equal priors and equal $|\Sigma_y|$: pick the class whose Mahalanobis distance is smaller

Picture — Two Classes, One Boundary

class $0$ class $1$ boundary

Different $\Sigma_y$ — the boundary curves (quadratic); equal $\Sigma$ would make it a line

Unknown Parameters — Back to MLE

  • Reality: $\mu_y, \Sigma_y, \pi_y$ are not given — estimate from data
  • Maximum likelihood — maximize $\log$-probability of the sample
  • Per class, the problem reduces to: fit one Gaussian to $N$ i.i.d. points
$$x^{(1)}, \ldots, x^{(N)} \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma) \qquad \text{find} \;\; \hat\mu, \hat\Sigma$$

The Log-Likelihood

$$\ell(\mu, \Sigma) = -\frac{Nn}{2}\log 2\pi \;-\; \frac{N}{2}\log|\Sigma| \;-\; \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N} (x^{(i)} - \mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (x^{(i)} - \mu)$$
  • Sum of $N$ copies of $\log$ Definition 1
  • Two unknowns: a vector $\mu$ and a matrix $\Sigma$ — differentiate in turn

Fit $\mu$ — the Easy Half

$$\begin{aligned} \nabla_\mu\, \ell &\stackrel{(\text{quadratic form})}{=} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \Sigma^{-1}(x^{(i)} - \mu) \;=\; \Sigma^{-1}\!\left( \sum_{i=1}^{N} x^{(i)} - N\mu \right) \\[4pt] \nabla_\mu\, \ell = 0 \;\;&\Longrightarrow\;\; \hat\mu = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} x^{(i)} \end{aligned}$$
  • The sample mean — regardless of $\Sigma$ (since $\Sigma^{-1}$ is invertible)

Fit $\Sigma$ — Reparametrize First

Differentiating in $\Sigma^{-1}$ is easier: set the precision $\Phi := \Sigma^{-1}$, use $|\Sigma| = |\Phi|^{-1}$:

$$\ell(\mu, \Phi) = -\frac{Nn}{2}\log 2\pi \;+\; \frac{N}{2}\log|\Phi| \;-\; \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N} (x^{(i)} - \mu)^\top \Phi\, (x^{(i)} - \mu)$$
  • $\log|\Sigma|$ flipped sign — now everything is increasing-friendly in $\Phi$
  • Next obstacle: the sum of quadratic forms — enter the trace trick

Technique — the Trace Trick

A scalar equals its own trace; trace is cyclic:

$$v^\top \Phi\, v \stackrel{(1\times 1)}{=} \operatorname{tr}\!\big( v^\top \Phi\, v \big) \stackrel{(\text{cyclic})}{=} \operatorname{tr}\!\big( v\, v^\top \Phi \big)$$
  • Turns a quadratic form into a linear function of $\Phi$
  • Linear in $\Phi$ $\Rightarrow$ trivial to differentiate

Collect the Data — the Scatter Matrix

$$\begin{gathered} S := \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{N} (x^{(i)} - \mu)(x^{(i)} - \mu)^\top \\[6pt] \Longrightarrow\quad \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{N} (x^{(i)} - \mu)^\top \Phi (x^{(i)} - \mu) = \operatorname{tr}(S\, \Phi) \end{gathered}$$
$$\ell(\mu, \Phi) = \text{const} \;+\; \frac{N}{2}\log|\Phi| \;-\; \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(S\, \Phi)$$
  • All $N$ data points compressed into one PSD matrix $S$

Technique — Two Matrix Derivatives

$$\begin{aligned} \nabla_X \operatorname{tr}(A X) &= A^\top &&\text{(trace is linear; exercise below)} \\[4pt] \nabla_X \log|X| &= X^{-\top} &&\text{(the matrix log-derivative; exercise below)} \end{aligned}$$
  • Both are the source's exercises — hints on the exercise slide
  • Sanity, $1\times 1$ case: $\frac{d}{dx}(ax) = a$, $\;\frac{d}{dx}\log x = 1/x$ ✓

Fit $\Sigma$ — Solve

$$\begin{aligned} \nabla_\Phi\, \ell &\stackrel{(\text{two identities})}{=} \frac{N}{2}\, \Phi^{-\top} - \frac{1}{2} S^\top \;\stackrel{(\text{symmetry})}{=}\; \frac{N}{2}\, \Sigma - \frac{1}{2} S \\[4pt] \nabla_\Phi\, \ell = 0 \;\;&\Longrightarrow\;\; \hat\Sigma = \frac{S}{N} \end{aligned}$$
  • $\Phi^{-\top} = \Sigma^\top = \Sigma$ and $S^\top = S$: both symmetric

Theorem 4 — Gaussian MLE

Theorem 4 (Gaussian MLE).

For i.i.d. $x^{(1)}, \ldots, x^{(N)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$:

$$\hat\mu = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i} x^{(i)}, \qquad \hat\Sigma = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i} (x^{(i)} - \hat\mu)(x^{(i)} - \hat\mu)^\top$$
  • Sample mean, sample covariance — the intuitive answers, now derived
  • Remark: $\hat\Sigma$ is biased; $1/(N-1)$ gives the unbiased version
  • Needs the centered scatter nonsingular — typically $N > n$ with full-rank data

GDA — the Full Pipeline

  • Fit: per class $y$, compute $\hat\mu_y$, $\hat\Sigma_y$ (Theorem 4) and $\hat\pi_y = N_y / N$
  • Predict: plug into the decision rule — largest $\hat\pi_y\, f_{\mathcal{N}(\hat\mu_y, \hat\Sigma_y)}(x)$
  • Closed form — no gradient descent, no tuning — if scatters are nonsingular
  • High-dimensional GDA needs covariance regularization
