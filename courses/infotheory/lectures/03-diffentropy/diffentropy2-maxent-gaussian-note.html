Differential Entropy 2 — Companion Notes

A Design Question — Motivating MaxEnt

Before any Lagrangian machinery, MaxEnt answers a concrete engineering question: given only a mean and a variance, which single density do you commit to? Picking $\mathcal{N}$, a bimodal mix, or a heavy tail each asserts structure you never measured. Choosing a density with higher entropy than the MaxEnt one is impossible without violating a constraint; choosing lower entropy silently injects an unmeasured assumption. The honest default maximizes uncertainty subject to exactly what is known — this is the operational content of Jaynes' principle (see below).

Lagrangian — Why Variational Calculus Works

Strictly, the variational derivative argument is informal: $f$ ranges over an infinite-dimensional space. The standard rigorous approach: the MaxEnt problem is convex (negative entropy is convex, constraints are linear), so KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient. The exponential-family form is the unique stationary point.

For non-compact support (e.g., $\mathbb{R}$), one must verify integrability. For variance-constrained MaxEnt on $\mathbb{R}$, the answer (Gaussian) is integrable; for unconstrained MaxEnt on $\mathbb{R}$, no finite-entropy maximizer exists.

Intuition — Why the Cross Term Vanishes

The clean reason the Gaussian wins, distinct from the formal KL algebra: the Gaussian's log-density is quadratic, $\log g(x) = c - \tfrac{x^2}{2P}\log e$. Cross-entropy $-\int f\log g$ is therefore an affine function of $\mathbb{E}_f[X^2]$ alone — it reads off nothing about $f$ beyond the second moment. Under the constraint $\mathbb{E}_f[X^2]\le P$, that term is pinned at the same ceiling for every admissible $f$. The only quantity still free to vary in $D(f\|g) = -h(f) - \int f\log g$ is $-h(f)$; since $D\ge 0$, $h(f)$ is maximized exactly when $f=g$. Geometrically: matching a Gaussian's log-density kills every discriminating term except entropy itself. This is the special case of the general KL trick below where $T_1 = x$, $T_2 = x^2$.

Why Gaussian for Variance — The KL Trick Generalized

The KL-based proof generalizes to any exponential-family MaxEnt: if the constraints are $\mathbb{E}[T_k(X)] = \alpha_k$ and the candidate density is $g \propto \exp(-\sum_k\lambda_k T_k(x))$, then for any $f$ with the same moments,

$$D(f\|g) = -h(f) - \int f\log g = -h(f) + \log Z + \sum_k \lambda_k \alpha_k = -h(f) + h(g),$$

using $\log g = -\log Z - \sum \lambda_k T_k$ and $\mathbb{E}_f[T_k] = \alpha_k = \mathbb{E}_g[T_k]$. Hence $h(f)\le h(g)$ for all $f$ matching the moments, with equality iff $f = g$.

Hadamard's Inequality — Direct vs Information-Theoretic Proofs

The direct proof uses Cholesky / Schur complements: write $K = LL^\top$ for lower-triangular $L$, then $\det K = \prod L_{ii}^2 \le \prod K_{ii}$ since $L_{ii}^2 \le K_{ii}$ (column $i$ of $L$ has Euclidean norm $\le$ that of $K$'s column $i$).

The information-theoretic proof on the slide is cleaner: $h(\mathbf{X})\le \sum_i h(X_i)$ for any random vector, with equality iff independent. Plugging in the Gaussian formula yields the inequality with equality iff $K$ is diagonal.

Hadamard — Correlation as Wasted Uncertainty

Interpretation of the corollary for covariance matrices: with the diagonal $K_{ii}$ (marginal variances) fixed, $\det K$ is maximized precisely at $K$ diagonal, i.e. at independence. Since $\det K$ is (up to the constant $(2\pi e)^d$) the entropy power / squared volume of the Gaussian, correlation always shrinks that volume. Off-diagonal structure is uncertainty-reducing: knowing one coordinate informs another, so the joint distribution occupies less effective volume than the product of its marginals would. The MaxEnt distribution under fixed marginal variances spends none of this structure — it is the independent (diagonal-$K$) Gaussian. Equivalently $h(\mathbf{X})\le\sum_i h(X_i)$ with equality iff independent, which is exactly the information-theoretic proof on the slide.

Conditioning Gaussians — Schur Complement

For $(X, Y)$ jointly Gaussian with covariance $K = \begin{pmatrix} K_{XX} & K_{XY} \\ K_{XY}^\top & K_{YY} \end{pmatrix}$:

  • Conditional mean: $\mu_{X\mid Y=y} = K_{XY} K_{YY}^{-1} y$.
  • Conditional covariance: $K_{X\mid Y} = K_{XX} - K_{XY} K_{YY}^{-1} K_{XY}^\top$ (Schur complement).

The conditional density is again Gaussian — a key fact used throughout signal processing, Kalman filtering, and Bayesian inference.

From this:

$$\det K = \det K_{YY} \cdot \det(K_{XX} - K_{XY} K_{YY}^{-1} K_{XY}^\top).$$

Hence $I(X;Y) = h(X) + h(Y) - h(X,Y) = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\bigl(\det K_{XX}\det K_{YY} / \det K\bigr)$.

Why Gaussian Noise Is Worst — Motivating EPI

Before the formal EPI statement, the framing question: across all additive noises $Z$ of a fixed power, which degrades a channel most? Structured noise (bimodal, bursty, low-entropy) is partly predictable, and a good code exploits that predictability to recover rate. Gaussian noise is the maximum-entropy noise for its power — featureless, nothing to exploit. So intuitively Gaussian is the worst-case additive noise at fixed power. EPI is the inequality that turns this intuition into a proof: it lower-bounds $h(Y)=h(X+Z)$ via $N(X+Z)\ge N(X)+N(Z)$ even when $Z$ is non-Gaussian, which is precisely the ingredient the AWGN capacity converse needs.

Intuition — Entropy Powers Add Sub-Additively

Read $N(\mathbf{X})$ as the "effective spread" of $\mathbf{X}$ (the variance of the isotropic Gaussian with the same entropy). Convolution smooths: $\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{Y}$ is flatter/de-peaked relative to either summand, so its entropy power grows. EPI says the growth is at least as fast as variances add: $N(\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{Y})\ge N(\mathbf{X})+N(\mathbf{Y})$. The equality case (independent Gaussians with proportional covariances) is the most efficient de-peaking — mixing two independent Gaussians spreads mass exactly like adding two variances. Any non-Gaussian pair "wastes" some of the smoothing budget: the sum de-peaks less efficiently than the optimal Gaussian would, so the inequality is strict. This is the additive-noise mirror of the MaxEnt statement: Gaussians are extremal both under a variance constraint and under convolution.

EPI — Proof Sketch (Stam)

Define $X_t = X + \sqrt{t}Z$ where $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. By de Bruijn,

$$\frac{d}{dt}h(X_t) = \tfrac{1}{2}J(X_t),$$

where $J$ is Fisher information. Stam proved that the entropy power $N(X)$ along the heat semigroup satisfies $\frac{d}{dt}N(X_t) = N(X_t) J(X_t)/d$.

The EPI then reduces to an inequality between Fisher informations of independent sums — the Fisher information form: $1/J(X+Y)\ge 1/J(X) + 1/J(Y)$ (Stam, 1959).

Modern proofs (Verdú & Guo, 2006) use the I-MMSE relation directly.

Why EPI Implies Gaussian-Hardest-Channel

For AWGN $Y = X + Z$ with $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$, EPI gives

$$N(Y) = N(X + Z) \ge N(X) + N(Z) = N(X) + \sigma^2.$$

So $h(Y) \ge \tfrac{d}{2}\log(2\pi e (N(X) + \sigma^2))$. Combined with $h(Y\mid X) = h(Z) = \tfrac{d}{2}\log(2\pi e\sigma^2)$:

$$I(X;Y) = h(Y) - h(Y\mid X) \ge \tfrac{d}{2}\log\!\left(1 + \tfrac{N(X)}{\sigma^2}\right).$$

Since $N(X) \le \mathrm{Var}(X)/d$ for any $X$ with given variance (actually $N(X)\le \mathbb{E}[\|X-\mathbb{E}X\|^2]/d$), the Gaussian saturates this bound.

Application — Vector Gaussian Channel Capacity

For MIMO AWGN $\mathbf{Y} = H\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{Z}$ with $\mathbf{Z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ and power constraint $\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{X}))\le P$:

$$C = \max_{Q:\,\mathrm{tr}(Q)\le P} \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det\!\left(I + \tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}HQH^\top\right).$$

Solved by SVD: $H = U\Sigma V^\top$, then optimal $Q = V\,\mathrm{diag}(P_1,\dots)\,V^\top$ with water-filling on the singular values $\sigma_i$.

This is the basis of all modern wireless communication.
