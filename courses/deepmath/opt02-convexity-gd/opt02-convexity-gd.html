01

One Update

Step against the gradient

Goal: Minimize One Differentiable $f$

$$\text{minimize } f(x) \text{ over } x \in \mathbb{R}^n, \qquad f:\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R} \text{ differentiable}$$
local min local max $f_{\text{low}}$ tail tends to $f_{\text{low}}$, never reaches it

Deterministic: the exact gradient $\nabla f(x)$ at every step

Nonconvex case: no minimizer assumed, only a lower bound

Schematic 1-D slice of one nonconvex $f$ (our illustration; formula in the note)

Recall: The Gradient Gives First-Order Change

$$\begin{aligned} \nabla f(x) &= \Big(\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}(x),\, \dots,\, \tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_n}(x)\Big) \in \mathbb{R}^n\\[6px] f(x + h) &= f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), h\rangle + o(\|h\|) \quad \text{as } h \to 0 \end{aligned}$$

Rate of change along a direction $v$:  $\langle \nabla f(x), v\rangle$

Local statement: it says nothing about one fixed, finite step

Small Steps Against the Gradient Go Downhill

long step (dashed): $f$ rises $x$ with $f'(x) \gt 0$ short step (solid): $f$ drops

Move along $-\nabla f(x)$: downhill for a small enough step

How far is safe? The smoothness constant $L$ decides

Illustration: $f(u)=u^2$ from $u=1.5$, steps to $u=0.9$ and $u=-1.8$ (our calculation)

$L$-Smoothness Bounds Gradient Change

Definition.

Differentiable $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ is $L$-smooth ($L \gt 0$) if for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$\|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\| \;\leq\; L\, \|x - y\| \qquad (\text{Euclidean norm})$$

A condition on the gradient, not on $f$ itself

Example: $f(x)=\tfrac12 x^2$ is $1$-smooth, yet $f$ is not Lipschitz

No convexity: smooth does not mean convex

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Lipschitz continuous gradient assumption”

One Fixed Step: $1/L$

Gradient descent, step $1/L$

  1. Input: smoothness constant $L$, any start $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$
  2. For $t = 0, 1, \dots, T-1$:  $x_{t+1} = x_t - \tfrac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_t)$
  3. Output: iterates $x_0, x_1, \dots, x_T$

The same update in all three guarantees

$L$ assumed known; no line search, no other step size

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD” (step $\alpha \le 1/L$; here $\alpha = 1/L$)

One Descent Inequality

An upper model and one square

Smoothness Gives a Quadratic Upper Bound

Lemma (descent lemma).

$f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ differentiable and $L$-smooth. For all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$f(y) \;\leq\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x),\, y - x\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|y - x\|^2$$

Linear model plus $\tfrac{L}{2}\|y-x\|^2$: valid for every finite step

A standard lemma, used here as a tool; the pictures illustrate it

No convexity needed; optional proof in the notes

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Descent lemma”

$x$ $f$ tangent at $x$

The tangent gives the local slope, not a safe upper bound

Smoothness Adds an Upper Model

$x$ $f$ tangent at $x$ upper model $Q_x$

$Q_x$ touches $f$ at $x$ and stays above it everywhere

Our drawing: $f(u)=u^2$, $x=-0.6$, $L=3$ ($f$ is $2$-smooth, so $L=3$ is valid)

Minimize the Model, Then Step

$x$ $x - g/L$ min of $f$ $f$ tangent at $x$ upper model $Q_x$ gap: $f \le Q_x$

Step to the model's minimizer, not to $f$'s minimizer

Our drawing: $f(u)=u^2$, $x=-0.6$, $L=3$; step to $-0.2$ (figs/gd_model_build.py)

Minimize a Safe Upper Model

Fix the base point $x$ and write $g = \nabla f(x)$. For every candidate $y$:

$$f(y) \;\leq\; Q_x(y) \;=\; f(x) + \langle g,\, y - x\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|y - x\|^2$$

$Q_x$ is a simple quadratic in $y$; $f$ may be anything smooth

Choose the next point by minimizing $Q_x$, not $f$

Inequality: descent lemma, Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5; model view: our exposition

Completing the Square Gives GD

$$\begin{aligned} Q_x(y) &= f(x) + \frac{L}{2}\Big(\|y - x\|^2 + \frac{2}{L}\langle g,\, y - x\rangle\Big)\\[4px] &= f(x) - \frac{1}{2L}\|g\|^2 + \frac{L}{2}\Big\|y - \Big(x - \frac{1}{L}g\Big)\Big\|^2 \end{aligned}$$

The square vanishes only at $y = x - \tfrac{1}{L}g$: the GD step

GD step = minimizer of $Q_x$, with value $f(x) - \tfrac{1}{2L}\|g\|^2$

Only $\|a + b\|^2 = \|a\|^2 + 2\langle a, b\rangle + \|b\|^2$; no integral, no Hessian

Our algebra on the descent lemma (Cornell CS4787/5777, Lecture 5)

The Step $1/L$ Decreases $f$

Lemma at $x = x_t$, $y = x_{t+1} = x_t - \tfrac{1}{L} g_t$ (the model minimizer), $g_t = \nabla f(x_t)$:

$$\begin{aligned} f(x_{t+1}) &\leq f(x_t) + \big\langle g_t,\, -\tfrac{1}{L} g_t\big\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\Big\|\tfrac{1}{L} g_t\Big\|^2\\[4px] &= f(x_t) - \frac{1}{L}\|g_t\|^2 + \frac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2 \;=\; f(x_t) - \frac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2 \end{aligned}$$

The right side is the minimum value of $Q_{x_t}$

No convexity: $f$ never increases; strict drop whenever $g_t \neq 0$

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD” (with $\alpha = 1/L$)
03

Nonconvex: Stationarity

Gradients vanish, minima not promised

Nonconvex Guarantee: Small Squared Gradient

Theorem (nonconvex, step $1/L$).

$f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ differentiable, $L$-smooth ($L \gt 0$), and $f(x) \geq f_{\text{low}}$ for all $x$. For any $x_0$ and every $T \geq 1$:

$$\min_{0 \leq t \lt T}\; \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2 \;\leq\; \frac{2L\,\big(f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}\big)}{T}$$

$\min$ over the iterates $t = 0, \dots, T-1$: the best of $T$ squared gradients

Not a minimum of $f$: $f_{\text{low}}$ need not be attained

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD” (stated for step $\alpha \le 1/L$)

Proof Step 1: Sum the Decreases

Add the descent inequality for $t = 0, \dots, T-1$; the $f$ terms telescope:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2L}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2 &\leq \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \big(f(x_t) - f(x_{t+1})\big)\\[4px] &= f(x_0) - f(x_T) \;\stackrel{(f \,\geq\, f_{\text{low}})}{\leq}\; f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}} \end{aligned}$$

A finite budget $f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}$ caps the total squared gradient

Only $f(x_T) \geq f_{\text{low}}$ is used: no minimizer needed

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD”

Proof Step 2: Minimum at Most Average

$$\begin{aligned} \min_{0 \leq t \lt T} \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2 &\leq \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(Step 1)}}{\leq} \frac{2L\,\big(f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}\big)}{T} \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

$T$ terms, so the smallest is at most their average

A bound on the best iterate, not on the last one

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD”

Every Gradient Norm Vanishes

Q: Does $\|\nabla f(x_t)\| \to 0$ for nonconvex GD?

A: Yes, for the whole sequence, under the same assumptions.

$$\sum_{t \geq 0} \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2 \;\leq\; 2L\,\big(f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}\big) \;\lt\; \infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \|\nabla f(x_t)\| \to 0$$

Nonnegative terms with a finite sum must tend to zero

$L$-smooth, $f \geq f_{\text{low}}$, step $1/L$; no last-iterate rate, no claim that $x_t$ converges

Step 1 bound for every $T$: Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5; the limit: our deduction

Stationary Does Not Mean Minimum

Q: Does nonconvex GD reach a local optimum at rate $1/T$?

A: No. The $1/T$ rate is for $\min_t \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2$ only.

$\|\nabla f(x)\| \leq \varepsilon$: what the theorem gives stationary, $\nabla f(x) = 0$: also maxima, saddles local minimum: not guaranteed global minimum

Scope: $L$-smooth, bounded below, step $1/L$, no further assumptions

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD” (limit may be a maximum or saddle)

The Square Changes the Rate

$$\begin{aligned} \min_{0 \leq t \lt T} \|\nabla f(x_t)\| &\leq \sqrt{\frac{2L\,\big(f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}\big)}{T}} \;=\; O\big(1/\sqrt{T}\big)\\[6px] \text{some } \|\nabla f(x_t)\| \leq \varepsilon,\ t \lt T \quad &\Longleftarrow\quad T \;\geq\; \frac{2L\,\big(f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}\big)}{\varepsilon^2} \end{aligned}$$

Bound for tolerance $\varepsilon/10$: $100\times$ the iterations

Constants $L$ and $f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}$ depend on the problem

Best iterate only: no $O(1/T)$ gradient-norm rate, no local minimum

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD”; $T$ count: our derivation
04

Convex: Optimality

Function gap at rate 1/T

Convexity Puts Chords Above

Convex $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$: for all $x,y$ and $\theta\in[0,1]$,

$$f\big(\theta x+(1-\theta)y\big)\le\theta f(x)+(1-\theta)f(y)$$

Graph on or below every chord

Payoff: every local minimum is global

$\le$, not $\lt$: flat pieces allowed

$y$ $x$ chord $f$

The Tangent Lies Below

$f$ convex and differentiable: for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^n$,

$$f(y)\ge f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),\,y-x\rangle$$

One gradient gives a global lower bound

Smoothness bounds $f$ above; convexity, below

So $\nabla f(x)=0$ means $x$ is a global minimizer

$x$ tangent at $x$ $f$

Assume an Optimum Exists

$\arg\min f$ $x^\star$ $x_t$ $\Delta_t$ $\sqrt{D_t}$ $f$

Assume some $x^\star\in\arg\min f$ exists

Not assumed unique: any minimizer works

$$\begin{aligned}f^\star&=f(x^\star)\\ \Delta_t&=f(x_t)-f^\star\ge0\\ D_t&=\|x_t-x^\star\|^2\end{aligned}$$

Plot: $f(u)=\max(|u|-1,0)^2$

Convex: $O(1/T)$ Function Gap

Theorem (smooth convex GD).

$f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ differentiable, convex, $L$-smooth; a minimizer $x^\star$ exists.

GD with step $1/L$ from any $x_0$. For every $T\ge1$:

$$f(x_T)-f^\star\;\le\;\frac{L\,\|x_0-x^\star\|^2}{2T}$$

Bounds the function-value gap at the last iterate

Sublinear: not a bound on $\|x_T-x^\star\|$, not geometric

Classical treatment: Nesterov, Introductory Lectures on Convex Optimization: A Basic Course, Springer (2004). Exact bound: Garrigos & Gower, arXiv:2301.11235v3 (2024), §3.1, Thm 3.4

Proof Plan: Spend Distance

Target: $\;\Delta_T\le\dfrac{L\,D_0}{2T}$, in five moves

  1. Descent: each step lowers $f$ by $\tfrac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2$
  2. Tangent at $x_t$: ties the gap $\Delta_t$ to $\nabla f(x_t)$
  3. Distance identity: each gap paid by $D_t-D_{t+1}$
  4. Telescope over $t=0,\dots,T-1$
  5. Last iterate: smallest gap $\le$ average gap

Recall: One-Step Decrease

Descent inequality: $f$ $L$-smooth, $g_t=\nabla f(x_t)$, $x_{t+1}=x_t-\tfrac1L g_t$

$$f(x_{t+1})\;\le\;f(x_t)-\frac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2$$

Subtract $f^\star$; smoothness only, no convexity yet:

$$\Delta_{t+1}\;\le\;\Delta_t-\frac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2$$

Use the Tangent at $x_t$

Tangent bound with $x=x_t$ and $y=x^\star$:

$$f^\star\;\ge\;f(x_t)+\langle g_t,\,x^\star-x_t\rangle \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Delta_t\;\le\;\langle g_t,\,x_t-x^\star\rangle$$

Insert into the descent step:

$$\Delta_{t+1}\;\le\;\langle g_t,\,x_t-x^\star\rangle-\frac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2$$

Expand the Squared Distance

Since $x_{t+1}-x^\star=(x_t-x^\star)-\tfrac1L g_t$:

$$\begin{aligned} D_{t+1} &= D_t-\tfrac{2}{L}\langle g_t,\,x_t-x^\star\rangle+\tfrac{1}{L^2}\|g_t\|^2\\ \tfrac{L}{2}\,(D_t-D_{t+1}) &= \langle g_t,\,x_t-x^\star\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2 \end{aligned}$$

Line 2 is exactly the right side of the tangent-plus-descent bound:

$$\Delta_{t+1}\;\le\;\frac{L}{2}\,\big(D_t-D_{t+1}\big)$$

Distances Telescope

Add the bound for $t=0,1,\dots,T-1$:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta_t \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\Delta_{t+1} &\;\le\; \tfrac{L}{2}\big[(D_0-\cancel{D_1})+(\cancel{D_1}-\cancel{D_2})+\cdots+(\cancel{D_{T-1}}-D_T)\big]\\ &\;=\; \tfrac{L}{2}\,(D_0-D_T)\;\le\;\tfrac{L}{2}\,D_0 \end{aligned}$$

Middle distances cancel; drop $D_T\ge0$

Left side: the $T$ gaps $\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_T$

The Last Gap Is Smallest

Descent makes gaps nonincreasing: $\Delta_T\le\Delta_t$ for $1\le t\le T$

$$T\,\Delta_T\;\le\;\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Delta_t\;\le\;\frac{L}{2}\,D_0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x_T)-f^\star\;\le\;\frac{L\,\|x_0-x^\star\|^2}{2T}\qquad\blacksquare$$

Monotone by the descent inequality, not by convexity

Last iterate here; nonconvex GD bounds only the best gradient

The Convex Proof in One Chain

$$\begin{aligned} T\,\Delta_T &\;\stackrel{\text{(monotone)}}{\le}\; \sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\Delta_{t+1} \;\stackrel{\text{(descent + tangent)}}{\le}\; \sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\Big(\langle g_t,\,x_t-x^\star\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2\Big)\\[10pt] &\;\stackrel{\text{(distance identity)}}{=}\; \tfrac{L}{2}\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\big(D_t-D_{t+1}\big) \;\stackrel{\text{(telescope)}}{=}\; \tfrac{L}{2}\,(D_0-D_T)\;\le\;\tfrac{L}{2}\,D_0 \end{aligned}$$

Sublinear $O(1/T)$, not linear: doubling $T$ only halves the bound

Only descent and the tangent bound: no co-coercivity, no PL condition

Strongly Convex: Geometric Decay

Distance shrinks every step

Add a Quadratic Floor

Differentiable $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ is $\mu$-strongly convex, $\mu\gt0$, if for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^n$,

$$f(y)\;\ge\;f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),\,y-x\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\,\|y-x\|^2$$

The tangent bound, plus a quadratic bowl of curvature $\mu$

With $L$-smoothness: from here on $0\lt\mu\le L$

Stronger than a unique minimizer: $x^4$ has one, yet fails for every $\mu\gt0$

Curvature Is Bounded Below

$f$ upper, curvature $L$ floor, curvature $\mu$ tangent $x$

$r(y)$: height of $f(y)$ above the tangent at $x$

$$\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|^2\le r(y)\le\frac{L}{2}\|y-x\|^2$$

Floor and upper model touch $f$ at $x$, with the same tangent

Curvature trapped between $\mu$ and $L$

Illustration: our drawing (figs/o3b_figs.py)

Strongly Convex: Geometric Convergence

Theorem (strongly convex GD).

$f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ $\mu$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth; $x^\star$ its unique minimizer.

GD with step $1/L$ from any $x_0$. For every $T\ge1$:

$$\|x_T-x^\star\|^2\;\le\;\Big(1-\frac{\mu}{L}\Big)^{T}\,\|x_0-x^\star\|^2$$

$q=1-\mu/L\in[0,1)$; $q=0$ means $\mu=L$ and $x_1=x^\star$

Same step $1/L$ as before, not $2/(\mu+L)$

Classical treatment: Nesterov, Introductory Lectures on Convex Optimization: A Basic Course, Springer (2004). Exact statement: Garrigos & Gower, arXiv:2301.11235v3 (2024), §3.2, Thm 3.6, Remark 3.7

Keep the Extra Curvature Term

Strong convexity at $x=x_t$, $y=x^\star$: $\;\Delta_t\le\langle g_t,\,x_t-x^\star\rangle-\tfrac{\mu}{2}D_t$

Same descent step and distance identity as the convex proof:

$$\begin{aligned} \Delta_{t+1} &\;\le\; \langle g_t,\,x_t-x^\star\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2-\tfrac{\mu}{2}D_t\\ &\;=\; \tfrac{L}{2}\,(D_t-D_{t+1})-\tfrac{\mu}{2}\,D_t \end{aligned}$$

$\mu=0$ gives back the convex step; no PL condition used

Nonnegative Gap Forces Contraction

$x^\star$ minimizes $f$, so $\Delta_{t+1}\ge0$:

$$0\;\le\;\frac{L}{2}\,(D_t-D_{t+1})-\frac{\mu}{2}\,D_t$$

Multiply by $2/L$ and move $D_{t+1}$ to the left:

$$D_{t+1}\;\le\;\Big(1-\frac{\mu}{L}\Big)\,D_t\;=\;q\,D_t$$

Each step shrinks the squared distance by at least the factor $q$

Repeat the Same Contraction

Apply $D_{t+1}\le q\,D_t$ for $t=T-1,\dots,0$:

$$D_T\;\le\;q\,D_{T-1}\;\le\;q^2\,D_{T-2}\;\le\;\cdots\;\le\;q^{T}\,D_0$$

Valid for every $T\ge1$; if $q=0$, then $D_T=0$

Since $1-u\le e^{-u}$: $\;q^T\le e^{-\mu T/L}$

Error falls by a constant factor every step

Function Values Follow Distance

Smoothness upper bound at $x^\star$, where $\nabla f(x^\star)=0$:

$$f(x_T)-f^\star\;\le\;\frac{L}{2}\,\|x_T-x^\star\|^2\;\le\;\frac{L}{2}\,q^{T}\,\|x_0-x^\star\|^2\qquad\blacksquare$$

Iterates and values converge to the optimum

Not just to some stationary point

Prefactor $\tfrac{L}{2}D_0$, taken from the distance bound

Linear Means Geometric

$1$ $10^{-1}$ $10^{-2}$ $10^{-3}$ $1$ $24$ iteration $T$ $1/T$ $q^T$

Linear rate: error $\le C\,q^T$ with $0\lt q\lt1$

Same thing as geometric: straight on a log scale

Sublinear: $1/T$ flattens out

Convex $O(1/T)$ is sublinear, not linear

Shapes only, illustrative $q$: not GD output
