01

Discrete to Continuous

Brownian motion, and VE / VP as limits of an SDE

Why Continuous Time

  • Gaussian reverse step is valid only for small $\beta_n$.
  • Shrink $\beta_n$: error vanishes, step count explodes.
$\Delta t \to 0$

From Random Walk to Brownian Motion

Same time span, finer grid, noise rescaled at each refinement.

Limit exists, and it is Brownian motion.

Definition — Brownian Motion

Definition (Wiener process).

  • $W_0 = 0$, and $t \mapsto W_t$ is continuous.
  • Increments over disjoint intervals are independent.
  • Gaussian increments, variance equal to elapsed time:
$$W_{t+\Delta t} - W_t \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}(0, \Delta t).$$

Increment scale is $\sqrt{\Delta t}$, not $\Delta t$.

Why the $\sqrt{\Delta t}$ Scaling

Split $[0,t]$ into $N = t/\Delta t$ steps of size $c\,Z_i$ with $Z_i$ i.i.d.

$$\mathrm{Var}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N} c\,Z_i\Bigr) = N c^2 = \frac{t}{\Delta t}\,c^2 \;\stackrel{!}{=}\; t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c = \sqrt{\Delta t}.$$

Key trick. Drift accumulates like $\Delta t$; noise accumulates like $\sqrt{\Delta t}$.

$(\sqrt{\Delta t})^2 = \Delta t$: squared noise is first order in time.

VE as an SDE

Discrete VE step: $X^{(n)} = X^{(n-1)} + \sqrt{\beta}\,Z^{(n)}$ with $Z^{(n)}$ standard normal.

Let one step span time $\Delta t$ and rescale the noise by $\sqrt{\Delta t}$:

$$X_{t+\Delta t} - X_t \;=\; \sqrt{\beta\,\Delta t}\,Z \;=\; \sqrt{\beta}\,\Delta W_t,\qquad \Delta W_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\Delta t).$$

Take $\Delta t \to 0$:

$$dX_t \;=\; \sqrt{\beta}\,dW_t.$$

VE Marginal — Consistency Check

Integrate directly: $X_t = X_0 + \sqrt{\beta}\,W_t$.

$$X_t \,|\, X_0 \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\bigl(X_0,\; \beta t\bigr).$$

Discrete chain after $n$ steps had variance $n\beta\,\Delta t = \beta t$. Same answer.

Variance grows without bound: variance exploding.

VP as an SDE

Discrete VP with step span $\Delta t$ and schedule $\beta_n = \beta_t\,\Delta t$:

$$\begin{aligned} X_{t+\Delta t} &= \sqrt{1-\beta_t\,\Delta t}\;X_t \;+\; \sqrt{\beta_t\,\Delta t}\;Z \\ &= \bigl(1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\beta_t\,\Delta t\bigr)X_t \;+\; \sqrt{\beta_t\,\Delta t}\;Z \;+\; O(\Delta t^2). \end{aligned}$$

Key trick. $\sqrt{1-u} = 1 - u/2 + O(u^2)$ turns the shrink factor into a drift.

$$dX_t \;=\; -\tfrac{\beta_t}{2}\,X_t\,dt \;+\; \sqrt{\beta_t}\,dW_t.$$

General Forward SDE

Both processes are special cases of

$$dX_t \;=\; f(X_t, t)\,dt \;+\; g(t)\,dW_t.$$

VE

Drift $f = 0$.

Diffusion $g(t) = \sqrt{\beta}$.

VP

Drift $f(x, t) = -\tfrac{\beta_t}{2}\,x$.

Diffusion $g(t) = \sqrt{\beta_t}$.

Drift and Diffusion, Pictured

$f(x,t)\,dt$ $g(t)\,dW_t$

The Solution Is a Distribution

$$X_t \;=\; X_0 + \int_0^t f(X_s, s)\,ds + \int_0^t g(s)\,dW_s.$$
$X_0$ $p_t$

Training needs the marginals $\{p_t\}$, not the paths.

02

Fokker–Planck Equation

The PDE that governs $p_t$

Two Views of One Process

$X_t$ random $p_t$ deterministic

The SDE is a rule for paths; we want a rule for densities.

Warm-Up — Pure Diffusion

No drift, constant noise: $dX_t = g\,dW_t$ started at $x_0$.

$$\partial_t\,p_t(x) \;=\; \frac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2\,p_t(x)\qquad\text{(heat equation)}.$$

Exercise. Check that $p_t = \mathcal{N}(x_0, g^2 t)$ solves it.

Fokker–Planck, 1D

Theorem (Fokker–Planck).

Let $dX_t = f(X_t,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dW_t$ with $X_0 \sim p_0$. The marginal density $p_t$ satisfies

$$\partial_t\,p_t(x) \;=\; -\partial_x\bigl(f(x,t)\,p_t(x)\bigr) \;+\; \frac{g^2(t)}{2}\,\partial_x^2\,p_t(x).$$

A deterministic PDE: all randomness has been integrated out.

Smoothness and decay of $f$, $g$, $p_t$ assumed throughout.

Reading the Equation

$-\partial_x(f\,p_t)$ $\tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t$

Drift moves the bump; diffusion spreads it.

Proof Setup — Why a Test Function

A test function $\phi$ is smooth with compact support: a probe for $p_t$.

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\phi(X_t)\bigr] \;=\; \int \phi(x)\,p_t(x)\,dx.$$

Left side moves by the SDE; right side moves by the PDE. Equate them.

$$X_{t+\varepsilon} \;=\; X_t + \varepsilon\,f(X_t,t) + \sqrt{\varepsilon}\,g(t)\,Z + o(\varepsilon),\qquad Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1).$$

Proof Outline

Target, required for every test function $\phi$:

$$\int \phi\,\partial_t p_t\,dx \;=\; \int \phi\,\Bigl[-\partial_x(f\,p_t) + \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t\Bigr]dx.$$
  1. Step 1. Taylor-expand $\phi(X_{t+\varepsilon})$ to second order.
  2. Step 2. Take expectation, divide by $\varepsilon$.
  3. Step 3. Integrate by parts, then strip $\phi$.

Step 1: Taylor Expand $\phi$

Write $\Delta = \varepsilon f(X_t,t) + \sqrt{\varepsilon}\,g(t)\,Z$ and expand around $X_t$:

$$\phi(X_{t+\varepsilon}) \;=\; \phi(X_t) + \phi'(X_t)\,\Delta + \tfrac{1}{2}\phi''(X_t)\,\Delta^2 + O(\Delta^3).$$

Only $\phi$, $\phi'$, $\phi''$ appear: second order suffices.

Exercise. Show the $O(\Delta^3)$ remainder is $o(\varepsilon)$ in expectation.

Proof (continued) — Which Orders Survive

  • $\varepsilon\,\phi'\,f$: order $\varepsilon$, keep.
  • $\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\phi'\,g\,Z$: order $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, but mean zero.
  • $\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon\,\phi''\,g^2 Z^2$: order $\varepsilon$, keep.
  • Cross term $\varepsilon^{3/2}\,\phi''fgZ$: negligible.

Key trick. Squaring $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ promotes the second derivative to first order in time.

Step 2: Take the Expectation

$Z$ is independent of $X_t$, with $\mathbb{E}[Z]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}[Z^2]=1$:

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\phi(X_{t+\varepsilon})\bigr] - \mathbb{E}\bigl[\phi(X_t)\bigr] \;=\; \varepsilon\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\phi'(X_t)\,f + \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\phi''(X_t)\Bigr] + o(\varepsilon).$$

Divide by $\varepsilon$ and let $\varepsilon \to 0$:

$$\partial_t\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\phi(X_t)\bigr] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{X\sim p_t}\Bigl[\phi'(X)\,f(X,t) + \tfrac{g^2(t)}{2}\,\phi''(X)\Bigr].$$

Step 3: Integrate by Parts

On the left, differentiate under the integral: $\partial_t \mathbb{E}[\phi(X_t)] = \int \phi\,\partial_t p_t\,dx$.

On the right, move derivatives off $\phi$; boundary terms vanish by compact support:

$$\begin{aligned} \int \phi'\,f\,p_t\,dx &\;=\; -\int \phi\,\partial_x(f\,p_t)\,dx, \\ \int \phi''\,\tfrac{g^2}{2}\,p_t\,dx &\;=\; \int \phi\,\tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t\,dx. \end{aligned}$$

Second line integrates by parts twice; both boundary terms drop.

Proof (continued) — Stripping $\phi$

$$\int \phi(x)\,\underbrace{\Bigl[\partial_t p_t + \partial_x(f\,p_t) - \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t\Bigr]}_{=\;h(x)}\,dx \;=\; 0 \quad \text{for all } \phi.$$

If $h$ is continuous and $\int \phi h = 0$ for every test $\phi$, then $h \equiv 0$.

Otherwise pick a bump supported where $h$ keeps one sign: the integral is nonzero.

Fokker–Planck holds pointwise. $\blacksquare$

Proof Recap — One Chain

$$\begin{aligned} \int \phi\,\partial_t p_t\,dx &\stackrel{\text{(definition)}}{=}\; \partial_t\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\phi(X_t)\bigr] \\ &\stackrel{\text{(Taylor)}}{=}\; \lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\phi'\Delta + \tfrac{1}{2}\phi''\Delta^2\bigr] \\ &\stackrel{\text{(expectation)}}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_{X\sim p_t}\bigl[\phi' f + \tfrac{g^2}{2}\phi''\bigr] \\ &\stackrel{\text{(by parts)}}{=}\; \int \phi\,\bigl[-\partial_x(f p_t) + \tfrac{g^2}{2}\partial_x^2 p_t\bigr]dx. \end{aligned}$$

True for every $\phi$, so the integrands agree pointwise.

Example — Where VP Settles

Constant schedule $\beta$, so $f = -\tfrac{\beta}{2}x$ and $g = \sqrt{\beta}$. Set $\partial_t p = 0$:

$$\begin{aligned} 0 &\;=\; \partial_x\Bigl(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\,x\,p\Bigr) + \tfrac{\beta}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; x\,p + \partial_x p = 0 \\ &\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \partial_x \log p = -x \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; p_\infty \propto e^{-x^2/2}. \end{aligned}$$

The standard normal prior is derived, not assumed.

Vector Form (Multi-D)

For $\mathbf{X}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with $d\mathbf{X}_t = f(\mathbf{X}_t,t)\,dt + g(t)\,d\mathbf{W}_t$:

$$\partial_t\,p_t(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; -\nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(f(\mathbf{x},t)\,p_t(\mathbf{x})\bigr) \;+\; \frac{g^2(t)}{2}\,\Delta\,p_t(\mathbf{x}).$$
  • Divergence $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v} = \sum_{i} \partial_{x_i} v_i$ replaces $\partial_x(f\,p_t)$.
  • Laplacian $\Delta p = \sum_{i} \partial_{x_i}^2 p$ replaces $\partial_x^2 p_t$.

Proof is identical with a multivariate Taylor expansion.

03

Anderson's Reverse SDE

A backward SDE with the same marginals

Flipping the Drift Is Not Enough

$p_0$ $p_T$

Noise keeps spreading backwards. Something must push mass uphill.

The Score Points Uphill

$p_t$

Zero at peaks, large where the density falls off.

Anderson's Theorem

Theorem (Anderson, 1982).

Let $dX_t = f(X_t,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dW_t$ with $X_0 \sim p_0$. The reverse-time process

$$d\bar{X}_t = \bigl(f(\bar{X}_t, t) - g^2(t)\,\partial_x \log p_t(\bar{X}_t)\bigr)dt + g(t)\,d\bar{W}_t,\quad \bar{X}_T \sim p_T$$

satisfies $\bar{X}_t \stackrel{(d)}{=} X_t$ for every $t \in [0,T]$.

Anderson, "Reverse-time diffusion equation models," Stochastic Processes and their Applications 1982.

Two SDEs, Same Marginals

$p_0$ $p_t$ $p_T$

Same diffusion $g$; the reverse drift adds $-g^2\,\partial_x\log p_t$.

Sanity Check — Gaussian Case

VP started from a point: $p_t = \mathcal{N}(\gamma_t x_0, \sigma_t^2)$, so the score is linear.

$$\begin{aligned} \partial_x \log p_t(x) &\;=\; -\frac{x - \gamma_t x_0}{\sigma_t^2}, \\ \text{reverse drift} &\;=\; -\tfrac{\beta_t}{2}\,x + \beta_t\,\frac{x - \gamma_t x_0}{\sigma_t^2}. \end{aligned}$$

With $\sigma_t^2 \lt 2$ the correction dominates, and backward integration recontracts mass toward $\gamma_t x_0$.

Proof Setup — Strategy

Let $\bar{p}_t$ denote the marginal of $\bar{X}_t$.

  1. Derive the PDE that $\bar{p}_t$ must satisfy.
  2. Show the forward marginal $p_t$ solves that same PDE.
  3. Conclude $\bar{p}_t = p_t$ by uniqueness.

Marginals are compared through their PDEs, never through paths.

Proof Outline

Target, at every $t$:

$$\partial_t p_t \;\stackrel{?}{=}\; -\partial_x\Bigl[\bigl(f - g^2\,\partial_x \log p_t\bigr) p_t\Bigr] - \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t.$$
  1. Step 1. Fokker–Planck for the time-reversed process.
  2. Step 2. Substitute $p_t$ using $(\partial_x\log p_t)\,p_t = \partial_x p_t$.
  3. Step 3. Conclude by uniqueness of the solution.

Step 1: Run the Reverse SDE Forward

Fokker–Planck needs increasing time. Set $Y_s = \bar{X}_{T-s}$ for $s \in [0,T]$.

Key trick. Negating $ds$ flips the drift sign; the noise term is unchanged.

$$dY_s = -\bigl(f(Y_s, T{-}s) - g^2\,\partial_y \log p_{T-s}(Y_s)\bigr)ds + g(T{-}s)\,d\tilde{W}_s.$$

Now a standard forward SDE, so the theorem applies.

Proof (continued) — Back to Original Time

Apply Fokker–Planck to $Y_s$, whose marginal is $q_s = \bar{p}_{T-s}$:

$$\partial_s q_s \;=\; \partial_y\Bigl[\bigl(f - g^2\,\partial_y \log p_{T-s}\bigr) q_s\Bigr] + \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_y^2 q_s.$$

Substitute $s = T-t$, so $q_s = \bar{p}_t$ and $\partial_s = -\partial_t$:

$$\partial_t \bar{p}_t \;=\; -\partial_x\Bigl[\bigl(f - g^2\,\partial_x \log p_t\bigr)\bar{p}_t\Bigr] - \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 \bar{p}_t.$$

Step 2: $p_t$ Solves That PDE

Put $\bar{p}_t = p_t$ on the right and use $(\partial_x\log p_t)\,p_t = \partial_x p_t$:

$$\begin{aligned} &-\partial_x\Bigl[\bigl(f - g^2\,\partial_x \log p_t\bigr)p_t\Bigr] - \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t \\ &\quad=\; -\partial_x(f\,p_t) + g^2\,\partial_x^2 p_t - \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t \\ &\quad=\; -\partial_x(f\,p_t) + \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t \;=\; \partial_t p_t. \end{aligned}$$

The last equality is forward Fokker–Planck.

Step 3: Uniqueness Closes It

  • $\bar{p}_t$ and $p_t$ solve the same linear PDE.
  • They agree at $t = T$: $\bar{X}_T \sim p_T$ by construction.
  • Such a terminal-value problem has one solution.

Hence $\bar{p}_t = p_t$ for all $t$. $\blacksquare$

Equality of laws at each fixed time, not of trajectories.

Proof Recap — One Chain

$$\begin{aligned} \partial_t p_t &\stackrel{\text{(forward FP)}}{=}\; -\partial_x(f\,p_t) + \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t \\ &\stackrel{\text{(split the term)}}{=}\; -\partial_x(f\,p_t) + g^2\,\partial_x^2 p_t - \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t \\ &\stackrel{\text{(score identity)}}{=}\; -\partial_x(f\,p_t) + g^2\,\partial_x\bigl[(\partial_x\log p_t)p_t\bigr] - \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t \\ &\stackrel{\text{(factor the drift)}}{=}\; -\partial_x\Bigl[\bigl(f - g^2\,\partial_x\log p_t\bigr)p_t\Bigr] - \tfrac{g^2}{2}\,\partial_x^2 p_t \\ &\stackrel{\text{(Step 1)}}{=}\; \partial_t \bar{p}_t. \end{aligned}$$

Why Anderson Matters

With the true score the reverse process is exact at every $t$.

Discrete chain

Reverse step assumed Gaussian.

Valid only for small $\beta_n$.

Reverse SDE

No per-step approximation.

Exactness bought with the score.

Generation: start at $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, integrate backwards with $s_\theta \approx \partial_x\log p_t$.

Where the Error Now Lives

  • Prior: $p_T$ is only close to $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$.
  • Score: $s_\theta$ is only close to $\partial_x\log p_t$.
  • Solver: the backward integration uses finite steps.

The Gaussian-step error is gone; all three survivors shrink with effort.

Next question: how to train $s_\theta$ without knowing $p_t$.

04

Score Matching

A tractable loss whose minimizer is $\partial_x \log p_t$

Why Learn the Score

The reverse SDE needs one unknown object, and only one.

Chain view

Learn per-step Gaussian parameters.

Inherits the step approximation.

Score view

Learn one field $s_\theta(x, t)$.

Exact in the perfect-score limit.

Goal: a loss we can evaluate whose minimizer is the true score.

The Natural Loss Is Intractable

Weighted Fisher-divergence objective across all noise levels:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \int_0^T \lambda(t)\,\mathbb{E}_{X_t \sim p_t}\Bigl[\bigl\|s_\theta(X_t, t) - \partial_x \log p_t(X_t)\bigr\|^2\Bigr]dt.$$
  • Sampling $X_t$: easy, run the forward process.
  • Evaluating $\partial_x\log p_t$: impossible, $p_t$ is unknown.

Key Idea — The Kernel Is Known

$p_t$ $p_{t\mid 0}$

The mixture is unknown; every component is known in closed form.

Denoising Score Matching

Theorem (Vincent, 2011).

With $C$ independent of $\theta$,

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \int_0^T \lambda(t)\,\mathbb{E}_{X_0}\mathbb{E}_{X_t\mid X_0}\Bigl[\bigl\|s_\theta(X_t, t) - \partial_x \log p_{t\mid 0}(X_t\mid X_0)\bigr\|^2\Bigr]dt + C.$$

Same minimizer, and every term is computable.

Vincent, "A connection between score matching and denoising autoencoders," Neural Computation 2011.

Proof Setup

Marginal as a mixture over the data:

$$p_t(x) = \int p_0(x_0)\,p_{t\mid 0}(x \mid x_0)\,dx_0.$$

Bayes posterior over the clean sample:

$$q(x_0 \mid x) = \frac{p_0(x_0)\,p_{t\mid 0}(x \mid x_0)}{p_t(x)}.$$

Proof Outline

Target identity, for each fixed $t$:

$$\mathbb{E}_{X_t}\bigl[\|s_\theta - \partial\log p_t\|^2\bigr] \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{X_0}\mathbb{E}_{X_t\mid X_0}\bigl[\|s_\theta - \partial\log p_{t\mid 0}\|^2\bigr] + C'.$$
  1. Step 1. Marginal score is a posterior average of kernel scores.
  2. Step 2. Expand both squares; only the cross terms interact.
  3. Step 3. Reassemble; the mismatch has no $\theta$.

Lemma — Score as an Average

Lemma (marginal score).

$$\partial_x \log p_t(x) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{X_0 \sim q(\cdot\mid x)}\bigl[\partial_x \log p_{t\mid 0}(x \mid X_0)\bigr].$$

The unknown score is an average of known kernel scores.

Weights are the posterior over which clean sample produced $x$.

Step 1: Proof of the Lemma

Differentiate the mixture, then convert each kernel derivative:

$$\begin{aligned} \partial_x \log p_t(x) &\;=\; \frac{1}{p_t(x)}\int p_0(x_0)\,\partial_x\,p_{t\mid 0}(x\mid x_0)\,dx_0 \\ &\;=\; \frac{1}{p_t(x)}\int p_0(x_0)\,p_{t\mid 0}\,\partial_x \log p_{t\mid 0}\,dx_0 \\ &\;=\; \int \partial_x \log p_{t\mid 0}(x\mid x_0)\;q(x_0\mid x)\,dx_0. \end{aligned}$$

Last line recognizes the Bayes posterior. $\blacksquare$

Step 2: Expand the Square

Write $s = s_\theta(X_t, t)$ and expand:

$$\mathbb{E}_{X_t}\bigl[\|s - \partial\log p_t\|^2\bigr] = \mathbb{E}_{X_t}\bigl[\|s\|^2\bigr] - 2\,\mathbb{E}_{X_t}\bigl[s \cdot \partial\log p_t\bigr] + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{X_t}\bigl[\|\partial\log p_t\|^2\bigr]}_{\text{no } \theta}.$$
  • Third term: constant in $\theta$, drop it into $C'$.
  • First term: $\mathbb{E}_{X_t} = \mathbb{E}_{X_0}\mathbb{E}_{X_t\mid X_0}$ trivially.

Proof (continued) — The Cross Term

Key trick. $s$ depends only on $X_t$, so it passes inside the inner expectation.

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_{X_t}\bigl[s \cdot \partial\log p_t\bigr] &\stackrel{\text{(Lemma)}}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_{X_t}\Bigl[s \cdot \mathbb{E}_{X_0\mid X_t}\bigl[\partial\log p_{t\mid 0}\bigr]\Bigr] \\ &\stackrel{\text{(pull in)}}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_{X_t}\mathbb{E}_{X_0\mid X_t}\bigl[s \cdot \partial\log p_{t\mid 0}\bigr] \\ &\stackrel{\text{(tower)}}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_{X_0}\mathbb{E}_{X_t\mid X_0}\bigl[s \cdot \partial\log p_{t\mid 0}\bigr]. \end{aligned}$$

Step 3: Reassemble

Both surviving terms now live under $\mathbb{E}_{X_0}\mathbb{E}_{X_t\mid X_0}$. Complete the square:

$$\mathbb{E}_{X_0}\mathbb{E}_{X_t\mid X_0}\bigl[\|s\|^2 - 2\,s\cdot\partial\log p_{t\mid 0}\bigr] = \mathbb{E}_{X_0}\mathbb{E}_{X_t\mid X_0}\bigl[\|s - \partial\log p_{t\mid 0}\|^2\bigr] + C''.$$

Two losses differ by a constant, so they have the same minimizer. $\blacksquare$

Proof Recap — One Chain

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_{X_t}\bigl[\|s - \partial\log p_t\|^2\bigr] &\stackrel{\text{(expand)}}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_{X_t}\bigl[\|s\|^2 - 2\,s\cdot\partial\log p_t\bigr] + C' \\ &\stackrel{\text{(Lemma)}}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_{X_t}\bigl[\|s\|^2 - 2\,s\cdot\mathbb{E}_{X_0\mid X_t}[\partial\log p_{t\mid 0}]\bigr] + C' \\ &\stackrel{\text{(tower)}}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_{X_0}\mathbb{E}_{X_t\mid X_0}\bigl[\|s\|^2 - 2\,s\cdot\partial\log p_{t\mid 0}\bigr] + C' \\ &\stackrel{\text{(complete square)}}{=}\; \mathbb{E}_{X_0}\mathbb{E}_{X_t\mid X_0}\bigl[\|s - \partial\log p_{t\mid 0}\|^2\bigr] + C''. \end{aligned}$$

Noisy Target, Correct Optimum

Least squares against a noisy target returns the conditional mean.

05

Kernel, Noise Prediction, DDPM

Closed-form kernel, training, sampling, and the discrete limit

The VP Kernel Is Gaussian

Proposition (VP transition kernel).

For $dX_t = -\tfrac{\beta_t}{2}X_t\,dt + \sqrt{\beta_t}\,dW_t$ with $\varphi(t) = \int_0^t \beta_s\,ds$,

$$X_t \mid X_0 \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\bigl(\gamma_t X_0,\; \sigma_t^2\bigr),\qquad \gamma_t = e^{-\varphi(t)/2},\quad \sigma_t^2 = 1 - \gamma_t^2.$$

Linear drift plus additive noise keeps Gaussians Gaussian.

Two ODEs, one for the mean and one for the variance, give the proof.

Proof — Mean ODE

Take expectations; the stochastic integral has mean zero.

$$\begin{aligned} m'(t) &\;=\; -\tfrac{\beta_t}{2}\,m(t),\qquad m(0) = X_0, \\ m(t) &\;=\; e^{-\varphi(t)/2}\,X_0 \;=\; \gamma_t X_0. \end{aligned}$$

Key trick. Integrating factor $e^{\varphi(t)/2}$ turns the drift into a derivative.

Proof — Variance ODE

The noise adds $\beta_t\,dt$ of variance while the drift removes $\beta_t v$:

$$\begin{aligned} v'(t) &\;=\; -\beta_t\,v(t) + \beta_t \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; (v-1)' = -\beta_t\,(v-1), \\ v(t) &\;=\; 1 + \bigl(v(0)-1\bigr)e^{-\varphi(t)} \;=\; 1 - \gamma_t^2 . \end{aligned}$$

Conditioning on $X_0$ gives $v(0) = 0$, hence $\sigma_t^2 = 1 - \gamma_t^2$. $\blacksquare$

Signal and Noise Budget

$1$ $\gamma_t^2$ $\sigma_t^2$ $T$

Variance preserving in continuous time: $\gamma_t^2 + \sigma_t^2 = 1$.

Conditional Score in Closed Form

Write the sample as $X_t = \gamma_t X_0 + \sigma_t \epsilon$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$.

$$\begin{aligned} \log p_{t\mid 0}(x \mid X_0) &\;=\; -\frac{(x - \gamma_t X_0)^2}{2\sigma_t^2} + \text{const}, \\ \partial_x \log p_{t\mid 0}(X_t \mid X_0) &\;=\; -\frac{X_t - \gamma_t X_0}{\sigma_t^2} \;=\; -\frac{\epsilon}{\sigma_t}. \end{aligned}$$

The regression target is the injected noise, rescaled by $\sigma_t$.

$\epsilon$-Reparameterization

Substitute $s_\theta(X_t, t) = -\epsilon_\theta(X_t, t)/\sigma_t$ into the score-matching loss:

$$\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(\theta) = T\;\mathbb{E}_{X_0,\,\epsilon,\,t}\left[\frac{\lambda(t)}{\sigma_t^2}\,\bigl\|\epsilon_\theta(\gamma_t X_0 + \sigma_t \epsilon,\; t) - \epsilon\bigr\|^2\right].$$
  • Time $t$ drawn uniformly on $[0,T]$.
  • Choosing $\lambda(t) = \sigma_t^2$ removes the weight entirely.

Example — Ornstein–Uhlenbeck

Constant coefficients $dX_t = -\beta X_t\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t$ give a closed-form kernel:

$$p_{t\mid 0}(x \mid X_0) = \mathcal{N}\left(e^{-\beta t}X_0,\;\frac{\sigma^2}{2\beta}\bigl(1 - e^{-2\beta t}\bigr)\right).$$

Read off $\gamma_t = e^{-\beta t}$ and the matching $\sigma_t^2$.

Training Algorithm

Continuous-time score matching
  1. Sample $X_0 \sim p_{\text{data}}$.
  2. Sample $t \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,T]$ and $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$.
  3. Form $X_t = \gamma_t X_0 + \sigma_t \epsilon$.
  4. Gradient step on $\bigl\|\epsilon_\theta(X_t, t) - \epsilon\bigr\|^2$.

No chain simulation: any $t$ in one draw.

Sampling by Euler–Maruyama

Reverse-SDE sampling
  1. Set $h = T/N$ and draw $X_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$.
  2. For $t = T, T-h, \ldots, h$: draw $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ and set
    1. $X_{t-h} = X_t - h\bigl(f(X_t,t) - g^2(t)\,s_\theta(X_t,t)\bigr) + \sqrt{h}\,g(t)\,Z$.
  3. Return $X_0$.

Step size $h$ is the only discretization left.

Same Loss, Two Derivations

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{\lambda}_n\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta\bigl(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_n}X^{(0)} + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_n}\,\epsilon,\; n\bigr)\bigr\|^2\Bigr].$$
  • Variational route: bound the likelihood, expand the KL terms.
  • Score route: denoising score matching on the VP SDE.

Both land on noise regression, with different weights $\tilde{\lambda}_n$.

Multi-Dimensional Extension

Isotropic diffusion $g(t) = \rho(t)\,I$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$:

  • Fokker–Planck: $\partial_t p_t = -\nabla\!\cdot(f\,p_t) + \tfrac{\rho^2}{2}\Delta p_t$.
  • Anderson: reverse drift uses $\nabla\log p_t$.
  • Score matching: the proof is unchanged, with inner products.

Lecture Recap

  • Limit: VE and VP become $dX_t = f\,dt + g\,dW_t$.
  • Fokker–Planck: the density obeys a deterministic PDE.
  • Anderson: reversing time costs exactly one score term.
  • Score matching: marginal loss equals kernel loss plus a constant.
  • Practice: noise regression; DDPM is the discrete case.
