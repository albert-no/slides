1. Pinsker's Inequality (information divergence — 30 points)

For distributions \(P,Q\) on a discrete alphabet, the total variation distance is \(\mathrm{TV}(P,Q) = \sup_A\big(P(A)-Q(A)\big)\). We will prove Pinsker's inequality \(\ \mathrm{TV}(P,Q) \le \sqrt{\tfrac12 D(P\|Q)}\ \) (nats), equivalently \(D(P\|Q)\ge 2\,\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)^2\).

  1. Show \(\mathrm{TV}(P,Q) = \tfrac12\sum_x |p(x)-q(x)|\), and that the sup is attained at \(A^\star=\{x: p(x)\ge q(x)\}\). (10 pts)
  2. Binary case. For Bernoulli parameters \(p,q\), prove \(d(p\|q):=p\log\frac pq + (1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q} \ge 2(p-q)^2\). Hint: fix \(p\), differentiate in \(q\), and use \(q(1-q)\le \tfrac14\). (12 pts)
  3. General case. Lift the binary bound to arbitrary alphabets using the data-processing inequality for KL. Hint: apply the map \(x\mapsto \mathbf 1\{x\in A^\star\}\). (8 pts)
Solution: Pinsker's Inequality.
  1. For any \(A\), \(P(A)-Q(A)=\sum_{x\in A}(p(x)-q(x))\), which is maximized by including exactly the \(x\) with \(p(x)\ge q(x)\), i.e. \(A^\star\). Since \(\sum_x(p-q)=0\), we have \(\sum_{x\in A^\star}(p-q) = -\sum_{x\notin A^\star}(p-q)=\sum_{x\notin A^\star}(q-p)\). Adding, \[ 2\big(P(A^\star)-Q(A^\star)\big) = \sum_{x\in A^\star}(p-q)+\sum_{x\notin A^\star}(q-p) = \sum_x|p-q|, \] so \(\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)=\tfrac12\sum_x|p-q|\).
  2. Fix \(p\) and set \(\varphi(q)=d(p\|q)-2(p-q)^2\). Then \(\varphi(p)=0\) and \[ \varphi'(q) = -\frac pq + \frac{1-p}{1-q} + 4(p-q) = \frac{q-p}{q(1-q)} + 4(p-q) = (p-q)\Big(4 - \frac{1}{q(1-q)}\Big). \] Since \(q(1-q)\le\tfrac14\), the factor \(4-\frac{1}{q(1-q)}\le 0\). Hence for \(q<p\), \(\varphi'(q)\le 0\) (so \(\varphi\) decreases to \(\varphi(p)=0\), giving \(\varphi\ge 0\) on the left), and for \(q>p\), \(\varphi'(q)\ge 0\) (so \(\varphi\) increases from \(0\), giving \(\varphi\ge 0\) on the right). Thus \(\varphi\ge 0\) everywhere, i.e. \(d(p\|q)\ge 2(p-q)^2\).
  3. Let \(T(x)=\mathbf 1\{x\in A^\star\}\). Under \(T\), \(P\) and \(Q\) push forward to Bernoullis with parameters \(P(A^\star)\) and \(Q(A^\star)\). The data-processing inequality for KL gives \[ D(P\|Q) \ge d\big(P(A^\star)\,\|\,Q(A^\star)\big) \ge 2\big(P(A^\star)-Q(A^\star)\big)^2 = 2\,\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)^2, \] using part (b) and then part (a). Rearranging yields \(\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)\le\sqrt{\tfrac12 D(P\|Q)}\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

2. Hellinger Distance and Le Cam's Inequalities (information divergence — 30 points)

Define the squared Hellinger distance \(H^2(P,Q)=\tfrac12\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\) and the Bhattacharyya affinity \(\rho(P,Q)=\int\sqrt{pq}\), so \(H^2 = 1-\rho\). Write \(H=\sqrt{H^2}\).

  1. Show \(H^2\) is an \(f\)-divergence (identify \(f\)) and that \(0\le H^2\le 1\). (8 pts)
  2. Prove the Le Cam sandwich relating Hellinger and total variation: \[ H^2(P,Q) \;\le\; \mathrm{TV}(P,Q) \;\le\; H(P,Q)\sqrt{2-H^2(P,Q)}. \] Hint: write \(2\,\mathrm{TV}=\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|\,(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\); bound below pointwise and above by Cauchy–Schwarz. (14 pts)
  3. Tensorization. For product measures \(P=\prod_i P_i\), \(Q=\prod_i Q_i\), show \(\rho(P,Q)=\prod_i\rho(P_i,Q_i)\), and hence for \(n\) i.i.d. copies \(1-H^2(P^{\otimes n},Q^{\otimes n})=(1-H^2(P,Q))^n\). Interpret. (8 pts)
Solution: Hellinger and Le Cam.
  1. With \(f(t)=\tfrac12(\sqrt t-1)^2\) (convex, \(f(1)=0\)), \(\int q\,f(p/q)=\tfrac12\int q(\sqrt{p/q}-1)^2 = \tfrac12\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2 = H^2\), so \(H^2\) is the \(f\)-divergence for this \(f\). By Cauchy–Schwarz \(\rho=\int\sqrt{pq}\le\sqrt{\int p}\sqrt{\int q}=1\), and \(\rho\ge 0\), so \(H^2=1-\rho\in[0,1]\).
  2. Write \(2\,\mathrm{TV}=\int|p-q|=\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|\,(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\).

    Lower bound. Since \(\sqrt p+\sqrt q\ge|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|\), \(\;2\,\mathrm{TV}\ge\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2 = 2H^2\), i.e. \(\mathrm{TV}\ge H^2\).

    Upper bound. By Cauchy–Schwarz, \[ 2\,\mathrm{TV}\le\sqrt{\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2}\;\sqrt{\int(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2} = \sqrt{2H^2}\,\sqrt{2+2\rho}. \] Since \(\rho=1-H^2\), \(\;2+2\rho=4-2H^2\), so \(2\,\mathrm{TV}\le\sqrt{2H^2(4-2H^2)}=2H\sqrt{2-H^2}\), i.e. \(\mathrm{TV}\le H\sqrt{2-H^2}\).

  3. \(\rho(P,Q)=\int\sqrt{\prod_i p_i\prod_i q_i}=\prod_i\int\sqrt{p_iq_i}=\prod_i\rho(P_i,Q_i)\). For \(n\) i.i.d. copies, \(\rho(P^{\otimes n},Q^{\otimes n})=\rho(P,Q)^n\), hence \(1-H^2(P^{\otimes n},Q^{\otimes n})=(1-H^2(P,Q))^n\). Interpretation: the affinity multiplies across independent samples, so as soon as \(H^2(P,Q)>0\) the affinity decays geometrically — combined with part (b), \(\mathrm{TV}(P^{\otimes n},Q^{\otimes n})\to 1\) and the two product distributions become perfectly distinguishable. This is the engine behind sample-complexity lower bounds in hypothesis testing.

3. The Bretagnolle–Huber Inequality (information divergence — 30 points)

Pinsker's bound \(\mathrm{TV}\le\sqrt{\tfrac12 D(P\|Q)}\) becomes vacuous once \(D(P\|Q)>2\). The Bretagnolle–Huber inequality \(\ \mathrm{TV}(P,Q)\le\sqrt{1-e^{-D(P\|Q)}}\ \) never is. Let \(\rho(P,Q)=\int\sqrt{pq}\) be the Bhattacharyya affinity.

  1. Show \(\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)^2 \le 1-\rho(P,Q)^2\). Hint: \(2\,\mathrm{TV}=\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\) and Cauchy–Schwarz. (10 pts)
  2. Show \(\rho(P,Q)\ge e^{-\frac12 D(P\|Q)}\). Hint: write \(\rho=\mathbb{E}_P[\sqrt{q/p}]\) and apply Jensen to the convex map \(u\mapsto e^{u}\). (10 pts)
  3. Combine (a),(b) to obtain Bretagnolle–Huber, and compare it to Pinsker numerically at \(D(P\|Q)=4\). (10 pts)
Solution: Bretagnolle–Huber.
  1. By Cauchy–Schwarz, \[ 2\,\mathrm{TV}=\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q) \le\sqrt{\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2}\sqrt{\int(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2}. \] Now \(\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2 = 2-2\rho\) and \(\int(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2 = 2+2\rho\), so the right side is \(\sqrt{(2-2\rho)(2+2\rho)}=2\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\). Hence \(\mathrm{TV}\le\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\), i.e. \(\mathrm{TV}^2\le 1-\rho^2\).
  2. \(\rho=\int\sqrt{pq}=\int p\sqrt{q/p}=\mathbb{E}_P\big[\sqrt{q/p}\big]=\mathbb{E}_P\big[e^{\frac12\log(q/p)}\big]\). Since \(u\mapsto e^u\) is convex, Jensen gives \[ \rho \ge e^{\,\frac12\,\mathbb{E}_P[\log(q/p)]} = e^{-\frac12 D(P\|Q)}. \]
  3. Combining, \(\mathrm{TV}^2\le 1-\rho^2\le 1-e^{-D(P\|Q)}\), so \(\mathrm{TV}(P,Q)\le\sqrt{1-e^{-D(P\|Q)}}\). The right side is always \(<1\), so the bound is never vacuous. At \(D=4\): Pinsker gives \(\sqrt{4/2}=\sqrt2\approx1.41\) (useless, since \(\mathrm{TV}\le1\) always), whereas Bretagnolle–Huber gives \(\sqrt{1-e^{-4}}\approx\sqrt{0.982}\approx 0.991\) — still a genuine constraint. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

4. A PAC-Bayes Generalization Bound (variational bound — 30 points)

Let \(S=\{z_1,\dots,z_n\}\) be i.i.d., a loss \(\ell(w,z)\in[0,1]\), population risk \(L(w)=\mathbb{E}_z[\ell(w,z)]\), and empirical risk \(\hat L(w)=\frac1n\sum_i\ell(w,z_i)\). Fix a data-independent prior \(P\) over hypotheses \(w\); the posterior \(Q\) may depend on \(S\). This problem turns the Donsker–Varadhan change-of-measure trick into a learning guarantee.

  1. Change of measure. For any function \(h(w)\) and any \(Q\ll P\), prove \(\ \mathbb{E}_{w\sim Q}[h(w)] \le D(Q\|P) + \log\mathbb{E}_{w\sim P}[e^{h(w)}]\). (8 pts)
  2. Fix \(\lambda>0\). Using Hoeffding's lemma \(\big(\mathbb{E}_S e^{\lambda(L(w)-\hat L(w))}\le e^{\lambda^2/(8n)}\) for each fixed \(w\big)\), show that with probability \(\ge 1-\delta\) over \(S\), \[ \mathbb{E}_{w\sim P}\Big[e^{\lambda(L(w)-\hat L(w)) - \lambda^2/(8n)}\Big]\le \tfrac1\delta. \] Hint: Fubini (\(P\) is data-independent) then Markov. (12 pts)
  3. Combine (a),(b) to show that, with probability \(\ge 1-\delta\), simultaneously for all \(Q\), \[ \mathbb{E}_{w\sim Q}[L(w)] \le \mathbb{E}_{w\sim Q}[\hat L(w)] + \frac{D(Q\|P)+\log\frac1\delta}{\lambda} + \frac{\lambda}{8n}. \] Interpret the role of \(D(Q\|P)\). (10 pts)
Solution: PAC-Bayes.
  1. \[ \mathbb{E}_Q[h]-D(Q\|P)=\mathbb{E}_Q\Big[h-\log\tfrac{dQ}{dP}\Big]=\mathbb{E}_Q\Big[\log\big(e^{h}\tfrac{dP}{dQ}\big)\Big] \le\log\mathbb{E}_Q\Big[e^{h}\tfrac{dP}{dQ}\Big]=\log\mathbb{E}_P[e^{h}], \] by Jensen (concavity of \(\log\)). Rearranging gives \(\mathbb{E}_Q[h]\le D(Q\|P)+\log\mathbb{E}_P[e^h]\).
  2. Let \(M=\mathbb{E}_P\big[e^{\lambda(L(w)-\hat L(w))-\lambda^2/(8n)}\big]\), a function of \(S\). Since \(P\) is independent of \(S\), Fubini and Hoeffding's lemma give \[ \mathbb{E}_S[M]=\mathbb{E}_P\Big[e^{-\lambda^2/(8n)}\,\mathbb{E}_S e^{\lambda(L(w)-\hat L(w))}\Big]\le\mathbb{E}_P\big[e^{-\lambda^2/(8n)}e^{\lambda^2/(8n)}\big]=1. \] \(M\ge0\), so by Markov \(\Pr_S\!\big(M\ge\tfrac1\delta\big)\le\delta\). Hence with probability \(\ge1-\delta\), \(M\le\tfrac1\delta\).
  3. On that event apply (a) with \(h(w)=\lambda(L(w)-\hat L(w))-\lambda^2/(8n)\): \[ \mathbb{E}_Q\big[\lambda(L-\hat L)-\tfrac{\lambda^2}{8n}\big]\le D(Q\|P)+\log M\le D(Q\|P)+\log\tfrac1\delta. \] The bound on \(M\) holds uniformly in \(Q\) (the event does not depend on \(Q\)). Dividing by \(\lambda\) and rearranging, \[ \mathbb{E}_Q[L]\le\mathbb{E}_Q[\hat L]+\frac{D(Q\|P)+\log\frac1\delta}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda}{8n}. \] The KL term \(D(Q\|P)\) is a complexity penalty: a posterior that strays far from the prior pays a larger generalization gap. (Optimizing \(\lambda\sim\sqrt{8n\,D(Q\|P)}\) yields the familiar \(\sqrt{D(Q\|P)/n}\) rate.) The change-of-measure inequality from (a) is exactly the one-sided Donsker–Varadhan bound, here applied to generalization rather than to mutual information.

5. The Gibbs Variational Principle (variational bound — 30 points)

Let \(Q\) be a reference distribution and \(f\) a function with \(Z:=\mathbb{E}_Q[e^{f}]<\infty\). Define the Gibbs (tilted) distribution \(P^\star\) by \(\dfrac{dP^\star}{dQ}=\dfrac{e^{f}}{Z}\). We prove the variational principle \[ \log Z \;=\; \sup_{P\ll Q}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_P[f]-D(P\|Q)\Big\}, \] attained uniquely at \(P^\star\).

  1. Verify that \(P^\star\) achieves the value: \(\ \mathbb{E}_{P^\star}[f]-D(P^\star\|Q)=\log Z\). (8 pts)
  2. Show that for every \(P\ll Q\), \(\ \mathbb{E}_P[f]-D(P\|Q)\le\log Z\), with equality iff \(P=P^\star\). Hint: the gap is a KL divergence to \(P^\star\). (12 pts)
  3. Specialize to recover the Donsker–Varadhan lower bound on mutual information: for any critic \(T(x,y)\), \(\ I(X;Y)\ge\mathbb{E}_{p(x,y)}[T]-\log\mathbb{E}_{p(x)p(y)}[e^{T}]\). (10 pts)
Solution: Gibbs Variational Principle.
  1. \(D(P^\star\|Q)=\mathbb{E}_{P^\star}\big[\log\tfrac{dP^\star}{dQ}\big]=\mathbb{E}_{P^\star}[f-\log Z]=\mathbb{E}_{P^\star}[f]-\log Z\). Hence \(\mathbb{E}_{P^\star}[f]-D(P^\star\|Q)=\log Z\).
  2. For any \(P\ll Q\), \[ \log Z-\big(\mathbb{E}_P[f]-D(P\|Q)\big) =\mathbb{E}_P\Big[\log\tfrac{dP}{dQ}-f+\log Z\Big] =\mathbb{E}_P\Big[\log\tfrac{dP/dQ}{e^{f}/Z}\Big] =\mathbb{E}_P\Big[\log\tfrac{dP}{dP^\star}\Big]=D(P\|P^\star)\ge0. \] Thus \(\mathbb{E}_P[f]-D(P\|Q)\le\log Z\), with equality iff \(D(P\|P^\star)=0\), i.e. \(P=P^\star\).
  3. Take \(Q=p(x)p(y)\) and \(f=T(x,y)\). The principle gives, for the choice \(P=p(x,y)\), \[ \mathbb{E}_{p(x,y)}[T]-D\big(p(x,y)\,\|\,p(x)p(y)\big)\le\log\mathbb{E}_{p(x)p(y)}[e^{T}]. \] Since \(D\big(p(x,y)\|p(x)p(y)\big)=I(X;Y)\), rearranging yields \(I(X;Y)\ge\mathbb{E}_{p(x,y)}[T]-\log\mathbb{E}_{p(x)p(y)}[e^{T}]\), the Donsker–Varadhan bound. The Gibbs principle thus exposes both the bound and its optimal critic: equality needs \(p(x,y)=P^\star\), i.e. \(T=\log\frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}+\text{const}\).

6. The Importance-Weighted Bound (IWAE) (variational bound — 30 points)

For a latent-variable model \(p(x)=\int p(x,z)\,dz\) and a proposal \(q(z\mid x)\), draw \(z_1,\dots,z_K\overset{\text{iid}}\sim q(\cdot\mid x)\) and let \(w_i=\dfrac{p(x,z_i)}{q(z_i\mid x)}\). Define the \(K\)-sample bound \[ \mathcal L_K = \mathbb{E}_{z_{1:K}}\!\left[\log\frac1K\sum_{i=1}^K w_i\right]. \]

  1. Show \(\mathcal L_1\) is the ELBO and \(\mathcal L_K\le\log p(x)\) for every \(K\). (8 pts)
  2. Show \(\mathcal L_K\) is non-decreasing in \(K\): \(\ \mathcal L_{K+1}\ge\mathcal L_K\). Hint: write the \((K{+}1)\)-average as the average over the \(K{+}1\) leave-one-out \(K\)-averages, then apply Jensen. (14 pts)
  3. Show \(\mathcal L_K\to\log p(x)\) as \(K\to\infty\) (state the integrability you use), so more samples tighten the bound to the true log-evidence. (8 pts)
Solution: IWAE.
  1. \(\mathcal L_1=\mathbb{E}_q[\log w]=\mathbb{E}_q\big[\log\tfrac{p(x,z)}{q(z\mid x)}\big]\), the ELBO. For any \(K\), since \(\log\) is concave, Jensen gives \[ \mathcal L_K=\mathbb{E}\Big[\log\tfrac1K\textstyle\sum_i w_i\Big]\le\log\mathbb{E}\Big[\tfrac1K\sum_i w_i\Big]=\log\mathbb{E}_q[w] =\log\!\int q\,\frac{p(x,z)}{q}=\log p(x). \]
  2. Let \(I_K=\frac1K\sum_{i=1}^K w_i\). For \(K{+}1\) samples, averaging the \(K{+}1\) leave-one-out means \(I_K^{(-j)}=\frac1K\sum_{i\ne j}w_i\) recovers the full average: \[ \frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{j=1}^{K+1} I_K^{(-j)} = \frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{j=1}^{K+1}\frac1K\sum_{i\ne j}w_i = \frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{i=1}^{K+1}w_i = I_{K+1}. \] Hence, by concavity of \(\log\), \[ \mathcal L_{K+1}=\mathbb{E}\big[\log I_{K+1}\big] =\mathbb{E}\Big[\log\tfrac{1}{K+1}\textstyle\sum_j I_K^{(-j)}\Big] \ge\mathbb{E}\Big[\tfrac{1}{K+1}\sum_j\log I_K^{(-j)}\Big] =\mathbb{E}\big[\log I_K^{(-1)}\big]=\mathcal L_K, \] since each \(I_K^{(-j)}\) is an average of \(K\) i.i.d. weights, distributed exactly like \(I_K\).
  3. Assume \(\mathbb{E}_q[w]=p(x)<\infty\). By the strong law of large numbers \(I_K=\frac1K\sum_i w_i\to\mathbb{E}_q[w]=p(x)\) almost surely, so \(\log I_K\to\log p(x)\). The sequence \(\mathcal L_K\) is monotone (part b) and bounded above by \(\log p(x)\) (part a); together with \(\log I_K\to\log p(x)\) and uniform integrability (e.g. \(\mathbb{E}_q[w\log^+ w]<\infty\)), \(\mathcal L_K\uparrow\log p(x)\). Thus the IWAE bound interpolates from the ELBO up to the exact log-evidence as \(K\) grows.
