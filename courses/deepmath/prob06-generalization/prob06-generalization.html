Why Generalization?

Train loss is not the goal

Two Numbers, One Training Run

Unknown at training time

what we actually care about

The gap

Test minus train

Minimizing one number, hoping about another: needs a theorem

The Setup — Data, Hypothesis, Loss

Data: i.i.d. draws from an unknown distribution $\mathcal{D}$

$$Z_1, \dots, Z_n \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{D}, \qquad Z_i = (X_i, Y_i)$$
  • Hypothesis $h$: a candidate model (classifier, predictor)
  • Loss $\ell(h, z) \in [0,1]$: how badly $h$ handles example $z$

Example: 0/1 loss, $\ell(h,(x,y)) = \mathbf{1}\{h(x) \neq y\}$

True Risk — What We Want

Expected loss on a fresh example:

$$R(h) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}}\big[\ell(h, Z)\big]$$
  • A fixed number for each $h$
  • Depends on $\mathcal{D}$: not computable

For 0/1 loss: $R(h)$ = true error rate of $h$

Empirical Risk — What We Compute

Average loss on the sample:

$$\hat{R}_n(h) \;=\; \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \ell(h, Z_i)$$
  • A sample mean: exactly a sample mean $\bar{X}_n$
  • Random: new sample, new value

Training loss, test-set accuracy: both instances of $\hat{R}_n$

Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM)

The universal recipe of machine learning:

$$\hat{h} \;=\; \operatorname*{argmin}_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \; \hat{R}_n(h) \qquad \text{over a class } \mathcal{H}$$
  • $\mathcal{H}$: the models the training procedure can output
  • Pick the one with the smallest training loss

SGD on a fixed architecture: approximate ERM over its weight settings

The Generalization Gap

The quantity that decides whether training was honest:

$$\text{gap}(h) \;=\; R(h) - \hat{R}_n(h)$$
  • Small gap: training loss is a trustworthy preview of reality
  • Large gap: the model memorized, numbers lie

Generalization = the gap is small, provably, with high probability.

The Overfitting Picture

model complexity error true risk training loss gap sweet spot

Training loss keeps falling. True risk turns back up: the gap grows.

Worked Example — The Memorizer

Labels: independent fair coins, unrelated to $x$. Model: a lookup table

$$h_{\text{mem}}(x) = \begin{cases} Y_i & \text{if } x = X_i \text{ (seen in training)} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
$$\hat{R}_n(h_{\text{mem}}) = 0, \qquad R(h_{\text{mem}}) = \tfrac{1}{2}, \qquad \text{gap} = \tfrac{1}{2}$$

Perfect training score, coin-flip reality: the gap can be huge

Why Concentration Is Not Enough

Concentration: for one fixed $h$, $\hat{R}_n(h) \approx R(h)$

  • Fixed means: chosen before seeing the sample
  • But ERM picks $\hat{h}$ after looking at the sample
  • $\hat{h}$ hunts for lucky fluctuations: exactly where bounds fail

Need: one guarantee covering every $h$ at once — uniform convergence.

The Route

Two tools, one assembly:

Hoeffding's lemma bounded $\Rightarrow$ sub-Gaussian Hoeffding's ineq. one $h$: exponential tail Union bound pay for many looks Uniform bound every $h$ at once ERM works

02

Recall — the Concentration Toolkit

Markov, Chernoff, MGF — refreshed, not re-proved

Recall — Markov & Chernoff

Recall (Chernoff bound). For any random variable $X$ and all $t \gt 0$:

$$\Pr(X \geq \alpha) \;\leq\; M_X(t)\, e^{-t\alpha}, \qquad M_X(t) = \mathbb{E}\big[e^{tX}\big]$$
  • Engine: Markov on the transformed variable $e^{tX}$
  • $t$ is free: optimize it at the end

Recall — MGF Rules

Recall (MGF calculus). For independent $X$, $Y$ and constant $c$:

$$M_{X+Y}(t) = M_X(t)\, M_Y(t), \qquad M_{cX}(t) = M_X(ct)$$
  • Independence turns sums into products
  • Scaling just rescales the argument

Both proved earlier in the course; used constantly today

Recall — The Gaussian MGF

Recall (Gaussian MGF). For $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{t(X - \mu)}\big] \;=\; \exp\!\left( \frac{t^2 \sigma^2}{2} \right) \quad \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R}$$
  • Proved by completing the square
  • Today's yardstick: this exact shape, $e^{t^2 \cdot (\cdot) / 2}$

What Chernoff Needed — and What We Have

The fair coin: computed $M(t)$ exactly, then optimized $t$

  • A loss $\ell(h, Z)$: distribution unknown (depends on $\mathcal{D}$, on $h$)
  • Exact MGF: unavailable
  • All we know: $\ell \in [0,1]$ — bounded

Today's first job: an MGF bound from boundedness alone.

03

Hoeffding's Lemma

Bounded ⇒ Gaussian-like MGF

The Goal — A Universal MGF Bound

Given only: $X \in [a, b]$ and $\mathbb{E}[X] = 0$. Wanted:

$$M_X(t) = \mathbb{E}\big[e^{tX}\big] \;\leq\; e^{\,c\, t^2} \qquad \text{for all } t, \text{ some constant } c$$
  • Same shape as the Gaussian MGF: $e^{t^2\sigma^2/2}$
  • $c$ must depend only on $a$, $b$ — not on the distribution

Three small techniques first, then the lemma

Technique — Variance of a Bounded Variable

Lemma (bounded variance). If $Y \in [a, b]$, then:

$$\mathrm{Var}(Y) \;\leq\; \frac{(b-a)^2}{4}$$
  • Width $b - a$ caps the spread — no distribution knowledge needed
  • The $4$: deviations from the midpoint are at most half the width

Technique — Proof of the Variance Bound

Compare to the midpoint $m = \tfrac{a+b}{2}$:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Var}(Y) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\big[(Y - \mathbb{E}[Y])^2\big] \;&\stackrel{(\text{mean minimizes})}{\leq}\; \mathbb{E}\big[(Y - m)^2\big] \\[4pt] &\stackrel{(|Y - m| \leq \frac{b-a}{2})}{\leq}\; \left( \frac{b-a}{2} \right)^{\!2} = \frac{(b-a)^2}{4} \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

First step: $c \mapsto \mathbb{E}[(Y-c)^2]$ is a parabola minimized at $c = \mathbb{E}[Y]$

Tight: $Y = a$ or $b$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each achieves it

Technique — Tilted Distributions

Reweight the pmf by $e^{tx}$, renormalize by $M(t)$:

$$p_t(x) \;=\; \frac{e^{tx}\, p_X(x)}{M_X(t)} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \sum_x p_t(x) = \frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]}{M_X(t)} = 1$$
  • A genuine distribution for every $t$
  • $t \gt 0$: mass shifts toward large $x$; $t = 0$: original

Key: $p_t$ lives on the same support as $p_X$ — still inside $[a,b]$

Tilting — the Picture

gray: original $p$ blue: tilted ($t \gt 0$) $a$ $b$

Tilting slides mass along the support — but never off it

Technique — Taylor with Lagrange Remainder

For twice-differentiable $f$, some $\xi$ between $0$ and $t$:

$$f(t) \;=\; f(0) + f'(0)\, t + \frac{f''(\xi)}{2}\, t^2$$
  • Exact equality: the error hides in $f''(\xi)$
  • Bound $f''$ everywhere $\Rightarrow$ bound $f$ everywhere

The CLT sketch used Taylor near $0$; today the remainder does the work

Theorem — Hoeffding's Lemma

Theorem 1 (Hoeffding's lemma, 1963).

If $X \in [a, b]$ almost surely and $\mathbb{E}[X] = 0$, then for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{tX}\big] \;\leq\; \exp\!\left( \frac{t^2 (b-a)^2}{8} \right)$$

A Gaussian MGF with $\sigma^2 = \tfrac{(b-a)^2}{4}$ — from boundedness alone

Hoeffding's Lemma — Proof Overview

Target: $\psi(t) = \log \mathbb{E}[e^{tX}] \leq \tfrac{t^2 (b-a)^2}{8}$

  1. Start flat: $\psi(0) = 0$ and $\psi'(0) = 0$
  2. Identify: $\psi''(t)$ = variance under the tilted distribution
  3. Cap: tilted variance $\leq \tfrac{(b-a)^2}{4}$ (bounded support)
  4. Integrate: Taylor with remainder, exponentiate

All three techniques, in order. Watch step 3: boundedness enters once.

Lemma Proof — Step 1: Start Flat

Log-MGF $\psi(t) = \log M(t)$, with $M(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]$:

$$\begin{aligned} \psi(0) \;&=\; \log M(0) \;\stackrel{(e^0 = 1)}{=}\; \log 1 \;=\; 0 \\[4pt] \psi'(t) \;&=\; \frac{M'(t)}{M(t)} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \psi'(0) \;\stackrel{(M'(0) = \mathbb{E}[X])}{=}\; \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{1} \;=\; 0 \end{aligned}$$

$M'(0) = \mathbb{E}[X]$: the moment machine. Zero mean kills the linear term.

Lemma Proof — Step 2: First Derivative Is a Tilted Mean

Differentiate under the expectation:

$$\psi'(t) \;=\; \frac{M'(t)}{M(t)} \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}\big[X e^{tX}\big]}{M(t)} \;=\; \sum_x x \, \frac{e^{tx} p_X(x)}{M(t)} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{t}[X]$$
  • $\mathbb{E}_t$: expectation under the tilted distribution $p_t$
  • Slope of the log-MGF = tilted mean

Lemma Proof — Step 2: Second Derivative Is a Tilted Variance

Quotient rule on $\psi' = M'/M$:

$$\psi''(t) \;=\; \frac{M''(t)}{M(t)} - \left( \frac{M'(t)}{M(t)} \right)^{\!2} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_t\big[X^2\big] - \big( \mathbb{E}_t[X] \big)^2 \;=\; \mathrm{Var}_t(X)$$
  • Same pattern as $M''(0) = \mathbb{E}[X^2]$, now at tilt $t$
  • Curvature of the log-MGF = variance under tilting

Lemma Proof — Step 3: Cap the Curvature

The tilted distribution still lives on $[a, b]$:

$$\psi''(t) \;=\; \mathrm{Var}_t(X) \;\stackrel{(\text{bounded variance lemma})}{\leq}\; \frac{(b-a)^2}{4} \qquad \text{for every } t$$

Boundedness enters here and only here: tilting moves mass, never widens the support.

Lemma Proof — Step 4: Integrate the Cap

Taylor with Lagrange remainder at $0$, some $\xi$ between $0$ and $t$:

$$\psi(t) \;=\; \underbrace{\psi(0)}_{0} + \underbrace{\psi'(0)}_{0}\, t + \frac{\psi''(\xi)}{2}\, t^2 \;\leq\; \frac{(b-a)^2}{8}\, t^2$$

Exponentiate both sides:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{tX}\big] = e^{\psi(t)} \;\leq\; \exp\!\left( \frac{t^2 (b-a)^2}{8} \right) \qquad \blacksquare$$

Hoeffding's Lemma — Proof Summary

$$\psi(t) \stackrel{(\text{Taylor})}{=} \frac{\psi''(\xi)}{2} t^2 \stackrel{(\psi'' = \mathrm{Var}_t)}{=} \frac{\mathrm{Var}_\xi(X)}{2} t^2 \stackrel{(\text{support} \subseteq [a,b])}{\leq} \frac{(b-a)^2}{8} t^2$$
  • The trick: read $\psi''$ as a variance, then cap it
  • Weight carrier: tilting preserves the support

Zero distribution knowledge used — only the interval $[a,b]$

Sanity Check — Fair Signs

$X = \pm 1$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each: mean $0$, $[a,b] = [-1,1]$

$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{tX}\big] = \frac{e^t + e^{-t}}{2} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{t^{2k}}{(2k)!} \;\stackrel{((2k)! \,\geq\, 2^k k!)}{\leq}\; \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(t^2/2)^k}{k!} = e^{t^2/2}$$

Lemma's promise: $\exp\!\big( \tfrac{t^2 \cdot 2^2}{8} \big) = e^{t^2/2}$ — same answer

Direct series check confirms the constant $8$ is right (and unimprovable here)

Corollary — Any Mean, Same Width

For $X \in [a,b]$ with any mean $\mu$: apply the lemma to $X - \mu$

$$X - \mu \in [a - \mu,\; b - \mu] \;\;\text{(same width)} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \mathbb{E}\big[e^{t(X - \mu)}\big] \leq \exp\!\left( \frac{t^2 (b-a)^2}{8} \right)$$
  • Centering shifts the interval, width unchanged
  • This is the form used from now on

Hoeffding's Lemma — What It Buys

  • Every bounded variable: MGF under a Gaussian envelope
  • Losses in $[0,1]$: envelope $e^{t^2/8}$, no matter the data distribution
  • Chernoff becomes usable without knowing $M(t)$

Variables with a Gaussian MGF envelope deserve a name: sub-Gaussian.

04

Sub-Gaussian Variables

One envelope, many distributions

Definition — Sub-Gaussian

Definition (sub-Gaussian, variance proxy $\sigma^2$).

$X$ with mean $\mu$ is $\sigma^2$-sub-Gaussian if for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{t(X - \mu)}\big] \;\leq\; \exp\!\left( \frac{t^2 \sigma^2}{2} \right)$$

$\sigma^2$ is a proxy, not the variance: any valid envelope constant

Example 1 — The Gaussian Itself

$X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$: the Gaussian MGF is

$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{t(X-\mu)}\big] \;=\; e^{t^2\sigma^2/2} \qquad \text{(equality, every } t\text{)}$$
  • $\sigma^2$-sub-Gaussian with the tightest possible proxy
  • The definition is calibrated so the eponym passes exactly

Example 2 — Every Bounded Variable

$X \in [a,b]$: Theorem 1 says $X$ is $\tfrac{(b-a)^2}{4}$-sub-Gaussian

Theorem — Sub-Gaussian Tail Bound

Theorem 2 (sub-Gaussian tails).

If $X$ is $\sigma^2$-sub-Gaussian with mean $\mu$, then for every $\epsilon \gt 0$:

$$\Pr\big( X - \mu \geq \epsilon \big) \;\leq\; \exp\!\left( -\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)$$

The MGF envelope forces Gaussian-type tail decay

Tail Bound — Proof Overview

Target: $\Pr(X - \mu \geq \epsilon) \leq e^{-\epsilon^2 / 2\sigma^2}$

  1. Chernoff: bound by $e^{-t\epsilon}\, \mathbb{E}[e^{t(X-\mu)}]$, free $t \gt 0$
  2. Envelope: replace the MGF by $e^{t^2\sigma^2/2}$
  3. Optimize: minimize the exponent over $t$

Exactly the Chernoff routine — with the lemma standing in for the true MGF

Tail Proof — Steps 1–2: Chernoff + Envelope

For any $t \gt 0$:

$$\Pr(X - \mu \geq \epsilon) \;\stackrel{(\text{Chernoff})}{\leq}\; e^{-t\epsilon}\; \mathbb{E}\big[e^{t(X-\mu)}\big] \;\stackrel{(\text{sub-Gaussian})}{\leq}\; \exp\!\left( \frac{t^2\sigma^2}{2} - t\epsilon \right)$$

Unknown distribution just left the argument: only $\sigma^2$ remains

Tail Proof — Step 3: Optimize the Free $t$

Minimize the parabola $g(t) = \tfrac{\sigma^2}{2} t^2 - \epsilon t$:

$$g'(t) = \sigma^2 t - \epsilon = 0 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; t^* = \frac{\epsilon}{\sigma^2} \;(\gt 0) \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; g(t^*) = \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\frac{\epsilon^2}{\sigma^4} - \frac{\epsilon^2}{\sigma^2} = -\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sigma^2}$$
$$\Pr(X - \mu \geq \epsilon) \;\leq\; e^{g(t^*)} = \exp\!\left( -\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sigma^2} \right) \qquad \blacksquare$$

Tail Bound — Summary & Two Sides

$$\Pr(X - \mu \geq \epsilon) \stackrel{(\text{Chernoff})}{\leq} e^{-t\epsilon} M_{X-\mu}(t) \stackrel{(\text{envelope})}{\leq} e^{\frac{\sigma^2 t^2}{2} - t\epsilon} \stackrel{(t^* = \epsilon/\sigma^2)}{=} e^{-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sigma^2}}$$

Lower tail: $-X$ is also $\sigma^2$-sub-Gaussian (envelope is even in $t$), so

$$\Pr\big( |X - \mu| \geq \epsilon \big) \;\leq\; 2 \exp\!\left( -\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)$$

The Tail Envelope — Picture

deviation from the mean tail probability Gaussian envelope $\exp\!\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$ actual tail (bounded: dies early)

Sub-Gaussian = the true tail never pokes above the Gaussian envelope

Closure Rule 1 — Scaling

If $X$ is $\sigma^2$-sub-Gaussian, what about $cX$?

$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{t(cX - c\mu)}\big] \;=\; \mathbb{E}\big[e^{(tc)(X - \mu)}\big] \;\stackrel{(\text{def, at } tc)}{\leq}\; e^{\frac{(tc)^2 \sigma^2}{2}} \;=\; e^{\frac{t^2 (c^2\sigma^2)}{2}}$$

$cX$ is $c^2\sigma^2$-sub-Gaussian: proxies scale like variances.

Closure Rule 2 — Independent Sums

Proposition (sums of sub-Gaussians).

$X_1, \dots, X_n$ independent, $X_i$ being $\sigma_i^2$-sub-Gaussian. Then:

$$\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \;\text{ is }\; \Big( \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2 \Big)\text{-sub-Gaussian}$$

Variance proxies add — just like true variances under independence

Sums — Proof (Three Lines)

Center each $X_i$; independence turns the sum into a product:

$$\mathbb{E}\Big[ e^{t \sum_i (X_i - \mu_i)} \Big] \;\stackrel{(\text{indep.} \Rightarrow \text{product})}{=}\; \prod_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}\big[ e^{t(X_i - \mu_i)} \big] \;\stackrel{(\text{each envelope})}{\leq}\; \prod_{i=1}^n e^{\frac{t^2\sigma_i^2}{2}} = e^{\frac{t^2}{2} \sum_i \sigma_i^2} \;\; \blacksquare$$

The MGF product rule carries all the weight

Consequence — Averages Concentrate

$X_i$ i.i.d., each $\sigma^2$-sub-Gaussian. Combine both closure rules:

$$\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i: \qquad \sum_i X_i \to n\sigma^2 \;\;\stackrel{(\text{scale by } 1/n)}{\longrightarrow}\;\; \frac{n\sigma^2}{n^2} = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$$

$\bar{X}_n$ is $\tfrac{\sigma^2}{n}$-sub-Gaussian: averaging shrinks the proxy by $n$.

05

Hoeffding's Inequality

Distribution-free exponential tails for averages

Theorem — Hoeffding's Inequality

Theorem 3 (Hoeffding's inequality, 1963).

$X_1, \dots, X_n$ independent, $X_i \in [a,b]$, common mean $\mu$. For $\epsilon \gt 0$:

$$\Pr\big( \bar{X}_n - \mu \geq \epsilon \big) \;\leq\; \exp\!\left( -\frac{2 n \epsilon^2}{(b-a)^2} \right)$$

Exponential in $n$ — using only the range $[a,b]$

Hoeffding — Proof Overview

Pure assembly of the lemma and the sub-Gaussian calculus:

  1. Lemma: each $X_i$ is $\tfrac{(b-a)^2}{4}$-sub-Gaussian (Thm 1)
  2. Calculus: $\bar{X}_n$ is $\tfrac{(b-a)^2}{4n}$-sub-Gaussian (rules 1+2)
  3. Tails: plug the proxy into Thm 2

No new idea — the sections were the proof

Hoeffding Proof — Step 1: Each Term

Center each variable; apply Hoeffding's lemma to $X_i - \mu \in [a - \mu, b - \mu]$:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[ e^{t (X_i - \mu)} \big] \;\stackrel{(\text{Thm 1, width } b-a)}{\leq}\; \exp\!\left( \frac{t^2 (b-a)^2}{8} \right)$$

Each term: $\sigma^2 = \tfrac{(b-a)^2}{4}$, regardless of its distribution

Hoeffding Proof — Step 2: The Average's Proxy

Sum rule, then scale rule with $c = \tfrac1n$:

$$\sum_{i=1}^n X_i:\;\; n \cdot \frac{(b-a)^2}{4} \qquad\stackrel{(\times\, 1/n^2)}{\Longrightarrow}\qquad \bar{X}_n:\;\; \frac{(b-a)^2}{4n}$$
  • Averaging divides the proxy by $n$
  • Mean of $\bar{X}_n$ is still $\mu$ (linearity)

Hoeffding Proof — Step 3: Read Off the Tail

Theorem 2 with proxy $\sigma^2 = \tfrac{(b-a)^2}{4n}$:

$$\Pr\big( \bar{X}_n - \mu \geq \epsilon \big) \;\leq\; \exp\!\left( -\frac{\epsilon^2}{2 \cdot \frac{(b-a)^2}{4n}} \right) \;=\; \exp\!\left( -\frac{2 n \epsilon^2}{(b-a)^2} \right) \qquad \blacksquare$$

The $2$ upstairs and the $8$ in the lemma are the same constant, moved around

Corollary — Two-Sided Hoeffding

Apply the same bound to $-X_i \in [-b, -a]$ (same width), add the tails:

$$\Pr\big( |\bar{X}_n - \mu| \geq \epsilon \big) \;\leq\; 2 \exp\!\left( -\frac{2 n \epsilon^2}{(b-a)^2} \right)$$
  • For losses in $[0,1]$: $\;\Pr\big( |\bar{X}_n - \mu| \geq \epsilon \big) \leq 2 e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$
  • This is the workhorse form for the rest of the lecture

Hoeffding — Proof Summary

$$\Pr(\bar{X}_n - \mu \geq \epsilon) \;\stackrel{(\text{Chernoff})}{\leq}\; e^{-t\epsilon}\, \prod_i \mathbb{E}\big[e^{\frac{t}{n}(X_i - \mu)}\big] \;\stackrel{(\text{lemma})}{\leq}\; e^{\frac{t^2(b-a)^2}{8n} - t\epsilon} \;\stackrel{(t^*)}{=}\; e^{-\frac{2n\epsilon^2}{(b-a)^2}}$$
  • The trick: lemma replaces the unknown MGF
  • Weight carrier: proxies add, then shrink by $1/n^2$

The Coin Returns — $\Pr(\bar{X}_{100} \geq \tfrac34)$

Fair coin, $[a,b] = [0,1]$, $\mu = \tfrac12$, $\epsilon = \tfrac14$, $n = 100$:

$$\Pr\big( \bar{X}_{100} \geq \tfrac34 \big) \;\leq\; e^{-2 \cdot 100 \cdot (1/4)^2} = e^{-12.5} \approx 3.7 \times 10^{-6}$$

  • Distribution-free: only the range enters

  • Perfect fit for losses: bounded by design

The Union Bound

Many hypotheses = many chances to be lucky

Theorem — The Union Bound

Theorem 4 (union bound / Boole's inequality).

For any events $A_1, \dots, A_m$ (no independence needed):

$$\Pr\!\left( \bigcup_{j=1}^m A_j \right) \;\leq\; \sum_{j=1}^m \Pr(A_j)$$

"Something bad happens" costs at most the sum of the bad probabilities

Union Bound — One-Line Proof

The indicator technique, pointwise:

$$\mathbf{1}\Big\{ \bigcup_j A_j \Big\} \;\leq\; \sum_{j=1}^m \mathbf{1}\{A_j\} \qquad \text{(left side } \leq 1\text{; right counts memberships)}$$

Take expectations; $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}] = \Pr$ on both sides $\;\blacksquare$

Equality iff the events are disjoint — overlap is double-counted

Union Bound — the Picture

sample space $A_1$ $A_2$ $A_3$ $A_4$ overlap counted twice

Union area $\leq$ sum of circle areas: overlap only helps

No independence, no structure: works for any events whatsoever

Many Chances to Be Lucky

Thought experiment: $m = 10{,}000$ useless models (true accuracy $50\%$)

  • Evaluate each on the same $n = 100$ test points
  • One model: $\Pr(\text{shows} \geq 75\%) \leq e^{-12.5} \approx 3.7 \times 10^{-6}$
  • Question: chance that some model shows $\geq 75\%$?

The best-looking of many random guessers can look brilliant

Worked Example — the Model Farm

$A_j$ = "model $j$ shows $\geq 75\%$ on the test set":

$$\Pr\!\left( \bigcup_{j=1}^{10^4} A_j \right) \;\leq\; \sum_{j=1}^{10^4} \Pr(A_j) \;\leq\; 10^4 \times 3.7 \times 10^{-6} \;=\; 3.7\%$$
  • Safe: with $\geq 96\%$ probability, no guesser fakes $75\%$
  • The factor $m$ multiplies the failure probability

Same Farm, Small Test Set

Shrink the test set to $n = 25$:

$$\text{one model: } e^{-2 \cdot 25 \cdot (1/4)^2} = e^{-3.125} \approx 0.044 \qquad \text{union: } 10^4 \times 0.044 = 440 \;\;(\text{vacuous})$$
  • Bound $\gt 1$: says nothing — and rightly so
  • Among $10^4$ guessers, several will fake $75\%$ on $25$ points

$n$ must grow with $\ln m$: the exponent must beat the head count

Budgeting Trick — Split the Failure Probability

Want total failure $\leq \delta$ over $m$ events? Give each event $\delta / m$:

$$\Pr(A_j) \leq \frac{\delta}{m} \;\;\text{for each } j \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \Pr\!\Big( \bigcup_j A_j \Big) \leq m \cdot \frac{\delta}{m} = \delta$$
  • Statistics name: Bonferroni correction (multiple comparisons)
  • Exponential tails absorb the split: $\delta/m$ costs only $+\ln m$ in the exponent

Union Bound — What It Buys

  • Converts per-hypothesis guarantees into simultaneous ones
  • Cost: factor $m$ in probability = $+\ln m$ inside an exponential
  • Zero assumptions: works however the events depend on each other

Hoeffding gives $e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$ per $h$; union bound pays $|\mathcal{H}|$: assemble now.

The Generalization Theorem

Uniform convergence over a finite class

Setup — Finite Hypothesis Classes

$\mathcal{H} = \{h_1, \dots, h_{|\mathcal{H}|}\}$: finitely many candidate models

Decision stumps

Threshold one of $d$ pixels: $|\mathcal{H}| = 256\, d$

Quantized nets

$k$ bits of weights total: $|\mathcal{H}| = 2^k$

Any model stored in $k$ bits: a finite class of size $2^k$

Setup — The Two Risks, Recalled

Recall. Sample $Z_1, \dots, Z_n \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{D}$; loss $\ell(h, z) \in [0,1]$:

$$R(h) = \mathbb{E}\big[\ell(h, Z)\big], \qquad \hat{R}_n(h) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \ell(h, Z_i)$$
  • ERM output: $\hat{h}$ minimizing $\hat{R}_n$ over $\mathcal{H}$
  • Best in class: $h^* = \operatorname*{argmin}_{h \in \mathcal{H}} R(h)$

Key Observation — Fixed $h$ Is Just a Coin

Fix $h$ before the sample. Set $W_i = \ell(h, Z_i)$:

  • $W_1, \dots, W_n$ i.i.d. (functions of i.i.d. $Z_i$)
  • $W_i \in [0,1]$, and $\mathbb{E}[W_i] = R(h)$
  • $\hat{R}_n(h) = \bar{W}_n$: a bounded sample mean
$$\Pr\big( |\hat{R}_n(h) - R(h)| \geq \epsilon \big) \;\stackrel{(\text{Thm 3, two-sided})}{\leq}\; 2 e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$$

The Trap — $\hat{h}$ Is Not Fixed

Tempting: apply the box above to $h = \hat{h}$. Invalid.

  • $\hat{h}$ is chosen by looking at $Z_1, \dots, Z_n$
  • $\ell(\hat{h}, Z_i)$ are not i.i.d. draws — $\hat{h}$ ties them together
  • ERM selects for hypotheses whose luck ran high

The model farm showed it: the best of $10^4$ guessers looks great at $n = 25$

The Fix — Bound Every $h$ at Once

Uniform convergence event: no hypothesis deviates

$$G_\epsilon \;=\; \Big\{ \; |\hat{R}_n(h) - R(h)| \leq \epsilon \;\;\text{ for all } h \in \mathcal{H} \; \Big\}$$
  • On $G_\epsilon$: whatever $h$ any algorithm picks, its gap is $\leq \epsilon$
  • Data-dependence handled by covering all choices in advance

Theorem — Finite-Class Generalization

Theorem 5 (uniform convergence, finite class).

$\mathcal{H}$ finite, $Z_i$ i.i.d., $\ell \in [0,1]$, $\delta \in (0,1)$. With probability $\geq 1 - \delta$:

$$\sup_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \big| R(h) - \hat{R}_n(h) \big| \;\leq\; \sqrt{ \frac{ \ln\!\big( 2 |\mathcal{H}| / \delta \big) }{ 2n } }$$

One sample, one guarantee, every hypothesis — the payoff of the whole lecture

Generalization Theorem — Proof Overview

Target: $\Pr\big( \exists h: |\hat{R}_n(h) - R(h)| \gt \epsilon \big) \leq \delta$ for the right $\epsilon$

  1. One $h$: two-sided Hoeffding, $2 e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$
  2. All $h$: union bound over $\mathcal{H}$, factor $|\mathcal{H}|$
  3. Invert: set the bound to $\delta$, solve for $\epsilon$

Hoeffding + union bound + algebra — nothing else

Proof — Step 1: One Hypothesis

Bad event for one hypothesis: $B_h = \big\{ |\hat{R}_n(h) - R(h)| \gt \epsilon \big\}$

$$\Pr(B_h) \;\stackrel{(\text{key observation})}{\leq}\; 2 e^{-2n\epsilon^2} \qquad \text{for each fixed } h \in \mathcal{H}$$
  • Valid per $h$: each $B_h$ concerns a fixed hypothesis
  • The $B_h$ overlap and depend on each other — irrelevant next

Proof — Step 2: Union Over the Class

Some hypothesis deviates = the union of the $B_h$:

$$\Pr\!\left( \bigcup_{h \in \mathcal{H}} B_h \right) \;\stackrel{(\text{Thm 4})}{\leq}\; \sum_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \Pr(B_h) \;\stackrel{(\text{step 1})}{\leq}\; 2\, |\mathcal{H}|\, e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$$

Dependence between hypotheses: exactly what the union bound ignores for free

Proof — Step 3: Solve for $\epsilon$

Set the failure budget to $\delta$ and invert:

$$2 |\mathcal{H}| e^{-2n\epsilon^2} = \delta \;\Longleftrightarrow\; e^{2n\epsilon^2} = \frac{2|\mathcal{H}|}{\delta} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \epsilon = \sqrt{ \frac{\ln(2|\mathcal{H}|/\delta)}{2n} }$$

With this $\epsilon$: $\Pr\big( \sup_h |\hat{R}_n(h) - R(h)| \gt \epsilon \big) \leq \delta \quad \blacksquare$

Generalization Theorem — Proof Summary

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr\Big( \sup_h |\hat{R}_n - R| \gt \epsilon \Big) \;&\stackrel{(\text{union})}{\leq}\; \sum_{h} \Pr(B_h) \;\stackrel{(\text{Hoeffding})}{\leq}\; 2|\mathcal{H}| e^{-2n\epsilon^2} \\[6pt] &\stackrel{(\text{set} = \delta)}{\Longrightarrow}\; \epsilon = \sqrt{\tfrac{\ln(2|\mathcal{H}|/\delta)}{2n}} \end{aligned}$$
  • The trick: pay for the search before it happens
  • Weight carrier: exponential tail beats the head count $|\mathcal{H}|$

The $\epsilon$-Tube Picture

hypotheses $h$ (ordered) risk true risk $R(h)$ train estimates (dots) ERM pick

With probability $1 - \delta$: every dot sits inside the $\pm\epsilon$ tube around its curve

Corollary — ERM Nearly Wins

Corollary (ERM guarantee).

On the event of Theorem 5 (probability $\geq 1 - \delta$), with $\epsilon = \sqrt{\tfrac{\ln(2|\mathcal{H}|/\delta)}{2n}}$:

$$R(\hat{h}) \;\leq\; \min_{h \in \mathcal{H}} R(h) \;+\; 2\epsilon$$

The trained model is within $2\epsilon$ of the best the class can do

Corollary — the Three-Hop Proof

On the tube event, with $h^*$ the true-risk minimizer:

$$R(\hat{h}) \;\stackrel{(\text{tube at } \hat{h})}{\leq}\; \hat{R}_n(\hat{h}) + \epsilon \;\stackrel{(\text{ERM: } \hat{R}_n(\hat{h}) \leq \hat{R}_n(h^*))}{\leq}\; \hat{R}_n(h^*) + \epsilon \;\stackrel{(\text{tube at } h^*)}{\leq}\; R(h^*) + 2\epsilon \;\;\blacksquare$$
  • Tube used twice (at $\hat{h}$, at $h^*$): hence $2\epsilon$, not $\epsilon$
  • Middle hop: pure definition of ERM — no probability

Why $2\epsilon$ — the Two Hops in a Picture

risk true risk of best $h^*$ train loss of $h^*$ train loss of ERM pick true risk of ERM pick ERM chose it hop 1 hop 2

Each hop crosses the tube once: $\epsilon$ at $h^*$, $\epsilon$ at $\hat{h}$ — total $2\epsilon$

Reading the Rate — $\ln |\mathcal{H}|$ Is Bits

Class of all models describable in $k$ bits: $|\mathcal{H}| = 2^k$

$$\epsilon \;=\; \sqrt{ \frac{k \ln 2 + \ln(2/\delta)}{2n} } \qquad \text{(description length } k \text{ enters linearly)}$$
  • $\ln|\mathcal{H}|$ = description length of a hypothesis
  • Complexity is measured in bits to write the model down

$\log M$ is the information in a choice among $M$ alternatives

Reading the Rate — Sample Complexity

Invert for $n$: gap $\leq \epsilon$ with confidence $1 - \delta$ needs

$$n \;\geq\; \frac{ \ln|\mathcal{H}| + \ln(2/\delta) }{ 2 \epsilon^2 }$$
  • Per bit of model: $\tfrac{\ln 2}{2\epsilon^2} \approx 139$ samples at $\epsilon = 0.05$
  • Precision dominates: halving $\epsilon$ quadruples $n$

Why ERM Works

And where the naive bound breaks

The License to Train

ERM minimizes a proxy. Theorem 5 certifies the proxy:

$$\begin{aligned} n \gg \ln|\mathcal{H}| \quad&\Longrightarrow\quad \hat{R}_n \approx R \text{ everywhere} \\[6pt] &\Longrightarrow\quad \text{minimizing } \hat{R}_n \approx \text{minimizing } R \end{aligned}$$
  • Training loss becomes a certified preview of test loss
  • That certification is what "learning is possible" means here

Model Size vs. Data — the Trade-Off

The corollary splits the achievable risk in two:

$$R(\hat{h}) \;\leq\; \underbrace{\min_{h \in \mathcal{H}} R(h)}_{\text{approximation: shrinks as } \mathcal{H} \text{ grows}} \;+\; \underbrace{2\sqrt{ \tfrac{\ln(2|\mathcal{H}|/\delta)}{2n} }}_{\text{estimation: grows as } \mathcal{H} \text{ grows}}$$

Richer class: better best-in-class, looser certificate — the U-curve from the opening, explained

The Trade-Off — Picture

More data $n$ pushes the dashed curve down: the optimal class grows with the dataset

Honest Slide — Deep Nets Break This Bound

ResNet-50: $25.6$M params $\times$ 32 bits $\Rightarrow \ln|\mathcal{H}| \approx 5.7 \times 10^8$

$$n = 1.28 \times 10^6 \text{ (ImageNet)}: \qquad \epsilon = \sqrt{ \tfrac{5.7 \times 10^8}{2.56 \times 10^6} } \approx 14.9 \;\;(\text{vacuous: loss} \leq 1)$$
  • Bound: gap $\leq 14.9$. Reality: gap $\approx$ a few percent
  • The bound is sufficient, not necessary
  • Uniformity over all of $\mathcal{H}$: too pessimistic for one trained net
  • Why overparameterized nets generalize: open research (implicit bias of SGD, and more)

What Survives at Deep-Learning Scale

  • The test-set bound: evaluation data is fresh, $|\mathcal{H}| = 1$ — Hoeffding applies exactly
  • Held-out validation, early stopping: union bound over the few models compared
  • The mechanism — concentration vs. selection pressure — explains leaderboard overfitting

Your $4{,}612$-sample test-set certificate is unconditional — whatever the model is.
