01

Forced Errors

When a weak model family must hallucinate

Poor Model ⇒ Errors, Precisely

Theorem (Kalai et al., 2025, §3.3.2).

$$\mathrm{err} \;\ge\; 2\,\mathrm{opt}(\mathcal{G}) \;-\; \frac{\max_c |\mathcal{V}_c|}{\min_c |\mathcal{E}_c|} \;-\; \delta.$$
  • $\mathrm{err}$: probability the model generates an error response
  • $\mathrm{opt}(\mathcal{G}) = \min_{g\in\mathcal{G}} \Pr_{x\sim D}[\,g(x) \ne f(x)\,]$: best error of any thresholded model in the family
  • $\mathcal{V}_c, \mathcal{E}_c$: valid / error responses to prompt $c$; $\delta$: the model's miscalibration

Errors are forced when $2\,\mathrm{opt}(\mathcal{G}) > \max_c|\mathcal{V}_c| / \min_c|\mathcal{E}_c| + \delta$. V3's failures alone do not show this.

Kalai, Nachum, Vempala, and Zhang, "Why Language Models Hallucinate", 2025 — §3.3.2 (from Theorem 1).

A Proven Case: Trigram Models

Two-option task: choose “her mind” or “his mind”; no abstention.

Theorem 3 → Corollary 2 (Kalai et al., 2025).

If $|\mathcal{V}_c| = 1$ and $C = \min_c |\mathcal{E}_c| + 1$: $\;\mathrm{err} \ge 2\,(1 - 1/C)\,\mathrm{opt}(\mathcal{G})$.

Prompts $c_1, c_2$ equally likely ⇒ $C = 2$, $\mathrm{opt}(\mathcal{G}) = 1/2$ ⇒ any trigram model has $\mathrm{err} \ge 1/2$.

Kalai, Nachum, Vempala, and Zhang, "Why Language Models Hallucinate", 2025 — §3.3.2, Theorem 3 and Corollary 2.
02

Calibration Error

From the diagram to one number

The Reliability Diagram

Bin predictions by confidence; plot accuracy against confidence per bin.

Each bar's height gap from the diagonal is that bin's miscalibration.

Expected Calibration Error

Average the per-bin gaps, weighted by how many points land in each bin.

$$\text{ECE} \;=\; \sum_b \frac{n_b}{n}\,\bigl|\,\text{acc}_b - \text{conf}_b\,\bigr|.$$

$n_b$ = points in bin $b$; $\text{acc}_b, \text{conf}_b$ = its accuracy and mean confidence.

Guo, Pleiss, Sun, and Weinberger, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", ICML 2017.

Reading ECE

Each term compares a bin's two numbers.

  • $\text{acc}_b$: fraction of the bin that is correct
  • $\text{conf}_b$: the bin's average stated confidence
  • $n_b / n$: the bin's share of all predictions

$\text{ECE} = 0$ means every bar sits on the diagonal.

Temperature Scaling

Divide the logits by one number $T$ before the softmax.

$$p_i \;=\; \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T)}.$$

$z_i$ = logits; fit $T$ on held-out data to minimize calibration error.

Guo, Pleiss, Sun, and Weinberger, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", ICML 2017.

What Temperature Does

One knob softens or sharpens, without changing which class wins.

  • $T > 1$: flatter probabilities, humbler confidence
  • $T < 1$: sharper probabilities, bolder confidence
  • $T = 1$: leaves the model untouched

The argmax is fixed, so accuracy never changes.

03

Conformal Coverage

A threshold that comes with a proof

The Coverage Guarantee

The prediction set $C(x)$ should contain the true answer with high probability.

$$\Pr[\,y \in C(x)\,] \;\ge\; 1 - \alpha.$$

Choose the miss rate $\alpha$; e.g. $\alpha = 0.10$ gives 90% coverage.

Angelopoulos and Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification", 2021.

Calibrating the Threshold

Score each held-out point by how "surprised" the model is at its true label.

  1. On $n$ calibration points, compute nonconformity scores $s_1, \dots, s_n$.
  2. Set $k = \lceil (n{+}1)(1{-}\alpha) \rceil$ and $\hat q = s_{(k)}$, the $k$-th smallest score; $\hat q = \infty$ if $k = n{+}1$.
  3. At test time, keep every label whose score is at most $\hat q$.

Distinct scores: test rank uniform on $\{1,\dots,n{+}1\}$. With ties (random tie-breaking), only the lower bound survives.

Angelopoulos and Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification", 2021.

Why It Holds

Exchangeable data (A1) + score fixed before calibration (A2) ⇒ the $n{+}1$ scores are exchangeable. A fixed score alone is not enough.

$$\Pr[\,s_{\text{test}} \le \hat q\,] \;\ge\; 1 - \alpha.$$

Across joint draws of calibration and test data, the test rank is uniform (distinct scores). Marginal: averaged over data, not per input.

Angelopoulos and Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification", 2021.

Proof: Setup

  • $s_i = s(X_i, Y_i)$ for $i = 1,\dots,n+1$; $s_{n+1}$ is the test score
  • $s_{(1)} \le \dots \le s_{(n)}$: sorted calibration scores; $\hat q = s_{(k)}$
  • Outline: covered $\iff$ test score ranks $\le k$; exchangeable ⇒ rank uniform
Angelopoulos and Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification", 2021 — Appendix D.

Proof: The Rank Step

Assume distinct scores and $k \le n$.

$$\begin{aligned} \Pr\bigl[Y_{n+1} \in C(X_{n+1})\bigr] &\stackrel{(1)}{=} \Pr\bigl[s_{n+1} \le s_{(k)}\bigr] \stackrel{(2)}{=} \Pr\bigl[\operatorname{rank}(s_{n+1}) \le k\bigr] \\ &\stackrel{(3)}{=} \frac{k}{n+1} \;\stackrel{(4)}{\ge}\; 1-\alpha. \end{aligned}$$
  • (1) definition: $y \in C(x) \iff s(x,y) \le \hat q = s_{(k)}$
  • (2) rank among all $n+1$ scores; distinct ⇒ events equal
  • (3) (A1) + (A2) ⇒ $(s_1,\dots,s_{n+1})$ exchangeable ⇒ rank uniform on $\{1,\dots,n+1\}$
  • (4) $k \ge (n+1)(1-\alpha)$ by the ceiling
Angelopoulos and Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification", 2021 — Appendix D.

Proof (continued): Ties and Edge Cases

  • Upper: $k < (n+1)(1-\alpha) + 1$ ⇒ $\frac{k}{n+1} < 1-\alpha+\frac{1}{n+1}$
  • Ties: break at random; rank $\le k$ still ⇒ $s_{n+1} \le s_{(k)}$, so $\Pr \ge \frac{k}{n+1}$; upper bound may fail
  • $k = n+1$ (only when $\alpha < \frac{1}{n+1}$): $\hat q = \infty$, $C = \mathcal{Y}$, coverage $1$
  • (A2) matters: a score fit on calibration data can break the exchangeability in (3)

$\blacksquare$ Coverage $\ge 1-\alpha$: any model, any distribution, any finite $n$.

Angelopoulos and Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification", 2021 — Appendix D (Theorem D.2 for the upper bound).

Semantic Entropy

Sample answers, cluster by meaning, take entropy over meaning clusters.

$$\text{SE} \;=\; -\sum_{c} p(c)\,\log p(c), \qquad p(c) = \sum_{a \in c} p(a).$$

$c$ = a cluster of same-meaning answers; high SE flags likely hallucination.

Farquhar et al., "Detecting Hallucinations in LLMs Using Semantic Entropy", Nature 2024.
