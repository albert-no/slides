Self-Attention: Query, Key, Value

Each token $s$ projects its hidden state $h_s$ to three vectors:

$$q_s = W_Q h_s,\;\; k_s = W_K h_s,\;\; v_s = W_V h_s.$$

Query scores past keys; softmax-weighted sum reads past values:

$$\alpha_{t,s} \propto \exp\!\bigl(\langle q_t, k_s\rangle/\sqrt d\bigr),\quad o_t = \sum_{s \le t}\alpha_{t,s}\,v_s.$$
Figure: Sergey Levine, CS182sp21 — cs182sp21.github.io.

What Is the KV Cache?

Decoder runs token-by-token.

Past keys and values never change — cache them.

At step $t{+}1$: compute only $q_{t+1}, k_{t+1}, v_{t+1}$; reuse the rest.

$K_1, V_1$ $K_2, V_2$ $K_3, V_3$ $\cdots$ $K_t, V_t$ $K_{t+1}, V_{t+1}$ new

Cache size: $\;2 \cdot L \cdot H \cdot d \cdot T$ scalars.

70B model @ $T{=}128$K $\;\Rightarrow\;$ $\sim 50$ GB.

Compression Constraints

Long context ($32\text{K}, 128\text{K}, 1\text{M}$ tokens) $\Rightarrow$ KV $>$ GPU memory.

  • Online — quantize each $K_s, V_s$ as it arrives, no second look.
  • Data-oblivious — one codebook, no per-model calibration.
  • Attention-preserving — what matters: $\langle q_t, k_s\rangle$, not $\|k - \widetilde k\|$.

01

Setup and the Rotation Idea

Rotation alone produces a known coordinate distribution

Why Scalar Quantization Fails

  • Round each coordinate independently.
  • Implicit assumption: energy spread evenly.
  • Reality (KV, embeddings): a few outliers dominate.
  • Low-bit SQ burns resolution on outliers.
Random rotation flattens energy — every coordinate $\sim 1/\sqrt{d}$, same law.

Random Rotation Creates a Known Law

Haar-uniform $\Pi \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$. Any fixed $x \in S^{d-1}$:

$$Y \;=\; \Pi x \;\sim\; \mathrm{Unif}(S^{d-1}), \qquad \sqrt{d}\,Y_j \;\Rightarrow\; \mathcal{N}(0, 1).$$

Why Gaussian-like. $\Pi$ Haar $\Rightarrow$ $\Pi x$ uniform on the sphere.

Marginal of one coordinate $=$ classical sphere–Gaussian duality (Poincaré's lemma).

Law depends only on $\|x\| = 1$. Algorithm is data-oblivious.

Numerical Picture: 128 Coordinates, Before and After

Fixed $x \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$, a few outlier coordinates dominate the energy:

$x_1, \ldots, x_{128}$ — a handful of large coordinates carry most of the energy (red).

Scalar quantization spends its budget on the 5 outliers; the other 123 coordinates starve.

After a Random Rotation: Energy Spread Evenly

Same vector, coordinates of $y = \Pi x$ for Haar-random orthogonal $\Pi$:

$y = \Pi x$ — every coordinate $\sim 1/\sqrt{d}$, no spikes.

Same energy $\|x\|^2 = \|y\|^2$, redistributed evenly — this is the known law $f_d$ from the last slide.

Two Metrics, Two Quantizers

(Slicing the sphere gives the exact density $f_d(t) \propto (1-t^2)^{(d-3)/2}$ — derivation in the notes.) With that law in hand:

MSE distortion

$$D_{\mathrm{mse}} \;=\; \mathbb{E}\|x - \widetilde x\|^2.$$

Reconstruct the vector.

Lloyd–Max on $f_d$: scalar codebook for the rotated-coord density.

Inner-product distortion

$$D_{\mathrm{prod}} \;=\; \mathbb{E}\bigl(\langle y, x\rangle - \langle y, \widetilde x\rangle\bigr)^{\!2}.$$

Reconstruct linear functionals.

QJL random sketch: $1$ bit/coord, unbiased projection.

MSE-optimal codebook is biased for inner products. Need a separate design.
02

MSE TURBOQUANT

Target $4^{-b}$ (Gaussian limit), algorithm, anchor, theorem

Recall: Gaussian Rate–Distortion $R(D)$

What is the best MSE exponent we can hope for at $b$ bits/coordinate?

Lemma 1: each rotated coordinate $\;Y_j \;\dot\sim\; \mathcal{N}(0, 1/d)$.

Gaussian source, squared-error distortion (Lecture 2, lossy2-gaussian-laplacian):

$$R(D) \;=\; \tfrac12 \log_2\!\bigl(\sigma^2 / D\bigr), \qquad \sigma^2 = \operatorname{Var}(Y_j) = \tfrac1d.$$

$R(D)$ says: to guarantee distortion $D$, you need at least this many bits.

We want the reverse question — fixing the bit budget $R = b$, what's the smallest reachable $D$?

Invert: Target $D(R) = 4^{-b}$

Solve $R(D) = \tfrac12\log_2(\sigma^2/D)$ for $D$ as a function of $R$:

$$R \;=\; \tfrac12\log_2(\sigma^2/D) \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; 2R \;=\; \log_2(\sigma^2/D) \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; D(R) \;=\; \sigma^2 \cdot 2^{-2R}.$$

Set $\sigma^2 = 1/d$ and $R = b$ (one rotated coordinate, $b$ bits):

$$D(R) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{d}\cdot 2^{-2b} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{d}\cdot 4^{-b}.$$

Sum over $d$ coordinates: $\;D_{\mathrm{mse}}(b) \;\asymp\; 4^{-b}$ — the exponent is derived, not assumed.

$4^{-b}$ is the Gaussian limit (Shannon LB, Lecture 2). TURBOQUANT must hit it.

Algorithm: TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{mse}}$

Public state: rotation $\Pi$, scalar codebook $\{c_1, \ldots, c_K\}$ for $f_d$, $K = 2^b$.

TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{mse}}$
  1. Encode$(x)$: rotate $y = \Pi \cdot x$.
  2. For each $j$: $\mathrm{idx}[j] = \arg\min_i |y_j - c_i|$.
  3. Return $\mathrm{idx}$   $\bigl(d \cdot b$ bits$\bigr)$.
  4. Decode$(\mathrm{idx})$: $\widetilde y_j = c_{\mathrm{idx}[j]}$, then $\widetilde x = \Pi^\top \widetilde y$.

Codebook offline via Lloyd–Max on $f_d$. No data-dependent training.

One-Bit Anchor: $1 - 2/\pi$

For $Y \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1/d)$ and sign quantizer $\widehat Y = a \cdot \operatorname{sign}(Y)$:

$$\mathbb{E}[(Y - a \cdot \operatorname{sign} Y)^2] = \mathbb{E} Y^2 - 2 a \cdot \mathbb{E}|Y| + a^2, \qquad a^\star = \mathbb{E}|Y| = \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi d}}.$$

At the optimum: $\mathbb{E}[(Y - a^\star \cdot \operatorname{sign} Y)^2] = \tfrac{1}{d}(1 - \tfrac{2}{\pi})$.

$$\text{total MSE at } b = 1 \;=\; 1 - \tfrac{2}{\pi} \;\approx\; 0.363.$$

Theorem: TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{mse}}$

Theorem (TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{mse}}$).

For every unit $x \in S^{d-1}$:

$$\mathbb{E}\|x - \widetilde x\|^2 \;\le\; \tfrac{\sqrt{3}\,\pi}{2} \cdot 4^{-b} \;\approx\; 2.72 \cdot 4^{-b}.$$

Matches the Gaussian limit $4^{-b}$ up to a small constant.

Proof idea: orthogonal invariance reduces $\|x - \widetilde x\|^2$ to a sum of $d$ i.i.d. scalar-quantization errors on $f_d$, then Panter–Dite bounds each. (Full proof in the notes.)

03

Lower Bound

Sphere SLB + Yao $\;\Rightarrow\;$ exponent $4^{-b}$ unbeatable

Yao + Constant Gap

Lecture 2's converse on $X \sim \mathrm{Unif}(S^{d-1})$ gives $D \ge 4^{-b}$ at $b$ bits/coord.

Yao's principle. Worst-input randomized cost $\ge$ best deterministic cost on the hardest input distribution.

Apply with $\mathrm{Unif}(S^{d-1})$: some $x$ forces $\mathbb{E}\|x - \widehat x\|^2 \ge 4^{-b}$ for every $bd$-bit quantizer.

$$4^{-b} \;\le\; D_{\mathrm{mse}}^\star(b) \;\le\; \tfrac{\sqrt{3}\,\pi}{2} \cdot 4^{-b}.$$
Constant gap $\Rightarrow$ exponent $4^{-b}$ is optimal.
04

Inner-Product Bias

Good MSE doesn't imply good linear-functional preservation

One-Bit MSE Shrinks Projections

Sign quantizer $\widetilde Y_j = a \cdot \operatorname{sign}(Y_j)$, $a = \sqrt{2/(\pi d)}$.

Rotational symmetry $\Rightarrow \mathbb{E}[\widetilde x \mid x] = \alpha \cdot x$.

$$\alpha \;=\; \mathbb{E}\langle x, \widetilde x\rangle \;=\; d \cdot a \cdot \mathbb{E}|Y_1| \;=\; d \cdot \dfrac{2}{\pi d} \;=\; \dfrac{2}{\pi}.$$

Hence $\mathbb{E}\langle y, \widetilde x\rangle = \tfrac{2}{\pi} \cdot \langle y, x\rangle$ — biased low.

Geometric Reason — Shrunken Norm

$x$ $\widetilde x$ $\|x\|=1$

$1$-bit reconstruction has constant magnitude per coordinate:

$$\|\widetilde x\|^2 \;=\; \|\widetilde Y\|^2 \;=\; d \cdot a^2 \;=\; \tfrac{2}{\pi}.$$

Same direction as $x$, but only $\sqrt{2/\pi} \approx 0.80$ as long.

Every projection shrunk by the same factor $\Rightarrow$ multiplicative bias.

Need an unbiased estimator — enter QJL.
05

QJL and Inner‑Product TURBOQUANT

One-bit unbiased sketch, applied to the residual

Johnson–Lindenstrauss Lemma

Theorem (JL, 1984).

For any $n$ points $x_1, \ldots, x_n \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and any $\varepsilon \in (0, 1)$, there exists a linear map $A: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^k$ with $k = \mathcal{O}\!\bigl(\varepsilon^{-2}\log n\bigr)$ such that

$$(1-\varepsilon)\,\|x_i - x_j\|^2 \;\le\; \|A x_i - A x_j\|^2 \;\le\; (1+\varepsilon)\,\|x_i - x_j\|^2 \quad \forall i, j.$$

A random Gaussian matrix $A = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt k}\,S$ with $S_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ works with high probability.

Pairwise distances survive an $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$-dim sketch — independent of the ambient dim $d$.

JL Intuition

Fix any unit $u \in \mathbb{R}^d$.

For $s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$:  $\langle s, u\rangle \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.

Stack $k$ rows of $S$. Then $\|S u\|^2 \sim \chi^2_k$.

Concentrates around $k$ with relative deviation $\mathcal{O}\!\bigl(1/\sqrt k\bigr)$.

  • $k = \Theta(\varepsilon^{-2}\log n)$ $\;\Rightarrow\;$ each of $\binom n2$ pairs survives w.p. $\ge 1 - 1/n^2$.
  • Union bound $\Rightarrow$ all pairs simultaneously.
Random projection preserves distances because $\chi^2$ concentrates · geometry of i.i.d. Gaussian rows.

JL Preserves Inner Products Too

From the polarization identity

$$\langle u, v\rangle \;=\; \tfrac14\bigl(\|u+v\|^2 - \|u-v\|^2\bigr),$$

a distance-preserving sketch is also an inner-product-preserving sketch:

$$\bigl|\langle A u, A v\rangle - \langle u, v\rangle\bigr| \;\le\; \varepsilon\,\|u\|\,\|v\|.$$
For TURBOQUANT we only care about $\langle q, k\rangle$ — JL is exactly the right tool.

From JL to QJL (1 bit / coord)

Plain JL stores $k$ floats per vector. To compress further, quantize the sketch with one bit per coordinate:

$$Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}(u) \;=\; \operatorname{sign}(S\, u) \;\in\; \{-1, +1\}^d.$$

Throws away magnitudes — keeps only the sign pattern of the projection. Strikingly, with the right decoder $Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}^{-1}$, this still gives an unbiased estimator of $\langle y, u\rangle$.

QJL = JL + 1-bit sign quantization. Next slide: precise statement.

QJL Definition

Let $S \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ have i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ entries. For $u \in S^{d-1}$:

$$Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}(u) \;=\; \operatorname{sign}(S u) \;\in\; \{-1, +1\}^d, \qquad Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}^{-1}(z) \;=\; \dfrac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{d} \cdot S^\top z.$$

One bit per coordinate. Unbiased estimator of $u$.

Lemma — Unbiased with $1/d$ Variance

Lemma (QJL).

$$\mathbb{E}_S\langle y,Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}^{-1}(Q_{\mathrm{qjl}}(u))\rangle = \langle y,u\rangle, \qquad \operatorname{Var}_S(\cdot)\;\le\;\dfrac{\pi}{2d}\|y\|^2.$$

Unbiasedness. Write $z_i = \operatorname{sign}(s_i^\top u)$, $T_i = (s_i^\top y)\,z_i$. Gaussian identity $\mathbb{E}[s\operatorname{sign}(s^\top u)] = \sqrt{2/\pi}\,u$ gives $\mathbb{E}\,T_i = \sqrt{2/\pi}\,\langle y,u\rangle$; sum and rescale:

$$\mathbb{E}_S\langle y,\widehat u\rangle = \tfrac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{d}\sum_{i=1}^d \mathbb{E}\,T_i = \tfrac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{d}\cdot d\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\langle y,u\rangle = \langle y,u\rangle.$$

Variance. $T_i$ i.i.d., Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\mathbb{E}\,T_i^2 \le \mathbb{E}(s_i^\top y)^2 = \|y\|^2$:

$$\operatorname{Var}_S\langle y,\widehat u\rangle = \tfrac{\pi/2}{d^2}\cdot d\cdot\operatorname{Var}(T_1) \;\le\; \tfrac{\pi/2}{d^2}\cdot d\cdot\|y\|^2 = \tfrac{\pi}{2d}\|y\|^2.$$

Two-Stage Decomposition

$x$ $\widetilde x_{\mathrm{mse}}$ $r$

$x = \widetilde x_{\mathrm{mse}} + r$.  Estimate each piece separately:

$$\langle y, x\rangle \;=\; \langle y, \widetilde x_{\mathrm{mse}}\rangle \;+\; \langle y, r\rangle.$$
  • MSE term ($b{-}1$ bits) — deterministic, biased.
  • QJL on $r$ ($1$ bit) — unbiased; variance $\propto \|r\|^2$.
Spend bits on MSE first — shrink $\|r\|$, then sketch.

Algorithm: TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{prod}}$

TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{prod}}$
  1. Encode$(x, b)$: $\mathrm{idx} = \mathrm{TQ}_{\mathrm{mse}}(x, b{-}1)$   $\bigl((b{-}1) \cdot d$ bits$\bigr)$.
  2. $r = x - \mathrm{TQ}_{\mathrm{mse}}^{-1}(\mathrm{idx})$,   $\gamma = \|r\|$.
  3. $q = \operatorname{sign}(S \cdot r) \in \{-1, +1\}^d$   $\bigl(d$ bits$\bigr)$. Return $(\mathrm{idx}, q, \gamma)$.
  4. Decode: $\widetilde x = \mathrm{TQ}_{\mathrm{mse}}^{-1}(\mathrm{idx}) + \gamma \cdot \tfrac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{d} \cdot S^\top q$.

Total budget: $b \cdot d$ bits. Stage 1 deterministic; stage 2 random.

Theorem: TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{prod}}$

Theorem (TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{prod}}$).

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\langle y, \widetilde x\rangle &\;=\; \langle y, x\rangle, \\ D_{\mathrm{prod}} &\;\le\; \tfrac{\sqrt{3}\,\pi^2\,\|y\|^2}{d} \cdot 4^{-b}. \end{aligned}$$

Bias: $\widehat{\langle y,x\rangle} = \langle y,\widetilde x_{\mathrm{mse}}\rangle + \widehat{\langle y,r\rangle}$, both terms exact/unbiased. Variance: QJL Lemma on $r$ gives $\operatorname{Var} \le \tfrac{\pi}{2d}\|y\|^2\|r\|^2$; substitute $\|r\|^2 \le \tfrac{\sqrt3\pi}{2}\cdot 4^{-(b-1)}$ (MSE bound, $b{-}1$ bits):

$$D_{\mathrm{prod}} \;\le\; \tfrac{\pi}{2d}\|y\|^2 \cdot \tfrac{\sqrt3\pi}{2}\cdot 4^{-(b-1)} \;=\; \tfrac{\sqrt3\,\pi^2\,\|y\|^2}{d}\cdot 4^{-b}.$$
06

KV Cache Quantization

What's actually quantized, and how

Which Quantizer for $K$ and $V$?

Keys $k_s$

$$\alpha_{t,s} \propto \exp\!\bigl(\langle q_t, k_s\rangle/\sqrt d\bigr).$$

Score is $\langle q, k\rangle$ — preserve inner product.

Values $v_s$

$$o_t = \sum_s \alpha_{t,s}\,v_s = \langle\alpha_{t,\cdot},V_{:}\rangle.$$

Output is linear in $v$ — preserve inner product.

Both $K$ and $V$ quantized via TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{prod}}$.

TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{prod}}$ Wraps TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{mse}}$

The two quantizers are not run separately — the IP version composes the MSE version.

One call to TURBOQUANT$_{\mathrm{prod}}(x, b)$
  1. Stage 1. $\mathrm{idx} \leftarrow \mathrm{TQ}_{\mathrm{mse}}(x, b{-}1)$.   Deterministic, biased.
  2. Residual. $r \leftarrow x - \mathrm{TQ}_{\mathrm{mse}}^{-1}(\mathrm{idx})$.   $\gamma \leftarrow \|r\|$.
  3. Stage 2. $q \leftarrow \operatorname{sign}(S r)$ (QJL, 1 bit).   Unbiased, variance $\propto \gamma^2$.
  4. Output. $(\mathrm{idx}, q, \gamma)$.   $b\cdot d$ bits total.

No separate $Q_{\mathrm{mse}}$ vs $Q_{\mathrm{prod}}$ choice at runtime — one composed call per vector.

Practical Pieces

  • Rotation $\Pi \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ — random Hadamard transform (RHT): $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$, not $d^2$.
  • Scalar codebook $\{c_i\}$ — precomputed offline by Lloyd–Max on $f_d$; shipped once with the model. No calibration.
  • Sketch matrix $S$ — deterministic from a PRNG seed; regenerated on the fly, never stored.
  • Per-token storage — $(b{-}1)\cdot d$-bit indices, $d$-bit sign vector, scalar $\gamma$. About $b\cdot d$ bits.

Dimensions Used in the Paper

$\Pi \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ is applied per attention head — $d$ = head dimension, not the full model dim.

KV cache

Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Ministral-7B-Instruct.

Head dim $\;d = 128\;$ for Llama-3.1.

Rotation block size $= 128$.

Nearest-neighbor search

GloVe — $d = 200$.

OpenAI3 embeddings — $d = 1536$ and $d = 3072$.

RHT is cheap because the rotated block is small ($d{=}128$ per head) — $\mathcal{O}(d \log d)$ per head, per token.

Empirical Behavior

Long-context decoding with TURBOQUANT-quantized KV cache (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, head dim $128$):

  • $\sim 3.5$ bits/channel — quality matches FP16 within noise.
  • $\sim 2.5$ bits/channel — gradual degradation begins.
  • Online + data-oblivious — the codebook never sees the user prompt.
Distortion exponent is information-theoretically optimal; quality numbers depend on the model and benchmark.

Recap

  • Rotation: turns any unit vector into a sphere point with known coordinate density $f_d$.
  • MSE TURBOQUANT: Lloyd–Max on $f_d$ achieves $D_{\mathrm{mse}}\le\tfrac{\sqrt 3\pi}{2}\cdot 4^{-b}$.
  • Lower bound: sphere SLB + Yao $\Rightarrow$ exponent $4^{-b}$ optimal.
  • Bias: MSE shrinks projections by $2/\pi$ at one bit.
  • QJL: one-bit unbiased linear-functional estimator with $\mathcal{O}(1/d)$ variance.
  • IP TURBOQUANT: two-stage; unbiased, $D_{\mathrm{prod}} \le \tfrac{\sqrt 3\pi^2\|y\|^2}{d}\cdot 4^{-b}$.
