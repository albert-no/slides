01

The Definition

Two worlds, one inequality

Neighboring Datasets

$D$ and $D'$ are neighbors if they differ in one person's record.

$$D \sim D' \iff |D \,\triangle\, D'| = 1.$$

$\triangle$ = symmetric difference: add, remove, or replace one row.

$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-Differential Privacy

Definition

A randomized $M$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP if for all neighbors $D \sim D'$ and every measurable $S \subseteq \mathrm{range}(M)$:

$$\Pr[M(D) \in S] \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon}\,\Pr[M(D') \in S] \;+\; \delta.$$

$\delta = 0$ gives pure $\varepsilon$-DP.

$\varepsilon$-DP: Dwork, McSherry, Nissim, and Smith, TCC 2006. $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ relaxation: Dwork, Kenthapadi, McSherry, Mironov, and Naor, EUROCRYPT 2006.

The Budget $\varepsilon$ and Multiplier $e^{\varepsilon}$

Rearranged, the definition bounds a log-odds ratio.

$$\left|\log \frac{\Pr[M(D) \in S]}{\Pr[M(D') \in S]}\right| \;\le\; \varepsilon \quad (\delta = 0).$$

$\varepsilon = 1 \Rightarrow e^{\varepsilon} \approx 2.7$: any outcome's odds shift by at most $2.7\times$.

What $\delta$ Buys

$\delta$ is an additive slack: a small probability the $e^{\varepsilon}$ bound fails.

  • Lets an $\varepsilon$-bound break on a rare outcome set
  • Needed to use Gaussian noise (unbounded tails)
  • Rule of thumb: $\delta \ll 1/n$ for $n$ people

A useful $\delta$ is cryptographically small, not merely "small".

Post-Processing

Any $f$ applied to a DP output stays DP.

Claim + one-line proof

If $M$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP, so is $f \circ M$. For any $T$, let $S = f^{-1}(T)$; then

$$\Pr[f(M(D)) \in T] = \Pr[M(D) \in S] \le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[M(D') \in S] + \delta.$$

The preimage $S$ inherits the guarantee. No extra budget spent.

02

Mechanisms

Calibrating noise to sensitivity

Sensitivity $\Delta q$

How much can one person move a query $q$? Take the worst case over neighbors.

$$\Delta q \;=\; \max_{D \sim D'} \; \lVert q(D) - q(D') \rVert.$$

A count has $\Delta q = 1$; $\ell_1$ norm gives Laplace, $\ell_2$ gives Gaussian.

The Laplace Mechanism

Add $\ell_1$-calibrated Laplace noise; this gives pure $\varepsilon$-DP.

$$M(D) = q(D) + \mathrm{Lap}\!\left(\frac{\Delta q}{\varepsilon}\right), \qquad p(z) \propto e^{-\varepsilon|z|/\Delta q}.$$

Scale $b = \Delta q / \varepsilon$: smaller $\varepsilon$ or bigger $\Delta q$ ⇒ wider noise.

Dwork, McSherry, Nissim, and Smith, "Calibrating Noise to Sensitivity", TCC 2006.

Why Laplace Works

The density ratio at any output is bounded by the triangle inequality.

$$\frac{p_D(z)}{p_{D'}(z)} = e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{\Delta q}\left(|z - q(D)| - |z - q(D')|\right)} \;\le\; e^{\varepsilon}.$$

Since $\lVert q(D) - q(D')\rVert_1 \le \Delta q$, the exponent never exceeds $\varepsilon$.

The Gaussian Mechanism

Add $\ell_2$-calibrated Gaussian noise; this gives $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP.

$$M(D) = q(D) + \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\; \sigma^2 I\right), \qquad \sigma \;\ge\; \frac{\Delta_2 q \,\sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}}{\varepsilon}.$$

Unbounded tails force $\delta > 0$; the workhorse inside private deep learning.

Dwork and Roth, "The Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy", 2014.

Randomized Response, Formally

Answer truthfully w.p. $\tfrac12$; else flip a fair coin. Privacy and de-biasing:

$$\varepsilon = \ln\frac{\Pr[\text{yes}\mid\text{true yes}]}{\Pr[\text{yes}\mid\text{true no}]} = \ln 3, \qquad \Pr[\text{yes}] = \tfrac12 p + \tfrac14.$$

Observe $70\%$ yes ⇒ solve $0.70 = \tfrac12 p + \tfrac14$ ⇒ true rate $p = 0.90$.

Warner, "Randomized Response", JASA 1965.
03

Private Learning

DP-SGD and composition

DP-SGD

Bound each example's influence, then hide it under noise.

  1. Per-example gradient $g_i = \nabla_\theta \ell(\theta, x_i)$.
  2. Clip: $\bar g_i = g_i / \max\!\left(1, \lVert g_i \rVert_2 / C\right)$.
  3. Add noise: $\tilde g = \tfrac{1}{B}\big(\sum_i \bar g_i + \mathcal{N}(0,\, \sigma^2 C^2 I)\big)$.
  4. Step $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\,\tilde g$; the accountant updates $\varepsilon$.
Abadi, Chu, Goodfellow, McMahan, Mironov, Talwar, and Zhang, "Deep Learning with Differential Privacy", ACM CCS 2016.

Clip Sets the Sensitivity

Clipping to $C$ makes the batch-sum sensitivity exactly $C$.

$$\Delta_2\Big(\textstyle\sum_i \bar g_i\Big) \le C \quad\Rightarrow\quad \sigma C = \text{Gaussian scale}.$$

Noise multiplier $\sigma$ is unitless: it scales with $C$, so tuning $C$ is free of privacy cost.

Composition

Budgets add up over many releases; training is thousands of steps.

$$\text{basic: } \left(\textstyle\sum_k \varepsilon_k,\; \sum_k \delta_k\right), \qquad \text{advanced: } \varepsilon' \approx \sqrt{2k\ln(1/\delta')}\,\varepsilon.$$

Advanced composition turns the linear sum into a $\sqrt{k}$ growth.

Dwork, Rothblum, and Vadhan, "Boosting and Differential Privacy", FOCS 2010.

Rényi DP and the Accountant

The tight accountant tracks a Rényi divergence, then converts to $(\varepsilon,\delta)$.

$(\alpha,\varepsilon)$-Rényi DP

$D_\alpha(M(D)\,\Vert\,M(D')) \le \varepsilon$ for order $\alpha > 1$. Gaussian steps compose by adding per-step Rényi costs, then a final conversion gives the reported $\varepsilon$.

This is what "moments accountant" computes at training scale.

Mironov, "Rényi Differential Privacy", IEEE CSF 2017.
