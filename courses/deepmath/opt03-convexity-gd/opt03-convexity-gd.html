One Update

Step against the gradient

Goal: Minimize One Differentiable $f$

$$\text{minimize } f(x) \text{ over } x \in \mathbb{R}^n, \qquad f:\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R} \text{ differentiable}$$
local min local max $f_{\text{low}}$ tail tends to $f_{\text{low}}$, never reaches it

Deterministic: the exact gradient $\nabla f(x)$ at every step

Nonconvex case: no minimizer assumed, only a lower bound

Schematic 1-D slice of one nonconvex $f$ (our illustration; formula in the note)

Recall: The Gradient Gives First-Order Change

$$\begin{aligned} \nabla f(x) &= \Big(\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}(x),\, \dots,\, \tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_n}(x)\Big) \in \mathbb{R}^n\\[6px] f(x + h) &= f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), h\rangle + o(\|h\|) \quad \text{as } h \to 0 \end{aligned}$$

Rate of change along a direction $v$:  $\langle \nabla f(x), v\rangle$

Local statement: it says nothing about one fixed, finite step

Small Steps Against the Gradient Go Downhill

long step (dashed): $f$ rises $x$ with $f'(x) \gt 0$ short step (solid): $f$ drops

Move along $-\nabla f(x)$: downhill for a small enough step

How far is safe? The smoothness constant $L$ decides

Illustration: $f(u)=u^2$ from $u=1.5$, steps to $u=0.9$ and $u=-1.8$ (our calculation)

$L$-Smoothness Bounds Gradient Change

Definition.

Differentiable $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ is $L$-smooth ($L \gt 0$) if for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$\|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\| \;\leq\; L\, \|x - y\| \qquad (\text{Euclidean norm})$$

A condition on the gradient, not on $f$ itself

Example: $f(x)=\tfrac12 x^2$ is $1$-smooth, yet $f$ is not Lipschitz

No convexity: smooth does not mean convex

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Lipschitz continuous gradient assumption”

One Fixed Step: $1/L$

Gradient descent, step $1/L$

  1. Input: smoothness constant $L$, any start $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$
  2. For $t = 0, 1, \dots, T-1$:  $x_{t+1} = x_t - \tfrac{1}{L}\nabla f(x_t)$
  3. Output: iterates $x_0, x_1, \dots, x_T$

The same update in all three guarantees

$L$ assumed known; no line search, no other step size

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD” (step $\alpha \le 1/L$; here $\alpha = 1/L$)

Running Example: $L = 4$

$f(x) = 2x_1^2 + \tfrac12 x_2^2$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$,  gradient $\nabla f(x) = (4x_1,\, x_2)$

$$\begin{aligned} \|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\| &= \|(4z_1,\, z_2)\|, \qquad z = x - y\\[4px] &= \sqrt{16 z_1^2 + z_2^2} \;\leq\; 4\,\|z\| \end{aligned}$$

Equality at $z = (1, 0)$: no smaller $L$ works

So $L = 4$ and the step is $1/L = 1/4$

One Descent Inequality

Smoothness bounds every step

Smoothness Gives a Quadratic Upper Bound

Lemma (descent lemma).

$f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ differentiable and $L$-smooth. For all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$f(y) \;\leq\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x),\, y - x\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|y - x\|^2$$

Linear model plus $\tfrac{L}{2}\|y-x\|^2$: valid for every finite step

No convexity needed

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Descent lemma”

The Upper Bound Touches $f$ at $x$

$x$ dashed: upper bound solid: $f$ thin: tangent at $x$

Equal at $y = x$, above $f$ everywhere else

Illustration: $f(u)=u^2$, $x=-0.6$, $L=3$ (our drawing; $f$ is $2$-smooth, so $L=3$ is valid)

Proof Step 1: Follow the Segment

$$\begin{aligned} h &= y - x, \qquad \rho(s) = f(x + s h), \quad s \in [0, 1]\\[4px] \rho'(s) &\stackrel{\text{(chain rule)}}{=} \langle \nabla f(x + s h),\, h\rangle\\[4px] f(y) - f(x) &\stackrel{\text{(FTC)}}{=} \rho(1) - \rho(0) = \int_0^1 \rho'(s)\, ds \end{aligned}$$

$s$ marks a point on the segment, not the iteration index $t$

FTC applies: $\nabla f$ is Lipschitz, so $\rho'$ is continuous

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Descent lemma”

Proof Step 2: Smoothness Bounds the Drift

Compare the slope at $s$ with the slope at $0$:

$$\begin{aligned} \rho'(s) - \rho'(0) &= \langle \nabla f(x + s h) - \nabla f(x),\, h\rangle\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(Cauchy-Schwarz)}}{\leq} \|\nabla f(x + s h) - \nabla f(x)\|\; \|h\|\\[4px] &\stackrel{(L\text{-smooth})}{\leq} L\,\|s h\|\; \|h\| \;=\; L s\, \|h\|^2 \end{aligned}$$

The points $x + sh$ and $x$ are $s\|h\|$ apart

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Descent lemma”

Proof Step 3: Integrating $s$ Gives $\tfrac{1}{2}$

$$\begin{aligned} f(y) &\stackrel{\text{(Step 1)}}{=} f(x) + \int_0^1 \rho'(s)\, ds\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(Step 2)}}{\leq} f(x) + \int_0^1 \big(\rho'(0) + L s\,\|h\|^2\big)\, ds\\[4px] &= f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x),\, y - x\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|y - x\|^2 \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

$\int_0^1 L s\, ds = \tfrac{L}{2}$: the coefficient in the lemma

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Descent lemma”

The Step $1/L$ Decreases $f$

Lemma at $x = x_t$ with $y = x_{t+1} = x_t - \tfrac{1}{L} g_t$ where $g_t = \nabla f(x_t)$:

$$\begin{aligned} f(x_{t+1}) &\leq f(x_t) + \big\langle g_t,\, -\tfrac{1}{L} g_t\big\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\Big\|\tfrac{1}{L} g_t\Big\|^2\\[4px] &= f(x_t) - \frac{1}{L}\|g_t\|^2 + \frac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2 \;=\; f(x_t) - \frac{1}{2L}\|g_t\|^2 \end{aligned}$$

No convexity: $f$ never increases; strict drop whenever $g_t \neq 0$

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD” (with $\alpha = 1/L$)

One Step Zeroes the Stiff Coordinate

Running example, step $1/L = 1/4$:

$$\begin{aligned} x^{+} &= x - \tfrac{1}{4}\,(4x_1,\, x_2)\\[4px] &= \big((1 - \tfrac{4}{4})\,x_1,\; (1 - \tfrac{1}{4})\,x_2\big) \;=\; \big(0,\; \tfrac{3}{4}x_2\big) \end{aligned}$$

Coordinate 1, curvature $4$: factor $0$, exactly zero after one step

Coordinate 2, curvature $1$: factor $\tfrac{3}{4}$ every step

Nonconvex: Stationarity

Small gradients, not minima

Nonconvex Guarantee: Small Squared Gradient

Theorem (nonconvex, step $1/L$).

$f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ differentiable, $L$-smooth, and $f(x) \geq f_{\text{low}}$ for all $x$. For any $x_0$ and every $T \geq 1$:

$$\min_{0 \leq t \lt T}\; \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2 \;\leq\; \frac{2L\,\big(f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}\big)}{T}$$

Best of the $T$ squared gradients $t = 0, \dots, T-1$; not a gap to a minimum

$f_{\text{low}}$ need not be attained: no minimizer assumed

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD” (stated for step $\alpha \le 1/L$)

Proof Step 1: Sum the Decreases

Add the descent inequality for $t = 0, \dots, T-1$; the $f$ terms telescope:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2L}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2 &\leq \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \big(f(x_t) - f(x_{t+1})\big)\\[4px] &= f(x_0) - f(x_T) \;\stackrel{(f \,\geq\, f_{\text{low}})}{\leq}\; f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}} \end{aligned}$$

A finite budget $f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}$ caps the total squared gradient

Only $f(x_T) \geq f_{\text{low}}$ is used: no minimizer needed

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD”

Proof Step 2: Minimum at Most Average

$$\begin{aligned} \min_{0 \leq t \lt T} \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2 &\leq \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(Step 1)}}{\leq} \frac{2L\,\big(f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}\big)}{T} \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned}$$

$T$ terms, so the smallest is at most their average

A bound on the best iterate, not on the last one

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD”

Stationary Does Not Mean Minimum

Q: Does nonconvex GD reach a local optimum at rate $1/T$?

A: No. The $1/T$ rate is for $\min_t \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2$ only.

$\|\nabla f(x)\| \leq \varepsilon$: what the theorem gives stationary, $\nabla f(x) = 0$: also maxima, saddles local minimum: not guaranteed global minimum

Scope: $L$-smooth, bounded below, step $1/L$, no further assumptions

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD” (limit may be a maximum or saddle)

GD Can Stay at a Maximum

$x_0 = \pi$: $f'(\pi) = 0$, $f''(\pi) = -1$ min min
$$x_{t+1} = x_t - \sin x_t \;\;(\text{step } 1/L = 1), \qquad x_0 = \pi \;\Rightarrow\; x_t = \pi \text{ for all } t$$

Assumptions hold: $f(x) = 1 - \cos x \geq 0$ and $L = 1$

Existence counterexample: exact start $x_0 = \pi$, not typical behaviour

The Square Changes the Rate

$$\begin{aligned} \min_{0 \leq t \lt T} \|\nabla f(x_t)\| &\leq \sqrt{\frac{2L\,\big(f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}\big)}{T}} \;=\; O\big(1/\sqrt{T}\big)\\[6px] \text{some } \|\nabla f(x_t)\| \leq \varepsilon,\ t \lt T \quad &\Longleftarrow\quad T \;\geq\; \frac{2L\,\big(f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}\big)}{\varepsilon^2} \end{aligned}$$

Bound for tolerance $\varepsilon/10$: $100\times$ the iterations

Constants $L$ and $f(x_0) - f_{\text{low}}$ depend on the problem

Best iterate only: no $O(1/T)$ gradient-norm rate, no local minimum

Cornell CS4787/5777 (Fall 2026), Lecture 5, “Convergence of GD”; $T$ count: our derivation

Convex: Optimality

Function gap at rate 1/T

Definition — Convex Function

Definition.

$f:\mathbb{R}^d \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is convex if for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and $t \in [0,1]$:

$$f\big(tx + (1-t)y\big) \;\leq\; t f(x) + (1-t) f(y)$$
  • Left: $f$ at a point on the segment from $y$ to $x$
  • Right: the chord value at the same point

First-Order Characterization

Property (tangent floor).

$f$ differentiable and convex. Then for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

$$f(y) \;\geq\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x),\, y - x\rangle$$
  • The tangent plane at any $x$ sits below the whole graph
  • Follows directly from the chord definition

Assumptions and Goal

  • $f$ convex, $L$-smooth, minimizer $x^\star$
  • Constant step $0 \lt \alpha \leq 1/L$; iterates $x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_T$
$$\text{Goal:}\qquad f(x_T) - f(x^\star) \;\leq\; \frac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha T}$$

§06 gave convergence; now convexity buys a rate

Theorem 8 — $O(1/T)$ Convergence

Theorem 8.

$f$ convex, $L$-smooth, minimizer $x^\star$; GD with $\alpha \leq 1/L$. Then

$$f(x_T) - f(x^\star) \;\leq\; \frac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha T} \;\;\stackrel{(\alpha = 1/L)}{=}\;\; \frac{L\,\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2T}$$

only the starting distance and $T$ appear — dimension-free

Proof Overview

  1. One step through the lid: guaranteed decrease  (Lemma 1)
  2. Bring in $x^\star$: convexity floor at $x$  (§02)
  3. Complete the square: gap $\leq$ change in squared distance  (TR)
  4. Telescope over $T$ steps  (TR: telescoping)
  5. Last iterate $\leq$ average  (monotone from step 1)

Step 1 — Guaranteed Decrease

$$\begin{aligned} f(x^+) &\stackrel{\text{(Thm 7 proof)}}{\leq} f(x) - \alpha\Big(1 - \frac{L\alpha}{2}\Big)\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\\[4px] &\stackrel{(\alpha \leq 1/L)}{\leq} f(x) - \frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \end{aligned}$$
  • $\alpha \leq 1/L \Rightarrow L\alpha/2 \leq 1/2$: coefficient at least $\alpha/2$
  • Notation: $x = x_{i-1}$, $x^+ = x_i$ for one generic step

Step 2 — Bring In the Optimum

$$\nabla f(x)^{\top}(x^\star - x) \;\leq\; f(x^\star) - f(x) \qquad \text{(convexity floor at } x\text{)}$$
$$\Rightarrow\quad f(x^+) - f(x^\star) \;\leq\; \nabla f(x)^{\top}(x - x^\star) - \frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$$

add the floor to Step 1 — the unknown $f(x)$ cancels out

Step 3 — the Distance Identity

$$\begin{aligned} f(x^+) - f(x^\star) &\stackrel{\text{(Step 2)}}{\leq} \nabla f(x)^{\top}(x - x^\star) - \frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(complete sq.)}}{=} \frac{1}{2\alpha}\Big(\|x - x^\star\|^2 - \|x^+ - x^\star\|^2\Big) \end{aligned}$$

Each value gap is paid in squared distance burned — a telescoping shape.

Step 4 — Telescope

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{i=1}^{T} \big(f(x_i) - f(x^\star)\big) &\stackrel{\text{(Step 3, each } i\text{)}}{\leq} \frac{1}{2\alpha} \sum_{i=1}^{T} \Big(\|x_{i-1} - x^\star\|^2 - \|x_i - x^\star\|^2\Big)\\[4px] &\stackrel{\text{(telescope)}}{=} \frac{1}{2\alpha}\Big(\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2 - \|x_T - x^\star\|^2\Big) \;\stackrel{\text{(drop)}}{\leq}\; \frac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha} \end{aligned}$$

$T$ inequalities added; the middle distances all cancel

Step 5 — From Sum to Last Iterate

$$f(x_T) - f(x^\star) \;\stackrel{\text{(monotone)}}{\leq}\; \frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T} \big(f(x_i) - f(x^\star)\big) \;\stackrel{\text{(Step 4)}}{\leq}\; \frac{\|x_0 - x^\star\|^2}{2\alpha T} \qquad \blacksquare$$
  • Step 1 made $f(x_i)$ non-increasing: the last term is the smallest
  • Smallest term $\leq$ average of the terms

Proof Summary — the Skeleton

$$\text{lid} \;\rightarrow\; +\,\text{floor at } x \;\rightarrow\; \text{complete the square} \;\rightarrow\; \text{telescope} \;\rightarrow\; \text{last} \leq \text{average}$$

Key trick: telescoping — gaps priced in squared distance; the sum collapses to endpoints.

Strongly Convex: Geometric Decay

Distance shrinks every step

Definition — $\mu$-Strongly Convex

Definition.

Convex $f$ is $\mu$-strongly convex if for all $x, y$:

$$f(y) \;\geq\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x), y - x\rangle + \frac{\mu}{2}\|y - x\|^2$$
  • Tangent floor plus a parabola of curvature $\mu$
  • $f$ curves at least like a quadratic, everywhere

The Two-Parabola Sandwich

$x$ lid: curvature $L$ $f$ floor: curvature $\mu$

$\mu$-strongly convex + $L$-smooth: $f$ trapped between two parabolas touching at $x$

Theorem 10 — Linear Convergence

Theorem 10.

$f$  $\mu$-strongly convex, $L$-smooth; GD with $0 \lt \alpha \leq \frac{2}{\mu + L}$. Then

$$\|x^+ - x^\star\|^2 \;\leq\; \Big(1 - \frac{2\alpha \mu L}{\mu + L}\Big)\, \|x - x^\star\|^2$$
  • Write $c = 1 - \frac{2\alpha\mu L}{\mu + L} \lt 1$: one step = one factor of $c$

Step 4 — Unroll

$$\|x_i - x^\star\|^2 \;\leq\; c\, \|x_{i-1} - x^\star\|^2 \;\leq\; \cdots \;\leq\; c^{\,i}\, \|x_0 - x^\star\|^2$$
  • GD is a contraction: every step multiplies the error by $c \lt 1$
  • The notes' word: the map $x \mapsto x - \alpha\nabla f(x)$ is contractive

Step 5 — Function Values Follow

$$f(x_i) - f(x^\star) \;\stackrel{\text{(Thm 3, right)}}{\leq}\; \frac{L}{2}\|x_i - x^\star\|^2 \;\stackrel{\text{(Step 4)}}{\leq}\; \frac{L}{2}\, c^{\,i}\, \|x_0 - x^\star\|^2 \qquad \blacksquare$$
  • Distance contracts $\Rightarrow$ value gap contracts, same $c$

The Goal — From $1/T$ to $c^{\,T}$

$$\|x_i - x^\star\|^2 \;\leq\; c^{\,i}\, \|x_0 - x^\star\|^2, \qquad c \lt 1$$
  • Exponentially fast — called the linear rate (a line on the log plot)
  • Price: assume $\mu$-strong convexity on top of $L$-smoothness
  • Tool to build: a stronger co-coercivity

$L = 4$, $\mu = 1$:  $\alpha = \frac{1}{4} \leq \frac{2}{5}$ $\checkmark$,  $c = 1 - \frac{2 \cdot \frac{1}{4} \cdot 4}{5} = 0.6$
