Why SGD?

One step should not cost a full pass

The Bill for One GD Step

$$\theta_{k+1} \;=\; \theta_k - \alpha\,\underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \nabla \mathcal{L}_i(\theta_k)}_{N \text{ gradients, every step}}$$
  • opt03's guarantees: all still true
  • The price tag: one step = one full pass over the data

And $N$ Is Millions

Vision

Millions of labeled images

one loss term each

Language models

Web-scale training text

the notes' own example

Consequence

Full $\nabla \mathcal{L}$: unaffordable per step

even once is a lot

The Idea — Sample, Don't Sum

$$\text{pick one index } i_k \text{ at random:}\qquad \nabla \mathcal{L}_{i_k}(\theta_k) \;\approx\; \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta_k)$$
  • One gradient per step — $N$ times cheaper
  • Wrong on every step, right on average

Trade exactness for cheapness. SGD is the training algorithm of deep learning.

Today's Four Results

Unbiasedness

$\mathbb{E}[g_k] = \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta_k)$ — sampling is honest

Convex rate

$O(1/\sqrt{K})$ for the averaged iterate — full proof

Mini-batching

$b$ samples: variance drops by $1/b$

Strongly convex rate

Decreasing steps: $O(\log k / k)$ — full proof

The Finite-Sum Objective

$N$ losses, one average; the running example

The Finite-Sum Objective

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathcal{L}_i(\theta), \qquad \mathcal{L}_i(\theta) = \ell\big(h_\theta(x^{(i)}),\, y^{(i)}\big)$$
  • One term per data point — ERM, exactly as in opt02
  • Recall opt02: model $h_\theta$, loss $\ell$, minimize the empirical risk

Full Gradient Descent, Priced

$$\theta_{k+1} = \theta_k - \alpha\, \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \nabla \ell\big(h_{\theta_k}(x^{(i)}), y^{(i)}\big)$$
  • Cost per step: $N$ backpropagations
  • $K$ steps: $K \times N$ gradient evaluations

opt03's theory is unchanged — only the price changed

Running Example — Three Losses

$$\mathcal{L}_i(\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\theta - c_i)^2, \qquad c_1 = 0,\; c_2 = 3,\; c_3 = 6$$
$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{3} \mathcal{L}_i(\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\theta - 3)^2 + 3$$
  • Minimizer $\theta^\star = 3$; optimal value $\mathcal{L}^\star = 3$
  • $N = 3$ — small enough to enumerate everything

Three Losses and Their Average

each data point pulls toward its own $c_i$; the average settles at $3$

The Full Gradient by Hand

$$\nabla \mathcal{L}_i(\theta) = \theta - c_i, \qquad \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \theta - 3$$
at θ = 0i = 1i = 2i = 3average

full gradient at $0$: also $-3$ — the average of the three

One Number to Watch

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) - \mathcal{L}^\star \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}(\theta - 3)^2$$
  • The gap: how much loss is left on the table
  • Every table today reports it — theorems bound its expectation
03

The Stochastic Gradient

Sampled, unbiased, noisy

The SGD Update

  1. Sample an index $i_k$ uniformly from $\{1, \dots, N\}$
  2. Compute one gradient: $g_k = \nabla \mathcal{L}_{i_k}(\theta_k)$
  3. Step: $\theta_{k+1} = \theta_k - \alpha\, g_k$

cost per step: $1$ gradient instead of $N$

Which Gradient Do You Get?

Running example at $\theta = 0$: three equally likely outcomes

a random variable whose average is the truth

Technique Review — Uniform Expectation

Recall (prob01). Discrete expectation: $\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_x x\, p(x)$

$$i \sim \mathrm{Unif}\{1,\dots,N\}: \qquad \mathbb{E}\big[a_{i}\big] = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{1}{N}\, a_i$$

a uniform index turns a sum into an expectation — and back

Theorem 1 — Unbiasedness

Theorem 1 (SGD is unbiased)

$i_k \sim \mathrm{Unif}\{1,\dots,N\}$, independent of the past. Then for any $\theta$:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\nabla \mathcal{L}_{i_k}(\theta)\big] \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \nabla \mathcal{L}_i(\theta) \;=\; \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta)$$

"wrong every step, right on average" — made precise

Proof

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\nabla \mathcal{L}_{i_k}(\theta)\big] \;\stackrel{\text{(prob01)}}{=}\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{P}(i_k = i)\, \nabla \mathcal{L}_i(\theta) \;\stackrel{\text{(unif)}}{=}\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \nabla \mathcal{L}_i(\theta) \;=\; \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta) \qquad \blacksquare$$
  • Two lines — the design makes it true
  • Uniform weights $1/N$ match the average in $\mathcal{L}$ exactly

Worked Check — Running Example

pointpossible g (prob 1/3 each)mean of gtrue gradient

unbiased at every point — the spread never changes

Unbiased Is Not Zero

$$\text{at } \theta^\star = 3:\qquad g \in \{3,\, 0,\, -3\}, \qquad \mathbb{E}[g] = 0,\qquad g \ne 0 \text{ w.p. } 2/3$$
  • At the minimizer: the average pull vanishes, individual pulls don't
  • SGD cannot sit still — it gets kicked off $\theta^\star$

The central tension of today. Noise never sleeps — even at the solution.

Gradient = Truth + Noise

$$g_k = \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta_k) + n_k, \qquad \mathbb{E}[n_k] = 0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(n_k) = \sigma^2$$
  • Running example: $n \in \{3, 0, -3\}$ at every $\theta$
  • Recall variance (prob01): $\operatorname{Var}(n) = \mathbb{E}[n^2] = \frac{9 + 0 + 9}{3} = 6$
  • So here $\sigma^2 = 6$, uniformly in $\theta$

In Practice — Epochs

  • Practice: sample without replacement until data is exhausted
  • One full cycle = one epoch; then reshuffle, repeat

today's analysis: with replacement (independent draws) — the clean case

The Error Recursion

$$e_k = \theta_k - 3:\qquad e_{k+1} \;=\; (1-\alpha)\, e_k \;+\; \alpha\, n_k, \qquad n_k \in \{3, 0, -3\}$$
  • Contraction $(1-\alpha)$ on the old error, fresh noise injected each step
  • $n_k$ independent of $e_k$ — the draw doesn't look at the iterate

Mean Dies, Variance Survives

$$\mathbb{E}[e_k] = (1-\alpha)^k\, e_0 \;\to\; 0$$
$$v_{k+1} \stackrel{\text{(prob05: indep. sum)}}{=} (1-\alpha)^2 v_k + \alpha^2 \sigma^2 \;\;\to\;\; v_\infty = \frac{\alpha\, \sigma^2}{2 - \alpha}$$
  • Recall prob05: independent terms — variances add
  • Fixed point: solve $v = (1-\alpha)^2 v + \alpha^2 \sigma^2$

06

The $O(1/\sqrt{K})$ Theorem

Full proof, one idea per slide

Notation — Conditioning on the Past

$$\mathbb{E}_k[\,\cdot\,] \;=\; \mathbb{E}\big[\,\cdot \;\big|\; \theta_0, \dots, \theta_k\big]$$
  • Freeze the trajectory so far; average only over the fresh draw $i_k$
  • Recall conditional expectation (prob07) — a function of the past
$$\textbf{Assumption (unbiased):}\qquad \mathbb{E}_k[g_k] = \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta_k)$$

uniform sampling delivers this — Theorem 1, conditioned on the past

The Variance Assumption, Honestly

$$\textbf{Assumption (bounded variance):}\qquad \mathbb{E}_k\big[\lVert g_k - \mathbb{E}_k[g_k] \rVert^2\big] \;\le\; \sigma^2 \quad \text{for all } k$$
  • An assumption, not a theorem — it can fail
  • Running example: variance $= 6$ at every $\theta$, so $\sigma^2 = 6$ ✓

for deep nets: taken on faith, checked empirically — be aware

Bounded Gradients from Lipschitz

Recall (opt03). $\mathcal{L}$ is $G$-Lipschitz: $\lvert \mathcal{L}(x) - \mathcal{L}(y) \rvert \le G \lVert x - y \rVert$ — equivalently $\lVert \nabla \mathcal{L} \rVert \le G$

$$\mathbb{E}_k\big[\lVert g_k \rVert^2\big] \;\stackrel{\text{(prob07: bias--variance)}}{=}\; \underbrace{\lVert \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta_k) \rVert^2}_{\le\, G^2} + \underbrace{\operatorname{Var}_k(g_k)}_{\le\, \sigma^2} \;\le\; G^2 + \sigma^2$$

notes write $L$ for this constant — renamed $G$ to dodge opt03's smoothness $L$

Running Example — the Constants

  • A quadratic is not globally Lipschitz — but the run stayed in $[0, 4.125]$
  • On $[-3, 9]$: $\lvert \nabla \mathcal{L} \rvert = \lvert \theta - 3 \rvert \le 6$, so take $G = 6$
$$G^2 + \sigma^2 = 36 + 6 = 42, \qquad D = \lvert \theta_0 - \theta^\star \rvert = 3$$

honest caveat: constants valid on the interval the run lives in

Theorem 2 — SGD on Convex Problems

Theorem 2 ($O(1/\sqrt{K})$ rate)

$\mathcal{L}$ convex, $G$-Lipschitz, minimizer $\theta^\star$; unbiased $g_k$ with variance $\le \sigma^2$; constant step

$$\alpha = \frac{\lVert \theta_0 - \theta^\star \rVert}{\sqrt{G^2 + \sigma^2}\,\sqrt{K+1}} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}(\bar{\theta}_K) - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star)\big] \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{G^2 + \sigma^2}\; \lVert \theta_0 - \theta^\star \rVert}{\sqrt{K+1}}$$

for the averaged iterate $\displaystyle \bar{\theta}_K = \frac{1}{K+1} \sum_{k=0}^{K} \theta_k$

Reading the Statement

  • Averaged iterate $\bar{\theta}_K$ — not the last one (§04's fix)
  • Expected gap — the guarantee averages over the sampling
  • $\alpha$ needs the horizon $K$ and distance $D$ — a theory dial
  • No smoothness needed — Lipschitz + convex suffice

rate $O(1/\sqrt{K})$ — slower than GD's $O(1/K)$, but each step is $N\times$ cheaper

Proof Overview

  1. Expand the squared distance after one step
  2. Unbiasedness under $\mathbb{E}_k$ — the true gradient appears
  3. Convexity floor — inner product $\ge$ gap (opt03)
  4. Second moment $\le G^2 + \sigma^2$
  5. Tower — total expectation (prob07)
  6. Telescope the sum over $k$ (opt03)
  7. Balance the two terms with $\alpha$
  8. Jensen — pass to the averaged iterate (prob01)

Step 1 — Expand the Square

$$\begin{aligned}\lVert \theta_{k+1} - \theta^\star \rVert^2 \;&\stackrel{\text{(update)}}{=}\; \lVert \theta_k - \alpha g_k - \theta^\star \rVert^2\\[4pt] &=\; \lVert \theta_k - \theta^\star \rVert^2 - 2\alpha \langle g_k,\, \theta_k - \theta^\star \rangle + \alpha^2 \lVert g_k \rVert^2\end{aligned}$$
  • Pure algebra — nothing probabilistic yet
  • Every term still random through $g_k$

Step 2 — Condition on the Past

$$\mathbb{E}_k \langle g_k,\, \theta_k - \theta^\star \rangle \;\stackrel{(\theta_k \text{ frozen})}{=}\; \langle \mathbb{E}_k[g_k],\, \theta_k - \theta^\star \rangle \;\stackrel{\text{(unbiased)}}{=}\; \langle \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta_k),\, \theta_k - \theta^\star \rangle$$
  • Under $\mathbb{E}_k$: $\theta_k - \theta^\star$ is a constant — slides out by linearity
  • The only place unbiasedness enters the proof

Technique Review — the Tangent Floor

Recall (opt03). $\mathcal{L}$ convex: $\mathcal{L}(y) \ge \mathcal{L}(x) + \langle \nabla \mathcal{L}(x),\, y - x \rangle$ — tangents stay below

$$y = \theta^\star,\; x = \theta_k:\qquad \langle \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta_k),\, \theta_k - \theta^\star \rangle \;\ge\; \mathcal{L}(\theta_k) - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star)$$

the inner product dominates the gap — same move as opt03's Theorem 8

Steps 3 + 4 — the One-Step Inequality

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_k \lVert \theta_{k+1} - \theta^\star \rVert^2 &\;=\; \lVert \theta_k - \theta^\star \rVert^2 - 2\alpha \langle \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta_k), \theta_k - \theta^\star \rangle + \alpha^2\, \mathbb{E}_k \lVert g_k \rVert^2 \\[4px] &\;\stackrel{\text{(floor, 2nd moment)}}{\le}\; \lVert \theta_k - \theta^\star \rVert^2 - 2\alpha \big(\mathcal{L}(\theta_k) - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star)\big) + \alpha^2 (G^2 + \sigma^2) \end{aligned}$$

both assumptions spent — the rest is bookkeeping

Reading the One-Step Inequality

$$\mathbb{E}_k \lVert \theta_{k+1} - \theta^\star \rVert^2 \;\le\; \lVert \theta_k - \theta^\star \rVert^2 \;-\; \underbrace{2\alpha \big(\mathcal{L}(\theta_k) - \mathcal{L}^\star\big)}_{\text{progress: } \propto\, \alpha} \;+\; \underbrace{\alpha^2 (G^2 + \sigma^2)}_{\text{noise rent: } \propto\, \alpha^2}$$
  • Each step: distance falls by the gap, rises by the rent
  • Small $\alpha$: rent $\alpha^2$ dies faster than progress $\alpha$ — hope!

Technique Review — Tower Property

Recall (prob07, Theorem 1). Tower property: $\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]\big] = \mathbb{E}[X]$

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}_k[\,\cdot\,]\big] = \mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\,] \qquad \text{(average over the past too)}$$

conditional statements become unconditional — one outer expectation

Step 5 — Total Expectation

$$\mathbb{E} \lVert \theta_{k+1} - \theta^\star \rVert^2 \;\stackrel{\text{(tower)}}{\le}\; \mathbb{E} \lVert \theta_k - \theta^\star \rVert^2 - 2\alpha\, \mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}(\theta_k) - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star)\big] + \alpha^2 (G^2 + \sigma^2)$$
  • Take $\mathbb{E}$ of both sides; tower collapses $\mathbb{E}\,\mathbb{E}_k$ to $\mathbb{E}$
  • Now an inequality between plain numbers, indexed by $k$

Technique Review — Telescoping

Recall (opt03). Telescoping sum: $\sum_{k=0}^{K} (a_k - a_{k+1}) = a_0 - a_{K+1}$ — the middle cancels

$$a_k = \mathbb{E} \lVert \theta_k - \theta^\star \rVert^2: \qquad \text{one-step inequalities stack into one global bound}$$

exactly the move that proved GD's $O(1/T)$ in opt03

Step 6 — Sum and Telescope

$$2\alpha \sum_{k=0}^{K} \mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}(\theta_k) - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star)\big] \;\le\; \underbrace{\lVert \theta_0 - \theta^\star \rVert^2 - \mathbb{E}\lVert \theta_{K+1} - \theta^\star \rVert^2}_{\text{telescoped}} \;+\; (K+1)\, \alpha^2 (G^2 + \sigma^2)$$
  • Sum the one-step inequality over $k = 0, \dots, K$; distances cancel in pairs
  • Drop $-\mathbb{E}\lVert \theta_{K+1} - \theta^\star \rVert^2 \le 0$ — only helps

Step 7 — the Two Terms Appear

$$\frac{1}{K+1} \sum_{k=0}^{K} \mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}(\theta_k) - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star)\big] \;\le\; \underbrace{\frac{\lVert \theta_0 - \theta^\star \rVert^2}{2\alpha (K+1)}}_{\text{progress term } \downarrow \text{ in } \alpha} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{\alpha}{2} (G^2 + \sigma^2)}_{\text{noise term } \uparrow \text{ in } \alpha}$$
  • Divide by $2\alpha(K+1)$ — the average expected gap on the left
  • §05's dial, now as a formula

Technique Review — Balancing Two Terms

Tool. Minimize $\varphi(\alpha) = \dfrac{a}{\alpha} + b\,\alpha$ over $\alpha \gt 0$

$$\varphi'(\alpha) = -\frac{a}{\alpha^2} + b = 0 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \alpha^\star = \sqrt{a/b}, \qquad \varphi(\alpha^\star) = 2\sqrt{ab}$$
  • Optimum: the two terms are equal — perfectly balanced

Step 8 — Balance with $\alpha$

$$a = \frac{D^2}{2(K+1)},\;\; b = \frac{G^2+\sigma^2}{2}: \qquad \alpha^\star = \frac{D}{\sqrt{G^2+\sigma^2}\,\sqrt{K+1}}$$
$$\text{value} \;=\; 2\sqrt{ab} \;=\; \frac{\sqrt{G^2 + \sigma^2}\; D}{\sqrt{K+1}}, \qquad D = \lVert \theta_0 - \theta^\star \rVert$$

the $\sqrt{K+1}$ in the rate is born here — the price of balancing noise

The Balance Point, Drawn

running example, $K = 99$: $\;0.045/\alpha + 21\alpha$ — equal terms at the bottom

Technique Review — Jensen

Recall (prob01, Theorem 2). Jensen: convex $f$ pushed through an average only goes down: $f\big(\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i x_i\big) \le \tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i f(x_i)$

$$\mathcal{L}(\bar{\theta}_K) = \mathcal{L}\Big(\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^K \theta_k\Big) \;\le\; \frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^K \mathcal{L}(\theta_k)$$

the average of the losses controls the loss of the average

Step 9 — Pass to the Average

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}(\bar{\theta}_K) - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star)\big] \;\stackrel{\text{(Jensen)}}{\le}\; \frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K} \mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{L}(\theta_k) - \mathcal{L}(\theta^\star)\big] \;\stackrel{\text{(Step 8)}}{\le}\; \frac{\sqrt{G^2+\sigma^2}\, D}{\sqrt{K+1}} \;\;\blacksquare$$
  • Jensen inside the expectation, then linearity
  • The telescoped average is exactly what Step 7 bounded

Proof Summary — the Skeleton

$$\text{expand} \;\stackrel{\text{unbiased}}{\longrightarrow}\; \text{true gradient} \;\stackrel{\text{convex}}{\longrightarrow}\; \text{gap} \;\stackrel{\text{tower}}{\longrightarrow}\; \mathbb{E} \;\stackrel{\text{telescope}}{\longrightarrow}\; \text{sum} \;\stackrel{\text{balance}}{\longrightarrow}\; \sqrt{K} \;\stackrel{\text{Jensen}}{\longrightarrow}\; \bar{\theta}_K$$

Key trick: pick $\alpha$ where progress and noise pay equally — $\sqrt{K}$ is the price.

07

Mini-Batching

Average $b$ samples: variance ÷ $b$

The Mini-Batch Update

$$\theta_{k+1} \;=\; \theta_k - \alpha\, \frac{1}{b} \sum_{i \in I_k} \nabla \mathcal{L}_i(\theta_k), \qquad I_k \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\},\;\; \lvert I_k \rvert = b \ll N$$
  • A random batch of $b$ indices — between SGD ($b=1$) and GD ($b=N$)
  • What deep learning actually runs

Still Unbiased

$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{b} \sum_{j=1}^{b} \nabla \mathcal{L}_{i_j}(\theta)\Big] \;\stackrel{\text{(linearity)}}{=}\; \frac{1}{b} \sum_{j=1}^{b} \mathbb{E}\big[\nabla \mathcal{L}_{i_j}(\theta)\big] \;\stackrel{\text{(Thm 1)}}{=}\; \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta)$$
  • Each batch member is unbiased; the average of unbiased is unbiased

Technique Review — Variance of Averages

Recall (prob05). i.i.d. $X_1, \dots, X_b$ with variance $\sigma^2$: $\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_b) = \dfrac{\sigma^2}{b}$

  • Independence: variances add; averaging divides by $b^2$
  • The engine of prob05's law of large numbers — reused here verbatim

Theorem 3 — Variance ÷ $b$

Theorem 3 (mini-batch variance)

Batch of $b$ indices drawn i.i.d. uniform (with replacement); per-sample variance $\sigma^2$. Then

$$\operatorname{Var}\Big(\frac{1}{b} \sum_{j=1}^{b} \nabla \mathcal{L}_{i_j}(\theta)\Big) \;=\; \frac{\sigma^2}{b}$$

notes: subset sampling, variance even smaller — proved here: the i.i.d. case

Proof

$$g^{(1)}, \dots, g^{(b)} \text{ i.i.d. copies of the single-sample gradient}$$

prob05's variance-of-averages, applied to gradients

Strongly Convex SGD

Decreasing steps: $O(\log k / k)$

The Regularized Problem

$$\min_x\;\; F(x) \;=\; f(x) + \frac{\mu}{2} \lVert x \rVert^2, \qquad f \text{ convex, } G\text{-Lipschitz}$$
  • Ridge shape — recall opt02's regularized least squares
  • Recall opt03: convex $+$ $\frac{\mu}{2}\lVert x \rVert^2$ is $\mu$-strongly convex

Kill the Floor — Shrink the Step

$$x_{k+1} \;=\; x_k - \alpha_k \big(g_k + \mu x_k\big), \qquad \alpha_k = \frac{1}{\mu (k+1)},\qquad x_0 = 0$$
  • $\mu$-term differentiated exactly; only $f$ is sampled: $\mathbb{E}_k[g_k] = \nabla f(x_k)$
  • §05: noise floor $\propto \alpha$ — a shrinking $\alpha_k$ drags the floor to $0$
  • No horizon needed — the schedule is anytime

Running Example, Rewritten

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \underbrace{7.5 - 3\theta}_{f(\theta)} + \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}\theta^2}_{\mu = 1}$$
  • Check: $\tfrac{1}{2}(\theta - c_i)^2 = \big(\tfrac{c_i^2}{2} - c_i \theta\big) + \tfrac{1}{2}\theta^2$ — average the first part
  • $f$ linear: convex, $G = 3$ honestly (no interval caveat this time)
  • Sampled gradient of $f$: $g_k = -c_{i_k}$, so $\sigma^2 = 6$ as before

Theorem 4 — Strongly Convex SGD

Theorem 4 ($O(\log k / k)$ rate)

$f$ convex and $G$-Lipschitz; $x^\star$ minimizes $F$; unbiased $g_k$ with variance $\le \sigma^2$; steps $\alpha_k = \frac{1}{\mu(k+1)},\; x_0 = 0$. Then

$$\mathbb{E}\big[F(\bar{x}_k) - F(x^\star)\big] \;\le\; \frac{2 (G^2 + \sigma^2)}{\mu} \cdot \frac{1 + \log(k+1)}{k+1}, \qquad \bar{x}_k = \frac{1}{k+1} \sum_{i=0}^{k} x_i$$

Reading the Statement

  • $\lVert x_0 - x^\star \rVert$ is absent — the schedule erases the start
  • $\log k / k$: eventually far faster than $1/\sqrt{k}$
  • Valid at every $k$ — no horizon tuning
  • Averaged iterate again

strong convexity turns noise tolerance into a near-$1/k$ rate

Proof Overview

  1. Unroll the recursion — the iterate is a convex combination of $-g_i/\mu$
  2. Second moment of the step $\le 4(G^2 + \sigma^2)$ (Jensen twice)
  3. One-step inequality via the strong-convexity floor (opt03)
  4. Plug the schedule — a weighted telescope with a vanishing head
  5. Harmonic sum $\le 1 + \log$ (integral comparison)
  6. Jensen — pass to the average (prob01)

Technique Review — Unrolling a Recursion

Recall (opt03). $y_{k+1} = \beta_k\, y_k + z_k$ unrolls: apply repeatedly, collect products

$$y_{k+1} \;=\; \Big(\prod_{s=0}^{k} \beta_s\Big) y_0 \;+\; \sum_{i=0}^{k} \Big(\prod_{s=i+1}^{k} \beta_s\Big)\, z_i$$

opt03 unrolled GD on quadratics this way — same move, new coefficients

Step 1a — Unroll the Update

$$x_{k+1} \;=\; (1 - \alpha_k \mu)\, x_k + \alpha_k \mu \Big(\frac{-g_k}{\mu}\Big) \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; x_{k+1} = \sum_{i=0}^{k} \theta_i \Big(\frac{-g_i}{\mu}\Big)$$
$$\theta_i \;=\; \alpha_i \mu \prod_{s=i+1}^{k} (1 - \alpha_s \mu) \qquad (x_0 = 0 \text{ kills the head term})$$

each step: blend the old iterate with the new target $-g_k/\mu$

Step 1b — the Weights Are Uniform

$$\alpha_i \mu = \frac{1}{i+1},\quad 1 - \alpha_s \mu = \frac{s}{s+1}: \qquad \theta_i = \frac{1}{i+1} \cdot \frac{i+1}{i+2} \cdot \frac{i+2}{i+3} \cdots \frac{k}{k+1} = \frac{1}{k+1}$$
  • The product telescopes — every weight equals $\frac{1}{k+1}$
  • So $\sum_i \theta_i = 1$: a genuine convex combination

Worked — SGD Becomes a Running Average

$$\text{running example: } \frac{-g_j}{\mu} = c_{i_j} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; x_{k+1} = \frac{1}{k+1} \sum_{j=0}^{k} c_{i_j} \;=\; \text{mean of sampled targets}$$

this schedule computes the sample mean — prob05's LLN, rediscovered as an optimizer

Technique Review — Splitting a Square

Recall (prob01, Jensen). $\lVert \cdot \rVert^2$ is convex: $\big\lVert \tfrac{a + b}{2} \big\rVert^2 \le \tfrac{1}{2} \lVert a \rVert^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} \lVert b \rVert^2$

$$\times\, 4:\qquad \lVert a + b \rVert^2 \;\le\; 2 \lVert a \rVert^2 + 2 \lVert b \rVert^2$$

the standard price for splitting a sum inside a square

Step 2a — Split the Step's Second Moment

$$\mathbb{E} \lVert g_k + \mu x_k \rVert^2 \;\stackrel{\text{(split)}}{\le}\; 2\, \mathbb{E} \lVert g_k \rVert^2 + 2 \mu^2\, \mathbb{E} \lVert x_k \rVert^2 \;\le\; 2 (G^2 + \sigma^2) + 2 \mu^2\, \mathbb{E} \lVert x_k \rVert^2$$
  • First term: §06's decomposition again — $\mathbb{E}\lVert g_k \rVert^2 \le G^2 + \sigma^2$
  • Second term: the iterate itself — needs Step 1

Step 2b — the Iterate Is Tame

$$\mathbb{E} \lVert x_k \rVert^2 = \mathbb{E} \Big\lVert \sum_i \theta_i \frac{-g_i}{\mu} \Big\rVert^2 \;\stackrel{\text{(Jensen, convex comb.)}}{\le}\; \sum_i \theta_i\, \frac{\mathbb{E}\lVert g_i \rVert^2}{\mu^2} \;\le\; \frac{G^2 + \sigma^2}{\mu^2}$$
$$\Longrightarrow\qquad \mathbb{E} \lVert g_k + \mu x_k \rVert^2 \;\le\; 4 (G^2 + \sigma^2)$$

convex combinations cannot blow up — the whole point of Step 1

Technique Review — the Strong Floor

Recall (opt03). $F$ $\mu$-strongly convex: $F(y) \ge F(x) + \langle \nabla F(x), y - x \rangle + \frac{\mu}{2} \lVert y - x \rVert^2$

$$y = x^\star:\qquad \langle \nabla F(x_k),\, x_k - x^\star \rangle \;\ge\; F(x_k) - F(x^\star) + \frac{\mu}{2} \lVert x_k - x^\star \rVert^2$$

beats §06's tangent floor by $\frac{\mu}{2}\lVert x_k - x^\star \rVert^2$ — this term buys the rate

Step 3a — Expand, Condition

$$\mathbb{E}_k \lVert x_{k+1} - x^\star \rVert^2 \;=\; \lVert x_k - x^\star \rVert^2 - 2 \alpha_k \big\langle \nabla F(x_k),\, x_k - x^\star \big\rangle + \alpha_k^2\, \mathbb{E}_k \lVert g_k + \mu x_k \rVert^2$$
  • Same expansion as §06 Step 1 — the step is now $g_k + \mu x_k$
  • Unbiasedness: $\mathbb{E}_k[g_k + \mu x_k] = \nabla f(x_k) + \mu x_k = \nabla F(x_k)$

Step 3b — Apply Floor and Moment

$$\mathbb{E}_k \lVert x_{k+1} - x^\star \rVert^2 \;\le\; (1 - \alpha_k \mu)\, \lVert x_k - x^\star \rVert^2 - 2 \alpha_k \big(F(x_k) - F(x^\star)\big) + 4 \alpha_k^2 (G^2 + \sigma^2)$$
  • The floor's extra $\frac{\mu}{2}\lVert x_k - x^\star\rVert^2$ became a contraction $(1 - \alpha_k \mu)$
  • Moment bound from Step 2 caps the rent

Step 4 — Plug the Schedule

$$\alpha_k = \frac{1}{\mu(k+1)}:\qquad 1 - \alpha_k \mu = \frac{k}{k+1},\qquad \frac{1}{2\alpha_k} = \frac{\mu (k+1)}{2}$$
$$F(x_k) - F(x^\star) \;\le\; \frac{\mu k}{2} \lVert x_k - x^\star \rVert^2 - \frac{\mu (k+1)}{2}\, \mathbb{E}_k \lVert x_{k+1} - x^\star \rVert^2 + \frac{2 (G^2 + \sigma^2)}{\mu (k+1)}$$

divide Step 3b by $2\alpha_k$, rearrange — note the coefficients $\frac{\mu k}{2}$ and $\frac{\mu(k+1)}{2}$

Step 5a — a Weighted Telescope

$$a_i = \frac{\mu\, i}{2}\, \mathbb{E} \lVert x_i - x^\star \rVert^2: \qquad \mathbb{E}\big[F(x_i) - F(x^\star)\big] \;\le\; a_i - a_{i+1} + \frac{2(G^2+\sigma^2)}{\mu (i+1)}$$
$$\sum_{i=0}^{k} (a_i - a_{i+1}) \;\stackrel{\text{(telescope)}}{=}\; a_0 - a_{k+1} \;\le\; a_0 \;=\; \frac{\mu \cdot 0}{2} \lVert x_0 - x^\star \rVert^2 \;=\; 0$$

The magic: zero weight at $i = 0$ — the schedule erases the start.

Technique Review — the Harmonic Sum

Tool. $\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{i} \;\le\; 1 + \log n$ — boxes under the curve $1/x$

Step 5b — Sum the Noise Rents

$$\begin{aligned}\sum_{i=0}^{k} \mathbb{E}\big[F(x_i) - F(x^\star)\big] \;&\le\; 0 + \sum_{i=0}^{k} \frac{2 (G^2 + \sigma^2)}{\mu (i+1)}\\[4pt] &\stackrel{\text{(harmonic)}}{\le}\; \frac{2 (G^2 + \sigma^2)}{\mu} \big(1 + \log(k+1)\big)\end{aligned}$$
  • Telescope contributed nothing — only the rents remain
  • Rents shrink like $1/i$: their sum grows only like $\log$

Step 6 — Jensen, Once More

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbb{E}\big[F(\bar{x}_k) - F(x^\star)\big] \;&\stackrel{\text{(Jensen, prob01)}}{\le}\; \frac{1}{k+1} \sum_{i=0}^{k} \mathbb{E}\big[F(x_i) - F(x^\star)\big]\\[4pt] &\le\; \frac{2 (G^2 + \sigma^2)}{\mu} \cdot \frac{1 + \log(k+1)}{k+1} \;\;\blacksquare\end{aligned}$$
  • Divide Step 5b by $k+1$; $F$ convex — the average passes inside

Proof Summary — the Skeleton

$$\begin{aligned}&\text{unroll} \;\stackrel{\text{convex comb.}}{\longrightarrow}\; \text{tame iterates} \;\stackrel{\text{strong floor}}{\longrightarrow}\; \text{contraction} \;\stackrel{\text{schedule}}{\longrightarrow}\; a_0 = 0\\[4pt] &\qquad\stackrel{\text{harmonic}}{\longrightarrow}\; \log \;\stackrel{\text{Jensen}}{\longrightarrow}\; \bar{x}_k\end{aligned}$$

Key trick: the schedule zeroes the telescope's head — only harmonically-shrinking rents remain.

Remark — the $\log$ Is Removable

  • This bound: $O(\log k / k)$ — already far beyond $O(1/\sqrt{k})$
  • The optimal rate for this problem class: $O(1/k)$ (stated, not proved)
  • Sharper averaging schemes remove the $\log$ — beyond today

honest scoreboard: our proof is one $\log$ away from optimal
